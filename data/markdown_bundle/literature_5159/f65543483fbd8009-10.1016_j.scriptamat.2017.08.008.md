High-strain-rate superplasticity of ﬁne-grained Mg–6Zn–0.5Zr alloy
subjected to low-temperature indirect extrusion

A commercial Mg–6Zn–0.5Zr alloy was subjected to low-temperature indirect extrusion for grain reﬁnement and
the tensile properties of the extruded alloy at 250 °C were investigated. After extrusion, the alloy showedﬁnely
recrystallized grains with an average size of 1.6 μm. High-strain-rate superplasticity was observed in the ﬁne-
grained alloy, which exhibited a tensile elongation of up to 800% at a strain rate of 0.01 s−1. Experimental results
demonstrating the occurrence of grain boundary sliding during the high-strain-rate superplastic deformation are
presented.

Developing Mg alloy extrusions with more competitive mechanical
properties is necessary to support the growing need for various light-
weight components in the automotive industry [1]. Commercial Mg
alloy extrusions, however, generally suffer from insuf ﬁcient strength,
which mainly results from a coarse-grained microstructure associated
with hot processing conditions [2]. This drawback in the mechanical
properties inevitably limits the applicability of extruded Mg alloys to
various weight-sensitive applications. One possible method to over-
come this issue in Mg alloys is to reduce the processing temperature
to a critical level below which grain growth is readily retarded, thereby
heightening the strength of the Mg alloys in the extruded state.
It has recently been shown that the microstructure of Mg alloys can
be effectively controlled by the application of a low-temperature indi-
rect extrusion process specially designed to enable artiﬁcial cooling dur-
ing indirect extrusion [3,4]. It has been demonstrated that this novel
extrusion process effectively provides grain re ﬁnement for Mg alloys,
resulting in considerably increased strength levels at room temperature
[3–6]. In fact, grain size is considered a crucial factor affecting the high-
temperature plasticity of metallic materials [7]. Fine-grained Mg alloys
processed by this novel extrusion process are thus expected to have en-
hanced high-temperature plasticity compared to that of samples sub-
jected to a typical extrusion process without arti ﬁcial cooling. In this
study, a commercial Mg –6Zn–0.5Zr (ZK60) alloy was subjected to a
low-temperature indirect extrusion process for grain re ﬁnement and
the tensile properties of the ﬁne-grained alloy at 250 °C were
investigated.
The analyzed composition of the ZK60 alloy was Mg –5.7 wt% Zn –
0.54 wt% Zr. A cylindrical extrusion billet was prepared by induction
melting in a graphite crucible under an inert atmosphere with a CO
2

at an initial billet temperature of 250 °C, a ram speed of 1.3 mm s−1,a n d

consisting of water and air were provided through inlets on the stem

air pressure were 1.8 l min

In order to assess the degree of internal strain remaining in the alloy
after different tensile elongations, kernel average misorientation (KAM)
maps were obtained at the same positions as those used to obtain the
EBSD orientation maps. Here, a maximum KAM value of 3 ° was set
for the calculation of the internal strain, which was performed by mea-
suring the local lattice distortion. As can be seen in Fig. 3, the degree of
internal strain apparently decreases as the tensile elongation increases.
The KAM values in Fig. 3a and b were measured and found to be 0.95
and 0.76 for the tensile elongations of 200 and 800%, respectively. Like-
wise, when only considering the recrystallized grains shown in Fig. 3c
and d, the degree of internal strain appears to decrease with increasing
tensile elongation from 200 to 800%. Such a relatively low degree of
internal strain even after a tensile elongation as large as 800% is
considered to be possible when GBS actively occurs during tensile
deformation.
The occurrence of GBS can be veriﬁed by observing sliding offsets be-
tween adjacent grains after deformation [19–25]. In this study, sliding
offsets vertical to the specimen surface were measured to evaluate the
GBS strain ( ε
gbs) using an AFM topology map after a tensile strain of
ε = 0.2 in the high-strain-rate superplastic condition (0.01 s −1 and
250 °C). Estimation of the GBS contribution ( εgbs =ε ) by measuring the
vertical sliding offsets has been reported previously [19,25].A ss h o w n
in Fig. 4a, the vertical sliding offset (v) values were found to differ in a
range of 50–100 nm depending on the positions on the specimen sur-
face, indicating that GBS did not occur in a fully homogeneous manner
within the specimen during the tensile deformation. The v values mea-
sured from the topology map correspond to εgbs values of 0.06 –0.12
when using the relationship εgbs ≅ 1.1⋅ v/L presented for a polished sur-
face [19,25]. This reveals that the maximum GBS contribution ap-
proaches ~60%, suggesting that GBS locally acts as a dominant
deformation mechanism during the high-strain-rate superplastic defor-
mation of the ZK60 alloy.
The present study shows that high-strain-rate superplasticity can
be realized at 250 °C when the ZK60 Mg alloy is processed by low-
temperature indirect extrusion for the purpose of grain re ﬁnement.
The extruded alloy showed a microstructure mainly consisting ofﬁnely
recrystallized grains with an average size of 1.6 μm, thereby exhibiting
a tensile elongation that was as large as 800% at a fast strain rate of
0.01 s
−1. Although such a ﬁne level of grain size has already been re-
ported in ZK60 alloys processed by equal channel angular extrusion
(ECAE) [13,14], high-strain-rate superplasticity in an ECAE-processed
alloy has rarely been reported to date. Instead,ﬁne-grained ZK60 alloys
subjected to ECAE have often been found to be superplastic at strain
rates not faster than 0.001 s
−1, revealing m values of 0.3 –0.55 [13,14].
As Watanabe et al. noted[13], the appearance of superplasticity at a rel-
atively slow strain rate in the ECAE-processed alloy can be attributed to
its non-equilibrium grain boundary structure in which dislocation mo-
tion to accommodate GBS may not occur as readily as compared to the
case of a stable grain boundary structure. On the other hand, the ﬁne
grains in the present extruded ZK60 alloy are considered to form via dis-
continuous DRX at the boundaries of parent grains and/or deformation-
induced twins during hot extrusion processing. Thus, these ﬁne grains
are believed to have a relatively stable grain boundary character. This
suggests that GBS and its accommodation process are more actively op-
erative in the present extruded alloy than in the ECAE-processed alloy
during hot tensile deformation.
Since GBS is a diffusional process that is strongly affected by grain
boundary diffusion, it depends on the strain rate during hot deforma-
tion: a slower strain rate is preferable for the occurrence of GBS, thereby
enhancing tensile plasticity. As shown above, there was a considerable
increase in tensile elongation with a decrease of the strain rate from
0.02 to 0.01 s
−1. However, due to an increase in exposure time at high
temperature, grain growth can become more problematic in the activa-
tion of GBS as the strain rate decreases, leading to a decrease in the
relative fraction of grain boundaries where rapid self-diffusion can
occur. In this respect, GBS is also affected by the presence of second-
phase particles, which play a role in grain boundary pinning. As men-
tioned above, the extruded ZK60 alloy has ﬁne MgZn
2 precipitates and
these precipitates can contribute to stabilizing the ﬁne-grained struc-
ture of the alloy, as reported previously[9,10]. The aforementioned ten-
sile elongation values greater than 800% are thus believed to be
somewhat due to the presence of these precipitates.
On the other hand, degradation of the MgZn
2 precipitates occurred
even under the high-strain-rate condition at 250 °C. As shown in
Fig. 4b, the extruded ZK60 alloy subjected to a small amount of tensile
deformation at 0.01 s −1 showed a precipitate-free zone (PFZ) as well
as precipitation of coarse MgZn 2 particles around grain boundaries,
and both of these features were not visible prior to the tensile deforma-
tion. This is ascribed to a combination of the initial dissolution of the
pre-existing precipitates around grain boundaries during the superplas-
tic deformation and subsequent heterogeneous precipitation at the
grain boundaries upon cooling, suggestive of the activation of a local dif-
fusional process, such as GBS, in the high-strain-rate superplastic ZK60
alloy.

In summary, ZK60 Mg alloy was subjected to a low-temperature in-
direct extrusion process and the tensile properties of the extruded alloy
at 250 °C were investigated. After extrusion, the alloy revealed a micro-
structure mainly consisting of ﬁne DRX grains with an average size of
1.6 μm. The extruded alloy showed high-strain-rate superplasticity at
0.01 s−1 with a tensile elongation as large as 800% and a strain rate sen-
sitivity exponent of 0.5. Direct microstructural observations using AFM
and TEM suggest the occurrence of GBS in the high-strain-rate super-
plastic Mg alloy.
