Abstract. Superalloys, used for turbine blades and jet engin es for airplanes, have low heat 
conduction and high strength at elevated temperatur e. These properties make it very difficult to 
obtain effective grinding processes with high accur acy. Superalloys with fine performance under 
high temperature circumstances attract great attent ion to be used as the material for turbine blades. 
It has been expected to establish reliable efficien t grinding of superalloys at the final grinding 
process. 
In this study, the influence of wheel wear, grinding ratio, and surface roughness, with the grinding 
process of superalloys, using metal bonded CBN whee l were carefully observed. Since superalloys 
have lower heat conduction and they cause a severe wheel wear, which leads to low grinding ratio. 
However, the high grinding ratio of about 800 and the stable figure of surface roughness of Rz=2µm 
were obtained when Co-base PWA647 and X45 were ground by using metal-bonded CBN wheel. 
Consequently, the Co-base superalloys perform rathe r well producing finer grinding process than 
the intensive Ni-base or Fe-base superalloys. 
 
1. Introduction 
Superalloys with fine performance under high temperature circumstances attract great attention to 
be used as the material for turbine blades and jet engines for airplanes. They have low heat 
conduction, which leads to low grinding ratio. Thes e properties make it very difficult to obtain 
effective grinding processes with high accuracy [1, 2]. It has been expected to establish reliable 
efficient grinding of superalloys at the final grinding process [3,4,5]. An efficient grinding cannot be 
performed because of the great amount of the wheel wear when the usual grinding wheels and the 
vitrified bonded grinding wheels are used. 
The metal bonded CBN wheel with low wear was used f or grinding of superalloys and the 
variations of wheel radial wear, grinding ratio and surface roughness in proportion to stock removal 
were discussed.

3. Results and discussion 
3．． ．．1 Variations of wheel radial wear with stock removal 
Grinding experiments were conducted on six differen t kinds of superalloys and the variations of 
wheel radial wear are shown in Figure1. In the exp eriments, wheel radial wear ∆R resulted in the 
relation of R26 > In718 > A286 > N80A > PWA647 ≒X45. 
As shown in Table 1, when the work material with high hardness and tensile strength is ground, it 
turns out that wheel wear becomes large. In the cas e of grinding of R26, a refractory alloy 
containing a large amount of Ni and Cr, shows low m achinability. Similarly, In718, A286, and 
N80A containing a large amount of Ni also show low machinability 
The initial wheel wear of Co-base superalloys PWA64 7 and X45 are the smallest and that the 
increase rate of the wheel wears rose steadily with the increase of the stock removal. This fact 
proves that slight wear type grinding is conducted. 
3．． ．．2 Variations of wheel working surface with stock removal 
Figure 2 shows the SEM observations of the variation of wheel working surface. After dressing, 
the micro-cutting edges are seen on the surfaces of all the abrasive grains. With the increase of 
grinding removal, a trench is formed on the both sides of each abrasive grain and the bond tail in the 
back of each abrasive grain becomes prominent. 
The abrasive grains which ground R26 and N80A are s uddenly worn as the stock removal 
increases, which makes the surfaces of the abrasive grains rounded

N80A are ground at Vw=2000 mm 3/mm. It shows that the frank face of the abrasive g rains which 
remove much of material wore with the increase of g rinding removal. The part of abrasive grains 
which ground R26 is greatly destroyed especially in the early stage of grinding. 
When PWA647 was ground the micro-cutting edges of a brasive grains after dressing could be 
seen up to the stock removal of 2000mm 3/mm, it is considered that the tips of abrasive gra ins wore 
slightly (figure2 (c)). Wear of the frank face of a grain when PWA647 is ground is also slight. It is 
because the hardness and tensile strength of PWA647 have lower values than those of R26 or N80A. 
In stock removal of 2000mm 3/mm and stock removal of 4000mm 3/mm, the variations of the 
abrasive grain were hardly detected.

3．． ．．5 Variations of cutting chips of abrasive grains 
As shown in Figure 6, the SEM was used to find out the type of cutting chips. 
In the grinding, cutting chips vary according to th e sharpness of the abrasive grains: flow type, 
tear type and the mixed type. As shown in Fig. 2 an d Fig. 5, the cutting edges of abrasive grains 
become rounded with their wear at the early stage o f grinding process when R26 and N80A are 
ground, and it makes the grinding streaks thick. 
The cutting chips in the grinding of R26 are of tea r type due to the clingy characteristics resulted 
from the high density of Ni and Cr, while they are of mixed type (tear and flow) in the grinding of 
N80A (Fig. 6 (b)). When PWA647 is ground, most cut ting tips are of flow type. And do not cling 
to abrasive grain (Fig. 6 (c)). 
The amount of wear and the wear forms of tools grea tly influence the sharpness of cutting edge 
of the abrasive grains as shown in Figs. 1, 2, and 3, and the types of cutting chips also vary 
according to them. The above data can suggest that the forms of cutting chips are determined by the 
amount of Ni or Cr. From the facts found out from t he experiments, in the case of grinding of R26, 
a refractory alloy that contains a large amount of Ni and Cr, shows low machinability.

4. Summary 
The metal bonded CBN wheel with low wear was used f or grinding of superalloys and the 
variations of wheel radial wear, grinding ratio and surface roughness in proportion to stock removal 
were discussed. The main results obtained from this study are as follows. 
1) Wheel radial wear ∆R resulted in the relation of R26 > In718 > A286 > N80A > PWA647 ≒X45. 
In the case of grinding of R26, a refractory alloy containing a large amount of Ni and Cr, shows 
low machinability. The wheel wear of Co-base superalloys PWA647 and X45 are the smallest. 
2) The abrasive grains which ground R26 and N80A ar e suddenly worn as the stock removal 
increases. The part of abrasive grains which ground R26 is greatly destroyed especially in the 
early stage of grinding. When PWA647 was ground the micro-cutting edges of abrasive grains 
after dressing could be seen up to the stock removal of 2000mm 
3/mm.
3) The grinding ratio is about 200 when R26 is grou nd and that of N80A is around 400 respectively. 
The grinding ratio when Co-base superalloys are ground at Vw=4000mm 3/mm exceeds the figure 
800. 
4) Surface roughness Rz of PWA647,X45,N80A and N286 mostly maintains the stable figures of 
from 2.0 to 2.4µm at Vw=3000mm 3/mm, while surface roughness of R26 and In718 maint ains 
Rz=3µm. 
5) The grinding streaks of PWA647 are relatively na rrow and the pile-up on the both sides of 
grinding streak is smaller.
