Mechanical behavior of nanocrystalline TiNi alloy produced
by severe plastic deformation
R. Z. Valiev • D. V. Gunderov • A. V. Lukyanov •
V. G. Pushin
Received: 10 April 2012 / Accepted: 11 May 2012 / Published online: 30 May 2012
/C211Springer Science+Business Media, LLC 2012
Abstract Nanocrystalline (NC) Ti 494Ni50:6 alloy with a
different mean grain size in a range of 20–100 nm has been
successfully produced using severe plastic deformation by
high pressure torsion and further annealing at various
temperatures. The features of microstructure and martens-
itic transformation of the NC TiNi have been studied.
During tensile tests the effects of the grain size on
mechanical properties at room and elevated temperatures
are studied and discussed. The NC TiNi exhibits an unusual
increased strength but low ductility at room temperature.
However, it demonstrates enhanced strength and ductility
at higher temperatures.
Introduction
TiNi alloys due to their superelasticity and shape-memory
effect are promising materials for many structural and
functional applications in engineering and medicine [ 1–3].
At the same time superior properties of TiNi will be very
attractive for various advanced applications because of their
small dimensions and increased functional characteristics.
Recent investigations testify that nanostructuring is an
effective way to enhance properties of various metals and
alloys with the help of severe plastic deformation (SPD)
[4–6]. During recent years a number of investigations have
been performed in our laboratories, dealing with nano-
structuring of the TiNi alloys [ 7–13] by two most popular
SPD techniques. These techniques are high pressure torsion
(HPT) and equal-channel angular pressing (ECAP). They
allow producing effectively nanostructured states and have
an inﬂuence on deformation behavior of alloys and mar-
tensitic transformations. It is shown that high pressure tor-
sion of the TiNi alloys results in the formation of an
amorphous structure. The further annealing results in the
formation of a homogeneous nanocrystalline (NC) state
[9–11]. Recent improvements of HPT processing for larger
samples, including the disks of 20 mm in diameter,
have allowed conducting of systematic investigations of
mechanical properties in the TiNi alloy with a nanocrys-
talline structure.
This paper reports the new results of the investigations
of structure and mechanical properties of the HPT-pro-
cessed Ti
49.4Ni50.6 alloy. The present work studies the
mechanical behavior of the NC TiNi alloy at various
temperatures and strain rates. Moreover, the effects of the
grain size on mechanical properties of the NC alloy are
discussed.

Results and discussion
TEM investigations showed that the initial rod after
quenching had an austenite B2 (CsCl-type) structure with a
grain size of about 50 lm. A small amount of the Ti
4Ni2O
precipitates (less than 0.5 %) was also found in the initial
material. TEM studies have shown that almost complete
amorphization occurred in the HPT-processed alloy.
Nanocrystals are spread in accidental clusters in the amor-
phous matrix (Fig. 1a). Based on the data of differential
scanning calorimetry and in situ experiments [ 10] homo-
geneous nanocrystalline structures with different grain sizes
in the alloy subjected to HPT were obtained by controlled
annealing at 400 /C176C and higher temperatures. TEM studies
determined that annealing at 400 /C176C (5 min) did not effect
on complete crystallization of amorphous phase (Fig. 1b).
However, after annealing at 400 /C176C within 20 and 60 min a
complete nanocrystallization took place and an average
grain size was about 20 nm (Fig. 1c). According to high
resolution electron microscopy studies [ 10] after such
annealing there are no regions of amorphous phase and
grain boundaries are well delineated, although there are still
small distortions of crystal lattice near some boundaries. A
subsequent increase of the annealing temperature results in
some grain growth. For example, annealing at 450 /C176C
(20 min) leads to a visible grain growth and the formation
of structure with a mean grain size of about 40 nm
(Fig. 1d). Annealing at 500 /C176C for 20 min results in the
occurrence of a homogeneous nanostructure with a grain
size of about 50 nm. Annealing at 550 /C176C (1 h) leads to a
more intense grain growth and formation of structure with a
mean grain size of about 300 nm. Thus, HPT processing
combined with annealing at varying temperatures has

Recent X-ray studies showed [ 13] that the amorphous
halo, observed after HPT, completely disappeared after
annealing at 400 /C176C within 1 h. Signiﬁcantly broadened
(110 and 200) peaks of the TiNi B2-phase indicated the
generation of the nanocrystalline microstructure. X-ray
proﬁle analysis reveals a crystallite size and microstrain
values of 31 ± 6 nm and 1.8 ± 0.4 %, respectively. The
X-ray data on a grain size after HPT and annealing at
400 /C176C for 1 h well correlate with the results of TEM
measurements with a mean grain size 30 nm.
Simultaneously, the presence of a (100) superlattice
peak demonstrates that a long-range ordering was achieved
in the annealed structure [ 13]. The long-range order
parameters were 0.92 and 0.87 for initial CG and nano-
crystalline structures of the TiNi alloy, respectively. This
indicates that a high level of ordering can be achieved for
different grain sizes. The lower degree of order in the
nanocrystalline state is obviously due to an additional
disorder introduced by grain boundaries.
It is known that due to non-homogeneity of torsion
strain the samples processed by HPT have a heterogeneous
microstructure along the radius [ 4, 7]. However, according
to TEM studies there was no signiﬁcant difference both at
the edge (R) and in the area in the half of the radius ( /C145R)
(Fig. 2a, b) of the microstructure of the samples after HPT
(n = 5) and annealing at 400 /C176C.
Measurements of the microhardness ( Hv) distribution
along a sample diameter (Fig. 3) determine that Hv has the
maximum value at the edge of the processed samples,
where the microstructure reﬁnement is the most strong. The
Hv has the minimum value in the central part. However,
microhardness level at the edge of HPT sample is close to
the one in the /C145R area, which testiﬁes the formation of
similar nanostructures in these areas. The TEM studies
given in Fig. 2 prove this fact. More signiﬁcant decrease in
Hv is observed in the center, that determines less micro-
structure reﬁnement. Thus, one can suppose that the area
from the edge to /C145R of the HPT disk samples ( n = 5) is
sufﬁciently uniformly reﬁned. This area makes up about
80 % of the sample’s square and volume.
Microhardness tests were also conducted along the
thickness of HPT ( n = 5 turns) sample ( *0.75 mm). The
results demonstrate that the microhardness values are
similar at various depths in the ﬁxed point. The difference
in the values does not exceed the measurement errors (see
Table 1) that testiﬁes the formation of considerably
homogeneous microstructure along the depth.

It is well-known that the strain-induced martensitic
transformation in the NiTi alloys has been observed to
proceed in a localized deformation manner under a wide
range of experimental conditions [ 16, 17]. The localized
deformation exhibits typically a Liiders-type stress plateau
of 5–7 % in tension and has been recognized as being
associated with the strain-induced martensitic transforma-
tion. The upper-lower yielding at the beginning of the
stress plateau has been associated with the nucleation of the
martensite, and the stress plateau as being associated with
the propagation of the martensite which has nucleated.
The stress of martensitic transformation ( r
m) of the
nanocrystalline alloy with a grain size of 20 nm is about
450 MPa (Fig. 4, curves 3 and 4), which is three times
higher than r
m in the initial CG state ( rm & 160 MPa). It
is clearly obvious that the value of rm increases while a
grain size decreases.
It is known that cooling even down to low temperatures
does not provide a necessary driving force for martensitic
transformation B2 ? B19
0 and MT is suppressed in the
TiNi alloy with a grain size less than 40 nm [ 14]. At the
same time, this experiment shows that strain-induced
martensitic transformation is possible in the TiNi alloy
with a grain size of 20 nm, i.e., MT is signiﬁcantly pro-
moted due to deformation.
The extension of the stress–strain curve area of phase
yield is about 5 % and more (Fig. 4, curves 2–5) depending
on a regime of annealing. Then, the signiﬁcant strain
hardening takes place. Also it is associated with the elastic
response of strain-induced martensite which results in high
level of dislocation yield stress. However, the samples
processed by HPT and annealed at 400 /C176C (1 h) did not
reach UTS and dislocation yield and showed a brittle
fracturing under stress of 1750 MPa (Fig. 4, curve 4).
According to [6] this phenomenon can be explained by the
appearance of some precipitations along grain boundaries
after annealing at T[ 400 /C176C. The reduction of annealing
time during the formation of a NC structure makes possible
to increase the ductility of the HPT-processed samples. In
particular, the samples annealed at 400 /C176C within 5 and
20 min have an elongation to failure of up to 12 and 16 %
and strength over 2000 MPa (see Fig. 4, curves 2 and 3).
The X-ray analysis of the alloy [ 12] showed that after
HPT and annealing at 400 /C176C within 20 min the sample had
a B2 phase (Fig. 5a). The gage section of the samples after
HPT, annealing and tension to failure at room temperature
was also subjected to X-ray analysis (Fig. 5b). The ﬁgure
shows clearly the appearance of B19
0 phase. This conﬁrms
that during tension and after the test of the B19 0 phase a
strain-induced martensitic transformation B2 ? B190
remained. The appearance of martensitic transformations in
the TiNi alloy with a grain size of 20 nm can be explained
by a high dislocation yield stress of the NC TiNi samples.

Thus, the NC TiNi alloy has a rather unusual mechanical
behavior during elongation both at room and increased
temperatures. At room temperature the alloy demonstrates
a very high strength and UTS [ 2000 MPa and a signiﬁ-
cant tensile elongation of 12–16 %. However, the ductility
is generally determined by strain-induced martensitic
transformation during deformation of the alloy even with a
small grain size of 20 nm. At dislocation level there is a
martensitic transformation due to generation and motion of
partial twinning dislocations. As a rule [ 18] a generation of
partial dislocations in nanocrystals is more energy efﬁcient
than complete lattice dislocations. It is evident that this
phenomenon provides the possibility for formation of MT
and deformation of the NC alloy. Besides, while achieving
of a dislocation yield stress, its value is close to UTS,
plastic deformation becomes limited and, consequently, s
sample fails. Certainly, such a behavior of the NC alloy is
not so attractive for its application as structural material.

Summary and conclusions
1. In the present study larger samples (disks of 20 mm in
a diameter and with a thickness of 0.75 mm) of the
nanocrystalline Ti
49.4Ni50.6 alloy have been success-
fully produced by high pressure torsion and further
annealing. Mostly, equiaxed and uniform nanograins
were keeping their sizes in a nanometer range during
annealing up to 500 /C176C( *0.5 T
m). This allowed
conducting systematic tensile mechanical tests of the
NC alloy in a wide temperature range.
2. Mechanical tests at room temperature demonstrate that
the NC alloy even with the lowest size of grains of
20 nm exhibits strain-induced martensitic transforma-
tion and a very high ultimate tensile strength of more
2000 MPa, but limited ductility.
3. While at elevated temperature of 400 /C176C the NC alloy
demonstrates a very interesting behavior with high
strength and ductility. Its nature is assumed to be
associated with the fact that grain boundary sliding
becomes an active mechanism of deformation.
