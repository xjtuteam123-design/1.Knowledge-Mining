Abstract: The strength and ductility trade -off dilemma has limited the wide application of TiAl-
based alloys. Here, a new insight into the potential for increasing the strength and ductility in a hypo-
peritectic Ti-48 at.% Al-8 at.% Nb alloy is accomplished by the electromagnetic levitation technique 
(EML). Moreover, a systematic analysis of the primary and subsequent peritectic solidification 
kinetics are conducted in the undercooling range of 308 K. Assisted with a high-speed camera, an in-
situ observation for the liquid-solid (primary β-Ti phase and peritectic α-Ti phase) interface migration 
is accomplished. When the alloy melt is undercooled to be 2 40 K, high-ordered nanotwins w ere 
observed in Ti-48 at.% Al-8 at.% Nb alloy, which forms a unique hierarchical microstructure. Upon 
further increasing the undercooling, the density of these nanotwins is significantly enhanced. The 
room-temperature compression results reveal that the strength and ductility increase up to 140% and 
150%, respectively. This is mainly ascribe d to the remarkable grain refinement, the formation of 
nanotwins with various orientation, the accumulation of dislocations and stacking faults, and the 
retention of metastable γ phase. The superior combinatio n of strength and ductility indicates the

1. Introduction 
The Nb -containing hypo -peritectic TiAl alloys are of great interest s due to their good 
oxidation resistance, excellent specific strength , outstanding fracture toughness and fatigue 
resistance, which are reckoned as an important candidate for the next generation engine. The 
addition of Nb can improve the oxidation resistanc e and strength of TiAl alloy. While, Nb 
segregation prones to occur, which has a great influence on ductility. As is known to all, the 
mechanical properties of alloys are closely related to their microstructure features. To promote 
the wide application of TiAl-based alloys, the possibility of microstructure modification, and, 
hence developing the strength without sacrificing ductility is one point of interests. However, 
recent studies showed that the improvement of strength induced by grain refinement alway s 
results in the reduction of ductility, which becomes the key issue as the strength and ductility 
trade-off dilemma in TiAl -based alloys. [1-2] Numerous methods (e.g., directional 
solidification[3], addictive manufacturing technique[4], plastic deformation[5] and multi-step heat 
treatment[6]) have been implemented to improve both the strength and ductility simultaneously. 
Wu et al. [7] have found that, about 99.6% and 8.1% improvement of the tensile strength and 
elongation were obtained for a cold -rolled and partial recrystallized titanium alloy, owning to 
the formation of heterogenous lamellar structure with soft lamellar embedded in f ine lamellar 
matrix. Deng et al. [8] proposed a novel method to fabricate a nanostru ctured Ti-45 at.% Al-8

at.% Nb alloy by cryo-milling and Spark Plasma Sintering. The results showed that the strength 
and ductility increase by about 9.4% and 102.4% respect ively, with the enhancement of 
sintering temperature from 1073 to 1373 K. However, these studies showed a significant 
increase in either strength or ductility of the alloy, and the rest one remained basically 
unchanged. Hence, the question remains whether a sharp rise in both the strength and ductility 
of alloys can be obtained simultaneously. Meanwhile, due to the complexity and relatively high 
cost of these deformation and heat -treatment processes, alternative methods are needed to be 
explored. 
Rapid solidification processing, which can induce remarkable grain refinement, is a simple 
and effective way to enhance the strength of the alloy. [9] Meanwhile, the formation of 
metastable phase and defects, such as dislocations and twinning, contributes to the enh anced 
ductility. At present, rare reports declared the enhancement of both strength and ductility via 
rapid solidification of TiAl -based alloy. As an important representative, electromagnetic 
levitation (EML) can eliminate heterogeneous nucleation induced by the container. Meanwhile, 
the alloy melt can be deeply undercooled below liquidus temperature. The retained metastable 
phase, remarkable grai n refinement and formation of nanotwins induced by EML processing 
can effectively enhance the properties of TiAl -based alloys. Nanotwins, as special coherent 
interface in relatively low energy state, occur when two crystals on both sides of twin boundary 
are geometrically symmetry. On one hand, twinning can act as grain boundaries to block the 
movement of dislocations, resulting in the enhancement of strength during plastic deformation. 
On the other hand, dislocations can not only be absorbed by nanotwins but slip along these twin 
boundaries, thus improving the plasticity of the material. [10] Recent study proves that the

formation of hierarchical nanotwins with different orientations can significantly enhance the 
strength and ductility of pure Ag.[11] It is worth investigating whether nanotwins can be obtained 
in TiAl-based alloys via EML. 
Many metallic materials undergo peritectic reaction during solidification, such as Ti-Al, 
Fe-Ti, Ni-Zr, Cu-Sn alloys and YBa2Cu3O7 superconductors.[12-13] For a peritectic reaction, to 
begin with, the primary phase precipitat es primitively, followed by the formation of the 
peritectic phase, which is accomplished by reactions between the primary phase and the liquid 
phase. The peritectic reaction considered to be controlled by solute diffusion, which cannot 
proceed thoroughly, especially when the alloy melt is being rapidly solidified . Hence, many 
interesting phenomena have been found, such as the retention of metastable phase, [14] direct 
formation of peritectic phase,[15] coupled growth of peritectic and primary phase .[16] Although 
a variety of microstructure morphologies were observed via peritectic transitions, studies on 
peritectic solidification kinetics of TiAl-based alloys are quite limited. Thus, it is essential to 
systematically investigate the effect of bulk undercooling on dend rite growth kinetics of 
peritectic TiAl-based alloy. 
In this study, rapid solidification kinetics of the primary and subsequent peritectic phase 
transition are studied in a hypo -peritectic Ti-48 at.% Al -8 at.% Nb alloy. The effect of 
undercooling on growth velocity of primary β -Ti, peritectic α-Ti phase, and the growth front 
morphologies are discussed. Meanwhile, high-ordered nanotwins were successfully fabricated 
by EML to evade the strength and ductility trade -off dilemma in the TiAl-based alloy. A 
systematic investigation into the microstructure evolution of the rapidly solidified alloy melt, 
the appearance and distribution characteristics, detailed or ientation relationship and atomic

2.6 Simultaneously enhanced strength and ductility of rapidly solidified Ti-48 at.% Al-8 
at.% Nb alloy 
The mechanical properties at 293 K and 873 K for the rapidly solidi fied Ti-48 at.% Al-8 
at.% Nb alloy are determined by compression test via a universal testing machine . Figure 8a 
shows the room-temperature compressive stress and strain curves of Ti-48 at.% Al-8 at.% Nb 
alloy at different undercoolings. In general, with the enhancement of stress, all the sa mples 
exhibit an obvious increase of compressive strain, indicating the occurrence of work hardening 
behavior caused by dislocation movement. The corresponding results are presented in Fig ure 
8b, in which the yield strength (YS) and ultimate compressive strength (UCS) at ΔT=108 K are 
440 and 887 MPa, respectively, and the compressive strain (CS) is 11.4%. With the increasing 
of bulk undercoolings from 108 to 170 K, the grain refinement effect brings about a steady

4. Experimental methods 
The hypo-peritectic Ti-48 at.% Al-8 at.% Nb master alloys with an average size of 7 mm 
were prepared by arc melting from high purity Ti (99.99%), Al (99.999%) and Nb (99.995%) 
for the EML experiments. The actual composition of the alloy used in this work is Ti: 44 at.%, 
Al: 48 at.%, Nb: 8 at.%. In order to form an uniform elemental combination, the melting process 
was repeated six times. Prior to the levitation and melting process, the whole instrument was 
evacuated to 8 10-3 Pa and backfilled with argon gas to 10 5 Pa. After being melted and 
superheated to about 100 K, the sample was cooled by helium gas to reach highly undercooled 
state. The primary and subsequent peritectic dendrite growth velocity was calculated as D Δt-1, 
wherein D is the diameter of the sample, and Δt the duration time of the recalescence event. 
Due to the relatively short duration time of the recalescence event, a high -resolution 
photoelectric detector (100000 Hz) was used to acquire the time accurately. Meanwhile, the 
interface morphology and propagation of primary and subsequent peritectic growth front were 
captured by a high-speed camera. 
Microstructure determination of samples after EML processing were conducted by means 
of a scanning electron microsc ope (SEM). Details about orientation relations hip and atomic 
microstructure features were characterized by using a FEI Talos F200X TEM and a double 
aberration-corrected TEM (FEI Themis Z). Specimens for TEM tests were electrolytic-polished 
in a mixture of methanol, n -butyl alcohol and perchloric acid (1:1:8 volume ratio) using a 
Fischione Model 110 electropolisher at 30 V and -30℃. For the rapidly solidified Ti-48 at.% 
Al-8 at.% Nb alloy at different undercoolings, compress ion tests at 293 K and 873 K for 
cylindrical samples ( φ 2.6mm  4.8 mm) were conducted at a strain rate of 0.01 s -1 using a
