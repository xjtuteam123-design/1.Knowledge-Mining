ABSTRACT
Ir-based alloys are regarded as a critical material for improving the service reliability of hot-end components in 
aerospace applications due to their superior high-temperature strength. However, the brittle performance and 
low strength of these alloys at room temperature limits their processing capacity. Therefore, this study in -
vestigates the pure Ir, Ir
–
7Rh, and Ir-7Rh-MRe (M = 0.5, 1.0, 1.5) alloys, revealing the effects of Rh and Re 
elements on the microstructure room-temperature plasticity and strength of the alloys. The results show that all 
alloys exhibit face-centered cubic (FCC) single-phase structure. The addition of Re can refine the grain size of the 
alloy, and the Ir-7Rh-1.5Re alloy has the smallest grain size. Secondly, Rh and Re elements exhibit distinct 
segregation at the dendritic scale, with Rh predominantly segregating in the interdendritic regions and Re pri -
marily segregating at the dendrite cores. After heat treatment, the alloying homogenization is improved. 
Compression testing results indicates that the Ir
–
7Rh alloy exhibites a yield strength of 612 MPa. The incor -
poration of Re lead to an increase in yield strength, with the Ir-7Rh-0.5Re, Ir-7Rh-1.0Re, and Ir-7Rh-1.5Re alloys 
achieving yield strengths of 669 MPa, 741 MPa, and 823 MPa, respectively. The addition of Rh was found to 
enhance the consistency of grain orientation, reduce the resistance to dislocation slip, and promote ductile 
fracture behavior, thereby contributing to a significant improvement in the overall toughness of the alloys.

In order to characterize the phase structure of the prepared samples,

Vickers microhardness value were conducted using a M-type Vickers

the Ir-7Rh-1.5Re alloy are uniformly distributed [ 26 , 27 ].

Table 4 summarizes the Vickers hardness (HV) of various iridium-

table, all alloys exhibit a reduction in hardness following heat treatment. 
For pure Ir, the hardness decreases from 322 HV to 307 HV after heat

–
7Rh alloy displays a higher initial hard -

–

and it decreases to 295 HV after heat treatment. Notably, the Ir-7Rh-

the hardness of these iridium-based alloys [ 36 ]. Furthermore, increasing 
the Re content in the Ir-7Rh-Re alloys tends to induce a gradual reduc -
tion in hardness, with the most pronounced reduction in hardness for the 
Ir-7Rh-1.5Re alloy..

4. Discussion
Precious metal iridium (Ir) has emerged as a candidate material for 
the development of advanced aerospace engines owing to its high 
melting point and excellent high-temperature strength. However, the 
brittle performance and low strength at room temperature limits its 
processing capacity. The results of this work found that the Re had 
benefit effect on the plasticity and strength of Ir-based alloys, and this 
section will concentrate on the Re effect on the microstructures and 
strength of Ir-based alloys.
4.1. Re effect on the dendritic structures
The introduction of rhenium (Re) promotes the formation of den -
dritic structure, which is primarily attributed to the enhanced consti -
tutional supercooling effect induced by Re addition, as illustrated in 
Fig. 4 . As a high melting point (3186 
◦
C) refractory metal, Re has a very 
low atomic diffusion coefficient, solid-liquid equilibrium partition co -
efficient k ₀ < 1 (0.7 – 0.9), and is excluded from the solid-liquid interface 
front during solidification. Due to the slow diffusion of Re, a high con -
centration gradient layer is formed in the liquid phase ahead of the 
interface, producing a thicker and more concentrated solute enrichment 
zone. This significant solute enrichment (resulting in a significant 
reduction in the liquid phase line temperature gradient at the front) 
combined with the slow diffusion properties of Re (stabilizing the 
enriched layer) results in a wider and more severe compositional sub -
cooling region for Re -added alloys with the same temperature gradient 
(G) and solidification rate (R) parameters. This enrichment lowers the

The mechanism for eliminating dendritic segregation via heat 
treatment relies on atomic diffusion kinetics at high temperature. When 
the alloy is heated to temperatures close to the solidus line, the thermal 
vibration energy of atoms increases significantly, thereby facilitating the 
diffusion process. According to Fick ’ s law of diffusion, the concentration 
gradient ( ∇ C) between dendritic stems (solute-depleted zones) and 
interdendritic regions (solute-enriched zones) provides a thermody -
namic driving force for atomic migration, inducing directional migra -
tion of solute atoms along the concentration gradient. The diffusion 
coefficient (D) and temperature (T) follow an Arrhenius relationship as 
D = D ₀ -exp( Q/RT), where D ₀ is the frequency factor, Q denotes the 
diffusion activation energy, R is the universal gas constant, and T is the 
thermodynamic temperature. During the high-temperature holding 
stage, alloying elements (Ir, Rh) enriched in interdendritic regions and 
solvent elements (Re) in dendritic stems undergo cross-regional diffu -
sion driven by chemical potential differences, gradually eliminating 
compositional inhomogeneities.

Ir

during plastic deformation, resulting in the formation of high-density 
dislocation regions. The dislocation lines within these regions exhibit 
dislocation tangles. This is shown in Fig. 13 (a). This phenomenon is 
particularly prominent in Ir
–
7Rh alloys, which not only increases the 
concentration of defects within the crystal, but also constitutes a new 
dislocation slip barrier, which has an important impact on the plastic 
deformation mechanism of the material. Through the trace addition of 
Re elements, the dislocation entanglement phenomenon in Ir-7Rh-1.5Re 
alloy is effectively suppressed, the dislocation density is significantly 
reduced, and the large grain size of the alloy provides favorable condi -
tions for the long-range slip of dislocations. As shown in Fig. 13 (b). In 
addition, the introduction of the dislocation pinning effect further en -
hances the yield strength of the material and strengthens the mechanical 
properties and microstructural stability of the alloy.
In summary. The addition of rhenium leads to grain refinement, 
increasing the grain boundary area, enrichment of rhenium at the solid- 
liquid interface improves melt supercooling, promotes nucleation and 
inhibits grain growth. Increased grain boundaries impede the movement 
of dislocations and improve microstructural stability. The addition of 
rhenium atoms has a pinning effect on the dislocations, which enhances 
the material ’ s ability to resist plastic deformation and improves the yield 
strength. At the same time, the addition of rhenium prompted the alloy 
grain orientation consistent, so that the alloy fracture mechanism from 
brittle to plastic, improve toughness.
5. Conclusions
This work prepared Ir, Ir
–
7Rh, and Ir-7Rh-MRe (M = 0.5, 1.0, and 
1.5) alloys and systematically studied the effects of Rh and Re elements 
on the microstructure and properties of the alloys. The following con -
clusions were obtained: 
1. All prepared alloys exhibit a face-centered cubic (FCC) single-phase 
solid solution structure. As the Re content increases, the grain size of 
the alloys gradually refines, Ir-7Rh-Re alloys present a dendritic 
morphology accompanied by significant dendritic segregation. This 
segregation is significantly decreased after homogenization heat 
treatment.
2. The yield strength of Ir
–
7Rh alloy is 621 MPa, and the yield 
strengths of Ir-7Rh-0.5Re, Ir-7Rh-1.0Re and Ir-7Rh-1.5Re alloys are 
669 MPa, 741 MPa, and 823 MPa, respectively. Ir-7Rh-1.5Re has the 
highest yield strength with an increase of 32.5 % compared to 
Ir
–
7Rh.
3. After homogenization heat treatment, the distribution of Rh and Re 
elements is more uniform, and the dendritic segregation of the alloy 
is obviously improved.
4. The addition of Re prompted the alloy grain orientation consistent, 
so that the alloy fracture mechanism from brittle to plastic, improve 
toughness.
