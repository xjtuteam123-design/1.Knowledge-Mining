HIGH TEMPERATURE BRITTLE INTERGRANULAR 
CRACKING IN HIGH STRENGTH NICKEL ALLOYS 
UNDOPED AND DOPED WITH S, Zr, AND/OR B--II. 
SOLUTE SEGREGATION ANALYSIS 
J. KAMEDA and A. J. BEVOLO 
Ames Laboratory, Iowa State University, Ames, IA 50011, U.S.A. 
(Received 10 January 1992; in revised form 8 June 1992) 
Abstract--In order to clarify the mechanism of high temperature brittle intergranular cracking in high 
strength nickel alloys, solute segregation at grain boundaries and on high temperature fracture surfaces 
has been examined by scanning Auger microscopy. Intergranular S segregation producing global 
embrittling effects was found to increase in the following order: alloys with low S or high S and Zr, high 
S and B, and high S. The grain boundaries extensively contained Ti or Zr rich sulfides in the S-doped 
alloys and B segregation inducing intergranular toughening in the alloy with high S and B. The alloys 
with low S, and high S and B showed more strongly S segregation on high temperature fracture surfaces, 
which was much greater compared with at grain boundaries, than with high S, and high S and Zr. The 
local stress intensification did not produce a remarkable S enrichment at grain boundaries except near 
sulfides. It is proposed that S fluxes from crack surfaces and stressed sulfides to the crack tip induce local 
embrittlement. The composition and temperature effects on the brittle cracking behavior are discussed in 
terms of the global and local embrittling effects. 
1. INTRODUCTION 
The preceding paper [1] has shown that the suscepti- 
bility of high strength nickel alloys to high tem- 
perature (HT) brittle intergranular cracking strongly 
depends on the alloy composition and test tem- 
perature. The composition dependence of the HT 
brittle intergranular cracking behavior controlled by 
stress was not highly correlated with that of stress 
relaxation due to the transient creep deformation. By 
estimating the activation energy at the stage I-II 
cracking transition, it was shown that the HT brittle 
intergranular cracking is controlled by S diffusion at 
grain boundaries and on crack surfaces. 
In the present paper, solute segregation at 
grain boundaries and on HT intergranular fracture 
surfaces in various alloy specimens load-interrupted 
during crack growth experiments was examined 
using scanning Auger microscopy (SAM). Based 
on the results of the cracking behavior [1] and 
SAM analyses, the mechanism of HT brittle inter- 
granular cracking in the high strength nickel alloys is 
qualitatively proposed. The effects of alloy compo- 
sition and temperature on the brittle intergranular 
cracking behavior are discussed using the proposed 
model.

4. DISCUSSION 
The present SAM analysis has demonstrated that 
much higher amounts of segregated S are found on 
HT intergranular fracture surfaces than at grain 
boundaries in the nickel alloys. The alloy compo- 
sition produced a quite different effect on S segre- 
gation at grain boundaries and on HT fracture 
surfaces. The stress intensification near the crack tip 
did not greatly enhance S segregation at grain bound- 
aries except near grain boundary sulfides. Moreover, 
it has been shown [1] that the activation energies 
relevant to HT brittle cracking are highly correlated 
with for S diffusion at grain boundaries and on crack 
surfaces. In this section, we attempt to propose 
mechanisms of HT brittle intergranular cracking in 
term of global and local embrittling effects caused by 
intergranular S segregation, and S fluxes on crack 
surfaces and near stressed grain boundary sulfides.

4.1. Mechanisms of local S enrichment and high 
temperature brittle intergranular cracking 
For the purpose of developing a qualitative model 
for S segregation-induced HT brittle intergranular 
cracking, the sequence of S segregation near the crack 
tip during the heating and/or loading of specimens is 
schematically illustrated in Fig. 17. Since precrack 
surfaces are fully covered with oxides and/or ad- 
sorbed O before heating the specimen [Fig. 17(a)], 
the S segregation behavior on oxidized surfaces was 
investigated in the LSA alloy. An Ar sputtered 
surface was initially oxidized at 573 K under oxygen 
partial pressure of 8 x l0 -6 Pa. Then the oxidized 
specimen was heated up to 923 K and the oxygen 
partial pressure was decreased to 1.4 x 10 -6 Pa which 
is greater than the mechanical testing condition. 
As shown in Fig. 18, the amount of adsorbed O 
decreased and that of segregated S conversely in- 
creased with increasing heating time. It appears that 
oxides and/or adsorbed O are dissolved into the grain 
interior in order to reach the surface equilibrium 
quantity of O which decreases with increasing tem- 
perature and decreasing oxygen partial pressure. The 
kinetics of S segregation on oxidized surfaces is 
controlled by the depleting rate of adsorbed O and 
depends on the crystallographic grain orientation [3]. 
From the in situ SAM observation of heated samples, 
it is expected that the S segregation on precrack 
surfaces is achieved by replacing adsorbed O during 
heating and before applying stress [Fig. 17(b)]. 
When stresses are applied to cracked specimens, 
diffusive S fluxes from precrack or HT crack surfaces 
to the crack tip are assumed to be driven by the

Furthermore, S fluxes are released from sulfides at 
stressed grain boundaries which are probably at- 
tributed to the annihilation flux of vacancies toward 
the sulfide interface. The S fluxes from crack surfaces 
and sulfides to the crack tip facilitate locally inter- 
granular decohesion [Fig. 17(c)]. It is also possible 
that sulfides accompanied by S enriched regions 
become nuclei of microcracks and the coalescence of 
several microcracks into a macrocrack gives rise to 
the crack growth. The relative magnitude of the 
global and local embrittlement which controls the 
brittle cracking behavior depends on the alloy com- 
position, temperature and da/dt, as discussed later. 
Although an atomistic model for S-induced inter- 
granular decohesion has not been fully developed, 
Briant and Messemer [10] showed that the embrittling 
effect of S is most likely to result from electron charge 
transfer of nickel bonding in the presence of S with 
high electronegativity. Moreover, Rice and Hirth 
[11, 12] have suggested that mobile embrittling solute 
has greater ability to decrease the grain boundary 
cohesion than immobile embrittling solute. Thus it 
is possible that the dynamic segregation of S more 
readily facilitates intergranular cracking at HT than 
nondiffusive S at LT. It must also be emphasized that 
the strong tendency of S segregation on free surfaces 
becomes an important factor for HT brittle cracking 
because a crack as free surfaces becomes a supplying 
source of embrittling S. 
It is recognized [13-15] that the local plastic flow 
near the crack tip influences the brittle cracking 
behavior. As mentioned in the part I [1], however, the 
susceptibility of the various alloys to HT brittle 
intergranular cracking was not highly correlated with 
the stress relaxation behavior in uniaxial compressive 
tests. In the LSA and HS alloys with low stress 
relaxation resistance, HT brittle intergranular crack- 
ing controlled by stress occurred at a lower applied 
K than in the HSZ and HSB alloys. This result 
implies that the dislocation activity near the HT 
intergranular crack tip where S segregation is accu- 
mulated might be different from in the bulk grain 
interior. Rice and Thomson [16] have indicated that 
an energy barrier for the formation of dislocation 
loops at the crack tip increases with decreasing 
surface energy. It is speculated that the creation of 
dislocations at the crack tip is substantially hindered 
by reducing the surface energy due to the S enrich- 
ment. Consequently, the S segregation-induced em- 
brittling effect controls HT brittle intergranular 
cracking more strongly than the local plastic flow. 
HT brittle intergranular cracks grow subcritically 
in a somewhat similar way to hydrogen-induced 
cracking although a stress independent regime of 
da/dt can not be observed. On a microscopic scale, 
two types of brittle cracking are considered to occur 
at HT, i.e. intermittently rapid growth and slow 
extension of cracks both of which are related to the

4.2. Effects of alloy composition and temperature on 
high temperature brittle intergranular cracking 
The composition and temperature dependence of 
the HT brittle intergranular cracking behavior is 
discussed in light of the global and local embrittling 
effects. In the part I [1], the stage II cracking behavior 
was found to be described by da/dt = do(K/Ko) n. As 
summarized in Table 1, the alloy composition and 
temperature affect Ko representing the transition stage 
from the crack nucleation to growth controlling 
process and n indicating the stress dependence of 
da/dt in a quite different manner. 
The relative magnitude of the global and local 
embrittling effects for the various alloys is summarized 
in Table 2. The global embrittling effect is increased 
by S segregation and decreased by B segregation at 
grain boundaries. The local embrittlement is induced 
by S fluxes from crack surfaces to the crack tip. 
Moreover, the S enrichment near unstressed and 
stressed sulfides contributes to the global and local 
embrittlement, respectively. Since the HS and HSZ 
alloys exhibited intergranular fracture even below 
300 K [3] which is related to the high amount of 
segregated S at grain boundaries and near sulfides 
(Figs 2 and 4), these alloys are considered to possess 
a stronger global embrittling effect than the LSA and 
HSB alloys. It is also important to note that with 
decreasing da/dt, and increasing applied K and test 
temperature diffusive S fluxes are intensified and 
thereby the local embrittling effect becomes stronger. 
In the HS and HSZ alloys, it is possible that rapid 
growth of cracks intermittently occurs at the stage II. 
The high amount of intergranular S segregation 
produces the highest value of n independent of the 
temperature in the HS alloy. In the HSZ alloys, S 
fluxes near stressed grain boundary sulfides control 
the brittle cracking behavior by facilitating the for-

5. SUMMARY 
Solute segregation at grain boundaries and on HT 
intergranular fracture surfaces has been investigated 
using SAM in various nickel alloy specimens load- 
interrupted at 873 and 923 K in an effort to gain a 
fundamental understanding of the mechanisms of HT 
brittle intergranular cracking. The obtained results 
are summarized as follows: 
1. An alloy with high S exhibited the highest S 
segregation at grain boundaries giving rise to global 
embrittling effects in all the alloys. However, the S 
segregation did not reach very high levels because of 
the formation of Ti rich sulfides. 
2. The addition of Zr mostly scavenged S by 
forming Zr rich sulfides in a S-doped alloy and the S 
segregation was reduced to the level of an undoped 
alloy. 
3. High amounts of B segregation producing 
toughening effects were found at grain boundaries 
in an alloy with high S and B. The intergranular 
S segregation slightly decreased due to the site 
competition between B and S. 
4. While clear evidence for stress-induced S segre- 
gation at grain boundaries was not found in S-doped 
alloys, a remarkable S enrichment was observed near 
stressed grain boundary sulfides. 
5. In all the alloys, S was much more strongly 
segregated on HT intergranular crack surfaces than 
at grain boundaries. The S segregation on HT frac- 
ture surfaces increased in the following order: the 
alloys with high S, high S and Zr, low S, and high S 
and B. 
6. S segregation on oxidized free surfaces was 
found to increase with heating time by dissolving 
adsorbed O into the grain interior under a simulated 
mechanical testing condition. 
7. On the basis of the SAM result, it is proposed 
that S fluxes from crack surfaces and stressed grain 
boundary sulfides to the crack tip induce local em- 
brittlement. The effects of alloy composition and 
temperature on HT brittle intergranular cracking are 
discussed in terms of the global and local embrittling 
effects.
