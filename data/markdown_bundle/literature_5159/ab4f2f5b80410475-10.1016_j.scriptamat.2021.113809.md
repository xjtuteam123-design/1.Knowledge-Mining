A mechanistic perspective on the kinetics of plastic deformation in 
FCC High Entropy Alloys: Effect of strain, strain rate and temperature

Strain rate sensitivity and activation volume that dictate the kinetics of plastic deformation along with 
strain hardening response determine the plastic deformation behaviour of novel high entropy alloys. Al- 
though, the deformation behaviour of FCC single-phase Cantor alloy exhibits some similarity to that of 
conventional FCC solid solutions, the deformation behaviour over a wide range of temperature, strain 
rate, strain and grain size is unexplored. More importantly, theoretical understanding of solid solution 
strengthening, stacking fault energy and dislocation activation in 100 percent solute high entropy alloys 
(HEAs) and their ﬂow kinetics is not yet established. This necessitates development of combined experi- 
mental and computational approaches to realise the mechanistic design of new single-phase and multi- 
phase transformative FCC HEAs to achieve optimum combination of strength and ductility in the novel 
complex concentrated alloys.

The advent of high entropy alloys also known as complex con- 
centrated alloys has been a renaissance in physical metallurgy as it 
has opened up alloys in the new compositional space that was un- 
explored till date with an opportunity to ﬁll in gaps in the property 
space offered by existing metallic materials [1-3] . In this context, 
one of the ﬁrst high entropy alloy (HEA) to be investigated, the 
single-phase FCC CoCrFeMnNi HEA, commonly known as the Can- 
tor alloy, is a classic example that offers excellent combination of 
strength, strain hardening ability, ductility and fracture toughness 
from cryogenic temperature to room temperature better than the 
existing conventional metals and alloys [4 , 5] . 
Deformation behaviour of metallic materials is characterized by 
motion of dislocations and their interaction with other crystalline 
defects at low homologous temperature and is dependent on in- 
trinsic parameters like crystal structure, stacking fault energy (SFE), 
grain size and dislocation density as well as extrinsic parameters 
like strain rate, strain and temperature of deformation. HEAs are no 
exception and the evolution of yield strength of Cantor alloy from 
different strengthening mechanisms like lattice friction, Hall-Petch 
hardening, Taylor hardening and solid solution strengthening has 
been the scope for many investigations [6-8] . It is well accepted 
that FCC HEAs have low SFE ( < 25 mJ/m 2 ) and show a strong de- 
pendence of strength on temperature and strain rate similar to that 
of FCC solid solutions but weaker than that of BCC metals [4 , 9-13] .

A schematic of the true stress-true strain behaviour of the Cantor 
alloy in respect of that of FCC, BCC metals and solid solutions is 
shown in Figure 1 . The friction stress for FCC HEAs is negligible 
like FCC metals but their lower SFE which can facilitate deforma- 
tion twinning and transformation induced plasticity indicates that 
there is a tremendous potential for microstructural engineering in 
HEAs [8-10 , 14] . The contribution of solid solution strengthening in 
HEAs has also been an area of extensive research and it is accepted 
that modiﬁed version of Labusch type solid solution strengthening 
model can reasonably predict solid solution strengthening in HEAs 
[15-17] . Though the theoretical understanding of dislocation struc- 
ture and character in HEAs which are ‘100 percent solute alloys’ 
is not well established, a linear superposition of strengthening by 
different aforementioned strengthening mechanisms is valid. The 
ﬂow behaviour and more importantly the kinetics of plastic defor- 
mation that governs the strain hardening and ductility is not yet 
established for HEAs. 
A traditional approach for deciphering the operative micro- 
mechanisms over a wide range of strain, strain rate and temper- 
ature is based on the determination of strain rate sensitivity and 
activation volume for crystalline and amorphous materials and has 
also been extended to HEAs. For dislocation-controlled deforma- 
tion processes, the activation volume can be thought as the volume 
associated with the sweeping of dislocation across a barrier. The 
physical activation volume can also be deﬁned as the stress deriva-

physical activation volume is always higher than the apparent ac- 
tivation volume and both parameters are used in the literature to 
decipher rate operating processes during deformation. The physi- 
cal activation volume can provide information on the evolution of 
mobile dislocation density as a function of strain, strain rate and 
temperature and can help in estimating ductility of a material. For 
FCC materials with high and medium SFE, deformation by octahe- 
dral slip followed with dynamic recovery leads to gradual decrease 
in mobile dislocation density that leads to onset of instability. Op- 
eration of twinning and phase transformation in low SFE FCC al- 
loys like twinning induced plasticity and transformation induced 
plasticity (TWIP and TRIP) steels [19] contribute to strain harden- 
ing and provide mobile dislocations at higher strain thereby con- 
tributing to excellent ductility at room temperature. On the other 
hand, Cantor alloy shows increased ductility at cryogenic temper- 
ature compared to room temperature due to enhanced twinning 
at 77 K [20] due to an optimum value of critical resolved shear 
stress for twinning dictated by the unique SFE and solid solution 
strengthening. 
The effect of strain rate and temperature on deformation be- 
haviour of Cantor alloy in tension has been extensively studied 
along with a series of investigations determining strain rate sen- 
sitivity using repeated stress relaxation tests and strain rate jump 
test [21 , 22] . It is agreed that the yield strength of the Cantor alloy 
has a strong temperature dependence ( Figure 2 (a)) with the yield 
strength increasing with decreasing temperature [4 , 23] . Multiple 
investigations from different groups show astonishingly consistent 
values for yield strength, ultimate tensile strength and ductility for 
different grain sizes for different strain rates ranging from 10 −5 to 
10 −1 s −1 for a wide range of temperature from 873 K to 4.2 K [4 , 
21-24] . TEM investigations have revealed planar nature of disloca- 
tions at room temperature at yield and low strain. On the other 
hand, cell structure is observed at high strains near the peak stress 
[4] while recovery is delayed at 77 K that contributes to formation 
of saturation dislocation substructure at higher strain before insta- 
bility sets in causing failure [25] . Samples tested at 293 K show 
some deformation twinning at higher strain while the propensity 
of twinning is enhanced in samples tested at 77 K indicating the 
activation of twinning at lower strain levels [26] . There is an in- 
crease in yield strength with strain rate in the quasistatic regime 
from 10 −5 to 0.1 s −1 in Cantor alloy with different grain sizes at 
room temperature and 77 K as observed in Figure 2 (b) [17] . The 
effect of strain rate is more pronounced at cryogenic temperature 
due to higher propensity of deformation twinning. It is agreed that 
the activation volume of Cantor alloy is lower compared to that of 
FCC metals and values from 15-500 b 3 (where ‘b’ is the magni- 
tude of the Burgers vector) have been reported in the literature 
at yield for a temperature range of 77 K to 873 K [21] . Unlike an 
exceptional correlation between the tensile properties of Cantor al- 
loy like yield strength, ultimate tensile strength and ductility as a 
function of strain rate and temperature, there is a lot of discrep- 
ancy in the strain rate sensitivity and activation volume data re-

HEAs including Cantor alloy can be considered as solid solutions 
with 100 percent solute content in a homogeneous matrix but 
some short-range chemical ordering is expected to be present. This 
is a statistical ordering and cannot be attributed to lack of homog- 
enization and is inherent to random solid solutions like HEAs. The 
character of short range order in Cantor alloy and its interaction 
with dislocations is yet to be established but it can contribute to 
lowering of the activation length of the dislocation line. A smaller 
length of dislocation line in a 100 percent solute matrix with un- 
even energy landscape coupled with shorter activation length con- 
tributes to lower activation area in low SFE Cantor alloy [29] . 
There is a decrease in the activation volume with increase in 
plastic strain and decrease in temperature for Cantor alloy [21 , 27] . 
Figure 3 (a) clearly shows that the activation volume is substan- 
tially reduced at 77 K and shows a further gradual decrease with 
plastic strain at 77 K unlike drastic decrease at room temperature. 
The effect of plastic deformation on the activation volume (V ∗) 
can be realised by plotting the evolution of strain rate sensitivity 
(m = MkT/V ∗, where M is the Taylor factor, k is the Boltzman con-

of the difference between ﬂow stress and plastic stress, commonly 
known as the Haasen plot [30] . Multiple investigations have shown 
that the Haasen plot for the Cantor alloy ( Figure 3 (b)) shows a 
straight line over a wide temperature and strain rate regime in- 
dicating a behaviour similar to that of conventional FCC alloys. It

Surprisingly, conﬂicting trends in the evolution of activation 
volume with strain showing a constant activation volume as well 
as increase and decrease in activation volume have been reported 
for Cantor alloy [21 , 27 , 28] . The decrease in activation volume 
is attributed to decrease in mean free path of forest dislocations 
while overcoming of short-range order is attributed for higher or 
constant activation volume. Detailed TEM investigations show tran- 
sition from planar slip to wavy slip leading to cell structure forma- 
tion in all the investigations but the presence or absence of short- 
range order and it’s evolution with strain is not yet established and 
warrants further analysis. 
Recent investigations have studied the mechanical properties of 
the Cantor alloy over a wide grain size regime from sub-micron to 
single crystals; however, strain rate sensitivity data is not existing 
across this grain size regime [4 , 22 , 23] . Figure 2 (c) shows that 
there is a change in slope of the Hall-Petch curve at low temper- 
ature indicating a strong inﬂuence of temperature on Hall-Petch 
strengthening [23] . One would expect the strain rate sensitivity to 
change from microcrystalline to nanocrystalline regime due to the 
dominance of grain boundary processes like Coble creep and grain 
boundary sliding in the former. These additional mechanisms are 
thermal in nature and can contribute to increase in strain rate sen- 
sitivity or decrease in activation volume for FCC metals and solid 
solutions. High pressure torsion processed Cantor alloy led to ex- 
treme grain reﬁnement of the order of 50 nm after 5 turns at room 
temperature. It was observed that there was no decrease in grain 
size with further deformation indicating that the saturation grain 
size was achieved in the Cantor alloy [8 , 31 , 32] . Instrumented in- 
dentation experiments show little change in the strain rate sensi- 
tivity and activation volume for Cantor alloy, which is unlike FCC 
metals and alloys that show drastic increase in strain rate sensitiv- 
ity and decrease in activation volume with increase in shear strain 
[32] . Activation volume studies using nanoindentation showed a 
slight decrease in activation volume for large shear strain similar 
to that of solid solution alloys. It is therefore, prudent to believe 
that the deformation behaviour of Cantor alloy is similar to that of 
dilute Al-Mg alloys or NiCo concentrated solid solution alloy with 
low SFE over a wider range of intrinsic parameter like grain size 
and dislocation density as well as extrinsic parameters like strain 
rate and temperature. 
Preliminary investigations indicate that there is a need to ex- 
pand the domain of investigation to cover a wide range of grain 
size, strain, temperature and strain rate regimes to build a thor- 
ough understanding of the operative micro-mechanisms in Can- 
tor alloy and the corresponding subset alloys like CoNi, CoNiCr 
and CoNiCrFe. Solid solution strengthening explained on the ba- 
sis of Labusch type models are based on the interaction of an edge 
dislocation with each and every atom in a homogeneous solvent 
for HEAs which are 100 percent solute alloys. Therefore, local dif- 
ference in size and modulus for each and every solute atom de- 
cides the extent of solid solution hardening in HEAs. Solid solution 
strengthening models for HEAs are based on deterministic interac- 
tion of an edge dislocation with solute atoms and there is a scope 
for development of a probabilistic model capturing the ﬂuctuations 
in local lattice parameter and modulus of Cantor alloy. Rigorous 
Haasen plot analysis is warranted to establish the character of ob- 
stacles and underlying assumption of constant strain rate sensitiv- 
ity during deformation or rectangular shape of the force displace- 
ment curve need to be veriﬁed. 
The role of SFE in promoting twinning at cryogenic temperature 
is well established and stacking fault energy engineering has been 
exploited to achieve the transformation induced plasticity effect 
in transformative HEAs which show a transition from FCC to HCP 
phase to achieve a remarkable combination of strength and ductil- 
ity [33 , 34] . Recent investigation has shown the unique aspect of 
bidirectional transformation from FCC to HCP phase and vice versa 
to achieve hierarchical nanolaminate structure of the FCC and HCP 
phase to achieve optimum combination of strength and ductility 
in a bidirectional transformation induced plasticity (B-TRIP) HEA 
Fe-30Ni-10Co-10Cr (at%) [35] . The B-TRIP effect can be attributed 
to local ﬂuctuations in SFE for the alloy and necessitates a funda- 
mental investigation into deﬁning global and local SFE for HEAs. 
A detailed investigation on the evolution of strain rate sensitivity 
and activation volume needs to be performed on these transforma- 
tive HEAs. The theoretical understanding of dislocation processes 
including twinning as well as transformation controlled by SFE is 
well established in conventional alloys and effort s are warranted 
to extend them to HEAs [36] . Similarly, modelling and simulation 
approaches at different length and time scales though challenging

In summary, despite few contradictory investigations, Cantor al- 
loy shows a behaviour similar to that of FCC solid solutions in the 
limited strain rate and temperature regime investigated till date. 
Development of a stochastic model for solid solution strengthen- 
ing that captures the local ﬂuctuations in size and modulus of 
HEAs like Cantor alloy and effect of local and/or global SFE is also 
necessary. Detailed analysis of ﬂow kinetics of Cantor alloy using 
Haasen plot approach will help in establishing the operative de- 
formation micro-mechanisms over the wide range of grain size, 
strain, strain rate and temperature. Similarly, effort s should be di- 
rected to develop theoretical understanding of dislocation struc- 
ture and character in Cantor alloy and employ multi length and 
time scale simulations to understand activation processes of dis- 
locations. It is expected that the knowledge generated from this 
exercise will enable us to develop HEAs similar to the Cantor al- 
loy and the recent B-TRIP alloy to exploit the synergy between dif- 
ferent strengthening and strain hardening mechanisms to achieve 
the holy grail of high strength with suﬃcient ductility in the near 
future.
