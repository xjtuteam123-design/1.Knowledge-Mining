Ni-based high-entropy alloys (HEAs), characterized by their face- 
centered cubic (FCC) matrix comprising principal elements (Ni, Fe, 
Co, Cr), have emerged as promising candidates for advanced structural 
applications under extreme environments [ 1 – 3 ]. These Ni-based alloys 
exhibit exceptional high-temperature mechanical stability, corrosion 
resistance, and resistance to hydrogen embrittlement [ 4 – 7 ]. Their per -
formance advantages are particularly relevant for rapid thermal pro -
cessing systems where components must withstand simultaneous

oxidation and annealing processes [ 8 ]. This combination of thermo -

HEAs as key materials for advanced thermal management components 
in semiconductor equipment [ 9 ]. Recent advances in alloy design stra -
tegies, particularly through microalloying with Al, Ti, and V, have 
demonstrated remarkable improvements in the strength-ductility syn -
ergy by promoting the formation of coherent L12-ordered nano -
