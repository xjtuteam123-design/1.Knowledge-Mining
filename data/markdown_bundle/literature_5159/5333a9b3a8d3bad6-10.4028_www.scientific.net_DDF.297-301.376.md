Phase-Field Simulation on Coarsening of the γγ γγ ’ Phase Particles in 
Ni-Based Superalloys Considering Elastic Inhomogeneity 
Yuhki Tsukada 1,a, Yoshinori Murata 1,b , Toshiyuki Koyama 2 
and Masahiko Morinaga 1 
1 Department of Materials, Physics and Energy Engineering, Graduate School of Engineering, 
Nagoya University, Nagoya 464-8603, Japan

atsukada@silky.numse.nagoya-u.ac.jp, bmurata@numse.nagoya-u.ac.jp 
Keywords: phase-field model, nickel-based alloys, elastic inhomogeneity, coarsening 
 
Abstract. A phase-field simulation is performed to examine the effect of elastic inhomogeneity 
between the 
γ and γ ’ phases on coarsening of the γ ’ phase in Ni-based superalloys. In the calculation 
of elastic strain energy, the mechanical equilibrium equation in elastically inhomogeneous system is 
solved by an iterative-perturbation scheme. On the basis of the elastic constants of a practical 
Ni-based superalloy, a series of simulations is performed in which both elastic anisotropy and shear 
modulus are varied independently. The variation of elastic anisotropy gives significant effect on 
both morphology and size distribution function of the γ ’ particles, whereas the variation of shear 
modulus gives little effect on them. Furthermore, it is found that the coarsening rate constant of the 
cubic growth raw changes and increases with increasing the standard deviation of the γ ’ size 
distribution. 
Introduction 
Ni-based superalloys, which are composed of the 
γ ’ phase (L1 2 structure) precipitated in the γ phase 
with face-centered cubic lattice, are applied to gas turbine materials because of their excellent 
mechanical properties such as creep strength at high temperatures. It is necessary to control the γ ’ 
volume fraction, morphology and size distribution of the ( γ + γ ’) two-phase microstructure, since the 
mechanical properties of the superalloys are strongly related to them. 
In recent years, the phase-field method has been applied for simulating microstructural evolution 
in materials. The phase-field approach describes a microstructure using a set of conserved and 
non-conserved field variables which change continuously across the interface regions separating the 
compositional or structural domains. The microstructural evolution can be simulated by a set of 
equations governing the evolution of the fields under the condition of achieving the minimum of the 
total free energy of the system, which includes chemical free energy, interfacial energy and elastic 
strain energy. In previous works on Ni-Al alloys [1-4], the difference in elastic constants between 
the γ and γ ’ phases (elastic inhomogeneity) have not been considered in the calculation of elastic 
strain energy, because the elastic inhomogeneity is less than 7% in the wide range of temperatures 
in Ni-Al binary system [5]. However, it is important to understand the effect of the elastic 
inhomogeneity on the microstructural evolution, because the elastic inhomogeneity reaches a value 
of about 13% in a practical Ni-based superalloy at high temperatures [6]. 
There are some mathematical approaches to incorporate elastic inhomogeneity into the 
calculation of elastic strain energy [7,8]. For example, Hu and Chen [8] proposed a very efficient 
phase-field model for elastically inhomogeneous systems, in which an iterative-perturbation scheme 
is used for solving directly the mechanical equilibrium equation. Hu and Chen concluded that a 
rather strong elastic inhomogeneity (> 50%) was required to produce precipitate morphology 
different dramatically from one obtained in elastically homogeneous systems. However, as Wang et 
al. [1] reported that the ordered nature of the precipitate played an important role in the

The purpose of this study is to examine the effect of elastic inhomogeneity on microstructural 
evolution of the γ ’ phase in Ni-based superalloys by the phase-field method. In the calculation of 
the elastic strain energy, the elastic constants of constituent phases are based on a practical alloy 
and the iterative-perturbation scheme is used for solving the mechanical equilibrium equation. 
Furthermore, four different types of anti-phase domains in the γ ’ phase are considered in this 
simulation.

Morphology. Figure 1 is the result obtained from the phase-field simulation using the elastic 
constants of the TMS-26 alloy and shows 2D morphological evolution of the γ ’ phase in the late 
stage of coarsening at 1373 K. The γ ’ phase is expressed as the white area in Fig. 1. It is found that 
the cuboidal shape of the γ ’ phase arranges along the <10> crystallographic direction of the γ matrix 
due to the anisotropic elastic interaction. 
There are three independent elastic quantities in cubic system, i.e., the bulk modulus ( B = 
C11 + C12 ), the shear modulus ( G = 2C44 ) and the anisotropy ( δ = 2C44 /( C11 -C12 )). In order to examine 
the effect of elastic inhomogeneity, only the anisotropy or the shear modulus was changed 
independently while keeping the other two quantities constant in the simulation. On the basis of the 
elastic constants of the TMS-26 alloy, each quantity was changed under the limitation that the 
Young’s modulus of the γ ’ phase is larger than that of the γ phase (Em< Ep) along all the 
crystallographic directions. Each of the quantities for the elastic constants of the TMS-26 alloy are 
(Bm, Bp)=(319, 351), ( Gm, Gp)=(176, 180) and ( δ m, δ p)=(3.59, 2.69). Here, the superscripts m and p 
represent the elastic constants of the γ and γ ’ phases, respectively.

particles having a radius (r) normalized by the average radius (r). In this study, according to a 
similar way reported by Vaithyanathan and Chen [2], the averaged particle size distribution was

spectrum shape are not affected by the variation of the shear modulus.

Discussion 
From the phase-field simulation based on the 
variation of only the anisotropy in the 
γ and γ ’ 
phases, it is found that the γ ’ phase becomes 
unstable shape with increasing the ratio of δ p to δ m 
(Fig. 2 (e)). In the previous work, Hu and Chen [8] 
showed that elastic energy became low when hard 
precipitates were surrounded by soft matrix 
comparing to that soft precipitates were surrounded 
by hard matrix. The simulation condition of Fig. 2 
(e) is the case that the γ ’ phase exhibits slightly 
larger Young’s modulus than the γ phase. This 
condition seems to be the reason why the cuboidal 
γ ’ phase became unstable in Fig. 2 (e). 
Vaithyanathan and Chen [2] studied the effect of precipitate volume fraction on the particle size 
distribution and showed that the increase in volume fraction of the γ ’ phase shifts the peak radius to 
smaller normalized radius and leads to broadening of the distribution. In the particle size 
distributions obtained in this study, the peak radius is r/r(t) = 0.7~0.8 (see Figs. 4 and 5), which 
is consistent with the position reported by Vaithyanathan and Chen [2], when the volume fraction of 
the precipitate is about 55%. It is interesting that the variation of the anisotropy affected only width 
of the distribution spectrum without changing the peak radius. In addition, as shown in Fig. 7, there

also reported previously [7,8], but it was examined based on the same elastic constants for both the 
matrix and the precipitate. In this study, however, the elastic constants for each phase are based on 
the practical ones for TMS-26 [6], and the variation of the shear modulus did not affect morphology, 
particle size distribution, and coarsening kinetics of the γ ’ phase. From the results, it can be said that 
the variation of the anisotropy has a dominant effect on microstructural evolution under the 
condition employed in this study. 
Summary 
2D phase-field simulation was carried out to examine the effect of elastic inhomogeneity on the 
microstructural evolution in Ni-based superalloys. Based on the elastic constants of a practical alloy, 
both anisotropy and shear modulus in the 
γ and γ ’ phases were varied independently. The variation 
of the anisotropy affected the morphology of the γ ’ phase and caused the cuboidal shape unstable 
when the ratio of δ p to δ m was large. Furthermore, the variation of the anisotropy affected only the 
width of the distribution spectrum of the γ ’ particle size without changing the peak radius. In 
addition, from the correlation between the standard deviation of the particle size distribution and the 
coarsening rate constant analyzed from the series of simulation results, it was found that the 
variation of the anisotropy also affected the coarsening kinetics of the γ ’ particles. On the other 
hand, the variation of the shear modulus gave little effect on the evolution of the ( γ + γ ’) two-phase 
microstructure.
