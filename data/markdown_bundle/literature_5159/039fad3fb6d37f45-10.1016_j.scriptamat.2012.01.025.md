Abstract—TMW/C210superalloys have superior mechanical properties up to 725 /C176C. Numerous annealing twins are present because
these alloys have relatively low stacking fault energies. Nanoscale deformation twins are the dominant mechanism during creep
and tensile testing at 725 /C176C. In addition to traditional strengthening mechanisms enhanced by higher Ti addition, twinning struc-
tures enhanced by higher Co addition also play an important role in strengthening coarse-grained alloys at high temperatures. The
eﬀects of twin strengthening are discussed.

1. Introduction
Disc superalloys used in aircraft engine and land-
based gas turbines mainly consist of c matrix and c0 pre-
cipitates. Ni 3Al-type c0 precipitates with L1 2 structure
are present in the disordered solid-solution face-centered
cubic (fcc) c matrix. A superior combination of mechan-
ical properties, including yield strength, creep resistance,
low-cycle fatigue and fatigue crack growth rate, are re-
quired at service temperatures. Conventional strengthen-
ing mechanisms, such as solid-solution strengthening,
work hardening, grain reﬁnement (grain boundary
(GB) strengthening) and precipitation strengthening,
have previously been used to develop superalloys for tur-
bines that combine good high-temperature performance
with increase thermal eﬃciency.
In order to increase the working temperature of disc
superalloys to above 700 /C176C it is necessary to increase the
volume fraction ofc
0 precipitates and the content of refrac-
tory elements. However, this leads to diﬃculties with hot
work processing and controlling the microstructure by cast
and wrought (C&W) processing, because a high level of
alloying elements is prone to segregation and a volume
fraction of c
0 over 50% causes poor hot workability, e.g.
cracking during forging process. Therefore, powder
metallurgy (PM) should be used to make these advanced
disc alloys. The disadvantages of PM are its high cost
and diﬃculty of preparing powder without inclusions,
which have a detrimental eﬀect on performance. Hence, a
strengthening method that can strengthen advanced disc
superalloys in the service temperature region (>700 /C176C)
and has no signiﬁcant eﬀect on hot workability is desired
to design new advanced C&W disc superalloys.
At the National Institute for Materials Science, Japan,
we have developed a new kind of C&W Ni–Co-base disc
superalloy (TMW
/C210alloys) for applications up to
725 /C176C. TMW alloys were designed by using a new con-
cept [1–6], i.e. combining the advantages of Ni-base and
Co-base alloys together. Increasing Co content can im-
prove the phase stability and lower the c0 solvus tempera-
ture, which widens its processing window and reduces the
stresses induced by controlled cooling and quenching. Ti
is a strong c
0 former and readily substitutes for Al in c0
phase for anti-phase boundary (APB) strengthening.
The TMW alloys exhibit better overall mechanical
properties at the intended service temperature region
(650–750 /C176C) than the most advanced commercial
C&W disc superalloy U720Li (which can be used up
to /C24 675 /C176C). The analysis of deformation mechanisms
indicates that annealing twins and nanoscale deforma-
tion twins play an important role in strengthening
TMW alloys; this represents a new strengthening meth-
od for designing advanced C&W disc superalloys [7].

Recent progress in materials science has shown that
nanograined and ultraﬁne-grained materials can be
eﬀectively strengthened by nanoscale coherent twin
boundaries (TBs) without adversely aﬀecting the ductil-
ity [8–11]. Lu et al. reported that maximum strength
could be achieved in nanotwinned Cu with a twin thick-
ness of 15 nm [9]. A Hall–Petch-type relationship in
nanotwinned Cu has also been established to quantify
the yield strength [12]. Nanoscale deformation twins
have been introduced in steels for strengthening, i.e.
high-strength twin-induced plastic (TWIP) steels. How-
ever, it has been reported that only when the grain size
is controlled to be around 1 lm can TWIP steels obtain
maximum strength combined with excellent ductility
[13,14]. Meanwhile, the strength of TWIP steel decreases
to half of its maximum strength at about 0.3 T
m
(Tm = melting point) [15]. The characteristic scale of
materials is known to have an important inﬂuence on
their performance [16]. It is not clear if nanotwins can
strengthen the coarse-grained (several tens of microns
to a few hundreds of microns) advanced engineering al-
loys with multiphase structure for applications at high
temperatures (above 0.5 T
m). The disc superalloys used
in aircraft engine and land-based gas turbines are exam-
ples of this kind of advanced engineering alloy.
In the present paper, the strengthening eﬀects of
nanoscale deformation twins combined with annealing
twins are investigated, and the prospects for future re-
search are discussed.
2. Experiment
A conventional C&W processing route was used to
produce U720Li and TMW alloys at Mitsubishi Materi-
als Corporation, Japan. All specimens were cut from
pancakes (440 mm in diameter and 65 mm in thickness)
heat treated as follows: 1100 /C176C/4 h/oil quenching, then
aging at 650 /C176C/24 h/air cooling +760 /C176C/16 h/air cool-
ing. All mechanical property testes were carried out at
Kobe Materials Testing Laboratory, which is a material
testing company in Japan accredited to Nadcap (Nad-
cap is the only world standard accreditation program
of special processes and products required by aerospace
prime contractors). The tests were conducted according
to ASTM E8/E21, ASTM E139, ASTM E606 and
ASTM E647 standard test methods for tensile, creep,
low-cycle fatigue and fatigue crack growth rate, respec-
tively. Transmission electron microscopy (TEM) discs
were prepared from the tested samples. The deformation
microstructures were investigated using Tecnai 20 and
JEM 2100F microscopes operated at 200 kV.
3. Results and discussion
3.1. Introducing twinning structures in disc superalloys
The TMW alloys all have similar microstructures
after heat treatment, as shown in Figure 1 . The alloys
consist of c matrix and c
0 precipitates. The grain size is
around 10 lm. Numerous annealing twins were formed
after heat treatment. The annealing twin thickness is
distributed over a wide range from several tens of nano-
meters to a few microns. The total twin length in area of
150 /C2 150 lm2 was measured to be 2.45 mm and
5.15 mm in U720Li and TMW-4M3, respectively.
It is well known that stacking fault energy (SFE) is
closely associated with the formation of twins. The ten-
dency for twinning is higher in metals and alloys with
lower SFE, and vice versa. We have designed a series
of Ni–Co-base alloys with varying chemical composi-
tion. The SFE of these alloys has been determined by
high-resolution TEM [17]. The Co content and SFE of
c matrix in ﬁve disc superalloys are given in Table 1 .
It can be seen that the SFE of c matrix decreases from
35.9 ± 3.7 mJ m
/C0 2 to as low as 19.9 ± 2.5 mJ m /C0 2, and
that the TMW alloys have lower SFEs than U720Li.
The correlation between chemical composition and
SFE has been analyzed. The results indicate that Co
content has a nonlinear relationship with SFE. The low-
er SFE can account for the longer annealing twin length
in TMW-4M3 compared to that found in U720Li.
The density of annealing twins is also related to the
hot-rolling process and subsequent annealing heat
treatment [18]. By tailoring the chemical composition
and chosing a suitable processing route, the density
of annealing twins in disc superalloys can be
controlled.

3.2. Deformation characteristics of disc superalloys with a
high density of annealing twins and low SFE
Among the TMW alloys, TMW-4M3 has the best
creep resistance and tensile strength. For example, a
improvement in operating temperature ability of 76 /C176C
was obtained with TMW-4M3 compared to U720Li
for creep testing at 630 MPa. In terms of creep stress,
TMW-4M3 can survive nearly twice as large a stress
compared with U720Li. The results of tensile tests
(Fig. 2 ) show that the ultimate tensile strength ( r
b)o f
TMW-4M3 is about 10% higher than that of U720Li
in the range of 25–725 /C176C. TMW-4M3 has yield
strengths ( r

whereas it has higher r0.2 than U720Li in the range of
650–750 /C176C. The discrepancy of r0.2 between TMW-
4M3 and U720Li gradually increases with increasing
temperature and reaches a maximum at 750 /C176C, i.e.
10% higher at 750 /C176C [1]. The deformation microstruc-
tures of TMW-4M3 and U720Li were systematically
investigated to clarify the underlying mechanisms.
For creep tests at 725 /C176C/630 MPa, Orowan looping
combining dislocation slip and climb and partial dislo-
cations shearing precipitates were the main creep mech-
anisms in U720Li, whereas nanoscale deformation
twinning was dominant in TMW-4M3 [19]. For tensile
tests, dislocation slip and/or climb prevailed in U720Li
up to 725 /C176C, which is consistent with Gopinath’s report
[20]. The deformation mechanism in TMW-4M3 varies
signiﬁcantly with temperature. At 25–650 /C176C the main
deformation mechanism involves dislocation pairs cut-
ting the precipitates. At 650–700 /C176C, the stacking faults
(SFs) shearing both the matrix and the precipitates grad-
ually changed to deformation twins. At 700–725 /C176C,
nanoscale deformation twinning was the controlling
mechanism. Figure 3 shows the typical deformation
microstructure and morphology of deformation twins.
The average twin thickness is about 10 nm. The activi-
ties of superdislocations, which diﬀer from those of
single-phase fcc metals or alloys, are of great importance
during the deformation of disc superalloys. Superdislo-
cations usually interact with c
0 precipitates in two
diﬀerent ways at low to intermediate temperatures
(/C24 25–800 /C176C), i.e. shearing of precipitates by coupled
a/2 <1 1 0> dislocation pairs or splitting into superpar-
tial dislocations [21]. The former creates APBs in c0
precipitates, whereas the latter forms stacking fault
(SFs). Hence, APB energy and SFE strongly inﬂuence
the transition between deformation mechanisms.
TMW alloys have higher APB energy and lower SFE
than U720Li alloy, and this diﬀerence is partly responsi-
ble for the variation in deformation mechanism.
The diﬀerent deformation mechanisms in TMW-4M3
and U720Li indicate that, in addition to traditional
strengthening mechanisms, nanoscale deformation twins
coupled with annealing twins play an important role in
the enhanced strength at elevated temperatures. Accord-
ing to our analysis, deformation twins may be beneﬁcial
to maintaining the high strength at elevated tempera-
tures. First, a high density of deformation twins intro-
duces numerous, though partly incoherent, TBs in the
alloy. TBs can interact with dislocations or SFs and
eﬀectively impede their movement. Second, nanoscale
deformation twins eﬀectively decrease the mean free
path of dislocation slip [22]. Third, the twinning stress
in fcc crystal is temperature insensitive while dislocation
slip and climb are highly temperature dependent. Our
experimental results indicate that twin strengthening is
still eﬀective at 725 /C176C, i.e. above 0.5 T
m, since the melt-
ing temperature of TMW-4M3 alloy is 1345 /C176C.

twinning [23]. Statistical measurements of twin spacing

images are listed in Table 2 . The results indicate that
both twin spacing and twin thickness decrease with

3.3. Inﬂuence of twinning on other mechanical properties
and hot workability
In aircraft engines and land-based gas turbines, disc
superalloys are used to hold the turbine blades as they
rotate at high speed. A superior combination of
mechanical properties is required for the disc alloy to
prevent yield and bursting. It is essential to strengthen
the alloy without compromising the low-cycle fatigue
(LCF) and crack growth rate (CGR).
Figure 4 shows the properties of LCF and CGR of
two alloys. Obviously, the LCF and CRG are not de-
graded while the creep resistance and yield strength
are enhanced. It has been reported that TBs in Cu
bicrystals result in an intrinsically higher fatigue crack-
ing resistance than that produced by GBs [24]. However,
it has been suggested that the propagation of a crack
along a TB can lead to thermomechanical fatigue failure
in Ni-base single crystal [25]. Disc superalloys are poly-
crystalline. The interactions of dislocations with TBs
and GBs, as well as TBs with GBs, are involved con-
cerned, unlike in single crystals. The eﬀects of twins on
the LCF and CGR in TMW alloys reequire further
investigation to achieve a more complete understanding.
Hot workability is also an important concern in the
designing C&W disc superalloys. High ﬂow stress during
thermomechanical processing will cause cracking. Pan-
cakes of TMW alloys 440 mm in diameter and 65 mm
thick have been successfully forged at 1100 /C176C with short
cracks at the surfaces [2]. It is still not known if deforma-
tion twinning occurs during forging at 1100 /C176C. How-
ever, the experimental results illustrate the same hot
workability of U720Li and TMW-4M3 alloys for engi-
neering applications. The deformation twinning may
have less inﬂuence on hot workability even if it occurs
at temperatures used for hot working.
3.4. Twin strengthening in disc superalloys
Several factors may aﬀect the strength of disc superal-
loys, including grain size, c
0 precipitates (size, distribution
and volume fraction), APB energy (APB strengthening),
as well as the SFE of c matrix. TMW alloys have similar
grain size and c0 precipitate levels as U720Li. Hence, the
APB energy and SFE of c matrix determined by chemical
composition are the main factors that aﬀect strength.
Typical chemical compositions of ﬁve alloys are given
in Table 1 . TMW alloys have higher Co content and Ti
content (Ti/Al ratio) than U720Li. On the one hand, a
higher Co content reduces the SFE of c matrix. On the
other hand, a higher Ti content (Ti/Al) ratio increases
the APB energy [26].
The strengthening contribution of APB (Ti eﬀect) and
SFE (Co eﬀect) can be roughly estimated. The relative
temperature capability of four alloys, which is shown
by the Larson–Miller plot of 0.2% creep strain at
630 MPa, is listed in Table 1 . By comparing TMW-24
and U720Li, a gain in temperature capability of at least
53 /C176C is attained due to a simultaneous increase of
10 wt.% Co and 1.2 wt.% Ti. A further addition of
0.6 wt.% Ti causes a further increase in the temperature
capability of 23 /C176C for TMW-4M3. However, TMW-24
has 3.2 wt.% more Co and 0.6 wt.% less Ti compared to
TMW-2, and the eﬀects of Co and Ti may balance each
other, which therefore results in the overall similar
temperature capability. This implies that Co and Ti ef-
fects in TMW alloys probably make a comparable
contribution to strengthening. The accurate quantitative
strengthening eﬀect of Co and Ti is under investigation.
The micromechanism of strengthening at temperatures
above 650 /C176C due to the Co eﬀect is related to twin
strengthening.

Numerous twins introduce large numbers of inter-
faces in materials. As we know, interfaces, such as sur-
face, GB, TB and phase interface, are very important
in determining the properties of materials. In Ni-base
single-crystal superalloys, the volume fraction of c
0 is
approximately 70% and the ﬁner interfacial dislocation
networks at c/c0 interface can eﬀectively strengthen the
single-crystal superalloy [27].
A TB is a special interface at which the arrangements
of atoms on one side are symmetric with those on the
other side. The interactions between TBs and disloca-
tions are unique due to the symmetric structure along
the TB. The dislocation reaction at a TB has been con-
ﬁrmed to be a=2 < 110>
M ! a=2 < 110>T þ a=6 <
11/C222>TB in a previous study [7]. Here, M and T denote
the matrix and the twin, respectively. Because this reac-
tion is energetically unfavorable, high resolved shear
stress is required for the dislocation to pass across the
TB, as demonstrated by a large number of simulations
[28–30]. Figure 5 shows the nanoscale deformation twins
in TMW-2 crept at 725 /C176C/630 MPa. Many dislocations
or SFs are present in the twinning area (marked as A)
and some of the TBs have lost coherency due to interac-
tion with dislocations. In contrast, fewer defects are
found in the matrix between two neighbor deformation
twins (marked as B). Previous observations have shown
that the TBs were not ﬂat at the atomic scale in the de-
formed samples because of the interactions of disloca-
tions with TBs. Many steps that were usually trapped
an a/2 <1 1 0> dislocation were formed along the TBs
at the side of matrix. Along the TBs, SFs were often ob-
served, the result of the slip of a/6 <1 1 2> Shockley
partials. These observations are in agreement with
recent simulation results [31].
The twin strengthening in TMW alloys clariﬁes three
doubts. First, this strengthening method can be success-
fully applied to coarse-grained advanced engineering al-
loys. Second, twin strengthening can maintain the
strengthening eﬀect up to high temperatures (above
0.5 T
m in the case of TMW alloys). Third, this new
strengthening method does not compromise other prop-
erties, such as LCF life, CGR and hot workability.
4. Prospects for future research
Twin strengthening in disc superalloys is new to both
theoretical research and engineering applications. The
following issues deserve further attention:
(1) Establish a model with which the SFE can be
accurately calculated. Disc alloys contain several
alloying elements. The interaction of alloying ele-
ments makes it diﬃcult to quantify the alloying
eﬀect on SFE. Empirical formulas have been con-
structed to estimate the SFE [32,33], although
their applicability to alloys with high Co and Ti
content should be carefully examined. A SFE
database for many disc alloys will be useful to
establish a model and resolve this problem to
some extent.
(2) Optimize the grain size. Generally, creep life and
crack growth resistance in structural materials
increase with increasing grain size, while LCF life
and tensile strength increase with decreasing grain
size [34]. Usually, the grain size of disc alloys is
controlled in the range of 10–50 lm when consid-
ering property trade-oﬀs. Our results indicate that
the annealing twin density is inversely propor-
tional to grain size, whereas deformation twin
density is reported to have positive grain size
dependence [23]. Therefore the optimum grain size
may be diﬀerent if the introduction of twin struc-
tures is considered.
(3) The rate-limiting process during deformation
twinning. It is envisaged that reordering may be
a rate-limiting process for deformation twinning
[21]. Since c
0 precipitate has L1 2 structure, a
pseudo-twin will be formed after shearing of a/6
<1 1 2> on the {1 1 1} plane without exchanging
Ni and Al atoms, which is a time- and tempera-
ture-dependent reordering process. However,
deformation twins in TMW-4M3 alloy were
observed at both creep tests with a minimum
creep rate of 3.5 /C2 10
/C0 7 s/C0 1 and tensile tests with
a strain rate of 6 /C2 10/C0 5 s/C0 1 at 725 /C176C. The nucle-
ation and growth of deformation twins needs to
be investigated in detail to fully understand the
rate-limiting process of deformation twinning.
5. Summary
The enhanced creep and tensile strength in newly
developed C&W TMW disc superalloys containing
higher Co and Ti have been discussed. The following
conclusions can be drawn:
(1) Nanoscale deformation twins combined with
annealing twins, in addition to the traditional pre-
cipitation strength, can eﬀectively strengthen
coarse-grained disc superalloys at high tempera-
tures (above 0.5 T
m), which oﬀers a novel
strengthening method for designing further
advanced superalloys.
(2) Lowering SFE is eﬀective at introducing twin
structures (annealing twin and nanoscale defor-
mation twin) in disc superalloys. A high Co con-
tent greatly inﬂuences the SFE.
