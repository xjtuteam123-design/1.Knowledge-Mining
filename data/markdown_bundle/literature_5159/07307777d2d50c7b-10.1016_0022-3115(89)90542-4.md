PHASE STABILITY AND HIGH-TEMPERATURE STRENGTHS 
OF HIGH MANGANESE-CHROMIUM-IRON BASE ALLOYS 
AS REDUCED RADIO-ACTIVATION MATERIALS 
Y. HOSOI, Y. OKAZAKI, N. WADE and K. MIYAHARA 
Department of Iron and Steel Engineering, Nagoya University, Furoh-cho, Chikusa-ku, 464 Nagoya, Japan 
Received 7 February 1989; accepted 13 July 1989 
The Fe-Cr-Mn alloy system has been proposed to replace the Fe-Cr-Ni alloy system in the construction of components 
for fusion energy devices. The present work is to investigate the potential properties of high Mn-Cr austenitic steels. 
Especially, the focus is placed on the microstructural stability and high-temperature mechanical properties of Fe-12% Cr-15% 
Mn-C-N alloys. By the combined addition of 0.2% C and 0.2% N to the Fe-12% Cr-15% Mn alloy, a good microstructural 
stability of austenitic phase and high-temperature tensile strengths comparable with those of Type 316 austenitic stainless steel 
are obtained. 
1. Introduction 
Modified Type 316 stainless steels PCA and JPCA 
are promising candidate alloys for the first wall materi- 
als of a fusion reactor. On the viewpoint of reduced- 
activation materials, however, attention has recently 
been paid on high Mn-Cr-Fe base alloys, because the 
decayed radio-activity of manganese is as much as 
one-tenth of nickel. 
The most demanded properties of structural materi- 
als served at high temperature are the microstructural 
stability and strength properties at high temperature. 
The present authors investigated the microstructural 
stability of simple ternary alloys of the Fe-Cr-Mn 
system, as a fundamental study of high manganese- 
chromium steels [f]. The results indicated that, in high 
Mn-Cr-Fe alloy, u phase formation was the most 
important problem for the microstructural stability at 
high temperature, and that the element Mn contributed 
much to the u phase formation. That is, (1) the u phase 
formed more easily with increase of Mn content in the 
range of 10 to 30% Mn, (2) the region of u-phase 
fo~ation was spread to lower concentration of Cr in 
the Fe-Cr-Mn diagram than that reported by Schaf- 
meister et al. [2], and (3) the u phase preferentially 
formed in the S-ferrite phase. 
In the present work, the effects of C and N, which 
are known to be strong austenite forming and stabiliz- 
ing elements, on the restraining of u phase formation

2. Experimental procedure

shown in table 1. These materials were melted in a 
vacuum induction furnace. After solution treatment at 
1373 to 1573 K for 3.6 ks, the materials were water

structure study and mechanical property tests, and some 
specimens were aged at 773 to 973 K for 3600 ks before 
the succeeding investigations. As for the microstructure 
study, X-ray diffraction analysis, optical microscope

electrolytic extraction analysis of precipitates were made.

3.6. A proposal to alloy design of a structural high 
Mn-Cr-Fe alloy 
Experimental results obtained in this study suggest a 
basic consideration on alloy design for reduced activa- 
tion iron base high Mn structural material as follows; 
(i) 15% Mn-12% Cr is a good combination of alloy- 
ing to prevent b-ferrite formation as a base composi- 
tion. 
(ii) combined addition of 0.2% C and 0.2% N is 
required to stable austenitic phase, 
(iii) combined addition of 0.2% C and 0.2% N is 
necessary to prevent u-phase formation in the tempera- 
ture range from 773 to 973 K during aging, and 
(iv) combined addition of 0.2% C and 0.2% N is very 
beneficial in increasing high-temperature strength and 
creep rupture strength. 
It is, of course, necessary to make more detailed 
studies on weldability, corrosion resistance, oxidation 
resistance, long-term creep test and irradiation damage, 
etc., in order to develop reduced activation iron base 
structural materials.
