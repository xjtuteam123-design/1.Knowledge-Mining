Mechanical Properties of Mo-12Si-8.5B-ZrO 2(Y2O3) Alloy
Composed of Different Proportions of Nano-
and Micro-Mo Grains
Jianhui Yan, XiaoFan Zhang, Yi Wang, JingWen Qiu, and ChenFei Song
Submitted: 5 March 2022 / Revised: 18 July 2022 / Accepted: 25 July 2022 / Published online: 9 September 2022
Ultraﬁne-grained or nanocrystalline Mo-12Si-8.5B alloys possess more excellent strength than those of the
coarse-grained ones, but they often exhibit limited fracture toughness at room temperature. It /C213 s very
important to simultaneously enhance the strength and toughness of Mo-12Si-8.5B alloy for their practical
applications. In the present study, nanometer Mo-ZrO
2(Y2O3) composite powder was successfully prepared
by a combination of sol-gel and one-step high-temperature reduction in hydrogen. A series of Mo-12Si-
8.5B-ZrO2 (Y2O3) alloys composed of different proportions of coarse and ﬁne Mo grains were fabricated via
spark plasma sintering using nano- and micro-Mo powders as raw materials. With an increase of the mass
ratio of the nanometer Mo to the micrometer Mo in the alloy, the hardness, ﬂexural strength and fracture
toughness ﬁrst increase and then drop. As the mass ratio of the nanometer Mo powder to the micrometer
Mo powder is 3:7, the bimodal structural alloy possesses the excellent comprehensive mechanical prop-
erties, including the hardness of 9.75 GPa, the ﬂexural strength of 630 MPa, and the fracture toughness of
12.82 MPa m
1/2. Grain reﬁnement of the Mo, grain boundary strengthening in the boundary between the
nanometer/micrometer Mo grains and the second-phase strengthening of the nano-ZrO 2(Y2O3) particles
attribute to the enhancement of the hardness and ﬂexural strength. The improved fracture toughness
mainly results from the crack arrest effect of the coarse grain Mo.
Keywords bimodal structure, ﬂexural strength, fracture
toughness, Mo-12Si-8.5B, ZrO 2 (Y2O3)
1. Introduction
Multiphase Mo-Si-B alloys are well known as promising
structural materials at high temperature used in the ﬁelds such
as heating elements, heat exchangers, and gas components (Ref
1-4). Through adjusting the content of Mo (Ref 5) or the mass
ratio of Si/B (Ref 6, 7), the phases and microstructure of the
alloys are altered, and their comprehensive properties (such as
mechanical and oxidation resistance) could be different.
Among the Mo-Si-B alloys, Mo-rich multiphase Mo-12Si-
8.5B alloy, which possesses the phase composition of Mo
ss +
Mo3Si + Mo 5SiB2, is extensively investigated. Compared
with Ni-based superalloys, Mo-12Si-8.5B alloy exhibits better
oxidation resistance at high temperature and excellent mechan-
ical properties owing to the appropriate mass fraction of Mo ss
and intermetallics phases (Mo 3Si, Mo5SiB2) (Ref 8). However,
its mechanical properties could not fully meet the needs of the
practical application. Generally, strength and ductility of
materials are exclusive. Therefore, at a given mass fraction of
Mo
ss, developing a Mo-12Si-8.5B alloy with simultaneous high
strength and fracture toughness remains a great challenge. An
ideal microstructure, which Mo
5SiB2/Mo3Si particles are
homogeneously dispersed in the continuous Mo ss matrix (Ref
9, 10), could be obtained using powder metallurgical (PM)
processing. The ultraﬁne-grained or nanocrystalline Mo-12Si-
8.5B alloys show excellent compression strength and improved
oxidation resistance at 1000-1300 /C176C (Ref 11, 12). However,
the alloys display an insufﬁcient fracture toughness
(/C249 MPa m
/C01/2), probably due to the low dislocation storage
capability in the ultraﬁne-grained Mo ss and the Mo5SiB2/Mo3Si
phases ( 13, 14).
Several studies have been done to further enhance the
strength/toughness of Mo-12Si-8.5B alloy. The ultraﬁne-
grained Mo-12Si-8.5B alloy with addition of 1.0 wt.% ZrB 2
possesses both high strength of 3.07 GPa and high toughness of
11.5 Mpa m /C01/2, respectively (Ref 15). The reasons for the
excellent strength/toughness are that the in situ formed nano-
sized ZrO2 particles block the dislocations movement and store
the dislocations, which increase the strength and the strain
hardening ability during the deformation process, respectively.
Additionally, rare earths (La, Y) (Ref 16) and rare earth oxides
(La2O3) (Ref 17) have a slight improvement on the
strength/ductility of Mo-12Si-8.5B alloy. Moreover, the alloy-
ing element Zr reacts with the SiO 2 of the grain boundaries and

reinforcing and toughening effects on pure Mo (Ref 27)o r
MoSi2 (Ref 28). In the early study, the effects of the
ZrO2(Y2O3) content on the mechanical properties of the Mo-
12Si-8.5B-ZrO2(Y2O3) were investigated when the mass ratio
of nano-Mo to micro-Mo was selected as 3:7 in the composite
(Ref 29). It is concluded that the composite exhibits excellent
mechanical properties when the ZrO 2(Y2O3) content is 1.5 or
2.5 wt.% (Ref 29). However, the relationship between mechan-
ical properties and the mass ratio of nano-Mo to micro-Mo in
the Mo-12Si-8.5B-ZrO
2(Y2O3) composite is not clear. In the
present work, a series of Mo-12Si-8.5B-ZrO 2 (Y2O3) alloys
with different proportions of nano- and micro-Mo grains were
fabricated via spark plasma sintering (SPS) using nanometer
and micrometer Mo powders, and the mechanical properties
and the relative mechanisms were also investigated.
2. Experimental Procedures
2.1 Preparation of Materials
Micron powders of Mo (1-3 lm, 99.9%), Si (2-4 lm,
99.9%), and B (10-20 lm, 99.5%) were from Qinghuangdao
ENO High-Tech Material Development CO., LTD. The self-
made nanometer Mo-ZrO2 (Y2O3) powder was prepared by sol-
gel, calcinations, and high-temperature hydrogen reduction, and
the details are as follows: (1) First, the raw materials of
((NH
4)6Mo7O24Æ4H2O), Zr(NO 3)4Æ5H2O, Y(NO 3)3Æ6H2O and
C6H8O7ÆH2O were dissolved in the distilled water according to
a certain proportion and adjust the PH value to 1. The mixture
was continuously heated to 85 /C176C with thermostat water bath
cauldron to form wet gel. (2) Then, the wet gel was dried at
110 /C176C for 10 h to obtain the dry precursor powder and
subsequently calcinated in furnace at 550 /C176C for 5 h. (3) At
last, the calcinated powder was reduced at 750 /C176C for 3 h in H
2
atmosphere to obtain nanometer Mo-ZrO 2(Y2O3) powder.
The mass ratios of nanometer Mo to micrometer Mo in the
Mo-12Si-8.5B-2.5 wt.% ZrO 2(Y2O3) alloy are 0:1, 1:9, 3:7.
5:5, 7:3, and 1:0, denoted MSBZ0, MSBZ10, MSBZ30,
MSBZ50, MSBZ70, and MSBZ100, respectively. To ensure
the constant content of ZrO
2(Y2O3) in the Mo-12Si-8.5B-
2.5 wt.%ZrO 2(Y2O3) alloys, Mo-ZrO 2(Y2O3) composite pow-
der with different mass fraction of ZrO 2(Y2O3) was prepared,
seen in Table 1. However, the nanometer ZrO 2(Y2O3)i n
MSBZ0 alloy was added in the form of ZrO 2(Y2O3) during the
milling process. The designed MSBZ0 and MSBZ100 alloys
exhibit a characterization without a bimodal microstructure, and
the others exhibit a bimodal microstructure. The powder was
mixed using a planetary milling (QM-3SP2) for 20 h with the
rotation speed of 200 rpm and ball-to-powder mass ratio of 1:1,
to obtain physically homogeneous composite powder. The
mixed powder was sintered using SPS furnace (SPS-20 T-10,
China) to fabricate Mo-12Si-8.5B-2.5 wt.%ZrO
2(Y2O3) alloy
with the dimension of U 50 9 5 mm. The sintering was
performed at a heating rate of 120 /C176C/min from room
temperature to 1600 /C176C, and sintered at a pressure of 40 MPa
with a dwell time of 6 min.
2.2 Testing of Properties
The apparent density of the SPSed sample was measured
using immersion method based on the Archimedes principle,
and water was used as the immersing medium. The relative
density was estimated assuming a theoretical density of 9.86 g/
cm
3 for Mo-12Si-8.5B-2.5 wt.%ZrO 2 (Y2O3) alloy. Flexural
strength was tested by three-point bending using ﬁve rectan-
gular sample with the dimension of 3 9 4 9 36 mm ( span:
30 mm) at a loading rate of 0.5 mm/min. Vickers hardness was
measured on a HV-30 hardness tester with 10 kgf load and 10 s
dwelling time, and the corresponding Vickers indentation was
used to determine the fracture toughness. The fracture tough-
ness value was calculated using the following equation:
K
IC = 0.0028 Hv 1/2Æ(F/L)1/2, where F is the applied load (N),
Hv is the Vickers hardness (GPa), and L is the average crack
length (m). For each sample, ten points at random positions
were tested and the average value was taken as the measure-
ment result of the hardness or the fracture toughness.

3.2 Microstructure of As-Sintered Mo-12Si-8.5B-2.5 wt%
ZrO2 (Y2O3) alloys
Figure 5 shows the XRD patterns of the as-SPSed samples
with different mass ratios of nano-Mo to micro-Mo powder in
the alloys. It is clear that the alloys are mainly composed of a-
Mo, Mo
3Si and Mo 5SiB2 phases, which is consistent with the
isothermal section of the Mo-rich part of the Mo-Si-B phase
diagram (Ref 30). The XRD peaks of the ZrO
2 (Y2O3) phase
are hardly observed due to its very low content.
The relative densities of all sintered samples are shown in
Fig. 6. As the content of nano-Mo powder in the alloy increases
from 0 to 30 wt.%, the relative density increases signiﬁcantly,
reaches the maximum value of 98.8% for the MSBZ30 alloy.
Increasing the mass fraction of nano-Mo powder, there is a
slight drop in the relative density for the alloys. Three main
factors contribute to the high relative density for the MSBZ10,
MSBZ30 and MSBZ50 alloys. First, the micrometer Mo
powder exhibits better plastic deformation than nanometer Mo
powder during the SPS process (Ref 18, 31), which causes the
mixed micrometer/nanometer powder to sinter easily. Second,
the sintering activation energy of nanometer Mo powder is
smaller than that of micrometer Mo powder, meaning the
smaller the particle size, the easier the sintering (Ref 32). With
adding nanometer Mo powder into micrometer Mo powder, the
bimodal powder exhibits good sintering ability due to nanome-
ter Mo powder having low activation energy. Third, the
nanometer Mo particles could easily ﬁll into the sintering pores
of the sintered body during the SPS process. However,
excessive adding of nanometer powder could lead to relatively
worse sinterability under the same sintering conditions (Ref
33). Therefore, adding an appropriate amount of nanometer Mo
powder into the micrometer Mo powder could effectively
improve the sintering property of the alloy.

3.3 Mechanical Properties of Mo-12Si-8.5B-2.5
wt.%ZrO2(Y2O3) Alloy
3.3.1 Hardness. The Vickers hardness of as-SPSed sam-
ples is shown in Fig. 9. With an increase of nanometer Mo-
ZrO2(Y2O3) content in the alloys, the Vickers hardness of the
Mo-12Si-8.5B-2.5 wt.%ZrO 2(Y2O3) alloy ﬁrst increases and
then decreases. When the content of nanometer Mo-
ZrO2(Y2O3) powder increases up to 30 wt.%, the hardness of
the bimodal MSBZ30 alloy reaches the maximum value of
9.75GPa, increased 20.1 and 10.8%, respectively, compared to
that of MSBZ0 and MSBZ100 alloys. It indicates that Mo-
12Si-8.5B-2.5 wt.%ZrO
2(Y2O3) alloy prepared from the mixed
nano/micro-Mo powder with the mass ratio of 3:7 could
obviously enhance the hardness.
3.3.2 Flexural Strength and Fracture Toughness. The
load–displacement diagrams of specimens performed by three-
point bending test are shown in Fig. 10. As the content of the
nano-Mo powder increases from 0 to 100 wt.%, the displace-
ment to failure ﬁrst increases from 0.34 to 0.57 mm and then
decreases to 0.36 mm. The plastic deformation in the bimodal
alloys is relative higher than that in the MSBZ0 and MSBZ100
alloys with unimodal structure. Therefore, a noticeable
improvement in ductility is observed in the bimodal alloy. In
the present study, the nano-grained structured Mo powder is
supposed to result in high strength and hardness, while the
presence of micro-grained structured Mo powder produces
remarkable plastic deformation and strain hardening. Because
the CG Mo grains have higher dislocation storage capability
than the FG Mo grains, the bigger the grains, the less hindrance
of the dislocations movement (Ref 34). Therefore, the dislo-
cations movement in the CG Mo grain is easier than that in the
FG Mo grains, which is beneﬁcial to the larger strain hardening
rate and higher toughness in the CG Mo. The ﬂexural strengths
of Mo-12Si-8.5B-ZrO
2(Y2O3) with and without bimodal
microstructure are presented in Fig. 11. The addition of nano-
Mo content in the alloy causes an initially signiﬁcant increase,
and then slight increase in the ﬂexural strength. The ﬂexural
strengths of the micro-grained MSBZ0 and nano-grained
MSBZ100 alloys are approximately 430 and 488 MPa, respec-
tively, and that of the bimodal MSBZ30 alloy exhibits the
largest ﬂexural strength value of 630 MPa, shows 46.5 and
29.1% more ﬂexural strength, compared to that of MSBZ0 and
MSBZ100 alloys. Compared with the hardness (10.42 GPa)
and ﬂexural strength (441 MPa) of Mo-12Si-8.5B alloy without
bimodal structure (Ref 19), the hardness of MSBZ30 only
decreased by 4.5%, while the ﬂexural strength increased by
52%. Recent studies done on the bimodal structural alloys, such
as 5083 alloy (Ref 35), Al-7.5 Mg alloy (Ref 24), Ti alloy (Ref
36), and Mo-Si-B alloy (Ref 37), exhibited the simultaneous
enhancement in strength and ductility. In this work, the Mo-
12Si-8.5B-2.5 wt.%ZrO
2(Y2O3) alloys with addition of

For the bimodal structural Mo-12Si-8.5B-ZrO 2(Y2O3) alloy,
the strength response includes the following strengthening
mechanisms: (1) an intrinsic strength ( r0 = ra-Mo + rMo3Si +
rMo5SiB2) results from various phases including a-Mo, Mo 3Si
and Mo 5SiB2; (2) grain reﬁnement from the nano-Mo particles
(ri); (3) Orowan strengthening from ZrO 2(Y2O3) particles
(Orowan rP); and (4) grain boundary strengthening from the
nanometer/micrometer Mo grain ( rHP). The total strength can
be simply expressed as r = r0+ ri+ rP+ rHP. With an increase
of the nano-Mo content from 0 to 30 wt.% in the alloys, the
ﬂexural strength and hardness increased, which is mainly
attributed to the grain reﬁnement strengthening of nano-Mo
particles. Because of the intrinsic brittleness of Mo
3Si and
Mo5SiB2 phases at room temperature, the strengthening effect
of ZrO 2(Y2O3) particles on the Mo 3Si and Mo 5SiB2 phases
could be neglected (Ref 38). The nano-ZrO 2 (Y2O3) ceramic
particles are distributed in the grain interior and grain boundary
of FG a-Mo region, as seen in Fig. 8. During the bending

phase, especially in the nano-Mo grains. Because the grain
boundary strengthening is a dislocation-based strengthening
mechanism, the grain boundary strengthening in Mo

densities. Therefore, the boundary strengthening effect is

alloy and high back-stress work hardening to enhance the
ductility.

transgranular fracture facets in the MSBZ100 alloy have the
characteristics of brittle fracture and there is no visible plastic
deformation. Moreover, the fracture morphologies of MSBZ30
and MSBZ50 alloys are rougher than that of MSBZ0 and
MSBZ100, which is related to the generation and propagation
path of the microcracks during the three-point bending test.

4. Conclusions
Nanometer Mo-ZrO 2(Y2O3) powder was successfully pre-
pared by a combination of sol-gel, calcinations, and subsequent
high-temperature hydrogen reduction. Mo-12Si-8.5B-
2.5 wt.%ZrO 2(Y2O3) alloys with/without a bimodal
microstructure were successfully fabricated via SPS technology
using nano- and micro-powder. The main ﬁndings can be
drawn as:
(1) Nanometer Mo-ZrO 2(Y2O3) composite powder was suc-
cessfully prepared by a combination of sol-gel and sub-
sequent one-step high-temperature reduction in
hydrogen. The agglomerated composite powder is made
of nanometer Mo-ZrO
2(Y2O3) particles with the size of
80-100 nm, and ZrO 2(Y2O3) particles are uniformly dis-
tributed in the Mo matrix.
(2) With an increase of the nanometer Mo content in the al-
loy, the hardness, ﬂexural strength and fracture tough-
ness ﬁrst increase and then drop. When the mass ratio
of the nanometer Mo powder to the micrometer Mo
powder is 3:7, the bimodal structural MSBZ30 possesses
the excellent comprehensive mechanical properties,
including the hardness of 9.75 GPa, the ﬂexural strength
of 630 MPa, and the fracture toughness of
12.82 MPa m
1/2.
(3) In the bimodal structural Mo-12Si-8.5B-
2.5 wt.%ZrO 2(Y2O3) alloys, the FG regions contribute
to the high strength while the CG regions offer good
fracture toughness. Grain reﬁnement of the Mo, grain
boundary strengthening in the boundary between the
nanometer/micrometer Mo grains and the second-phase
strengthening of the nano-ZrO
2(Y2O3) particles attribute
to the increase of the hardness and ﬂexural strength.
The enhanced fracture toughness mainly results from the
crack arrest effect of the coarse grain Mo.
