ABSTRACT
The discovery of W-free γ / γ ′ Co-based alloys has catalyzed the pursuit of next-generation high-temperature 
materials, aiming to surpass the limitations of Ni-based counterparts. The newly developed γ / γ’ Co-Ni-Al-Nb- 
based superalloys have emerged as highly promising candidates, however, studies exploring various alloying 
effects on their performance remain limited, underscoring the need for further investigation. In the present study, 
a comprehensive design approach combining DFT, CALPHAD, and experiments was employed to develop the Co- 
x Ni- y Al-3Nb ( x = 19, 23, 30; y = 8, 10, 12; in at%) base alloys. Furthermore, the effects of V, Cr, and Ta on the 
γ / γ’ morphology, γ’ solvus temperature, density, Vickers hardness and yield strength at elevated temperatures of 
the base alloy were experimentally evaluated. The results reveal that both Ni and Al are conducive to enhancing 
the γ ′ -phase stability and reducing the density. An increase in Ni leads to an improvement in strength but a 
reduction in ductility, establishing a critical trade-off. The screened Co-23Ni-8Al-3Nb alloy demonstrates a 
desirable combination of mechanical properties of high yield strength (YS: 738 MPa), high specific yield strength 
(SYS: 88.92 MPa/g ⋅ cm
-3
), ultimate tensile strength (UTS: 1369 MPa), as well as good ductility (EL: 19 %). 
Through the alloying treatment, we developed a new γ / γ’ Co-23Ni-8Al-3Nb-2V-4Ta alloy that exhibits 
outstanding thermal stability, with a γ ′ solvus temperature of 1170 
◦
C, and superior room-temperature SYS value 
of 122.1 MPa/g ⋅ cm
-3 
and high-temperature YS values: 1076 MPa at room-temperature, 1054 MPa at 700 
◦

681 MPa at 800

C. These properties are comparable to or surpass those of most reported γ / γ ′ Co-based su -
peralloys and the commercial Ni-based superalloy Mar-M-247 across the temperature range from room- 
temperature to 800

1. Introduction
Nickel-based superalloys have long been the primary materials for 
high-temperature components in aircraft engines, combustion cham -
bers, and gas turbines, due to their exceptional thermomechanical per -
formance [1] . Despite their widespread use, however, Ni-based 
superalloys are approaching their operational temperature limits due to 
the inherent melting point of Ni, which posing significant challenges in 
efforts to further enhance engine efficiency and performance [2,3] . Co -
balt boasts a melting point marginally superior to that of Ni [4] . The 
recent discovery of a new class of Co-Al-W alloys has sparked a wave of 
renewed interest in Co-based superalloys, aiming to surpass the limita -
tions of Ni-based counterparts [5] . The Co-Al-W alloys exhibit a typical 
morphology of L1

γ’ -Co

the γ matrix, analogous to that of Ni-based superalloys, providing a 
pathway to higher temperature capabilities. Compared with 
first-generation commercial Ni-based superalloys [6] , the γ / γ ′ 
Co-Al-W-based alloys exhibit a 50 – 150 
◦

demonstrating their potential as high-temperature materials for 
advanced propulsion systems. However, the initial enthusiasm for γ / γ ′ 
Co-Al-W superalloys has been tempered by several challenges, such as 
high density ( > 9.0 g/cm
3
) owing to the inclusion of W [7] and narrow 
γ / γ ′ two-phase region [5] .
Extensive efforts have been devoted to design and develop W-free 
γ / γ ′ Co-based superalloys. Explorations into ternary and quaternary 
systems have shown potential, such as Co-Al-Mo-Nb [8] , Co-Al-Mo-Ta

2. Methods
2.1. Theoretical calculations
For exploring the synergistic effects of Ni and Al on the γ’ (Co, 
Ni)
3
(Al, Nb) phase of Co-Ni-Al-Nb-based superalloys, high-throughput 
DFT calculations were employed by the exact muffin-tin orbitals 
(EMTO) method [21 – 23] . The one-electron Kohn-Sham equations were 
solved within the scalar-relativistic approximation and the softcore 
scheme [24] . The Perdew-Burke-Ernzerhof (PBE) was employed to 
describe the exchange-correlation functional [25] . To ensure the 
convergence and accuracy of structural optimizations, the Brillouin 
zones for B2, D0
19
, and L1
2 
structures were sampled by 25 × 25 × 25, 
9 × 9 × 9, and 21 × 21 × 21 k-point meshes, respectively. The 
solid-solution chemical and magnetic randomness was realized within 
the coherent potential approximation (CPA) [26,27] .
After the analysis of DFT calculated results, thermodynamic 
calculations were performed using CALPHAD method to determine the 
fraction of main phases in the Co-Ni-Al-Nb-based alloy, facilitating the 
selection of appropriate alloy compositions to avoid the negative impact 
of undesirable phases ( e.g., B2 phase) in the subsequent design of target 
alloys.
In this study, a thermodynamic database of Co-Ni-Al-Nb superalloys 
was developed by combining six binary systems: Co-Ni [28] , Co-Al [29] , 
Co-Nb [30] , Ni-Al [29] , Ni-Nb [31] , and Al-Nb [32] , as well as four ternary 
systems: Co-Ni-Al [29] , Co-Ni-Nb, Co-Al-Nb, and Ni-Al-Nb [33] . Within 
these, the Ni – Al, Co – Ni – Al, and Ni – Al – Nb systems were re-optimized 
using CALPHAD due to inconsistencies in existing thermodynamic 
models and parameter incompatibilities. Phase thermodynamic param -
eters were optimized by fitting experimental phase equilibria and alloy 
thermodynamic properties via the PARROT module in Thermo-Calc
[34] . The remaining ternary systems, Co-Ni-Nb and Co-Al-Nb, were 
calculated based on the thermodynamic parameters of their respective 
binary subsystems. Using this Co-Ni-Al-Nb multicomponent database, 
the phase equilibria and various thermodynamic properties (such as 
enthalpy of formation, entropy of formation, Gibbs free energy, activity, 
and phase transformation driving force) of binary, ternary, and multi -
component systems in the Co-Ni-Al-Nb system can be calculated. Uti -
lizing this database, several new γ / γ’ Co-based superalloys have been 
successfully developed [35 – 38] .
2.2. Experimental procedures
Experimental alloy ingots were arc-melted with high-purity raw 
metals (Co, Ni, Al, Nb, V, Cr, Ta; > 99.9 % purity) in a Ti-getter argon 
atmosphere. Each ingot was flipped and re-melted at least seven times to 
ensure compositional homogeneity. The as-cast alloy was homogenized 
at 1200 
◦
C for 48 h to eliminate compositional segregation, followed by 
aging at 800 
◦
C for 168 h to precipitate a fine γ’ phase microstructure. 
Air cooling was applied after each heat treatment step to avoid potential 
intergranular cracking.
Microstructural characterizations were characterized by scanning 
electron microscopy (SEM, Zeiss SUPRA55 SAPPHIRE), X-ray diffrac -
tometer (XRD, Shimadzu XRD-7000), and differential scanning calo -
rimeter (DSC, NETZSCH 404 F3). SEM specimens were polished to a 
mirror finish, etched with suitable reagents, then cleaned and dried to 
yield clear micrographs. The γ’ area fraction(V

were evaluated using the “ ImageJ ” software package [13] . The AR of 
particles was defined as a/b, where a denotes the length of the long side 
(major axis) and b represents the length of the short side (minor axis). To 
ensure data accuracy, statistical analysis was conducted on at least 100 
γ’ phase particles. XRD specimens were polished to remove visible 
scratches. The scanning was conducted at an angle range of 20 – 100 
◦

with a scanning rate of 8 
◦

under argon atmosphere at a heating rate of 15

phase transformation temperatures.
Cylindrical specimens with a diameter of 6 mm and a length of 9 mm 
were sectioned from the aged samples by electro-discharge machining,

◦

3.2. Experimental observation of Co-xNi-yAl-3Nb alloys
Combining the results from DFT and CALPHAD calculations, it has 
been revealed that excessively high Ni may compromise ductility, 
whereas insufficient Ni leads to a substantial decrease in strength 
accompanied by the precipitation of the B2 phase. Additionally, varia -
tions in Al exert a modulating influence on the effects imparted by Ni, 
either enhancing or mitigating them to a certain degree. Consequently, 
we conducted experimental investigations for a series of Co- x Ni- y Al-3Nb 
alloys ( x = 19, 23, 30; y = 8, 10, 12, in at%; hereafter abbreviated as 
x Ni y Al) to systematically evaluate the influence of Ni and Al content on 
the microstructure and performance of Co-Ni-Al-Nb quaternary alloys.
The nominal compositions of Co- x Ni- y Al-3Nb alloys are provided in 
Table 1 . The measured compositions as determined by SEM energy 
dispersive spectroscopy (EDS) analysis are also shown in Table 1 . The 
results confirm that the measured compositions are consistent with the 
nominal compositions, without significant deviation. Fig. 3 presents the 
SEM microstructures of the x Ni y Al ( x = 19, 23, 30; y = 8, 10, 12) alloys

As clearly shown in Fig. 3 , uniformly distributed γ’ precipitates 
embedded in the γ matrix are identified in the grain interior for all the 
x Ni y Al, together with the coarsened γ’ precipitates at grain boundary 
junctions within parts of the alloys. The XRD results further reveal that 
all x Ni y Al alloys exclusively exhibit diffraction peaks corresponding to 
the γ and γ’ phases, confirming the absence of other secondary phases, 
such as B2, thereby implying the robustness of the aforementioned 
thermodynamic calculations (see supplementary Figure S1 ). From 
Fig. 3 , it also indicates that the alloys with 19 at% Ni exhibit numerous 
large, coarsened γ’ precipitates along the grain boundaries. As the Ni 
increases and the Al decreases, these coarsened γ’ precipitates gradually 
diminish, even disappearing entirely. Additionally, in all x Ni y Al alloys, 
the γ’ phase manifests as elongated structures. The morphology of these 
elongated structures changes with varying Ni and Al: with increased Ni 
and decreased Al, these large elongated structures transform into 
smaller cuboidal-like forms. This morphological and size variation of the 
γ’ phase may be related to changes in lattice misfit induced by different 
Ni and Al, as previously reported [4] . On the other hand, quantitative 
analysis of γ’ volume fraction was conducted using ImageJ, with the 
specific values indicated in Fig. 3 . Consistent with the aforementioned 
CALPHAD calculations, increasing Al enhances the γ’ volume fraction at 
any given Ni composition. Taking the alloy with 19 at% Ni as an 
example, increasing Al from 8 to 12 at% raises the γ’ volume fraction 
from 30 % to 50 %. Similarly, at any fixed Al content, an increase in Ni 
also elevates the γ’ volume fraction, although interestingly, excessively 
high Ni leads to a decrease in the volume fraction. For example, in alloys 
with 12 at% Al, the 23Ni12Al alloy exhibits the highest γ’ volume 
fraction of 63 %, whereas the 19Ni12Al and 30Ni12Al alloys display 
volume fractions of 50 % and 57 %, respectively. These results highlight 
the complex synergistic effects of Ni and Al content on microstructural 
morphology. Over all, the alloys with 23 at% Ni exhibit a relatively high 
γ’ volume fraction within the x Ni y Al series.

To further investigate the influence of Ni and Al on the strength- 
ductility relationship of Co-Ni-Al-Nb-based alloys, room-temperature 
tensile tests were conducted on three representative alloys: 23Ni8Al, 
23Ni10Al, and 30Ni10Al. Fig. 5 presents the engineering stress-strain 
curves of these alloys at room-temperature. As shown, the ultimate 
tensile strength (UTS) of 30Ni10Al reaches 1200 MPa, but it exhibits a 
relatively low fracture strain (elongation, EL) of only 9.1 %. In contrast, 
the UTS of 23Ni10Al reaches 1359 MPa, accompanied by an EL of 
21.1 %. Similarly, 23Ni8Al demonstrates a UTS of 1369 MPa and an EL 
of 19.0 %, both markedly higher than those of 30Ni10Al. Fractographic 
analysis using SEM (see Fig. 5 ) further elucidates the failure mechanisms 
of these alloys. Morphological analysis reveals that the fracture surfaces 
of 23Ni8Al and 23Ni10Al are dominated by numerous equiaxed dim -
ples, indicating a ductile failure mode. Notably, 23Ni8Al exhibits deeper 
dimples compared to 23Ni10Al. In contrast, the fracture surface of 
30Ni10Al displays a mixed morphology of shallow dimples and occa -
sional quasi-cleavage facets. The presence of quasi-cleavage facets likely 
accelerates crack propagation during deformation, potentially contrib -
uting to the premature failure of this alloy. These results indicate that a 
moderate decrease in Ni content can simultaneously enhances both 
strength and ductility, whereas a moderate reduction in Al improves 
strength at the expense of ductility. This observed trend aligns well with 
the qualitative predictions derived from DFT calculations (see Fig. 1 ).

4. Conclusions
Through the combination of DFT/CALPHAD calculations and 
experimental investigation, the Co- x Ni- y Al-3Nb ( x = 19, 23, 30; y = 8, 
10, 12; in at%) base alloy with enhanced mechanical properties was 
firstly designed. Then the individual and synergistic effects of V, Cr, Ta 
on various properties of the base alloys were experimentally evaluated 
to meet the requirements for increasing the mechanical capability and 
decreasing the density. The main conclusions can be summarized as 
follows: 
(1) Theoretical predictions indicate that appropriate increases in Ni 
and Al contents can simultaneously enhance γ ′ stability and lower 
alloy density. Increasing Ni content promotes γ ′ fraction while 
suppressing the B2 phase, but may compromise ductility. A 
balanced composition of Co-23Ni-8Al-3Nb was identified as 
optimal, exhibiting favorable phase stability and serving a basis 
for subsequent alloying with V, Cr, and Ta.
(2) Alloys in the Co-xNi-yAl-3Nb series exhibit well-defined γ / γ ′ 
microstructures with suppressed secondary phase formation. 
Higher Ni and lower Al contents promote fine, cuboidal γ ′ pre -
cipitates within grains and reduce coarsening at grain bound -
aries, while exerting minimal influence on solvus and liquidus 
temperatures. V addition induces γ ′ rafting and misalignment, 
especially at grain boundaries. Cr mitigates grain boundary 
agglomeration but promotes intragranular rafting and results in 
minor B2 phase formation. Ta addition effectively suppresses 
both rafting and secondary phase formation, resulting in a more 
uniform γ ′ distribution.
(3) The optimized 23Ni8Al alloy offers a balanced combination of 
mechanical properties, with a YS of 738 MPa, T
