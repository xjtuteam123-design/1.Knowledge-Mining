Partial Phase Relationships in Ir-Nb-Ni-Al and Ir-Nb-Pt-Al
Quaternary Systems and Mechanical Properties of Their Alloys
C. HUANG, Y. YAMABE-MITARAI, X.H. YU, S. NAKAZAWA, and H. HARADA
Two quaternary systems, Ir-Nb-Ni-Al and Ir-Nb-Pt-Al, were successively investigated to assess their pos-
sible use in ultra-high-temperature applications. The phase relationships concentrated on the fcc/L1 2
two-phase region were primarily established, and the mechanical properties were studied. Ir-Nb-Ni-Al
quaternary alloys around the Ir-rich or Ni-rich corners of the Ir-Nb-Ni-Al tetrahedron showed a coherent
fcc/L12 two-phase structure, analogous to that of Ni-base superalloys; however, most of the alloys presented
three or four phases with two types of L1 2 phases. Although these alloys showed a high compressive
strength at high temperature, they exhibited a higher creep rate than Ir-base binary and ternary alloys.
Another quaternary system, Ir-Nb-Pt-Al, showed promising results. Only an fcc/L1
2 two-phase structure
was found in all the alloys investigated with compositions ranging from the Ir-rich side to the Pt-rich
side, and the lattice misfit between the fcc and L1
2 phases was small. The high-temperature strength at
1200 °C of Ir-Nb-Pt-Al alloys was higher than that of Ir-Nb-Ni-Al alloys with the same Ir content (at. pct).
Moreover, Ir-Nb-Pt-Al alloys exhibited excellent creep resistance at 1400 °C and 100 MPa.
I. INTRODUCTION
THE increasing demands for materials with higher tempera-
ture capability require a new generation of high-temperature
alloys that perform better than Ni-base superalloys. As the
coherent fcc/L1
2 two-phase structure results in excellent creep
resistance for Ni-base superalloys, one approach is to develop
a new superalloy with a microstructure analogous to that of the
Ni-base superalloy but based on elements with higher melting
points.[1,2]
Iridium, which belongs to the platinum group metals, has
a high melting temperature, an fcc crystal structure, and some
properties that make it superior for high-temperature applica-
tions. The investigation on Ir-base binary and ternary alloys
showed promising results, such as fcc/L1
2 two-phase structure
formation and higher high-temperature strength.[3–7] However,
the ductility of these alloys was not satisfactory, as they usu-
ally presented an intergranular fracture.
The addition of alloying elements is one method for improv-
ing the ductility of alloys. Ir-Nb-Ni ternary alloys presented
more ductility than the Ir-Nb binary alloy;
[7] however, the
improvement was insufficient for the type of application sought.
Accordingly, quaternary alloys were developed. Because Ni-
Al and Pt-Al alloys have good ductility, two quaternary systems,
Ir-Nb-Ni-Al and Ir-Nb-Pt-Al, were successively investigated
to assess their possible use in ultra-high-temperature applica-
tions. The idea for designing the quaternary alloys is to mix
two types of binary alloys, both of which have an fcc/L1
2 two-
phase structure. Accordingly, the fcc/L12 two-phase structure is
expected to form in the quaternary alloys. The phase relation-
ships concentrated on the fcc/L1
2 two-phase region in these two
quaternary systems were primarily established, and mechani-
cal properties were studied. The main results are summarized
in this article.
II. EXPERIMENTAL PROCEDURE
Ingot samples were produced using an arc-melting method
in an Ar atmosphere. After being arc melted, one sample
from each quaternary system, Ir-Nb-Ni-Al and Ir-Nb-Pt-Al,
was analyzed by the fluorescence X-ray method to determine
its actual composition, which was found to be very close to
the nominal value. This indicated the reliability of the com-
position of alloys obtained by arc melting.
Cylindrical samples, 3 mm in diameter and 5 to 6 mm in
length, were cut from each ingot by an electron discharge
machine and then heat treated at different temperatures in a
vacuum furnace for 168 hours. The heat-treated samples were
polished for X-ray diffraction (XRD) analysis to get information
about the phase structure.
The composition of each phase was analyzed by electron
probe microanalysis (EPMA). A classical correction program
ZAF (atomic number, absorption, and fluorescence) was
applied to correct the results. Nine points were detected for
each phase, and the final results were the average value.
To understand the phase transformation, differential ther-
mal analysis (DTA) was conducted in an Ar atmosphere for
the as-cast samples.
Compression tests were conducted for the heat-treated sam-
ples in a vacuum atmosphere with an initial strain rate of 4 /H11003
10
/H110023/s. Constant-load compression creep tests were carried out
in a vacuum under certain conditions for one alloy with the
most promising microstructure, which was selected from each
quaternary system.
The microstructures of heat-treated and deformed samples
were observed by scanning electron microscope (SEM) and
transmission electron microscope (TEM).

Eighteen samples were investigated. They were designed
by combining two types of binary alloys, Ir-Nb and Ni-Al,
in different proportions. Six samples had the compositions
around the Ir-rich side, and 12 samples had the composi-
tions around the Ni-rich side.

composition analysis, XRD pattern, and DTA analysis, the

IV. Ir-Nb-Pt-Al QUATERNARY SYSTEM
A. The Phase Relationship
Two main problems were found in the Ir-Nb-Ni-Al qua-
ternary system for the development of high-temperature
material. The first is the separation of the two L1 2 phases,
which increases the difficulties for alloy design. The second
is the large lattice misfit between the fcc and L1 2 phases,
which easily induces the coarsening of L12 precipitates. These
disadvantages indicate that the combination of the two binary
systems of Ir-Nb and Ni-Al was not the best choice. Our atten-
tion then shifted to another new quaternary system, Ir-Nb-Pt-
Al, where the binary system of Pt-Al instead of that of Ni-Al
was applied. An fcc-Pt/L1
2-Pt3Al two-phase structure also
exists in the Pt-Al system, which meets the basic requirement
for the formation of an fcc/L12 two-phase structure in the Ir-
Nb-Pt-Al quaternary system. The most important point is that
the lattice parameters of Ir
3Nb and Pt3Al were close to each
other. They were 3.8915 and 3.8775 A, respectively. However,
from the Pt-Al binary phase diagram, Pt
3Al has different struc-
tures at different temperatures.[22] The L12 structure only exists
at temperatures above 1290 °C, and it changes to D0’c at a
lower temperature. Fortunately, it was reported that the L1
2-
Pt3Al structure could be stabilized at room temperature by
adding alloying elements. Hill’s study[23] indicated that the
Ti, Cr, and Ta in the ternary alloys of Pt-Al-X (X /H11005Ti, Cr, Ta)
could stabilize the L12 structure, and the L12-Pt3Ti and L12-
Pt3Cr structures in the two binary systems were thought to be
the main reason for the stabilization of L12-Pt3Al. Ir3Nb also
had an L12 structure and was, therefore, expected to stabilize
the L12 structure in the quaternary Ir-Nb-Pt-Al system.
Seven alloys were prepared for the investigation. Two types
of binary alloys, Ir-Nb and Pt-Al, were used to form the
quaternary alloys. The alloy compositions, the heat-treatment
conditions, and the phase constituents determined on the basis
of the results obtained from SEM and TEM observations
and the results of DTA and XRD pattern analysis
[24] are
shown in Table II. No phase transformation was detected,
which indicated that the L1 2-Pt3Al structure was stabilized
to the room temperature in the Ir-Nb-Pt-Al quaternary system.
The stabilization of L1
2 structure was attributed to the addi-
tion of Nb.[25] Figure 5 presents the established phase relation-
ship concentrating on the fcc and L12 phases. In this quater-
nary system, not only the fcc-Ir(Pt) phase but also the L1 2-
Ir3Nb(Pt3Al) phase formed a continuous solid solution from
the Ir-rich to the Pt-rich side, showing a large promising
compositional range with the two phases.
Moreover, the X-ray profiles showed that the peaks from
fcc-Ir(Pt) and L1
2-Ir3Nb(Pt3Al) were very close, which indi-
cated a small lattice misfit between these two phases. The
lattice misfit value was estimated to be about 0.4 pct.

V. COMPARISON OF THE TWO QUATERNARY
SYSTEMS
Two quaternary systems, Ir-Nb-Ni-Al and Ir-Nb-Pt-Al,
were successively investigated, and a summary of their com-
parison is as follows.
(1) On microstructure: In the Ir-Nb-Ni-Al system, the
desired microstructure, fcc/L12 two-phase structure, could
only form in the limited regions around the Ir-rich or
Ni-rich corner; however, in the Ir-Nb-Pt-Al system, this
type of structure could form from the Ir-rich to the Pt-
rich side, providing greater flexibility of alloy design.
(2) About lattice misfit between the fcc and L1 2 phases: It
is well known that lattice misfit plays an important role
in precipitate shape evolution and coarsening, and fur-
ther affects the mechanical properties of the alloys. The
lattice misfits between the fcc and L1
2 phases in the
Ir-Nb-Pt-Al alloys are smaller than those in the Ir-Nb-
Ni-Al alloys, which implies that Ir-Nb-Pt-Al alloys have
an advantage in regard to mechanical properties because
of the lower coarsening rate of the L1
2 precipitates.
(3) On high-temperature strength: The Ir-Nb-Pt-Al alloys
presented higher strength than the Ir-Nb-Ni-Al alloys
under the same Ir content at 1200 °C mainly due to the
extensive partition behavior of Ir into fcc matrix and
higher microstructure stability.
Based on the preceding comparisons, the Ir-Nb-Pt-Al qua-
ternary system showed superiority over the Ir-Nb-Ni-Al qua-
ternary system. For the most part, Ir-Nb-Pt-Al alloys can
meet the demands of applications at high temperatures,
because they present a promising microstructure, analogous
to that of the Ni-base superalloys, great high-temperature
strength, and adequate creep resistance (at 1400 °C). Accord-
ingly, the Ir-Nb-Pt-Al quarternary system was found to have
great potential for application in the high-temperature area.
VI. CONCLUSIONS
Investigations of the two Ir-base quaternary systems, Ir-
Nb-Ni-Al and Ir-Nb-Pt-Al, were carried out in the sequence
of phase relationship and mechanical properties.
In the Ir-Nb-Ni-Al quaternary system, the fcc/L1
2 two-
phase structure could only form in a limited area, around the
Ir-rich and Ni-rich corners. In most of the alloys studied, there
were more than two phases with two types of L1
2 phases. The
reason for the separation of the two L12 phases was believed
to be the large difference in their lattice parameters. From the
compression tests, the Ir-Nb-Ni-Al quaternary alloys had a
much better ductility than the Ir-base binary and ternary alloys,
and all of these alloys presented sufficiently great high-
temperature strength. However, the creep resistance was not
strong; and the creep rate of the quaternary alloys was higher
than that the binary and ternary alloys, mainly due to the larger
lattice misfit between fcc and L1
2 phases.
On the basis of the findings regarding the Ir-Nb-Ni-Al sys-
tem, an additional quaternary system, Ir-Nb-Pt-Al, was inves-
tigated. The main reason for selecting Pt-Al instead of Ni-Al
was that the lattice parameters of L1
2-Pt3Al and L1 2-Ir3Nb
are close to each other. The fcc/L12 two-phase structure was
found in all of the alloys investigated, and the lattice misfit
between fcc and L1
2 phases was small. The partial phase rela-
tionship was established. Not only the fcc-Ir(Pt) phase but also
the L1
2-Ir3Nb(Pt3Al) phase formed a continuous solid solution
from the Ir-rich side to the Pt-rich side. Studies of their mechan-
ical properties indicated that the Ir-base Ir-Nb-Pt-Al quater-
nary alloys have nice ductility, and the compression strength
at 1200 °C of the Ir-Nb-Pt-Al alloys was higher than that of
the Ir-Nb-Ni-Al alloys with the same Ir content. Moreover,
Ir-Nb-Pt-Al alloy showed strong creep resistance at 1400 °C.
Ir-Nb-Pt-Al seems to be a very promising system for the devel-
opment of high-temperature materials.
