abstract
Although it is important to measure the lattice mis ﬁt and orientation in Ni-based single-crystal super-
alloys to predict their mechanical properties, it is dif ﬁcult to measure the exact orientation and lattice
misﬁt owing to deviations between the surface and growth direction using conventional x-ray diffrac-
tion. Therefore, we developed the growth directional alignment method to measure the orientation and
misﬁt accurately via high-resolution x-ray diffraction. Using this method, we obtained the dominant
orientation information both for the out-of-surface and in-surface directions. Moreover, we could ach-
ieve suf ﬁcient angular resolution during diffraction to measure the lattice mis ﬁt between the
g and g’
phases.

1. Introduction
The superior creep resistance of Ni-based single-crystal (SC)
superalloys is because of two unique phases in Ni-based alloys: the
long-range-ordered L1 2 g0 phase coherently embedded in the
faceecentered-cubic g matrix [ 1e4]. The difference in the lattice
constants between g and g0 determines the initial microstructures
and induces microstructural evolution during creep deformation.
Generally, the morphology and size of the g0 precipitates sensitively
relies on the magnitude of the mis ﬁt; the sign of the mis ﬁt de-
termines the type of directional coarsening of g’ (called rafting)
under tensile or compressive creep deformation [ 5,6]. The lattice
misﬁt closely related to the morphology and mechanical properties
is usually de ﬁned as follows:
d ¼ 2 ag’ /C0 ag
ag’ þ ag

where ag and ag’ are the lattice constants of the g and g0 phases,
respectively.
The Ni-based SC superalloys are conventionally grown along the
[100] direction, which is opposite to the heat ﬂux direction; these
superalloys have a dendritic microstructure along the solidi ﬁcation
axis irrespective of whether they are produced by the spiral grain
selector method or the SC seed method. Because of the random
characteristics of nucleation at the very early stage of solidi ﬁcation,
the surface orientation deviates from the [100] growth axis [ 7]. As
the deviation of the orientation angle from the [100] growth axis
and tilting direction can signi ﬁcantly in ﬂuence creep resistance
[8e10], it is very important to measure the orientation of Ni-based
SC superalloys to predict their mechanical properties.
Despite the importance of determining the aforementioned two
(lattice mis ﬁt and orientation angle) constants of superalloys,
achieving accurate results is not easy. The main obstacle for
measuring lattice mis ﬁt is the similarity of the crystal lattices be-
tween the g and g’ phase. The value of the mis ﬁt between these
phases is very small, usually less than 0.3%; moreover, the highly
similar crystal space groups cause overlapping of their diffraction
peaks. In laboratories, the most common method to measure lattice
misﬁt is x-ray diffraction using a powder diffractometer. The
reﬂection geometry of powder x-ray diffraction, in which the
divergent and diffracted beams are focused at a ﬁxed radius from

To decompose the diffraction peak into its constituents repre-

(HRXRD), or synchrotron or neutron diffraction is needed. Despite
the unique advantages of these techniques for analyzing lattice
misﬁt[ 16,17], the high equipment costs and limited accessibility

terials scientists. There were several tries to measure lattice con-

previous studies on lattice misﬁt using HRXRD is not actual HRXRD.
To measure precise lattice constant using HRXRD, a highly mono-

exact diffraction condition should be needed. However, absence of
parallel incident x-ray beam [ 12] or measurement without align-
ment of the specimen at exact diffraction condition [ 14,18,19] can

For measuring the off-orientation angle from the [100] growth
direction, usually electron back-scattered diffraction (EBSD) or
rotating x-ray diffraction (ROXRD) methods [20,21] have been used.
Though EBSD can provide images of the orientation parallel and
perpendicular to the [100] direction, it has disadvantages such as
additional sample preparation and only local identi ﬁcation. As for
ROXRD, results show that there is low resolution between dif-
fracted peaks, and only information regarding the off-orientation
angle along the out-of-surface plane can be obtained [ 22].
Therefore, in this study, we determined the [100] directional
alignment of a Ni-based SC superalloy to measure the mis ﬁt and
off-orientation angle from the [100] direction accurately via
HRXRD. Without alignment at optimized diffraction condition, we
cannot obtain diffraction peak with enough intensity since in-
tensity of incident x-ray beam from HRXRD is much less than that
from conventional powder diffraction. Using this technique, we
obtained information regarding orientation along the [100]
direction and the tilting direction, similar to the EBSD method.
Based on the orientation information, we measured the precise
lattice constants of the g and g’ phases that provided enough
angular resolution to analyze the lattice mis ﬁt.
2. Materials and methods
The alloy used in the present work was CMSX-4, one of the most
famous second generation SC superalloys, supplied by Cannon-
Muskegon with a nominal composition of Ni-5.6Al-6.5Cr-9.0Cr-
0.6Mo-3.0Re-1.0Ti-6.5Ta-6.0W-0.1Hf (wt.%). The CMSX-4 mother
ingot was remelted by vacuum induction technique and then
directionally solidiﬁed into a 13-mm diameter cylindrical bars in an
investment casting cluster mold in a Bridgman furnace with a
withdrawal rate of 5 mm/min [23]. Conventional spiral selector was
utilized to initiate single crystal growth. Several grains nucleated
during directional solidi ﬁcation can grow into the spiral selector
and only one grain survives after competitive growth in the spiral
selector.
For single crystal casted alloy, solution and aging heat treat-
ments of CMSX-4 were given as follows using tubed furnace under
an argon atmosphere: 1320 /C14 C/2 h (solution heat treatment),
114 0/C14 C/2 h ( ﬁrst aging heat treatment), and 871 /C14 C/20 h (second
aging heat treatment). The solution heat treatment is performed to
dissolve the eutectic g/g0 and to reduce microsegregation and the
aging heat treatment is performed to control uniform growth of the
g’.
After heat treatment, cylindrical SC casted bar was cut to 1 mm
thick using wire EDM processing for x-ray diffraction experiment.
The EDM process was used to minimize the effects of temperature
damage on the surface and was processed as thin as possible for
uniform temperature control. EBSD specimen was prepared by
conventional polishing with a colloidal-silica suspension.
X-ray diffraction was performed by powder x-ray diffractometer
(Rigaku, Smartlab.) with Cu rotating anode and HRXRD diffrac-
tometer (Bruker, D8 discover) with Cu sealed tube source, a four-
circle goniometer and various optics as shown in Fig. 1 (a) . In-situ
HRXRD measurements at high temperatures were conducted us-
ing an Anton Paar DHS 900 stage with a graphite heater. The
specimen was loaded into the vacuum dome to reduce the effect of
surface oxidation and air scattering at high temperatures. EBSD was
carried out on electropolished samples on an SU-6600 scanning
electron microscope using orientation imaging microscopy analysis
software (provided by TexSEM Laboratories, Inc.).

carefully optimized the resolution and intensity of (400) diffraction

4. Conclusion
In this study, we developed a [100] directional alignment
method for a Ni-based SC superalloy to measure the off-orientation
angle from the [100] direction and lattice mis ﬁt accurately using
HRXRD. We measured the off-orientation angle (
d) and tilting di-
rection ( fp) by obtaining four rocking curves as functions of the
azimuthal angles. Moreover, the f-scans obtained for (100), (1 1 1),
and (202) diffractions provided the in-plane orientation informa-
tion of the superalloy. This measurement can save costs and time
required for EBSD measurements; it can overcome the limitation of
EBSD, which presents only local information, by obtaining the
dominant in-plane orientation information in the whole specimen.
Using the alignment information to obtain 2 qeu coupled scans
using HRXRD, we showed that (400) diffractions at high angles can
be measured. Optimization of the various optics can control the
intensity and resolution of (400) diffraction peaks to deconvolute
them into peaks for the g and g’ phases. Our developed [100]
directional alignment method can generate convincing lattice
misﬁt data to predict the mechanical properties of superalloys in a
scientiﬁc and practical manner. Furthermore, combination of
HRXRD and alignment method can be utilized to the other single
crystal superalloys like Co-based superalloy [ 29] or High Entropy
Alloy (HEA) [ 30,31] of which the lattice mis ﬁt between matrix and
precipitate is the critical parameter to determine mechanical
properties.
