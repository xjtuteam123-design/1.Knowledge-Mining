ABSTRACT
This work investigates mechanisms of irregular microstructure evolution and creep damage localization induced 
by the prior room-temperature plastic deformation applied to Ni-based single crystal superalloys, AM1 and 
CMSX-4 Plus, in between solution and aging treatments. Dislocation climb similar to what occurs during the 
high-temperature low-stress creep is the main mechanism of the microstructure evolution occurring around pre- 
deformation slip bands, and subsequently leading to the formation of the bands with coarsened γ / γ ′ micro -
structure. Presence of “ ready-to-shear ” dislocations inside the coarsened γ ′ phase is confirmed by a stereo-pair 
anaglyphs of scanning transmission electron microscopy. This native dislocation structure before the high- 
temperature/low-stress creep test is the origin of creep damage localization observed for the pre-deformed 
specimens. The comparison of two alloys revealed that magnitude of plastic activity (precipitation coarsening 
and dislocation development) inside the bands with the coarsened microstructure is notably larger for AM1 what 
consequently explains the creep properties degradation under all creep conditions.
1. Introduction
Ni-based single crystal (SX) superalloys are commonly used for tur -
bine blades and vanes of aero-engine and power generation gas turbines 
owing to their excellent high-temperature durability and environmental 
resistance [ 1 – 3 ]. Microstructure of Ni-based superalloy generally con -
sists of a combination of disordered solid solution strengthened 
face-centered cubic (FCC) γ -matrix and ordered L1
2 
intermetallic pre -
cipitation hardening γ ′ -precipitates that gives strong deformation resis -
tance at high-temperatures. In order to achieve optimal γ / γ ′ 
microstructure having cubic γ ′ -precipitates with an average edge length 
of nearly 0.4 – 0.5 µ m and γ ′ volume fraction of 60 – 70 %, solution heat 
treatment (HT) and one or more aging HTs are applied to the Ni-based 
SX superalloy components during manufacturing process. This enables 
the materials to have the best creep resistance at elevated temperatures 
[ 4 – 6 ].
In reality, arranging microstructure of Ni-based SX superalloy com -
ponents to perfectly homogeneous with optimal precipitate shape and 
size is not easy due to the complex turbine blade geometry. This often 
leads to local unintentional plastic deformation (PD) during production 
processes such as casting and heat treatments [ 7 , 8 ]. The effects of 
pre-straining have been studied mainly in two industrial relevant situ -
ations: Pre-straining of as-cast (before solution HT) material and after 
full HT. The first case intends to understand the effect of pre-strain on 
the recrystallization mechanisms during solution HT. In this scenario, 
PD can be introduced at temperatures ranging between room tempera -
ture (RT) up to supersolvus temperature by tensile, compression, 
indentation, and grit-blasting to mimic thermomechanical plastic strains 
introduced during solidification and demolding processes [ 9 – 15 ]. The 
second case, pre-straining after full HT, usually simulates complex

Few studies focused on the effect of HT process subsequent to un -

Within this context, this study aims to discuss the effect of prior PD 
between solution and aging HTs particularly on the defect and disloca -
tion structures and the evolution mechanism during the formation of 
microstructure coarsened bands. By bridging the mechanisms between 
the formation of microstructure coarsened band and the development of 
coarse and inclined γ ′ -raft structure, the origins of creep damage local -

superalloys are identified. Moreover, pre-deformation mechanisms and 
the dislocation structure evolution between two tested materials, AM1 
and CMSX-4 Plus Ni-based SX superalloys, are compared and discussed 
in order to understand differences of creep behaviors reported in the 
previous studies. The discovery in the present work potentially creates 
critical consequences for the aerospace industry as one might have to 
consider the impact of pre-deformation on Ni-based SX superalloys ’ 
mechanical performance much more seriously.

Scanning transmission electron microscopy (STEM) was used for 
multi-scale high-resolution characterization of the microstructure at 
length scales down to nanometers. STEM foils tilted into specific low 
index crystallographic zones were investigated using an image- 
aberration corrected and monochromated Thermo Fisher Scientific 
(TFS) Titan-Themis 30 – 300 kV S/TEM. Defect contrast was obtained by 
low-angle annular dark-field (LAADF) STEM or by two-beam diffraction 
contrast imaging (DCI) using both bright field (BF) and high-angle 
annular dark-field (HAADF) STEM detectors [ 38 ]. By simply adjusting 
the microscope camera length, and thus the acceptance angle of the 
annular detector, it was possible to transit from HAADF Z-contrast im -
aging mode performed by exclusively collecting electrons which have 
been scattered to high angles to the small-angle scattered electrons 
sensitive to defect contrast [ 39 ]. Specific crystal orientations and excited 
diffraction vectors forming diffraction contrast in LAADF/BF-STEM DCI 
were determined from corresponding reciprocal space Kikuchi band 
patterns and by using the convergent beam electron diffraction (CBED).
Tilting experiments were combined with the stereo STEM-DCI mi -
croscopy to evaluate the crystallography and the spatial distribution of 
defects [ 40 , 41 ]. Stereo method is based on providing separate images 
for both eyes of the observer. The two images of a stereogram are taken 
at two angular positions of the foil and the corresponding rotation angle 
is close to viewing angle of the observer. When exploiting DCI in stereo 
STEM, two micrographs need to be taken at the same magnification and 
under the same two-beam diffraction contrast condition. Two stereo 
STEM-DCI micrographs are color coded using the freeware Anaglyph 
Maker Version 1.08 to obtain a 3D anaglyph [ 41 , 42 ]. Using a special 
pair of red-cyan colored glasses yields the correct 3D impression. The 
data were collected and processed using the TFS Velox software. In 
particular, the STEM images were corrected for possible drift and 
scanning beam distortions using the drift corrected frame integration 
(DCFI) function of Velox.
Chemical composition was analyzed from the data collected by 
Super-X energy dispersive X-ray spectroscopy (EDS) detector in Titan- 
Themis STEM. The data were collected and processed using the TFS 
Velox software. In particular, the raw data in the original spectral maps 
were quantified using standard Cliff-Lorimer (K-factor) fit (default k- 
factors available in Velox were used as well as the Brown-Powell 
empirical ionization cross-section model), including absorption correc -
tion and background subtraction.
3. Results
3.1. Pre-deformation of Ni-based SX superalloy specimens
Stress-strain curves associated with the tensile pre-deformation of 
Ni-based SX superalloys (as-solutioned AM1 and CMSX-4 Plus, and fully 
heat treated AM1) are plotted in Fig. 3 . In the as-solutioned state, AM1 
has higher yield strength at RT compared to that of CMSX-4 Plus, which 
is consistent with the tensile properties of the same alloys after full heat- 
treatment reported in previous studies [ 43 , 44 ]. The 3rd gen. Ni-based 
SX superalloys generally have a lower APB energy than the 1st gen. 
superalloys, and therefore, γ ′ precipitate shearing can occur at lower 
stress [ 43 – 45 ]. The highest yield stress achieved by AM1 after aging 
treatments (AM1-HT-RTPD) is a result of optimized size and shape of γ ′ 
precipitates during aging HTs [ 5 , 43 , 46 , 47 ]. γ / γ ′ microstructure opti -
mization is generally designed for creep properties; however, this also 
improves yield strength of 〈 001 〉 oriented Ni-based SX superalloys at RT 
[ 5 , 47 ].

4. Discussion
Results of detailed multi-scale electron microscopy-based charac -
terization presented in previous sections provide a strong base for the 
discussion of the processes involved in the microstructure evolution of 
two different alloys AM1 and CMSX-4 Plus. Three different perspectives 
are discussed: 1) Tensile pre-deformation behaviors, 2) kinetics of 
microstructure evolution, and 3) dislocation arrangements and 
elemental segregations. Having discussed the nature of the microstruc -
ture coarsened bands, the origins of creep damage localization [ 26 ] at 
high temperature creep conditions of two SX superalloys are explained.

5. Conclusions
Minor prior plastic strain (~0.8 %) was introduced at room tem -
perature into the as-solutioned Ni-based single crystal superalloys AM1 
and CMSX-4 Plus followed by aging heat treatments. This study was 
designed to mimic the pre-deformation of a turbine blade material that 
can be introduced during the processing. Associated microstructural 
changes were analyzed and characterized at different length scales to 
understand the microstructure evolution behavior and mechanisms 
leading to damage localization during high temperature creep defor -
mation. The following conclusions can be made from the present study. 
• Dislocation climb is the main mechanism of dislocation and micro -
structure evolution during aging treatment after the pre-deformation 
of as-solutioned Ni-based SX superalloys. This mainly occurs during 
the first stage of aging treatment at 1100 
◦
C for AM1 and 1165 
◦
C for 
CMSX-4 Plus. The process is similar to that occurring at the begin -
ning of primary creep stage in high-temperature low-stress “γ ′ -rafting 
regime ” creep.
• The effect of pre-deformation on γ ′ precipitate size and shape of 
CMSX-4 Plus is minor compared to that of AM1 which exhibits
