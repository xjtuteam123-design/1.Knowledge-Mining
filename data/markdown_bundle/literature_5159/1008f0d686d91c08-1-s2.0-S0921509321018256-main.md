ABSTRACT 
Ultrahigh strength is achieved in low-cost Mg-4Sm-0.6Zn-0.4Zr alloy by a traditional extrusion process, with 
tensile yield strength over 450 MPa, which exceeds the majority of heavy rare-earth (RE) alloyed Mg-RE 
extrusion alloys. The alloy showed a typical bimodal microstructure, consisting of fine recrystallized grains 
with random orientations and coarse hot-worked grains with strong basal texture. And profuse Mg
3
Sm particles 
were dynamically precipitated in recrystallized grains boundary as well as hot-worked grains interior during 
extrusion. The ultrahigh yield strength of the alloy is closely related to the combined effect of fine recrystallized 
grains, highly textural hot-worked grains, and numerous nanoscale particles, independent of the accepted age- 
hardening in those heavy RE containing Mg alloys. Low cost, high strength, and simple extrusion process 
make the present alloy gets more fantastic potential in industrial applications than other Mg-RE extrusion alloys. 
1. Introduction 
Magnesium (Mg) alloys as the lightest metallic structural material 
attract great attention in the aerospace, automotive, and electronics 
industries as a result of the demand for weight-saving [ 1 – 3 ]. However, 
the industrial applications of wrought Mg alloys are still limited 
compared with aluminum (Al) alloys mainly because of their low 
strength, high cost, and poor corrosion resistance [ 3 ]. Currently, a 
massive effort has been made to develop high-performance Mg alloys. It 
is widely accepted that rare earth (RE) elements are very effective 
alloying to improve the mechanical properties of Mg alloys. In partic -
ular, heavy RE elements such as Gd and Y normally have a high solu -
bility in Mg at high temperatures, and it is expected to produce 
substantial precipitation strengthening response for a sharp fall of the 
solubility at low temperatures [ 1 , 2 , 4 ]. From this, loads of 
Mg-Gd-Y-based extrusion alloys readily reached high yield strength over 
400 MPa, comparable to medium-strength Al extrusion alloys. Yu et al. 
[ 5 ] reported the highest record of the yield strength (~500 MPa) in 
Mg-11.7Gd-4.9Y-0.3Zr extrusion alloy among all Mg-RE alloys 
processed by conventional extrusion technology. Moreover, Xu et al. [ 6 ] 
developed an ultra-strong and ductile Mg-8.2Gd-3.8Y – 1Zn-0.4Zr (wt%) 
extrusion alloy having a yield strength of 466 MPa and an elongation to 
failure of 14.5%. Achieving high strength in such Mg-RE-based alloys lie 
basically on the formation of a large proportion of fine grains and 
considerable precipitates, while other strengthening contributions such 
as texture are less [ 4 – 6 ]. Nevertheless, these Mg-RE alloys contain a 
mass of RE addition of at least 12 wt%, which greatly increases the cost 
so that restricts the range of application heavily. Also, massive RE 
addition gives rise to an increased density and processing temperature of 
the alloys, which is undesirable for the use of Mg alloys. Therefore, we 
look forward to decreasing RE addition in those of high-strength Mg-RE 
alloys but without the loss of strength. But if the only simple decrease of 
RE addition, whether the high strength over 450 MPa can still be ob -
tained? Given that most of the heavy REs usually existed in the Mg 
matrix as in the form of solutes instead of second-phase particles during 
hot extrusion, thereby providing prerequisite for subsequent ageing 
inducing precipitation hardening, which will be the most crucial point 
for ensuring high strength obtained in high RE-alloyed Mg-RE alloys [ 4 ,

3.4. Mechanical properties 
Fig. 9 exhibits the typical engineering stress-strain curves of the as- 
extruded alloy and peak-aged alloy under tension and compression 
modes. The corresponding tensile and compressive properties are sum -
marized in Table 1 . The remarkably differential shape of tensile and 
compressive curves indicates significant tension-compression aniso -
tropic existed in the present alloy. The as-extruded alloy exhibits ul -
trahigh tensile yield strength of 430 MPa and then increases to 458 MPa 
after peak-ageing treatment. However, there is a low compressive yield 
strength of only 305 MPa, thus leading to an obvious compression- 
tension yield asymmetry (CYS/TYS) ratio of 0.71. The compressive 
curves are sigmoid with a concave region after yielding subsequently 
rapid strain hardening, which reflects a clear indicator of a twin-

strongly textural Mg extrusion alloys [ 12 , 18 ]. After peak-ageing, the 
compression-tension yield asymmetry is still unchanged in the alloy 
although former reports claimed the improvement of the yield asym -
metry by basal precipitates [ 21 ]. 
4. Discussion 
4.1. Strengthening mechanism 
Based on the aforesaid analysis, the high tensile yield strength of the 
present alloy depends mainly on the following microstructural charac -
teristics: (i) bimodal microstructure consisting of fine recrystallized 
grains and strongly textural hot-worked grains and (ii) amounts of 
dynamical precipitated Mg
3
Sm particles.

4.2. Comparison with other Mg-RE extruded alloys 
Fig. 10 summarizes the dependence of tensile yield strength on 
contained RE content for comparison between the present alloy and 
other extruded Mg-RE-based alloy [ 4 – 7 , 10 – 12 ]. One can see that the 
majority of high-strength Mg-RE extrusion alloys are majorly concen -
trated in heavy RE alloyed Mg alloys. Also, at least 12% RE alloying is a 
prerequisite in those alloys of strength over 400 MPa. Most heavy RE 
alloyed Mg alloys contain Zn to create a long period stacking order 
(LPSO) structure that is capable of yielding a much surprising 
strengthening effect, such as medium RE addition of Mg-6.8Y-2.5Zn 
even having high strength of ~400 MPa. This structure cannot form in 
light RE alloyed Mg alloys yet. Consequently, achieving high strength in 
those of light RE alloyed Mg extrusion alloys is full of great challenges. 
Considering that Sm and Nd have a higher solubility in Mg than other 
light RE elements, thereby they should yield more dynamical pre -
cipitates during extrusion so as to enable high strength [ 10 , 11 ]. As ex -
pected, Mg – Sm and Mg – Nd extrusion alloys can readily obtain a high 
strength of over 350 MPa in spite of low RE addition. The present alloy 
has only 4% RE, but the strength exceeds 450 MPa. As far as the authors 
are aware until now no Mg alloys with such a low RE alloying can obtain 
the ultrahigh strength of the present alloy. Specifically, the strength of 
the present alloy is far higher than those of the congeneric Mg – Sm or 
Mg – Nd based alloys, better than the vast majority of Mg-Gd-Y extrusion 
alloys, even comparable to that of T8-treated 2024 Al alloy with TYS of 
~450 MPa [ 32 ]. As a result, this comparison shows that the present 
alloy has a more significant superiority in terms of strength than other 
Mg-RE-based extrusion alloys only from the point of RE content, which 
is promising for large-scale industrial applications. 
By the way, the present alloy exhibits a larger asymmetry, with the 
disparity between the tension and compression yield strengths over 100 
MPa, than that of heavy RE alloyed Mg extrusion alloys [ 5 , 6 ]. Such large 
disparity principally results from the large proportion of hot-worked 
regions with strong basal texture, of which {10 1 2} twinning is more 
easily activated under compressive mode but not under tensile mode 
along with the ED. It was reported that ageing precipitates can improve 
tension-compression asymmetry, in particular where basal precipitates 
enable the inhibition of the nucleation and propagation of {10 1 2} 
twinning only since they cause back stress to surmount the shear stress 
that is needed for the activation of the {10 1 2} twinning [ 22 ]. But un -
fortunately, the present alloy after ageing still shows a large disparity 
possibly owing to a limited number of basal γ’’ precipitate. Our recent 
work shows that introducing nano-spaced stacking faults lamellar 
structure in coarse hot-worked regions can much improve 
tension-compression asymmetry of highly textural Mg-Sm-Yb extrusion 
alloy [ 33 ]. Therefore, it is considered to the modify microstructure of 
hot-worked regions in the present alloy for reducing the gap between the 
tension and compression yield strengths, without loss of tensile yield 
strength, which is further work. 
5. Conclusion 
In summary, ultra-high-strength is achieved in a low-RE-alloyed Mg 
alloy by a simple low-temperature and low-speed extrusion process. The 
present alloy reveals extraordinarily high strength and acceptable 
ductility; a TYS of 458 MPa and an elongation of 4.8%. The micro -
structure of the present alloy consisted of a typical bimodal
