ABSTRACT 
The newly developed Mg – Y – Sn extrusions are prepared by adding Sn (0.6 and 1.5 wt%) to Mg – 6Y. The optimized 
Mg – 6Y-1.5Sn exhibited superior elevated-temperature strength up to 300 
◦
C, charactered by the pronounced 
strain hardening. Specifically, the yield stress and ultimate tensile strength of this alloy at 300 
◦
C are 186 ± 3 
MPa and 328 ± 7 MPa, respectively, which are 64% and 93% higher than that of the highly RE-alloyed WE54. Sn 
addition to Mg – 6Y introduces a large number of thermal-stable micron/nano-scale Sn
3
Y
5 
precipitates. Mg – 6Y- 
1.5Sn, which contained more nano-scale precipitates, exhibited better elevated-temperature strength than that of 
Mg – 6Y-0.6Sn. The grain structure transitioned from a uniformed equiaxial grain in Mg – 6Y to a thermal-stable 
multiscale grain structure in Mg – 6Y-0.6/1.5Sn, which consisted of fiber textured un-DRXed area with high 
dislocation density and randomly orientated DRXed grains with low dislocation density. The exceptional 
elevated-temperature strength strongly correlated to the combined thermal-stable multiscale precipitates and 
grain structures. 
1. Introduction 
As one kind of the most important heat-resistant magnesium (Mg) 
alloys, Mg-RE based alloys [ 1 – 3 ], have the potential to be applied in 
powertrain system of automotive or aerospace parts. Admittedly, binary 
Mg-RE alloys are incapable to satisfy the industrial requirements due to 
their limited properties. Thus, alloying is necessary for achieving high 
temperature performance of Mg-RE based alloys. Integrated multiple RE 
elements in Mg alloys is a reliable strategy to improve the room tem -
perature and high temperature mechanical properties of Mg alloys 
[ 4 – 6 ]. Accordingly, some heat-resistant alloy systems, such as 
Mg-Gd-Y-Zr and Mg – Y-Nd-Zn-Zr, were developed [ 7 – 11 ]. The addition 
of Gd and Y to Mg can produce dense precipitates, which exhibit less 
diffusivity and good coherency to the Mg matrix. Some non-RE elements 
are also effective. The addition of Zr resulted in grain refinement [ 11 ] 
and Zn introduced long-period-stacking-ordered (LPSO) phases [ 12 ] to 
the matrix, which can impede basal slip below 400
◦
C, and thus improve 
mechanical properties [ 13 ].

et al. [ 14 ] and Zhao et al. [ 15 ] studied the mechanical properties of 
Mg – Sn – Y and indicated that the addition of Sn and Y contributed to the 
formation of Mg

the high temperature (HT) strengthening for Mg – Sn – Y alloy. Our recent

We studied their HT mechanical properties and detected that the Sn

2. Materials and experimental procedures 
The raw materials of Mg – 6Y, Mg – 6Y-0.6Sn, Mg – 6Y-1.5Sn alloys are 
formed by commercially pure Mg (99.9 wt %), high pure Sn (99.99 wt%) 
and Mg – Y (30 wt%) intermediate alloy respectively. Hereafter, all 
compositions are in mass percentage. The charging completely melted at 
approximately 710 
◦
C in an electric resistance furnace and the melt is 
then poured to a preheated low carbon steel mold and air cooled to 
obtain cylindrical ingots with a diameter of 90 mm. Subsequently, the 
ingot was homogenized at 500 
◦
C for 10 h, cooled in air. The large cy -
lindrical ingots are processed into small cylindrical ingots with 
φ 30 ​ mm × 30 ​ mm. Finally, the small cylindrical ingots are extruded 
into bars of 10 mm at 400 
◦
C and with the extrusion ratio of 9, extrusion 
speed of 2 mm/s, extrusion temperature of 400 
◦
C condition, followed 
by immediate quenching. 
Rectangular dog-bone-shaped tensile specimens with a gauge of 8 
mm (L) × 2.5 mm (L) × 2 mm (T) were employed for tensile tests by 
using MTS precision universal tester (CMT5105). The tensile tests were 
performed at room temperature, 200 
◦
C and 300 
◦
C with a strain rate of 
0.48 mm/min along the extruded direction (ED). All tests were repeated 
at least 3 times to obtain a representative result. The actual compositions 
were detected by inductively coupled plasma optical emission spec -
trometer (ICP-OES), as shown in Table 1 . Microstructural observations 
were analyzed via scanning electron microscope (SEM, TESCAN VEGA II 
LMU) accompanied by energy-dispersive spectroscopy (EDS, INCA En -
ergy, Oxford Ins), electron backscatter diffraction (EBSD) technique, and 
atomic resolution scanning transmission electron microscopy (STEM, 
JEOL JEM-2100F). All EBSD data were analyzed by using the HKL- 
Channel 5 software. The sample were thinned in ion beam thinner for 
TEM observations. To investigate the microstructural stability during 
HT tension, a quasi-in-situ tension test was performed on Mg – 6Y-1.5Sn 
at 300 
◦
C by interrupting the process at the desired strains. The trans -
formations of the precipitates and grain structure before and after 
deformation were detected by SEM and EBSD. A more detailed 
description of the quasi-in-situ analysis technique can be found in our 
previous work [ 17 ]. Geometrically necessary dislocation (GND) density 
is estimated based on EBSD data using Nye ’ s tensor using MTEX MAT -
LAB tool box [ 18 ], and details of the calculation can be found in our 
previous work [ 19 ].

To explore the strengthening mechanism of Mg – Y – Sn alloy, the 
microstructure evolution with Sn addition in Mg – 6Y alloy is analyzed. 
Fig. 3 (a-c) shows the SEM images, and EDS results of Mg – 6Y, Mg – 6Y- 
0.6Sn and Mg – 6Y-1.5Sn alloys. As shown in Fig. 3 (a), the EDS results 
show the small fraction of Y-rich phases (area fraction is ~0.13%) in the 
Mg – 6Y alloy. While with the addition of Sn, the precipitates fraction in 
Mg – 6Y-0.6Sn and Mg – 6Y-1.5Sn increased to 3.1% and 4.1%. As Fig. 3 (b, 
c) shown, in Mg – 6Y-0.6Sn and Mg – 6Y-1.5Sn, the precipitate exhibits 
multiscale morphologies including the irregular block with 1 – 2 μ m in 
diameter (indicated by green arrow) and nanoscale particles (indicated 
by blue arrow). Additionally, the size and amount of the precipitates in 
Mg – 6Y-0.6Sn and Mg – 6Y-1.5Sn are different. The enlarged images in 
Fig. 3 (b, c) show that more nanoscale precipitates exist in Mg – 6Y-1.5Sn.

identified as the high-temperature strengthening phase Sn3Y5 [ 32 ]

morphology of the phases were characterized by using TEM, as shown in 
Fig. 3 (d). The results of TEM-EDS indicate that the elemental types of the 
nanoscale precipitates are Sn and Y. Thus, after Sn addition in Mg – 6Y, 
the precipitates changes from Y-rich phases to multiscale Sn

It is known that the major strengthening microstructure features for 
metal materials include grain boundaries, precipitates, solute atoms, 
residual dislocations and homogeneously distributed nano-particles 
[ 43 ], etc. In our study, two grain scales are observed in the Mg – Y – Sn 
alloys, and these multiscale grains consist of un-DRXed area with a 
strong fiber texture and DRXed grains with random orientations. Several 
studies indicated the un-DRXed regions and DRXed regions play 
different roles in the strength contribution by impeding the dislocation 
movement [ 44 – 46 ]. According to Xu and Fan ’ s research [ 44 ], in 
different tensile stages, the dislocation accumulation rates of fine and 
coarse grains are not consistent. These inconsistent dislocation accu -
mulations produce different slip systems in multiscale grains, resulting 
in the simultaneous increase in strength and plasticity of alloys with 
mixed grains structure [ 44 ]. Hence, the multiscale grains structure can 
coordinate plastic deformation and improve the mechanical properties 
of the alloys. 
Moreover, the GND density of Mg – Y – Sn alloys is an order of 
magnitude greater than that of Mg – 6Y. Residual high density disloca -
tions in un-DRXed regions also exert dislocation strengthening. In the 
DRXed region, the residual dislocation density is quite low. However, 
significant grain refinement improves yield strength at RT. Besides, with 
the addition of Sn, two scales precipitates emerged. The micron-scale 
precipitates distribute along the ED, and nano-scale precipitates 
diffusely distribute in Mg – Y – Sn alloys ( Fig. 3 ). Some studies show that 
the micron-scale precipitates increase strength mainly through the load- 
beating effect, and they have limited effect on the dislocation movement 
[ 47 ]. As for the nano-scale particles, the strength is prominently 
enhanced by the Orowan mechanism [ 47 – 49 ]. Therefore, the mixed 
grain structures, multiscale precipitates and high residual dislocations 
are probably the origin of the superior HT strength of Mg – 6Y-0.6Sn and 
Mg – 6Y-1.5Sn alloys. 
4. Conclusions

properties at both RT and HT by adding trace Sn (0.6 wt% and 1.5 wt%) 
to Mg – 6Y. The addition and increase of Sn can improve the mechanical 
properties of the Mg – Y alloy at RT and HT. Specifically, The UTS of
