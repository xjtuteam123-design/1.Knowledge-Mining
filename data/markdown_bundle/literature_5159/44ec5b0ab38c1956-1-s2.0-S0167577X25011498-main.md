ABSTRACT
The advancement of modern industry demands high-performance structural materials capable of withstanding 
high temperatures. In this work, a Co-Cr-Ni-Ti medium entropy alloy (MEA) was successfully fabricated via dual- 
wire arc additive manufacturing (D-WAAM). This investigation analyzes the microstructure and high- 
temperature mechanical properties of the Co-Cr-Ni-Ti MEA. The results show that the alloy exhibits a den -
dritic structure and is composed of FCC, L1
2
, and η phases. It exhibits excellent high-temperature tensile strength, 
retaining 699 MPa at 700 
◦
C. The ultimate tensile strength and elongation reach up to 793 MPa and 16.7 % at 
600 
◦
C, which are superior to alloys prepared by other methods. The fabricated MEA is a promising candidate for 
high-temperature structural applications in many fields.
1. Introduction
The challenge of developing high-performance structural materials 
for high-temperature applications persists, as industrial advancements 
raise the operating temperatures of mechanical components across 
various fields. In recent years, medium/high entropy alloys (M/HEAs) 
have garnered significant attention due to their unique design strategies 
and properties [ 1 ]. Specifically, the CoCrNi MEAs with a face-centered 
cubic (FCC) structure exhibit satisfactory mechanical properties at 
room and low temperatures [ 2 , 3 ], but their high-temperature strength 
remains insufficient for industrial applications.
Adding elements to introduce strengthening phases in the FCC ma -
trix is an effective approach to enhance high-temperature performance 
[ 4 , 5 ]. The strengthening phases can significantly suppress dislocation 
motion during deformation and activate synergistic strengthening 
mechanisms [ 6 , 7 ]. Additive manufacturing (AM) demonstrates signifi -
cant potential for synthesizing M/HEAs through its customizable 
composition and in situ alloying capabilities [ 8 , 9 ]. Compared with other 
powder-based additive manufacturing technologies, wire arc additive 
manufacturing (WAAM) not only improves material utilization effi -
ciency but also overcomes limitations in material size while reducing the 
cost of raw materials and equipment [ 10 ]. Dual-wire arc additive 
manufacturing (D-WAAM), an innovative form of WAAM, overcomes 
the compositional constraints of single-wire systems through enhanced 
flexibility [ 8 ]. However, there is limited research on the high- 
temperature mechanical properties of M/HEAs prepared by WAAM.
In this study, the Co-Cr-Ni-Ti MEA was successfully fabricated via D- 
WAAM with twisted wire, and its microstructure and high-temperature 
mechanical properties were systematically investigated. The results 
demonstrate that the Co-Cr-Ni-Ti MEA has better high-temperature 
mechanical properties than the existing alloys prepared by other 
methods.
2. Material and methods
In this study, the Co-Cr-Ni-Ti MEA was fabricated using a dual-wire 
cold metal transfer welding platform (as shown in Fig. 1 (a) ). Custom- 
designed Co-Cr-Ni twisted wire (1.2 mm diameter) and pure Ti wire 
(0.8 mm diameter) were used, and a 304 stainless steel plate was used as 
the substrate. The Co-Cr-Ni twisted wire was formed by twisting two 
Cr30Ni70 alloy wires and one pure Co wire, each with a diameter of 0.6 
mm, as shown in Fig. 1 (b) . The specific deposition process parameters of 
D-WAAM were as follows: twisted wire feeding speed of 7 m/min, Ti 
wire feeding speed of 1.5 m/min, traveling speed of 5 mm/s, welding 
voltage of 21.3 V, and welding current of 187 A. Pure argon was used as 
the shielding gas at a flow rate was 15 L/min.

3. Results and discussion
As shown in Fig. 2 (a), the XRD pattern of Co-Cr-Ni-Ti MEA shows the 
sample consists of FCC and η phases. Fig. 2 (b) shows a characteristic 
dendritic (DR) microstructure, where dark gray areas correspond to the 
FCC phase and bright white areas represent the η phase, consistent with 
the XRD analysis. EDS mapping in Fig. 2 (c) indicates Ni-Ti enrichment 
in the interdendritic (ID) blocky η phase, while Co-Cr segregation 
dominates in the DR regions. Due to the rapid cooling rate of WAAM, the 
alloy undergoes non-equilibrium solidification, leading to significant 
microsegregation. In addition, the Ni-Ti atom pair ( 35 kJ/mol) ex -
hibits a more negative mixing enthalpy than other atom pairs [ 11 ], fa -
voring the formation of Ni/Ti-rich intermetallic compounds.
The microstructure was further characterized using TEM. The TEM 
image in Fig. 3 (a) shows that in addition to the blocky η phase in the ID 
region, spherical precipitates were also observed among neighboring η 
phases. Fig. 3 (b) and (c) show the high-resolution TEM images of the 
interface between the blocky precipitates and the matrix, and between 
the spherical precipitates and the matrix, respectively. The Fast-Fourier

4. Conclusions
In this work, the Co-Cr-Ni-Ti MEA was successfully fabricated via D- 
WAAM, and its microstructure was systematically investigated along 
with the evaluation of high-temperature mechanical performance. 
Microstructural analysis showed that the alloy exhibits a dendritic 
structure, consisting of the FCC, spherical L1
2
, and blocky η phases. At 
600 
◦
C, the alloy demonstrated balanced mechanical properties with a 
yield strength of 547 MPa, ultimate tensile strength of 793 MPa, and 
elongation of 16.7 %. These results indicate that the alloy exhibits 
excellent tensile properties compared to alloys prepared by other 
methods.
