ABSTRACT 
When a power module is under a continuous electrical load, a temperature effect is induced by the 
current load in the module configuration. The joint material therefore has long-term temperature and 
mechanical loadings under supplied power. A long-t erm temperature load can change the material and 
mechanical properties, including void ing, cracking, creeping and fractur ing. Au/20Sn eutectic alloy, a 
highly temperature resistant material, is typically used for electric interconnections in high-power 
modules. The Au/20Sn is converted into AuSn and an Au
5Sn intermetallic compound (IMC) by solid 
liquid inter-diffusion (SLID) bonding to form joints with high melting points. In this study, a test 
vehicle based on an actual power module was designed and fabricated to inves tigate and understand the 
material properties and mechanical behavior of Au/20Sn solder under a temperature load. The joint 
microstructure exhibited variation under different thermal treatment conditions such as temperature and 
load durations. The shear strength test was conducted to ex amine the mechanical st rength of the joints 
under different thermal load conditions. The failure mode of the joint was further determined using 
fracture morphology after the shear test. Finally, th e shear strength of Au/20Sn was identified to inves-
tigate the high temperature resistance of joints unde r different temperatures. The mechanical strengths 
of joints under different temperature loads are expressions of different mechanical characteristics and can 
be used to determine reliability at an intended high application temperature. 
Keywords: Mechanical strength, Au/20Sn eutectic alloy, Intermetallic compound, Solid liquid inter- 
diffusion, High-power modules, Shear strength test. 
1. INTRODUCTION 
The power module, as shown in Fig. 1, is an important 
component for power control systems and is broadly 
used in power supplies, e.g., automated controls, motors 
and renewable energies. An insulated gate bipolar 
transistor (IGBT) is a 3-terminal power semiconductor 
device that presents little switching loss and with a fast 
switching rate. However, IGBT die has some drawbacks, 
e.g., the electrical load may cause the chips generating 
excessive thermal energy, creates Joules heating and 
increases component temperature. The mismatch of 
coefficient of thermal expansion (CTE) among different 
materials may also create m echanical failure and cause 
long term reliability problems of the power module. The 
aluminum bonding wire is the most popular interconnec-
tion for power modules (Fig. 2 (a)). High percentage of 
the heat will be dissipated by bottom cooling system of 
the power module [1]. Due to the increasing demand of 
high power applications, high power densities with high 
operating temperature power modules have received 
more attention [2]. More powerful chips can be used in 
power modules as the processing capability of the semi-
conductor improves [3, 4]. As the limitation of heat

2. MATERIALS AND METHODS 
2.1 Au/20Sn Eutectic Alloy 
The Au/Sn material characteristics have been studied 
in many literatures [6-9]. The Au/Sn binary phase 
diagram ([10], Fig. 3) shown that the Au/20Sn alloy 
melting point is 278°C. At a processing temperature 
exceeding 278°C, the Au/20Sn eutectic alloy melts, 
reacts and transforms into ma terials with high melting 
points: - and -phase IMCs. The -AuSn and -Au
5Sn 
phase IMCs have melting points of 419°C and 522°C, 
respectively. 
The mechanical strength of the bonding joints of a 
package device declines dur ing long-term operation. 
The thickness of an IMC incr eases with rises in aging 
temperature or extensions of aging time. However, 
temperature loads decrease its mechanical properties. 
The mechanical strength of a joint can be used to deter-
mine the weakest material and ease fractures. Reliabil-
ity tests such as the thermal or power cycling tests can be 
conducted to quickly evaluate the mechanical strength of 
a joint and module reliability. In one study, the shear 
strengths of high temperatur e lead-free solders, Zn-Sn, 
Au-Sn and Pb-Sn alloys, were determined via a thermal 
cycling test (TCT) conducted at -40°C to 125°C, and 
Au-Sn exhibited the highest shear strength [11]. 
Another study showed that the shear strength of Au/20Sn 
was double that of the Pb-10Sn and Ag paste after treat-
ment at a high temperature. Mechanical strength de-
creases or electrical resistan ce increases when the aging 
time extends or aging temperature increases [3, 4, 12, 13]. 
Consistent with the aforementioned references, Au/20Sn 
has the potential for use in advanced power modules due 
to its ease of processing, material properties and stable 
mechanical strength. Moreover, Au/20Sn eutectic solder 
has several advantages over conventional and lead-free 
solder, including flux-less bo nding, a high mechanical 
strength [3-5, 11-13] and high corrosion characteristics 
[14-16].

3. EXPERIMENTAL RESULTS 
When the reflow temperature exceeded 278°C, the 
Au/20Sn preform melted and r eacted with the Ni/Au to 
form IMCs. The test vehicle was reflowed in a vacuum 
reflow machine at 300°C for 90 sec. After soldering, 
some parts of the specimens were retained in the 
as-bonded condition, and others were thermally treated 
for durations of 250, 500, 750, 1,000 and 2,000 h at 
150°C, 200°C and then 250°C. Interfacial reaction 
behavior was observed. Furthermore, the cause of vari-
ation in the microstructure morphology and changes in 
the material strength under different treatment conditions 
were investigated.

Mechanical properties such as shear strength, fatigue

The solder joints in a power module are always subjected 
to cyclic temperature and me chanical loads during oper-

shear strength and fracture su rfaces must be analyzed to 
determine the weakest material in the package joint. 
3.2.1 Shear Test Condition
 
In this study, the shear test was conducted to estimate 
the effect of thermal load on the mechanical strength of 
Au/20Sn solder joints as a function of aging tempera-
tures for different durations. The fracture behavior of 
the joints was affected by the interfacial reaction between 
materials during a temperatur e load. Excessive growth 
of an IMC might have affected the reliability of the joints 
due to the brittleness of the IMC. The stress concentra-
tion was related to the volume variation or the entire 
consumption of the joint material composition, which 
might have influenced the mechanical strengths of the 
joints. The shear tester machine (Dage 4000, England) 
was used to measure the shear strengths. Figure 13 
shows a schematic diagram of the shear test conditions. 
The shear height was 200 m above the substrate and the 
shear speed was 150 m/s. Seven test vehicles were 
used under each test condition to calculate the average 
shear strength.

3.3 Discussion 
In summary, the mechanic al strengths of the as- 
bonded joint and Au 5Sn IMC declined as the application 
temperature increased. Th e degradation in joint 
strength and application temperature exhibited a recip-
rocal relationship. The main fracture in the AuSn joints 
occurred at the interface between the NiSn IMC and Ni/P 
metallization layer. The mechanical strength of the 
AuSn IMCs was higher than th e adhesive strength of the 
interface between the NiSn IMC and Ni/P. Studies 
have shown that microstructure variations and increases

package joints under UBM or assembly conditions.

4. CONCLUSIONS 
Due to the increasing demand of high power applica-
tions, high power densities with high operating tempera-
ture power modules have received more attention. This 
study adopted high melting point Au/20Sn eutectic alloy 
as the joint material for double-sided power module. 
The shear test was conducted to determine the shear 
strength of the Au/20Sn material at various temperatures. 
The Au/20Sn eutectic alloy transformed IMCs (AuSn 
and Au
5Sn) with high melting points after bonding. 
The microstructures and co rresponding mechanical 
strengths under various thermal treatment conditions 
were determined. The microstructure of the joints 
changed from AuSn and Au
5Sn IMCs into a full Au 5Sn 
IMC under a temperature load. The IMCs formed 
faster at higher temperatures. The microstructural 
variation and IMC formation slightly affected the 
mechanical strength of the Au/20Sn solder, which 
retained its stable mechanical strength after thermal 
treatment. The full Au
5Sn IMC had a higher mechani-
cal strength than the as-bonded joint (AuSn and Au 5Sn 
IMCs) at room temperature. The mechanical strength 
of the joint material under various temperature loads was 
measured to determine their temperature-dependent 
mechanical properties and hi gh temperature resistance 
capability. A reciprocal relationship was observed 
between material strength and temperature. The 
mechanical strengths of the as-bonded joint and Au
5Sn 
IMC declined by about 50% as the temperature rose from 
room temperature to 250°C.
