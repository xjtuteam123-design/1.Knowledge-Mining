ABSTRACT
A modiﬁed phase-ﬁeld model coupling viscoplastic constitutive equations has
been built up to simulate the creep process of nickel-base single-crystal super-
alloys at 1223 K/300 MPa. The kinematic and isotropic hardening effects as well
as interactions of slip systems are included in the present model. Under the
external tension along [001] direction, the plastic strain prefers to concentrate in
the channel vertical to [001] direction, promoting c0 precipitate to raft along the
direction vertical to [001] direction. The interactions between slip systems alter
the value of plastic strain and thus the stress ﬁeld in inner c channel. In turn, the
stress ﬁeld readjusts the plastic deformation. The simulative results and
experimental data are in good agreement in the initial creep stage. In addition,
this modiﬁed model gives a possibility to simulate the microstructure evolution
during cycle fatigue.
Introduction
Nickel-base single-crystal superalloys have emerged
as the important high-temperature structural mate-
rials, like turbine blades, when the resistance to
creep, fatigue and environmental degradation is
required [ 1]. The excellent high-temperature perfor-
mance of such alloys originates from the
microstructure of L1 2 intermetallic compound Ni 3Al
(c0 precipitate) coherently embedded in the Ni-rich
face-centered cubic (FCC) c matrix.
During the process of creep loading at high tem-
peratures, the initial cubic c0 precipitate tends to
coarsen directionally (i.e., c0 rafting) and arrange at
regular intervals in c matrix. At the same time, a large
amount of plastic deformation, caused by dislocation
glide and climb, occurs in c channel. Notably, the
dislocations can interact with the adjacent c0 precip-
itates as well as the neighboring dislocations, affect-
ing the deformation mechanism and the
corresponding creep performance of nickel-base
superalloys [2–6]. Thus, it is signiﬁcant to understand
the coupling evolution of c0 precipitate and disloca-
tions and to obtain the relationship between
microstructure evolution and macroscopic creep
behavior.
As well known, the plastic deformation takes place
mostly in c channels and the main driving force for c0

was described through the macroscopic strain evo-

creep rate vs. time curve in the initial stage of the
transition creep was obtained. Nevertheless, the ani-
sotropic calculations of inelastic strain evolution were
lacked in this model. Gaubert and his co-workers
[10–12] proposed an elasto-viscoplastic model which
considered the elastic heterogeneity and plastic ani-
sotropy, as well as long-range order of c
0 precipitate.
The ﬁrst and secondary creep steps could be simu-
lated by this method with the viscoplastic parameters
determined from experiments. It should be pointed
out that the inﬂuence of dislocation interactions on
hardening was neglected in this model. Recently, Wu
et al. [ 17] built a phase-ﬁeld model, coupling with
continuum dislocation dynamics, to explain the raft-
ing mechanisms. One advantage of this model is that
the short-range dislocation–dislocation interactions
have been taken into account. However, as well as the
model developed by Tsukada [ 18, 19], this model just
suites for the isotropic materials. In brief, the quan-
titative simulation of rafting phenomenon and the
prediction of creep properties at high-temperature
creep still need further research.
The main aim of the present article is to propose a
phase-ﬁeld model coupling the viscoplastic consti-
tutive equations which are able to account for not
only the kinematic hardening but also the isotropic
hardening effect. The inﬂuence of different
interactions between the 12 slip systems on the
hardening is included in the isotropic hardening
effect.

It is known that creep deformation can be divided
into three stages: primary creep with the generation
and multiplication of dislocation among c-channels;
secondary creep with constant creep strain rate due
to the balance between dislocation annihilation and
multiplication; tertiary creep with an increased creep
strain rate due to the c
0-shearing and microstructure
damage. In the present article, the generation and
multiplication of dislocation are achieved through the
activation of slip systems; thus, the primary creep
could be simulated reasonably. However, the other
two stages of creep cannot be simulated in current
due to the fact that the factors of dislocation annihi-
lation, microstructure damage and c
0-shearing are
difﬁcult to take into account in the present model.
The relevant work is under way to overcome these
difﬁculties. Besides, based on the description of
hardening models in Part 2, it can be found that both
the inﬂuences of monotonic loading and cycling
loading on the hardening are taken into account in
the present viscoplastic constitutive model. There-
fore, when coupled this viscoplastic constitutive
model with phase-ﬁeld model, the microstructure
evolution under cycle loading (i.e., cycle fatigue)
could also be simulated.
Conclusions
A phase-ﬁeld model coupling viscoplastic constitu-
tive equations has been built up and used to simulate
the creep of nickel-base single-crystal superalloys at
1223 K/300 MPa. Both kinematic and isotropic
hardening effects are included in the present model.
Under the external tension along [001] direction, the
plastic strain in c horizontal channel is way above
that in c vertical channel, contributing to the forma-
tion of plate-like c
0 precipitate. In inner c channel, the
changing tendency of elastic strain is proportional to
that of the stress, while the changing tendencies of
plastic strain and stress are no longer analogous. The
interactions between slip systems alter the value of
plastic strain and then the distribution of stress. The
distribution of stress, in turn, affects the plastic
deformation. Besides, the simulative result and
experimental data are in good agreement in the initial
creep stage.
