Abstract : The effect of matrix microstructure on the fatigue strength of a 
SP700 titanium alloy matrix composite unidirectionally reinforced with SiC 
fiber, SCS-6, has been studied. Three kind of composite materials associated 
with different matrix microstructure were prepared by giving the different heat 
treatments at elevated temperatures below and above the β-transus temperature 
of the matrix alloy after the fabrication process. The load-controlled high-cycle 
fatigue tests were conducted under a stress ration of 0.1 at room temperature in 
air. It was shown that the fatigue strength was significantly changed by the 
matrix microstructure. The mechanism was discussed, based on the 
measurement of the interfacial shear strength by the fiber push-out tests and the 
fatigue properties of the matrix alloy. 
 
Keywords : SP700 titanium matrix composite, SiC fiber, Fatigue test, Fatigue 
strength, Matrix microstructure, Matrix fatigue properties, Push-out test, 
Interfacial shear strength

Introduction 
 
Titanium alloy matrix composites reinforced with continuous fibers have received 
considerable interests as the materials under severe service conditions, because they 
generally possess many attractive characteristics: higher specific strength and higher 
specific stiffness, compared with conventional monolithic materials [1,2]. On applying 
these composite materials to actual components, fatigue fracture is one of critical issues to 
be understood, or clarified [3-5]. Many researchers have confirmed, on the other hand, that

properties of industrial titanium alloys, matrices of the composites, are strongly influenced by 
their microstructures relevant to the thermo-mechanical treatment processes. Thus it is expected 
that the fatigue strength of the titanium alloy matrix composites is improved by controlling the 
matrix microstructures. 
The effect of matrix microstructure on the fatigue strength of a SP700 titanium alloy matrix 
composite unidirectionally reinforced with SiC fiber has been studied in this work. The role of 
the matrix in the fatigue strength of the composites was discussed, by comparing with the 
fatigue properties of the matrix alloy. 
 
 
Experimental procedure 
 
The composite material tested is a Ti-4.5Al-3V-2Fe-2Mo titanium alloy (denoted by SP700; a 
trademark by NKK, Japan) reinforced unidirectionally with continuous SiC fibers, designated 
as SCS-6 which is supplied by Textron Specialty Materials, USA. The SCS-6 fiber is a −β 
SiC fiber of 140 mµ diameter with a carbon rich graded silicon carbide coating of 3 mµ 
thickness on the surface. The thin sheet of the composite of which thickness was 1.5 mm was 
fabricated by vacuum hot pressing at the temperature of 800 °C under the pressure of 49 MPa 
for 0.5 hour alternating layers of thin-foils of the SP700 alloy and woven fabrics of the SCS-6 
fibers. The volume fraction of the fibers in the composite is about 30 %. 
Three kind of composite materials with different matrix microstructure were prepared by giving 
the different heat treatments at elevated temperatures below and above the −β transus 
temperature (900 ± 5 °C) of the matrix alloy after the fabrication. The conditions of the heat 
treatments are 1) as-fabricated, 2) at 880 °C for 1 hour and 3) at 950 °C for 0.5 hour. 
Hereinafter, these materials will be denoted by the as-fab., DA and β materials, respectively. 
Monolithic SP700 titanium alloy was given the same heat treatments to be compared with the 
composite materials. The conditions of heat treatments are summarized in Table 1. 
From the composite and the matrix alloy sheets, dumbbell-type specimens, which had the gage 
section of 1.5 mm(t) x 2 mm(W) and the full-length of 50 mm (Fig. 1), were cut by an 
electric-discharge machining, where the longitudinal direction of the specimens was arranged 
parallel to the SiC fibers. The surface of all the specimens was polished using a disc grinder 
before the tests. Load-controlled high-cycle fatigue tests were carried out under a stress ratio of 
0.1 at room temperature in air. The fiber push-out tests were also conducted at room 
temperature to measure the interfacial shear strength, by using the sliced plate specimens of 
which dimensions are about 1 mm(t) x 1.5 mm(W) x 10 mm(L). 
The fiber push-out test apparatus is illustrated in Fig. 2 [5].

Results 
 
Mechanical properties of matrix alloys 
The mechanical and the fatigue properties of the matrix titanium alloys with different 
microstructures are summarized in Table 2 and Fig. 3, respectively. It is found from Table 2 
that the mechanical properties of the titanium alloys at room temperature are almost 
independent of their microstructures. On the other hand, the fatigue strength is remarkably 
changed depending on their microstructures. 
 
Matrix microstructure and interfacial shear strength of composites 
The microstructures of the matrix in the composites are given in Fig. 4, which indicates a 
significant change of the microstructures by the heat treatments. The microstructure of the 
as-fab. material reveals an equi-axied primary βα / structure. The β and DA materials 
exhibit coarse Widmannstatten lamellar structure and equi-axied bimodal structure of primary a 
and transformed βα / structures, respectively. The average grain sizes of the as-fab, DA and 
β were about 2.1 mµ and 200 mµ , respectively. The volume fraction of a phase decreased 
in the order of the as-fab., DA and β materials.

Fatigue strength of composite materials 
S-N curves of the respective composite materials are summarized in Fig. 9. It is found that the β 
and DA materials reveals the fatigue strength remarkably higher than that of the as-fab. material 
at room temperature: the fatigue life can be improved by controlling the matrix microstructure. 
The similar trend can be also seen in the monolithic matrix alloys (Fig. 3); an indicating that the 
fatigue strength of the matrix may be a primary factor dominating the fatigue strength of the 
composite in the present composite system. However, it should be noted that the above aspect 
does not support all the phenomena. For example, while the fatigue life of the β material is 
longer than that of the DA material, the relation is reversed in the monolithic matrix alloy.

According to the observation on the fatigue fracture process, it was found that the fatigue cracks 
were initiated at a very early stage of cyclic loading. They invaded the whole matrix part of the 
specimen surface, and then arrested by the fiber bridging for long term, followed by the 
catastrophic failure in final. The fracture surfaces of the as-fab. and β materials are given in 
Fig. 10. The fracture surfaces are relatively flat associated with the slight fiber pullout. This 
must be relating to the high matrix/fiber interface adhesion strength in the present composite 
system. 
 
 
Discussions 
 
The interfacial shear strength in the present composite material is significantly high (Fig. 7). 
For example, the strength in SCS-6/Ti-24A1-11Nb system was about 80 MPa; about one-third 
of the present material [5]. When the matrix/fiber adhesive strength is high enough, the 
composites must behave in such a manner that the matrix and the fiber are subjected to a 
homogeneous deformation under an axial loading without sliding. The observation that the fiber 
pull-out was not noticeable on the fatigue fracture surface supports this aspect. Thus, the fatigue 
strength of the matrix alloy must directly influence that of the present composite material. 
The effect of microstructure on the fatigue crack growth rate in the SF700 matrix alloy is shown 
in Fig. 11 as a function of stress intensity factor range, ∆ K. It is found that the fatigue crack 
propagation resistance is the lowest and the highest in the as-fab. and β materials, 
respectively. This trend is almost similar to that in Ti-6A1-4V alloy [7]. It is worth noting that 
the above resistance fairly corresponds to the fatigue strengths shown in Fig. 8. This suggests 
that the resistance to fatigue crack propagation in the matrix alloy may especially dominate the 
fatigue strength of the composite material. 
 
 
Conclusion 
 
The effect of matrix microstructure on the fatigue strength of a unidirectional SCS-6/SP700 
composite has been investigated. It was shown that the fatigue life of the composite was 
significantly improved by controlling the matrix microstructure. It was also suggested that
