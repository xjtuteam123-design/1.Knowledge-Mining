1. Introduction
Ni–Cr-base superalloys have been widely used in gas turbine and
nuclear power plant industry since they have excellent high tempera-
ture mechanical properties and good corrosion resistance. The increas-
ing industry demands for the improved ef ﬁciency of gas turbine and
heat transport systems, one of the critical technical issues, have been the
major driving force for those systems compatible with higher operating
temperatures (≥950 °C) [1–5]. Up to the present two different technical
approaches, to develop either new Ni–Cr-base high temperature alloys
or protective ceramic coatings for Ni –Cr-base superalloys, have been
made to achieve good mechanical properties and high oxidation re-
sistance at high temperature environments. Extensive research has
been conducted to verify the high temperature properties of Ni–Cr-base
superalloys, but much work has not been done on the protective ceramic
coatings which can provide the enhanced wear and thermal oxidation
resistance [6–11 ]. In this work, 2.5–3.0 µm thick Al2O3 and TiAlN layers
were coated on two well-known Ni –Cr-base superalloys, Hastelloy X
(Ni–22Cr–18Fe–9Mo–1.5Co–1Mn–1Si) and Inconel 617 (Ni –22Cr–
12.5Co–9Mo–1Al), by rf/dc magnetron sputtering and an Arc Discharge
technique, respectively and comparative study on the thermal oxidation
behavior of Al
2O3- and TiAlN-coated samples has been carried out.
2. Experimental
2.5–3.0 µm thick Al 2O3 and TiAlN coating layer was deposited on
Hastelloy X (Ni –22Cr–18Fe–9Mo–1.5Co–1Mn–1Si, Haynes Interna-
tional, Inc.) and Inconel 61 7 alloy (Ni –22Cr–12.5Co–9Mo–1Al, High
Temperature Metals Inc.) substrates. TiAlN coating layer was deposited
on the polished substrates by an Arc Discharge technique and substrate
temperature and chamber pressure wereﬁxed at 450 °C and 1 × 10− 5 Torr
during deposition process. Al 2O3 coating layer was deposited by rf/dc
magnetron sputtering in the reactive argon –oxygen gas mixtures at
substrate temperature of 500 °C and process pressure of 3 × 10 − 2 Torr.
The structural property of each sample was characterized by X-ray
diffraction (XRD) and scanning electron microscopy (SEM) before and
after annealing at 1000 °C for 24–48 h. Wear resistance of as-deposited
and annealed (1000 °C for 24 h) samples was measured by a ball on disk
type wear testing system under the condition of test load 5 kg, rotation
speed 100 rpm. Thermal cyclic oxidation test was conducted at 1000 °C
for up to 48 h and thermal cycle was given at 12 h-intervals. After each
cycle, surface morphology, near surface composition and mass change
per unit area were examined by SEM–EDS and electronic balance.

boundary region were not observed, suggesting that less amount of Cr
has diffused homogeneously to the surface and Hastelloy X has higher

4. Summary and conclusions
Comparative study on the thermal oxidation behavior of bare, Al2O3-
or TiAlN-coated Hastelloy X and Inconel 61 7 was conducted. Cr2O3 scales
are formed along the grain boundaries due to the vigorous surface
diffusion of Cr in Inconel 61 7 after annealing while less amount of Cr has
diffused homogeneously to the surface in Hastelloy X. Al 2O3-coated
samples showed a signiﬁcant enhancement in thermal oxidation resis-
tance by either decreasing the amount of diffused Cr to the surface
(Inconel 61 7) or preventing the diffusion of Cr (Hastelloy X) near the
surface. TiAlN-coated Inconel 61 7 also showed improved thermal oxi-
dation resistance than bare Inconel 61 7 , but TiAlN coating was found to
have a negative effect on the thermal oxidation resistance of Hastelloy X
due to its poor adhesion strength. Al2O3 is thought to be a very promising
coating layer for Ni–Cr-base superalloys against thermal oxidation.
