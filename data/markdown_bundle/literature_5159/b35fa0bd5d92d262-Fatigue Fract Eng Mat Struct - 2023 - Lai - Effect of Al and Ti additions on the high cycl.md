Abstract
For the application of medium-entropy alloys (MEAs) in engineering, high
cycle fatigue behavior of CoCrNi-based MEAs with different Al and Ti addi-
tions was investigated at room temperature (RT) and 350 /C14 C in this study.
When Al and Ti contents were increased from 1 to 4 at.% and 2 to 3 at.%,
respectively, yield and fatigue strength increased by 70% and 43%, respectively,
hardness and ultimate tensile strength increased by 40%, and fracture elonga-
tion was >37%. Between RT and 350
/C14 C, the effect of temperature on the tensile
and fatigue strength was not significant. The fatigue crack propagation resis-
tance increased with increase in Al and Ti contents. Based on the fatigue
strength determined in the study, fatigue life prediction equations were
derived. Microstructure analysis indicated that higher tensile and fatigue
strength were attributed to uniformly distributed precipitates of nanoscale L1
2
particles formed in the FCC matrix.
KEYWORDS
fatigue strength, high cycle fatigue, medium-entropy alloy
Highlights
• Fatigue strength increased by 70% when Al and Ti increased to 4 and 3 at%,
respectively.
• Between RT and 350 /C14 C, fatigue strength was not significantly affected by
temperature.
• Strength was increased by the uniformly distributed precipitates formed in
FCC matrix.

High-entropy alloys (HEAs) and medium-entropy alloys
(MEAs) are multiprincipal element alloys (MPEAs).
Since they were first reported in 2004, there has been
increasing research on MPEAs. 1 Recent advances in alloy
design have significantly expanded the composition
range and research field of alloys. Because of the unique
composition and microstructure, MPEAs have several
unique properties, such as mechanical characteristics,

Among the great number of MEAs studied till now,
CoCrNi MEAs exhibit the best synergy of ductility and
strength.23 CoCrNi MEAs have high strain hardening
due to the combined effect of dislocation glide and twin-
ning; however, their strength, such as yield stress and
ultimate tensile strength, are not sufficiently large
because of the single-phase FCC structure.
24 Strength can
be increased by refining grain size. When the grain size
of CoCr 1.3Ni was decreased to 50%, yield and fatigue
strength increased by /C24 15%.25 When solute atoms were
introduced into the CoCrNi base alloy, strength was
enhanced without significant decrease in ductility. 26,27 Al
and Ti were added to CoCrNi-based MEAs to form sec-
ondary phases, resulting in precipitation hardening.
When 3 at.% Al and 3 at.% Ti were added to a CoCrNi-
based MEA, yield and ultimate tensile strength increased
by 70% and 40%, respectively; the strengthening was
attributed to nanoscale L1
2 (Ni, Co, Cr) 3(Ti, Al)-type par-
ticles.28 When 5 at.% Al and 5 at.% Ti were added into a
CoCrNi-based MEA, yield and ultimate tensile strength
increased five and two times, respectively. 29 However,
the fatigue behavior of CoCrNi-based MEAs with differ-
ent Al and Ti contents at different temperatures still
remains to be understood.
In this study, the fatigue behavior of CoCrNi-based
MEAs with different Al and Ti contents was investigated
at room temperature (RT) and 350
/C14 C. Fatigue tests were
conducted at two temperatures, and the fatigue S-N
curves were obtained. The microstructures and fatigue
fracture surfaces of the CoCrNi-based MEAs were exam-
ined to analyze the strengthening mechanism.

3.1 | Microstructure
Figure 3 shows the EBSD profiles of the microstruc-
tures of AT12 and AT43. Equiaxed grains were
observed for both the alloys (Figure 3A,D). The aver-
age grain sizes of AT12 and AT43 were 130 and
135 μm, respectively. Figure 3B,E shows the Kernel
average misorientation (KAM) maps of AT12 and
AT43, which show slightly high values on the upper
and lower parts, and identical distributions for both
alloys, indicated that the dislocation density was iden-
tical and can be ignored.
Figure
4 shows the XRD profiles of the two alloys,
and these were used to identify the phases present. There
were three obvious diffraction peaks at 2 θ = 45/C14 ,5 2 /C14 ,
and 76 /C14 , which corresponded to (111), (200), and (220)
planes, respectively, of the FCC phase. Similar results
were reported. 23,25 The corresponding angle of each dif-
fraction peak of AT43 was slightly smaller than that of
AT12. The lattice constant, α, of AT43 was slightly larger
than that of AT12 because the added Al and Ti caused
lattice distortion.
29 Nanosized second-phase precipitates
possibly existed due to the added Al and Ti; however,
these could not be detected via XRD because of the
peak broadening effect and a small scattering-factor
difference.
28,35
The nanosized second-phase precipitates were
observed in the microstructure using TEM. Figure 5A,B
shows the bright-field and dark-field TEM images,

Material strength can be increased using methods such
as precipitation hardening, solid-solution hardening,

boundary strengthening. Tensile properties of CrCoNi

24 However, when Al and Ti were

fatigue strength increased significantly to 522 MPa

25 but εf decreased to 14%.
For the CoCrNi-based MEAs, the strength slightly
increased when N and Al were added, respectively
(such as B1 and C1 –C3). When Al and Ti were added,
the strength increased significantly and the ductility
was sufficiently good (such as D1 –D4); however,
the contents of Al and Ti should be sufficient
(e.g., comparing D3 and D4). When Al and Ti contents
increased to 5% each, σ
y increased more than five
times and εf remained at 38%, for example, comparing
A9 and D2, because high-content and coherent
nanoprecipitates were uniformly distributed in the FCC
matrix.
29 Fracture toughness typically decreases with
decrease in ductility. Therefore, adding Al and Ti to
CrCoNi MEAs is a good method to increase fatigue
strength and keep good fracture toughness. In addition,
stacking faults and HCP lamellae were observed in
CrCoNi MEAs with ultrafine grains (0.75 μm),
25 but these
were not observed in this study. This may be because the
grain size was very large (135 μm) in this study. There-
fore, future research should examine the effect of grain
size and Al and Ti content on the fatigue behavior of
CoCrNi-based MEAs.

4 | CONCLUSIONS
The fatigue behavior of CoCrNi-based MEAs with differ-
ent Al and Ti contents at RT and high temperatures was
investigated in this study. The main conclusions are as
follows:
1. Comparing Co
32Cr28Ni33Al4Ti3 and Co 32Cr32-
Ni33Al1Ti2, the yield strength of Co 32Cr28Ni33Al4Ti3
increased by 70%, ultimate tensile strength and hard-
ness increased by 40%, fatigue strength at 10 7 fatigue
cycles increased by 43%, and fracture elongation was
>37%.
2. The fatigue crack propagation resistance of Co
32Cr28-
Ni33Al4Ti3 was better than that of Co 32Cr32Ni33Al1Ti2.
When test temperature was between RT and 350 /C14 C,
the effect of temperature on tensile and fatigue
strengths was negligible.
3. Microstructure analysis indicated that the increase of
strength for Co 32Cr28Ni33Al4Ti3 was attributed to uni-
formly distributed nanoscale L1 2 (Ni, Co, Cr) 3(Ti, Al)-
type particles formed in the FCC matrix.
4. Better fatigue strength can be obtained by decreasing
grain size and increasing Al and Ti content; however,
it may decrease the ductility. Future research should
examine the effect of grain size and Al and Ti contents
on the ductility and fatigue strength of MEAs.
