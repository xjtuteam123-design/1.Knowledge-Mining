Effects of trace elements Hf and Si on oxidation
behavior of Co-Ni-Al-W-based superalloys
at 900°C
Fanjie Zhou, Chunmei Ma, Yuheng Zhang, Jialu Wang and Huadong Fu
Institute for Advanced Materials and Technology, University of Science and Technology Beijing, Beijing, China
Abstract
Purpose – This study aims to control the oxidation resistance of Co-based deformed superalloys by adding trace elements Hf and Si.
Design/methodology/approach – The effects and mechanism of trace elements Hf and Si on the oxidation behavior of Co-Ni-Al-W-based forged
superalloys were investigated by cyclic oxidation at 900°C.
Findings – The results show that the addition of trace elements Hf and Si does not affect the type of surface oxides of Co-Ni-based superalloys, and
the oxidation layers of the alloys are TiO 2, spinel, Cr 2O3, TaTiO4,A l2O3 and TiN from outside to inside. However, the addition of elements can affect
the activity of Cr and Ti elements; decrease the formation of TiO 2 and TaTiO 4 layers, which are harmful to the oxidation performance; and then
improve the oxidation resistance of the alloy.
Originality/value – The relevant research results can not only optimize the microalloying element content of Co-Ni-Al-W-based superalloys, but
also provide a new perspective for the composition optimization design of superalloys.
Keywords Trace elements, Cyclic oxidation, Wrought superalloys, Element activity
Paper type Research paper
1. Introduction
With the continuous improvement of the performance of aero-
engines and gas turbines, new generation of high-temperature
structural materials beyond Ni-based superalloys are expected
to be developed with high bearing temperature and other high-
temperature performance. The novel
g 0-strengthed Co-(Ni)-
Al-W-based superalloy (Sato et al., 2006; Li et al., 2019; Azzam
et al. ,2 0 1 8) is expected to become a key high-temperature
structural material in aerospace, energy power, petrochemical
and other industries because of its high solid liquidus, great
thermal fatigue properties and excellent hot corrosion
resistance. Compared with the Ni-based wrought superalloy,
the novel Co-(Ni)-Al-W-based superalloy has a larger hot
working range and more excellent mechanical properties
(below 850°C). However, the high-temperature mechanical
properties (above 900°C) are still insufﬁcient.
At present, the research on Co-(Ni)-Al-W-based deformed
superalloys mainly focuses on the composition design ( Feng
et al., 2012; Pandey et al., 2019), high-temperature mechanical
properties (Gao et al. , 2019 ; Liang et al. , 2021a , 2021b) and
deformation mechanism of the alloys Povstugar et al. ,2 0 1 4).
The main elements of the alloys are basically determined
(Ooshima et al.,2 0 1 0; Kolb et al.,2 0 1 8) while the optimization
of micro-alloying elements is underway. For example,
Neumeier et al. (Neumeier et al. ,2 0 1 5; Freund et al. , 2017 )
developed a new high-performance wrought Co-based
superalloy, which had a larger processing window and better
resistance creep performance at 750°C compared with U720Li
alloy. The authors also had previously developed a series of new
high-performance wrought Co-based superalloys with low
density, great
g /g 0 two-phase stability and large hot working
window (Zhang et al., 2018; Zhang et al., 2019). The research
results above provided an important reference for the
development of high-performance wrought Co-based
superalloys.
In addition to high-temperature mechanical properties and
microstructure stability, oxidation resistance is also the key
factor to determine the engineering application of superalloys.
In the past decade, researchers have focused on the oxidation
behavior of simple Co-(Ni)-Al-W alloys (
Forsik et al. ,2 0 1 8;
Migas et al., 2018) and the in ﬂuence of main alloying elements
(Ni, Cr, Al, etc.) on the oxidation behavior of alloys ( Migas
et al., 2021; Berthod et al., 2021a, 2021b). However, drawing
on the research experience of Ni-based superalloys, the
addition of micro-alloying elements will also have a crucial
impact on the oxidation resistance of the alloy while affecting
the mechanical properties and microstructure stability of the
alloy ( Klein et al. , 2011 ; Liang et al. , 2021a , 2021b). In
Ni-based superalloys, the research on Si element mainly
focuses on the effects on the formation of Al
2O3. The addition

and Si on the oxidation behavior of Co-Ni-Al-W superalloy at
900°C was experimentally investigated in this paper, which may
lay a foundation for promoting the engineering application of the
new wrought Co-Ni-based superalloys.
2. Methods
According to the previous studies of the authors, 50 g-alloy
ingots were prepared by vacuum arc melting equipment after
repeated melting for eight times. The three alloys were named
0Hf0Si, 0.05Hf0.2Si and 0.1Hf0.4Si, respectively. The
nominal compositions of the alloys are shown in
Table 1.
After grinding and cleaning the alloy samples, they were
encapsulated in a quartz glass tube ﬁlled with argon for solid
solution heat treatment: 1100°C/6 h1 1130°C/6 h 1 1160°C/4
h 1 1180°C/4 h. After the solution heat treatment, the samples
were cold rolled with 10% and 25% reduction, respectively.
After the cold-rolled samples were kept at 1075°C for 4 h to
complete recrystallization, the double-stage aging was
completed at 900°C/4 h 1 750°C/12 h. Cut-off samples with a
size of 15 /C2 10 /C2 3m m
3 were put into a quartz crucible with a
lid and baked at 1100°C to constant weight in advance. The
samples were weighed together with the crucible and then put
into the KSL-1200X box furnace heated to 900°C in advance,
for circulating oxidation experiment. The samples were taken
out every 10 h and cooled to room temperature in the air. A
precision balance with an accuracy of 0.0001 g was used for
measuring the oxidation weight gain of the samples, and the
total oxidation time was 300 h. After the oxidation
experiments, the cooled samples were nickel plated, and the
cross-sections were polished for subsequent characterization of
the oxidation layer using ﬁeld emission scanning electron
microscopy (SEM). EBSD data of the samples were acquired
by scanning an area of 250
m m /C2 200 m m in 0.2 m m step.
The oxidized surfaces of the oxidized samples were analyzed by
X-ray diffraction (XRD). Zeiss GEMINISEM 500ﬁeld emission
scanning electron microscopy (SEM) and backscatter modes
were used to observe the oxidized surface and the cross-section
of the oxidation layer. Alloying element activities were
calculated using TIN-Ni9 dataset in the Thermo Calc 2019a
software package.

3.2 Cyclic oxidation weight change
Figure 2 shows the weight gain of the three alloys during the
cyclic oxidation at 900°C. During the oxidation process, the
oxidation weight gain of the 0Hf0Si alloy is obviously larger

curves of the two alloys cross at 90 h. After 90 h of cyclic
oxidation, the oxidation weight gain of 0.05Hf0.2Si alloy is
smaller than that of the 0.1Hf0.4Si alloy, and the relevant
results will be discussed in detail in the Discussion section.

4. Discussion
The addition of an appropriate amount of Hf and Si is
beneﬁcial to the oxidation resistance of the alloy, which
improves the oxidation resistance of Co-Ni-Al-W-based
superalloys by affecting the adhesion of the alloy oxidation layer
and the phase composition of the oxidation layer. Under the
experimental conditions of this paper, multi-angle research
results have conﬁrmed that the oxidation resistance of the alloy
is: 0.05Hf0.2Si> 0.1Hf0.4Si > 0Hf0Si. The speciﬁc discussion
and analysis are as follows.
4.1 Alloy oxidation layer analysis
The oxidation layer of the alloy is composed of continuous and
discontinuous layers. If continuous and dense oxidation layers
such as Cr
2O3 and Al 2O3 can be formed, it will effectively
protect the alloy matrix (Lai, 2007; Berthod, 2005; Teng et al.,
2022). Multilayer oxidation layers, composed of continuous
and discontinuous layers, are observed in the three Co-Ni-Al-
W-based superalloys in this paper. The distribution of the
oxides is shown in
Figure 7.
As observed from the previous experimental results, the
morphology and thickness of the oxidation layers within the
three alloys are different; however, they have same oxidation

4.3 Cracking and spalling of the oxidation layer
The main reason for the oxide peeling off from the matrix is
that the difference in thermal expansion coefﬁcient between the
oxide and the matrix produces thermal stress, which will
deteriorate the oxidation resistance of the alloy. The oxidation
layer stress depends on the volume ratio PBR (Pilling-Bed
Worth Ratio) of oxide and consumable metal in the reaction.
When PBR > 1, compressive stress will form in the oxidation
layer (
Birks et al., 2006; Erdogan et al., 2019). When the PBR is
much larger than 1, the above stress will lead to cracking and
peeling of the oxidation layer.
Through calculation, we obtained that the PBR value of
TaTiO4 is 3.42, which is easy to cause cracking and peeling
of the oxidation layer. The position of the TaTiO 4 layer in
Figure 9 is shown in red curve. In the 0Hf0Si alloy, a continuous
and dense TaTiO4 layer with a thickness of 1–2 m mi sf o r m e da t
the bottom of the oxidation layer. After adding Hf and Si, the
interface between TaTiO
4 in the alloy oxidation layer and
matrix has become indistinguishable. After the addition of Hf
and Si, the amount of TaTiO
4 is signiﬁcantly reduced, and a
continuous TaTiO 4 oxidation layer cannot be formed. The
compressive stress of the alloy oxidation layer is reduced, and
the tendency of the oxidation layer to crack becomes smaller. So
the oxidation resistance of the alloy is improved.
In this study, effects of Hf and Si on the cyclic oxidation
behavior of alloys at 900°C are explored experimentally.
However, it is dif ﬁcult to elucidate the effect of alloying
elements on the oxidation behavior of the alloy during the
initial oxidation period using the cyclic oxidation method. In
the next step, we will explore the initial oxidation behavior of
the alloy by means of thermogravimetric analysis, to better
guide the micro-alloying research of Co-Ni-Al-W-based
superalloys for engineering applications.
5. Conclusion
This study investigates the effects of Hf and Si on the cyclic
oxidation behavior of Co-Ni-Al-W-based superalloys at 900°C,
which provided guidance for the development of new wrought
Co-Ni-based superalloys. The main conclusions of this study
are as follows:
/C15 Hf and Si addition can improve the oxidation resistance by
affecting the activity of Cr and Ti elements. The oxidation
resistance of the 0.05Hf0.2Si alloy is the best under the
experimental conditions in this study.
/C15 The addition of Hf and Si inhibits the formation of TiO 2
and TaTiO 4 oxidation layers, which are harmful to the
oxidation performance.
