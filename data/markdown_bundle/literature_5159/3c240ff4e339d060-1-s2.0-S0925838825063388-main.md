ABSTRACT
The strength-ductility trade-off remains a significant challenge in the design of high-performance structural al -
loys. In this study, a Ti-Zr-Nb-Ta-Mo body-centered cubic (BCC) multi-principal element alloy with partial 
recrystallization was fabricated via cold rolling followed by intermediate-temperature annealing. The alloy 
demonstrates an exceptional combination of yield strength (1079.2 MPa) and elongation (19.4 %). Through a 
combination of systematic experimental characterization and first-principles calculations, we show that this 
outstanding performance arises from dual-scale (medium- and long-range) chemical fluctuations (CFs), which 
induce heterogeneous local environments that govern dislocation behavior. Specifically, Zr-enriched “ soft ” re -
gions provide favorable slip pathways, while Nb-, Ta- and/or Mo-enriched “ hard ” regions raise unstable stacking 
fault energies, impeding glide and promoting cross-slip. Long-range hard domains further reduce dislocation 
entanglement, preserving ductility. This synergistic soft-hard interplay, supported by bond-order analyses and 
stacking fault energy calculations, highlights controlled chemical fluctuations as an effective strategy for opti -
mizing strength-ductility synergy and guiding the design of next-generation structural materials.
1. Introduction
High-performance structural materials have been targeted for cen -
turies due to their high strength, ductility, and fatigue resistance, 
highlighting the demand for continuous efforts in compositional opti -
mizations and innovative processing techniques [1,2] . Under such a 
context, multi-principal element alloys (MPEAs) [3 – 5] have emerged as 
a key direction for materials science, with core innovation in the utili -
zation of configurational entropy to modulate phase formation and 
microstructure to achieve high performance.
Body-centered cubic (BCC) MPEAs readily achieve high strength, 
thermal stability, exceptional corrosion resistance, outstanding wear 
resistance, excellent irradiation tolerance, and good biocompatibility, 
which merits make them highly promising for a wide range of applica -
tions [6 – 10] . Concerning impressive yield strength (~1 GPa at ambient 
temperature), however, their inherently low or negligible tensile 
ductility significantly hinders large-scale applications [11,12] . Such 
strength-ductility trade-off primarily arises from the dominance of 
straight screw dislocation planar slip during plastic deformation, which 
fails to promote the dislocation interactions necessary for effective strain 
hardening [13,14] . Recently, chemical heterogeneities, including 
short-range order and local chemical fluctuations (CFs), have been 
explored as a novel method to enhance ductility in refractory MPEAs 
[15 – 17] . As demonstrated by these pioneer researchers, the heteroge -
neities can effectively impede dislocation motion by trapping, thus 
enhancing dislocation interactions and significantly contributing to 
strain hardening [18] .
BCC MPEAs have a relatively high recrystallization temperature, 
high grain coarsening onset temperature, and slow grain-coarsening 
behavior during post-deformation annealing [19] . These factors pro -
vide an excellent window for the advanced design of high-performance 
MPEAs, allowing for the development of heterogeneous structures

2.1. First-principles simulation
In our work, all first-principles calculations were conducted using 
spin-polarized density functional theory (DFT) as implemented in the 
Vienna ab initio simulation package (VASP) [26] . The electronic 
exchange-correlation energy was described by the generalized gradient 
approximation of Perdew – Burke – Ernzerhof (GGA-PBE) functional [27]
and the projector augmented-wave (PAW) method [28,29] . Bulk models 
with 120 atoms were constructed with a body-centered cubic (BCC) 
supercell of 3 × 4 × 5. During our calculations, the kinetic energy cutoff 
was set at 450 eV, together with the total energy and force converged to 
10 ⁻⁵ eV and 0.02 eV/Å, respectively.
MPEAs models: The 120-atom supercell was constructed to model 
TZN MPEAs, using the special quasi-random structures (SQSs) supercells 
as the basic reference. Our model started with a series of supercells with 
different levels of chemical short-range orderings (SROs), scanning to 
get the energetically favorable configuration and model the chemical 
fluctuations as determined by MPEAs nature. For selected supercells, the 
volumes and equilibrium lattice constants were optimized before coor -
dinate optimization. As determined by the dimension size, the 3 × 2 × 2 
Monkhorst-Pack k-point was used for the supercell. The atomic positions 
were allowed to relax until the forces on each atom were less than 
0.02 eV/Å, while the shape and volume of the supercell were fixed 
during relaxation.

3.2. Mechanical behavior
The tensile stress-strain curves are presented in Fig. 7 (a1), with 
corresponding comparisons to the as-cast alloy in Fig. 7 (a2). Significant 
improvements in mechanical properties are observed after cold rolling 
and subsequent annealing. Among the tested specimens, TZN-640 ex -
hibits the highest yield strength of ~1123.1 MPa but shows limited 
ductility, with an elongation to failure of ~10.3 % (about 50 % lower 
than the as-cast alloy). In contrast, TZN-740 achieves a yield strength of 
1079.2 MPa, representing ~10 % improvement over the as-cast alloy, 
while maintaining excellent ductility with 19.4 % elongation. Further 
increasing the annealing temperature leads to a gradual reduction in 
yield strength, as observed for TZN-920, although the values remain 
higher than those of the as-cast condition.
A comparison of specific yield strength and fracture elongation be -
tween the present TZN alloys and previously reported representative 
BCC MPEAs is shown in Fig. 7 (b). Benefiting from its relatively low 
density (5.4 g cm ⁻ ³), due to the absence of heavy elements such as Hf 
and W and reduced fractions of Nb, Ta, and Mo, the TZN system achieves 
a favorable balance of specific strength and plasticity. Notably, TZN-740 
demonstrates the most attractive combination of properties, with its 
specific strength surpassing most reported BCC MPEAs [8,14,39 – 48] .

4. Discussion
Our experimental outcomes demonstrated that TZN alloy exhibits an 
exceptional strength-ductility synergy during partial recrystallization 
stages, a characteristic seldom observed in other body-centered cubic 
(BCC) alloys [20,49] . For instance, TZN-740 exhibits a tensile strength 
increase of ~93 MPa relative to the as-cast alloy, while maintaining a 
fracture elongation of ~19.4 %. This unusual combination originates 
from heterogeneous non-recrystallized regions and dual-scale chemical 
fluctuations, which jointly enhance strength without sacrificing 
ductility.
4.1. Mechanisms for achieving strength-ductility balance through partial 
recrystallization
Our experimental results demonstrate that the TZN alloy exhibits an 
exceptional strength-ductility synergy during partial 
recrystallization — a behavior rarely reported in other BCC alloys [20, 
49] . Strength enhancement in partially recrystallized alloys is generally 
attributed to dislocation-mediated mechanisms. However, in BCC alloys 
with stable phases, the persistence of non-recrystallized regions con -
taining high-density dislocations often causes a pronounced loss of 
ductility, primarily due to the limited number of operable slip systems. 
Remarkably, the partially recrystallized TZN-740 alloy maintains an 
excellent balance between strength and ductility, motivating further 
investigation into the underlying mechanisms that sustain ductility in

4.2. Computational investigation of CFs
As revealed above, CFs generated by cold rolling and subsequent 
annealing significantly enhance the strength and ductility of TZN 
MPEAs. This research encompasses both M-CFs and L-CFs. Following a 
detailed explanation of the mechanical influence of L-CFs in the pre -
ceding sections, the primary focus now turns to the mechanistic role of 
M-CFs. To clarify the relationship between M-CFs and mechanical 
properties, especially the mechanism at the electronic level, density 
functional theory (DFT) calculations have been conducted to further 
reveal the underlying mechanism associated with the CFs-determined 
block effect. For comparison, a reference structure with chemical 
randomization bulk supercells (denoted as Bulk-RD x ) has been con -
structed with a special quasirandom structure (SQS) technique alongside 
the bulk supercells exhibiting CFs (denoted as Bulk-CF x ). As listed in 
Fig. S2 , the electronic configuration with the lowest objective function 
and moderate energy among the six SQS supercells has been selected for 
further investigation. Nonetheless, quantitatively characterizing the 
composition of M-CFs at the atomic scale presents significant challenges. 
Given this constraint, our Bulk-CF x models were designed not as direct 
replicas of actual M-CFs configurations, but as representative approxi -
mations to probe their general behavior.
The formation of both M-CFs and L-CFs is governed by the same 
fundamental physical principles-thermodynamic driving forces and ki -
netic pathways [15,47] . Their apparent difference in scale stems from 
the dominant phase transformation mechanism: M-CFs are primarily 
associated with spinodal decomposition, which is continuous and 
diffusion-controlled, leading to fine-scale compositional waves. In 
contrast, L-CFs (nanoparticles) typically form via nucleation and 
growth, a process that requires surpassing a critical energy barrier and 
results in distinct, larger-scale precipitates. This mechanistic distinction 
is ultimately dictated by the system ’ s specific position on the free energy 
curve and the prevailing kinetic conditions [21,24] .
Here, combining phase diagram calculations ( Fig. 1 ), EDS ( Table 1 ), 
and STEM-HAADF images ( Fig. 8 (a), we preliminarily infer that the 
manifestation of M-CFs in the TZN MPEA is attributed to the segregation 
of (Ti, Zr) and (Nb, Ta, Mo). This phase separation behavior is consistent 
with the thermodynamic driving force governed by mixing enthalpy, as 
reported in prior studies [51] . Consequently, the mixing enthalpy be -
tween elements has been used to investigate CFs. Specifically, positive 
mixing enthalpy indicates endothermic mixing, reflecting the repulsion 
or incompatibility between elements, while negative values signify 
exothermic mixing due to attractive interactions. A mixing enthalpy 
near zero suggests neither significant energy release nor absorption 
during mixing, implying neutral interactions where the outcome is 
influenced by external factors like temperature and pressure. It was 
observed that the mixing enthalpy values for the Ti-Zr and Nb-Ta atomic

5. Conclusions
In summary, we demonstrate that stable body-centered cubic (BCC) 
multi-principal element alloys (MPEAs) can achieve an excellent 
strength-ductility synergy through partial recrystallization, obtained by 
cold rolling followed by intermediate-temperature annealing. The 
optimized Ti-Zr-Nb-Ta-Mo alloy exhibits a remarkable combination of a 
yield strength of 1079.2 MPa and an elongation to failure of 19.4 %. 
Such superior mechanical performance originates from dual-scale 
chemical fluctuations (M-CFs and L-CFs), which induce heterogeneous 
local environments that govern dislocation behavior. Specifically, Zr- 
enriched regions act as “ soft ” domains, lowering the energy barrier for 
dislocation motion and providing preferential slip pathways, while Nb-, 
Ta-, and/or Mo-enriched regions serve as “ hard ” domains, elevating 
unstable stacking fault energies ( y
usf
) to impede dislocation glide and 
promote cross-slip. Furthermore, the emergence of the long-range 
“ hard ” domain has significantly alleviated the dislocation entangle -
ment in the recrystallized regions, thereby ensuring the tensile plasticity 
of our alloy. This soft-hard interplay, confirmed by bond-order analyses 
and stacking fault energy calculations, effectively balances strength and 
ductility.
Beyond reporting high-performance MPEAs, our findings highlight 
controlled chemical fluctuations as a promising design strategy for 
tailoring the strength-ductility synergy. Furthermore, this study un -
derscores the utility of computational chemistry in unraveling complex 
multi-element interactions, thereby offering guidance for the rational 
development of next-generation structural materials.
