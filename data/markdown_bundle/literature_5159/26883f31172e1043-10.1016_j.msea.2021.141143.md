ABSTRACT 
Substantial residual tensile stress tends to accumulate in currently available high-Cr ferritic martensitic steels 
that are subjected to cyclical heat treatment, which leads to premature brittle fracture. By tailoring the alloy 
composition, this thermal cycling can be exploited to induce a high number density of nanoprecipitates and 
phase transformations countering residual tensile stresses. In this work, three new variants of ferritic-martensitic 
steels have been designed with computational thermodynamics to meet the goals of mitigating residual tensile 
stresses by lowering martensite start temperatures and of enhancing mechanical strength and irradiation sink 
strength by increasing the number density of nanoprecipitates. Cast materials were subjected to cyclical heat 
treatment. The thermally cycled samples were evaluated with mechanical testing and microstructural analysis to 
identify the optimal composition in which figures of merit include low residual stress and a high density of 
nanoscale MX (M = metal, X = C/N) precipitates, leading to high yield strength with reasonable ductility. The 
noticeably higher density of nanoprecipitates in the optimal alloy favor its higher yield strength, which is sup -
ported by the microstructure-derived yield strength calculation and precipitation kinetics simulation. 
1. Introduction 
High-Cr (9 – 12 wt %) ferritic martensitic (FM) steels are of particular 
interest to both the fission and the fusion communities because of their 
excellent resistance to irradiation-induced void swelling and low ther -
mal expansion compared with austenitic steels [ 1 ]. FM steels are envi -
sioned as promising materials for in-core materials in several proposed 
advanced fission reactor concepts [ 2 – 4 ] and for the structural materials 
in various fusion reactor concepts [ 5 – 8 ]. Development of FM steels 
started in the 1940s. The 10

Currently available high-Cr FM steels are designed and produced in a 
wrought condition with final normalization and temper heat treatment 
[ 16 ]. These FM steels are not directly applicable to thermal cycle pro -
cessing that are commonly seen in welding and additive manufacturing. 
Substantial residual tensile stress was reported in Grade 91 FM steels 
subjected to welding process [ 17 ]. This thermal cycle processing lead to 
premature brittle fracture [ 18 ], and scrutinized control of welding pa -
rameters and additional heat treatment is required to recover the 
ductility [ 17 , 19 , 20 ]. On the other hands, mitigating residual tensile 
stress and promoting residual compressive stress in thermally cycled FM 
steels would be an effective way to improve the ductility of FM steels 
without a need of additional heat treatment. The reduction of residual 
tensile stress can be achieved by lowering the martensite starting ( M
S
) 
temperature of FM steels [ 18 ]. Designing new alloys with tailored 
composition is a fundamental and effective approach to reduce the M
S 
temperature of FM steels [ 21 – 23 ], and Mn is one of the effective ele -
ments for doing so [ 22 , 23 ]. 
In this work, we designed three variants of FM alloys via computa -
tional thermodynamics, and down-selected optimal compositions that 
can withstand thermal cycles. New alloys were fabricated through 
casting (with water-cooled copper molds to simulate rapid solidifica -
tion), followed by the rapid heating and cooling treatment in a Gleeble ® 
thermomechanical system that has been widely used to simulate weld- 
heat – affected zone microstructures [ 24 ]. The resulting materials were 
then evaluated for the mechanical and microstructural performances 
that provide the performance factors necessary to identify the alloy 
compositions with preferred properties for further use for joining or 
additive manufacturing. In this work, we not only completed the 
composition screening, but also performed precipitation kinetics simu -
lation to understand the composition effect on the precipitation 
behavior.

5. Conclusion 
Currently available high-Cr FM steels suffer from brittle fracture due 
to residual tensile stresses after cyclical thermal cycles. This problem 
inspired the development of new FM steels with the goals of mitigating 
residual tensile stress and of enhancing volume fractions of MX pre -
cipitates. Three composition-variant FM steels were designed via CAL -
PHAD with the aim of balancing reductions in M
S 
temperatures to 
promote residual compressive stress, and increases in the volume frac -
tion of the MX precipitates to improve mechanical and irradiation sink 
strength. Samples were fabricated through casting and subsequently 
subjected to cyclical thermal history. Microstructural and mechanical 
properties were carefully evaluated to identify the optimal composition. 
A nonviable composition (AM3) is effectively screened out, based on its 
inferior calculated sink strength due to the absence of MX precipitates 
and its brittle fracture likely originating from the deformation-induced 
instability of retained austenite. The optimal composition (AM2) dem -
onstrates superior yield strength, impact toughness, and sink strength 
with reasonable ductility.
