ABSTRACT
High-entropy alloys (HEAs), also referred to as multi-principal ele -
mental alloys, are the newest group of advanced materials that has 
emerged with the concept of novel alloy design. Near-equal atomic 
ratio of multi-principal elemental parts is the basis for the design of 
HEAs whereas mostly 1 or 2 principal elements are used for design 
of conventional alloy. For a wide range of structural applications, 
many various compositions in large number with a different com -
bination of promising properties are derived from the emergence 
of HEAs. The strength and sluggish diffusion at elevated tempera -
ture are the two main features among various properties of HEAs 
which have attracted the attention of the materials researcher 
community. The new novel materials for superior high- 
temperature properties need to be developed for the high- 
temperature applications as compared to superalloys which is 
a major concern for the materials researcher group. From this 
paper, HEAs have promised to be a coming generation high- 
temperature materials which can be the replacement of Ni-based 
superalloys. High-temperature mechanical properties, microstruc -
tural stability, and phase stability of HEAs are the main focus of this 
review paper and also this state-of-the-art review will be of benefit 
to understand HEAs for high-temperature applications.

1. Introduction
In order to obtain promising changes in chemical, physical and mechanical properties, 
the process of alloying technique is used by which one or more elements in small 
amounts are added to the primary metal [ 1 ]. This novel technique emerged from 
accidental discovery that plays a vital role in the field of physical metallurgy processes 
[ 2 ]. In nearly about 3000 BC, the first alloy, i.e. bronze composed from the alloy of Sn and 
Cu, was discovered by humans [ 3 ]. For the development of alloys constituted from multi- 
principal elements, so many decades were taken for this ultimate discovery of HEAs that 
has emerged in 2004 [ 2 , 4–8 ] but earlier the principal of classical physical metallurgy was 
the basis for the understanding of the formed complex microstructures that were derived 
from the design of alloy having multi-principal element. By middle of binary-phase 
diagrams, intermediate phases were clearly noticed which shows that there is a limit in

was demonstrated by Cantor et al. and Yeh et al. whereas their work was based on multi-

principal elements [ 4 , 9–11 ]. For the making of simplest microstructures rather than 
forming anticipated complex phases and compound, the configurational entropy-based 
theory was postulated by Yeh et al. In their proposed work, they found that there would 
be reduction in the free energies of alloys systems if the number of elements is increased, 
resulting in increasing the configurational entropy and it finally stabilises the solid 
solutions [ 4 , 12–14 ].
From the many research studies, it is found that for formations of solid solutions, the 
hypothesis which is configurational entropy-based is an open topic in the area of material 
science [ 15–17 ]. The observation of phase separation and particular compositional 
change in most of the high entropy alloy was found in literatures [ 16 , 17 ]. For the sake 
of single-phase observation, configurational entropy plays vital roles whereas non- 
configuration entropy and enthalpy play an important role in the stability of phases 
according to the thermodynamic study of multi-component alloy [ 15 ].
The characteristics of individual element, interatomic correlation, processing tem -
peratures and alloy meting temperature are such important parameters on which random 
solid solution formation depends. In short, it is said that for inherent characteristics of 
high entropy alloys were found such as (i) Cocktail effects - composite effects on final 
property, (ii) effects of sluggish diffusion to slow diffusion kinetic (iii) effects of lattice 
distortion - it affects property significantly and (iv) effects of entropy - for stabilising the 
formation of solid solutions [ 2 , 5 ]. By the use of quasi-binary approach, in the HEA 
system comprising Cr-Fe-Co-Mn-Ni, investigation of interdiffusion coefficient was done 
by Tsai et al. [ 18 ]. From the conclusion of their work, it was found that there was high 
normalised activation energy found in high entropy alloys than referenced metals and no 
sensitivity to compositions on the diffusion coefficient was observed.
At 1000 degree Celsius in the HEAs system comprising Cr-Fe-Co-Ni, interdiffusion 
was studied by Kulkarni et al. [ 19 ] and they found that diffusional interactions are strong 
for this system. Since the system Cr-Fe-Co-Ni is extremely important element of many 
HEAs, it is concluded that no ignorance should be done for the diffusion interaction 
while explaining diffusions in high entropy alloys. There is a requirement for more 
detailed studies to explain the kinetics of diffusion in HEAs based on available literature 
survey on fusional analysis of high entropy alloys. The enhanced properties and unique 
alloy design are the reasons for the research based on high entropy alloys despite role of 
entropy in the formation of multiphase and solid solution. That is why material scientists 
are continuously developing new materials having outstanding resistance to softening 
and stabilised microstructures at higher temperatures for high-temperature structural 
applications and these are of prime interest for the researcher and material scientists 
nowadays. Because of unique properties of HEAs such as better oxidation and wear 
resistance, outstanding fracture toughness at cryogenic temperatures, remarkable char -
acteristics of age hardening, superb thermal stability and strength retention at elevated 
temperature, great potential of HEAs for various structural applications at elevated 
temperature is observed in the literature survey [ 6 , 10 ]. As compared to conventional

transformations that takes place above 773 K. In order to determine high temperature 
phase stabilities for alloys, microstructure morphologies (initial) are the key parameters 
that come from differences in microstructure evolutions between interdendritic and 
dendritic spaces. In the current work, fully isolated dual phases structures may maximise 
the operating temperatures of dual phase high entropy alloys by the enhancement of 
higher temperature phase stabilities of alloy without sacrificing its specific yield 
strengths. Therefore, it is concluded that dual phase HEAs can be regarded as high- 
temperature structural materials that can be achieved by alloy design for microstructural 
morphology and also by heat treatment to it.
