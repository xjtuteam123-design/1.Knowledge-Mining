ORIGINAL RESEARCH ARTICLE
Microstructure and Mechanical Properties of Novel
Ti-Fe-Nb Alloys with High Speciﬁc Strength and Low
Elastic Modulus
Qingwei Luo, Fei Jiao, Jia Li, and Peiyou Li
Submitted: 23 April 2025 / Revised: 22 June 2025 / Accepted: 16 July 2025
The novel Ti-Fe-Nb alloys was obtained by replacing some Ti elements with a small amount of Nb element.
With the increase of Nb content, the particle sizes and contents of a-Ti and TiFe phases in Ti-Fe-Nb alloys
are further reduced, while the corresponding content and particle sizes of b-phase are further increased.
The Ti 75Fe20Nb5 alloy has the yield strength of 1615 MPa and maximum plastic strain ( >21.3%). The
elastic modulus of Ti 75Fe20Nb5 alloy is 1.6-5 times that of dense and hard cortical bone (10-30 GPa). The
novel Ti-Fe-Nb ternary alloys with excellent mechanical properties are expected to become a biomaterial in
the medical ﬁeld and a structural material in the engineering ﬁeld.
Keywords Biomedical materials, Elastic modulus, Mechanical
properties, Ti-Fe-Nb alloy
1. Introduction
Titanium alloys have been widely used in aerospace,
medical equipment, and automotive industries due to excellent
speciﬁc strength, corrosion resistance, and biocompatibility
(Ref [ 1-6]). Ti-Fe-based alloys, as an important branch of
titanium alloy systems, are highly favored by materials
researchers based on low cost, high strength, ductility and
toughness (Ref [ 7-10]). Especially in Fe-rich titanium alloys
(such as Ti-Fe alloy system), the addition of Fe element reduces
the alloy cost and improves strength of alloy through phase
transformation control (Ref [ 7-11]). However, the Ti-Fe alloys
still face challenges in engineering applications, such as low
plasticity and the large precipitation of brittle intermetallic
compounds (such as TiFe phase) (Ref [ 12]), which limit the
engineering applications of Ti-Fe-based alloys.
To improve the mechanical properties of Ti-Fe-based alloys,
researchers have introduced b-phase stabilizing elements (such
as Nb, Mo, Sn) through micro alloying strategies in recent years
(Ref [13-16]). By regulating microstructure, mechanical proper-
ties of alloy can be enhanced (Ref [ 17-19]). Due to its excellent
grain reﬁnement ability, solid solution strengthening effect, and
inhibitory effect on brittle phases, Mo and Nb elements were
added (Ref [13, 14, 20]). Research has shown that the addition of
a trace of Nb (usually < 5 at.%) can effectively suppress
precipitation of brittle TiFe phases in TiFe alloys, while
promoting the uniform distribution of small b/a phases, thereby
improving the strength plasticity matching of the alloy (Ref [7, 9,
20, 21]). When Nb element is added to Ti
65.5Fe34.5 alloy, the
ductility and strength of Ti-Fe-Nb alloys are higher than the
corresponding values of Ti
65.5Fe34.5 alloy (Ref [ 21]). The
improvement of alloy strength comes from particle reﬁnement,
while the improvement of ductility comes from the increase of
plastic Ti phase and the decrease of brittle phase content (Ref [ 7,
12, 21]). Therefore, adding Nb element to Ti-Fe-based alloys can
improve ductility, strength, and toughness of alloys. For the
binary Ti
65.5Fe34.5 alloy with the high Fe content, the high content
of TiFe phase leads to low toughness (Ref [ 21]). Due to the
reported low toughness and plastic strain of the ternary Ti-Fe-Nb
alloy, although it has high compressive strength, it still cannot
meet the requirements of engineering applications. To improve
the toughness and plastic strain of ternary Ti-Fe-Nb alloys, it is
necessary to re select binary Ti-Fe-based alloys. According to
previous reports, the binary Ti
80Fe20 alloy has a lower Fe content
and greater ductility (Ref [ 7, 12]). In this paper, the Ti 80Fe20
(at.%) alloy was selected as the base alloy, and by adding trace
amounts of Nb element and reducing Ti content, the synergistic
effect of Nb on the microstructure (including grain size, phase
composition, and precipitation behavior) and mechanical prop-
erties (tensile strength, plasticity, and hardness) of the alloy was
systematically investigated. Analyze the changes in mechanical
properties by combining quasi-static compression tests and
nanoindentation techniques. The research results provide refer-
ence value for designing low-cost, high-strength and ductile Ti-
Fe-based alloys.

Nb content, they correspond to the a -Ti phase region. The
precipitation of phases is not only related to the addition of Nb,
but also to the mixing enthalpy of Ti and Fe atomic pairs. The
mixing enthalpy of Ti and Fe is -17 kJ mol
-1 (Ref [ 27]), and a
larger negative mixing enthalpy indicates a greater interaction
between atomic pairs. During the cooling process of alloy melt,
the atomic pairs of Ti and Fe have a strong attraction and are
prone to form crystalline phases. Although Fe and Nb atoms
also have a large negative mixing enthalpy (-16 kJ mol
/C0 1 (Ref
[27]), the low Nb content has little effect on the large-scale
precipitation of TiFe phase. Due to the rapid diffusion of Ti, it
is easy to form the a -Ti phase, resulting in lower Fe and Nb
content in the region of the a phase. When a large number of Fe
atoms and Ti atoms form a crystal phase, due to the strong
attraction of Fe and Nb, there are a large number of Ti atoms
and Nb atoms around the TiFe phase. Nb atoms can slow down
the diffusion of Ti atoms, so a large number of Ti atoms form
the b -Ti phase.
For Ti
75Fe20Nb5 alloy, when more Nb elements replace Ti
elements, the ability of Nb atoms to slow down the diffusion of
Ti atoms will also be enhanced, thereby suppressing the
precipitation of a large number of a phases. Due to the negative
mixing enthalpy of Fe-Nb atom pair, Nb atoms also slow down
the binding of Fe and Ti atoms, thereby reducing the content of
TiFe phase. As the reduction of TiFe phase, more Ti atoms
diffuse, thereby promoting the increase of a -Ti phase content.
Therefore, the microstructure of Ti
75Fe20Nb5 alloy is a mixed
structure composed of a large number of b phases and a large
number of a phases. In the point scan on the right side of
Fig. 4(A), in the area of the ﬁve marked points, the change in
Fe atom content is small, and the corresponding change in Ti
atom content is also small. The addition of Nb element
indicates that Ti and Fe atoms tend to be more evenly
distributed (Fig. 4(B) and (C)). In Fig. 4(D), the lower Nb
content still corresponds to the region of the a phase. In
Fig. 4(C), the distribution of Fe element tends to be more
uniform than that in Fig. 3(C). In the element distribution
scanned along the line in Fig. 4(E), it can also be observed that
the energy spectrum of Fe element changes relatively little.
Therefore, with the increase of Nb content, Nb element can not
only stabilize the b phase, but also inhibit the precipitation of a
and TiFe phases.

also suggests that the alloy has smaller plastic deformation.

The Ti 75Fe20Nb5 alloy has high strength and low elastic

reference value for creep experiments, thereby saving experi-
