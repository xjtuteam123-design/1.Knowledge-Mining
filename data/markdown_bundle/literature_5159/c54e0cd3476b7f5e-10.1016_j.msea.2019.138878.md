Abstract 
In conventional powder metallurgy, tungsten (W) sin tering is commonly carried 
out at over 2000 , which inevitably causes undesired grain growth an d degraded 
strength. To address this issue, we introduced a fe asible and scalable method to 
prepare dense and strong W alloys. The process rout e involved a wet-chemical 
technique (solution combustion synthesis), by which , uniformly blended W/La 2O3 
composite powder with a particle diameter of approx imately 36 nm was obtained. 
Subsequently, with the advantage of the high activi ty of the as-synthesized composite 
powder, we successfully prepared W-La 2O3 alloy with a relative density of 95.0% 
using pressureless sintering at a temperature as lo w as 1500 . Owing to the low 
sintering temperature and the pinning effect of the in situ-generated oxide particles on 
the grain boundaries, the alloy could achieve a gra in size of 0.57 µm. With the grain 
boundary and intragranular-oxide strengthening, the microhardness and compressive 
strength reached 684.0 HV 0.2 and 2119 MPa, respectively, which are comparable to or 
even higher than those reported in the literature. 
Keywords: Tungsten alloys, ultrafine-grained, solution combus tion synthesis, 
pressureless sintering.

1. Introduction 
Tungsten (W) has been widely used in various engine ering areas due to its high 
melting point, excellent thermal conductivity, low thermal expansion and superior 
mechanical properties at high temperatures.[1-3] Co nventional powder metallurgy 
using micron-sized raw powders has been intensively employed to produce W alloys 
owing to its near-net shaping and cost-effective ad vantages.[4, 5] However, 
pressureless sintering, the most commonly used dens ification method in powder 
metallurgy, of W employs a sintering temperature of over 2000 , which is not only 
energy-consuming but also, more importantly, causes unwanted grain growth. In this 
regard, the addition of some rare earth metal oxide (RO x) particles (such as ThO 2,[6-8] 
La 2O3[9, 10] and Y 2O3[11-17]) to the W matrix is an efficient way to sup press W 
grain growth during high-temperature sintering. Nev ertheless, this addition produces 
another critical issue in that the RO x particles usually slow down or even suppress the 
densification of the matrix.[18-20] Although hot is ostatic pressing (HIP),[21, 22] 
deformation processing,[9, 11, 23-25] spark plasma sintering (SPS)[14, 15, 26] and 
microwave sintering[27] have been conducted to addr ess this problem, these methods 
face remarkable challenges in fabricating large-sca le or complex-shaped W-based 
products, which are commonly prepared by pressurele ss sintering. Therefore, how to 
manufacture W or W-based alloys with fine grains an d a high relative density via a 
pressureless sintering process is very important for industrial applications. 
Nanosized raw powders can effectively promote sinte ring compared to 
micro-scale powders because finer powders have a hi gher surface energy.[28] In this 
case, W nanopowders are favorable for densification promotion and grain refinement 
during the pressureless sintering process.[29, 30] Unfortunately, it is hard to produce 
uniformly blended and nanosized RO x/W composite powders by mechanical milling, 
the most common technique,[11, 12, 31-33] which is difficult to uniformly disperse 
the small amounts of RO x particles into W matrix and could produce detrimen tal 
phase due to the contaminations arising from the mi lling equipment and media. 
Wet-chemical methods, such as co-precipitation, sol -gel and hydrothermal processes,

which are able to achieve homogeneous mixing at the molecular level, offer some 
advantages in the preparation of complex and highly homogeneous composite 
nanopowders.[13, 14, 34-36] In this study, we combi ned solution combustion

composite nanopowder as a high-quality raw material for pressureless sintering to

minutes).[37-40] Simultaneously, owing to the release of gases during the combustion 
process, one-dimensional W 18 O49 with a high specific surface area was obtained 
directly using SCS, leading to a low reduction temp erature and a high reduction 
efficiency. The uniformly blended W/La 2O3 nanopowder reached a relative density of 
95.0% after pressureless sintering at 1500 , which is a temperature reduction of 
more than 500  for W-based alloys compared to the industrial sint ering temperature. 
The in situ -generated La 2O3 nanoparticles effectively inhibited grain growth b y 
pinning the grain boundaries, and finally, we succe ssfully prepared UFG W-La 2O3 
alloy with an optimal microstructure of submicron W grains (~0.57 µm) and 
nanometric La 2O3 particles, most of which were located in the grain s interior. The 
as-prepared UFG W-La 2O3 alloy had the highest compressive strength reporte d in the 
literature of 2119 MPa and an excellent microhardne ss of 684.0 HV 0.2 . Notably, this 
strategy offers a promising pathway for the manufac ture of large-scale or 
complex-shaped W products and opens great potential applications for other metals, 
such as molybdenum (Mo), iron (Fe), copper (Cu) and nickel (Ni).

4. Discussion 
4.1 Solution combustion synthesis process 
In the mixed solution, the W source, La source, fue l and oxidizer exist as 
[H2W12 O40 ]6-, La 3+ , NO 3
-, NH 4
+ and glycine molecules. Upon heating to a moderate 
temperature, the solution begins to concentrate and forms a gel. As the heating time is 
extended, nitrogen oxides and NH 3 gases form during the decomposition of 
ammonium nitrate. Meanwhile, the decomposition of glycine yields mainly NH 3, CO 2, 
H2O, and solid glycylglycine and 2,5-piperazindione.[ 40] Then, gel combustion is 
triggered by the exothermic reactions between nitro gen oxides and NH 3. The heat 
emitted at the beginning can provide the energy req uired for subsequent reactions 
without the need for additional energy supply. Duri ng the combustion process, La 2O3 
particles form by decomposition of lanthanum nitrate. Simultaneously, two successive 
endothermic steps are responsible for W 18 O49 formation during combustion. In the 
first step, WO 3 particles form by decomposition of ammonium metatu ngstate. The 
second step involves the reduction of WO 3 by excess NH 3 to W 18 O49 . The needle-like 
morphology can be explained by the strong anisotrop y of the W 18 O49 crystal structure 
or the solid-liquid-gas processes, which requires f urther investigation. The gases 
released make the reaction products porous and loose, leading to the ultrahigh specific 
surface area and reaction activity of the products. 
During the reduction process, the nucleation of the tungsten crystal at a certain

## Page 16

14 
 
site of the nanoneedle surface is a type of in situ nucleation. It has a low nucleation 
energy barrier and thus a high nucleation rate due to the low elastic energy constraint 
and highly exposed surface area.[43] In addition, t he precursor has a high specific 
surface area resulting from the emission of gases d uring the combustion reaction. In 
this regard, the temperature required to reduce the as-synthesized precursor should be 
decreased, effectively lowering the energy consumpt ion and cost. In addition, during 
the reduction process, the phase evolution of W 18 O49 follows W 18 O49 →β-W→α-W, 
and the amount of β-W is small.[41, 43] Coalescence and coarsening of W particles 
during the reduction process rarely occur because o f the relatively low reduction 
temperature (700 ), which causes the high sintering activity of the as-prepared W 
powders.

At present, numerous techniques have been developed to prepare UFG W alloys. 
For instance, Yar et al. first produced nanocrystal line W-1wt%Y 2O3 powder via a 
modified solution chemical reaction. The sample sin tered by SPS showed a relative 
density of 87% and a grain size of 0.49 µm.[26] Nevertheless, these techniques have 
difficulty producing W components with complex shap es or large sizes. Due to the 
machinability issues resulting from the high hardne ss and brittleness of W alloys, 
conventional press forming followed by pressureless sintering methods to fabricate 
UFG W components with complex shapes or large sizes are urgently demanded. Wang 
et al. fabricated pure W bulk material with an aver age grain size of ~585 nm by 
pressureless sintering of high density green parts pressed by a warm compaction 
method.[63] However, warm compaction needs to be ca rried out in a custom-made 
press mold, and more importantly, pure W will exper ience extremely rapid grain 
growth and then deterioration of the mechanical properties at an elevated temperature. 
In this study, UFG WL10 alloy (0.57 µm) was prepared by conventional cold-press 
forming and pressureless sintering using a composit e nanopowder synthesized by a 
combustion-based method for the first time. The res ults indicate that we can also 
fabricate UFG W parts with a complex shape (e.g., a divertor in a nuclear fusion 
tokamak) or a large size using near-net shaping tec hniques and a pressureless 
sintering process. Futhermore, these UFG W parts ca n be used at ultrahigh 
temperatures without significant mechanical propert y deterioration due to their 
excellent microstructural stability at elevated tem peratures. We also prepared a 
large-sized W-1wt% La 2O3 bulk specimen (~9 mm in diameter and ~35 mm in height) 
using CIP and pressureless sintering. The sample sh own in Fig. 12 achieved an ~95% 
relative density under the same conditions, verifyi ng that our strategy is possible for a 
lager sample size and in industry.

5. Conclusion 
In conclusion, UFG W-La 2O3 alloy has been successfully prepared by SCS and 
pressureless sintering. A needle-like W 18 O49 /La 2O3 composite was prepared by SCS, 
and a W-based composite powder with a particle size of 36 nm was obtained using 
hydrogen reduction. The as-synthesized W nanopowder has ultrahigh sintering 
activity and can achieve a 95.0% relative density a fter being sintered at 1500  for 2 
h. Meanwhile, its grain size is only 0.57 µm, an order of magnitude smaller than the 
value of 4.92 µm for PW, due to the addition of La 2O3, which can effectively inhibit 
grain growth by pinning the GBs. With the GB and in tragranular-oxide strengthening, 
the microhardness and compressive strength reach 68 4.0 HV 0.2 and 2119 MPa, 
respectively, which are comparable to or even highe r than those reported in the 
literature. The strategy offers a promising industr ial pathway for the fabrication of 
large-scale, complex-shaped and high-performance W- based as well as other metallic 
products.
