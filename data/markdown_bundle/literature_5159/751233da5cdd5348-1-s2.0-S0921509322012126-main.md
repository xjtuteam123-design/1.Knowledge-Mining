ABSTRACT 
The content and the existing form of refractory elements significantly affect the performance in service of su -
peralloys, which has gained extensive attention in this field. In this study, basic influence rule of diverse Ta and 
Ti contents on the microstructure and properties of the novel γ
′
phase strengthened Co – Ni-based superalloys is 
clarified by thermodynamic calculation and experimental research. Under the premise of a total Ta and Ti 
content of 5 at%, increasing Ta content elevates the solvus temperature of γ
′
phase and volume fraction of Laves 
phase, and also reduces the volume fraction of β phase in the as-cast alloys. Moreover, the average size of γ
′
phase 
goes down and the coarsening of γ
′
phase is inhibited simultaneously in the alloy after aging treatment. 
Consequently, increasing the content of Ta can enhance the microstructure stability and elevates the Vickers 
hardness and yield strength of the alloy. However, the density and cost of the alloy have to be raised. Meanwhile, 
the variation of Ta and Ti contents affects the oxidation performance of the alloys by influence the volatilization 
of W and Mo oxides. 2Ta3Ti and 3Ta2Ti alloys possess favorable oxidation resistance at 1050 
◦
C. Considering the 
effect of Ta and Ti elements and normal performance requirements of Ni-based superalloys, 3Ta2Ti alloy has 
more impressive engineering application prospects. This work provides a solid foundation for optimizing the 
composition and improving the properties of Co – Ni-based superalloys. 
1. Introduction 
Co – Ni-based superalloys are key candidates for high-temperature 
structural materials of hot components of aero-engines and gas tur -
bines due to their significant thermal corrosion resistance, thermal fa -
tigue resistance and respectable creep properties, which are one of the 
research hotspots in the superalloys field [ 1 – 8 ]. At present, the research 
on Co – Ni-based superalloys mainly focuses on alloys composition 
design, microstructure stability analysis, and mechanical properties 
improvement of the alloy [ 2 , 3 , 9 – 14 ]. 
For example, Ta and Ti are the elements that have the most signifi -
cant effect on the microstructural stability and mechanical property 
enhancement of Co – Al – W-based alloys. The addition of 4 at% Ta and 1 
at% Ti can significantly increase the solvus temperature of the γ
′
phase, 
inhibit the coarsening of the γ
′
phase, and significantly enhance the 
creep properties of the alloys [ 15 – 18 ]. 
Besides, it has been shown that Ta and Ti have a significant role in Ni- 
based superalloys. The low diffusion rate of Ta elements in the matrix 
can effectively inhibit the coarsening of the γ
′
phase of Ni-based su -
peralloys [ 19 , 20 ]. Meanwhile, Ta elements can improve the stacking 
fault energy of the γ
′
phase, thus improving the mechanical properties of 
the alloy [ 21 , 22 ]. In addition, element Ta can also reduce the minimum 
creep rate of Ni-based superalloys and improve the creep life of the alloy 
[ 23 ]. The element Ti can increase the yield strength of the alloy by 
increasing the volume fraction of the γ
′
phase [ 24 ], while when the Ti 
element content increases it causes the precipitation of the ŋ phase, 
which is detrimental to the microstructural stability of the alloy [ 25 ]. It 
can also increase the creep resistance of the alloy, and improve the creep 
life of the alloy [ 26 ]. 
In the Co – Ni-based superalloys developed after a large number of Ni 
elements were added to the Co – Al – W alloy system, Ta and Ti elements 
should retain some of the effect in Ni-based superalloys and Co – Al – W- 
based superalloys. Therefore, Ta and Ti elements should have a signif -
icant influence on the microstructure and mechanical properties of the 
alloys. However, the effects of the incorporation of Ta and Ti in

4. Discussion 
4.1. Effect of Ta and Ti on as-cast microstructure of alloys 
In section 3.2 , the study on the effect of refractory elements on the 
interdendritic phase of as-cast microstructure of the alloys shows that 
with the total amount of 5 at% Ta and Ti, the increase of Ti content 
elevates the volume fraction of β phase in the interdendritic phase of the 
alloys, yet reduces that of Laves phase. Inversely, the increase of Ta 
content elevates the volume fraction of Laves phase in the interdendritic 
phase of the alloys, yet reduces that of β phase. 
Laves phase is AB
2 
type compound, in which A atom can be Ti, Nb, 
Ta, Mo, W, etc., and B atom can be Fe, Co, Ni, etc. Relevant studies have 
depicted that the increase of Ta will get rise to a stronger precipitation 
tendency of Laves phase in the alloys. Moreover, Ta element itself is a 
Laves forming element with a large atomic radius, which tends to enrich 
in the interdendritic region during solidification, and further leads to an 
increase in the volume fraction of the interdendritic Laves phase in the 
alloys. Besides, it has been reported that Ti element is the forming 
element of β phase [ 33 ]. Thus, the increase of Ti will further elevate the 
volume fraction of β phase in the interdendritic phase of as-cast 
microstructure. 
Relevant literature and our previous studies [ 30 , 33 ] reveal that 
Laves phase will first nucleate and β phase follows during the solidifi -
cation of superalloys, while ( γ + γ
′
)
e 
eutectic precipitates in the final 
solidification stage [ 37,38 ]. Therefore, combined with relevant 
literature and studies, it is inferred that the precipitation order of the 
as-cast microstructure of the alloys is Laves phase →β phase → ( γ + γ
′
)
e 
eutectic. As shown in Fig. 12 , the enrichment of Ta, Mo, Nb and so on 
occurred in the early formation of the interdendritic phase ( Fig. 12 (a)). 
For its high solvus temperature, the nucleation and growth of Laves 
phase occurred first. During the growth of Laves phase, because the Al 
content in Laves phase is minor, more Al elements will be discharged 
into the residual liquid phase. Simultaneously, with the temperature 
decrease and the enrichment of Al and Ti in residual liquid, β phase 
nucleated and grew ( Fig. 12 (b)). At the later stage of solidification, the 
( γ + γ
′
)
e 
eutectic structure was formed in the residual liquid phase with 
the further decrease of temperature ( Fig. 12 (c)). 
In summary, different enrichment levels of Ta, Ti and other re -
fractory elements affect the volume fraction of the intergranular phase 
of the alloys, and the enrichment of elements like Mo, Nb, Ta will form
