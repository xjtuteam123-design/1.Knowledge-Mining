high temperature mechanical properties were investigated to facilitate the development of Al-Ce-Mg alloys. The

1. Introduction
Aluminum alloys are widely used in aerospace and transportation 
sectors due to their lightweight, high specific strength and excellent 
corrosion resistance [1] . The development of heat-resistant Al alloys to 
replace titanium alloys in moderate temperature applications (200 ~ 
450 
◦
C) has important applications, which not only reduce the weight of 
components, but also save costs [2 – 4] . However, most of the conven -
tional heat-resistance phases (e.g. Mg
2
Si and Al
2
Cu) in Al alloys are 
unstable and will rapidly coarsen above the temperature of 250 
◦
C, 
resulting in a significant reduction in the high-temperature properties of 
Al alloys [5,6] . Therefore, the demand for developing new heat-resistant 
Al alloys is constantly increasing.
At present, many studies have been conducted on the development of 
heat-resistant aluminum alloys of different alloy systems. Al-Sc alloys 
were investigated due to their exceptional age hardening by the pre -
cipitation of Al
3
Sc L1
2 
particles [7] . Jia et al. [8] investigated additive 
manufactured Al-Mn-Sc alloy, where Sc forms Al
3
Sc strengthening pre -
cipitates, and the other alloying elements act as solid solution 
strengtheners. The alloy showed excellent room temperature yield 
strength. However, even small amounts of Sc dramatically increase the 
cost of the alloys, and the effect of precipitation strengthening degrades 
at elevated temperatures [9] . Another approach to enhancing the 
high-temperature performance of Al alloys is to form eutectic particle, 
such as widely concerned Al-Ni and Al-Ce alloys. The diffusion rates of 
Ni and Ce are slower within the Al matrix, and the micro-scale eutectic 
phase that is naturally produced during solidification has excellent 
thermal stability [10,11] . The Al-Ni alloy contains fine Al

The Al-Ce system as the basis of a new family of Al-based alloys has 
received more and more attention and development. On the one hand,

due to the eutectic microstructure [20] , which helps the alloy to be 
manufactured by additive manufacturing (AM) methods.
AM is a manufacturing method that uses a high-energy heat source to 
melt raw materials and then deposits them layer by layer to form 3D 
components. AM method brings new opportunities for the manufacture 
of complex structural components [21] . Laser powder bed fusion (LPBF) 
is the most commonly used AM method for manufacturing Al-Ce alloys, 
and it can effectively improve the mechanical properties of Al-Ce alloys. 
Zhou et al. [22] manufactured Al-10Ce alloy by LPBF using 
gas-atomized powders for the first time, and found that LPBF Al-10Ce 
alloy has excellent yield and tensile strengths at room temperature, 
which are superior to the cast alloy. Stinehart et al. [23] fabricated LPBF 
Al-10Ce alloy and investigated its high temperature creep property. The 
LPBF Al-10Ce alloy show excellent creep resistant.
To further enhance the mechanical properties of Al-Ce alloys, various 
elements have been added, such as Cu [24] , Mn [25] , and Mg [26,27] . 
Mg has high solubility in α -Al, and it provides a significant solid-solution 
strengthening effect to Al-Ce alloys. Many experiments observed that Mg 
remains in solid solution upon solidification in cast Al-Ce-Mg alloys [28, 
29] . Moreover, Mg is most effective strengthening alloying elements on 
weight basis [17] . LPBF Al-Ce-Mg alloys show the superior hardness and 
tensile strength compared to as-cast Al alloys [30,31] , and they exhibit 
extreme resistance to thermal coarsening up to 400 
◦
C and superior 
strength retention after extended thermal exposure [32] . Lv et al. [33]
also found that due to the excellent thermal stability of microstructure, a 
LPBF Al-Ce-Mg alloy maintained 95 % of its tensile strength and 
increased by 1.5 times of its elongation after heat exposure at 400 
◦
C for 
1 h. There are scarce studies on the high temperature creep properties of 
LPBF Al-Ce-Mg alloys, which limits the evaluation of their 
high-temperature service performance. To address this issue, Rakhmo -
nov et al. [13] investigated the hardness and creep-resistances of a LPBF 
hypoeutectic Al-Ce-Mg alloy. The alloy shows high hardness which at -
tributes to Orowan strengthening from the Ce-rich precipitates and 
solid-solution hardening from Mg. The alloy exhibits much lower 
steady-state strain rates and two creep regimes at 300 º C, including the 
diffusional creep at low stresses and the dislocation creep at higher 
stresses. Although many studies have shown that LPBF Al-Ce-Mg alloy 
exhibits excellent mechanical properties and stability, the alloys fabri -
cate by LPBF are expensive, especially for mass production of large 
structural components.
Wire-based directed energy deposition (WA-DED) is an alternative 
method to powder-based processes. WA-DED has higher deposition rates 
and lower cost. More importantly, the additional oxygen input from the 
feedstock is significantly reduced compared to the LPBF [34,35] , which 
is beneficial to improving the relative density and performance of alloy. 
To date, there is very little literature on WA-DED of Al-Ce-Mg alloys. Our 
research group designed alloy composition and first fabricated the 
thin-walled Al-Ce-Mg alloy component by WA-DED. Then the 
microstructure [36] and tensile strength [6,37] of WA-DED Al-Ce-Mg 
alloy were studied, and the alloy showed the enhanced tensile properties 
compared with the as-cast Al-Ce-Mg alloy. However, the mechanical 
properties of WA-DED Al-Ce-Mg alloys at different elevated tempera -
tures have not been carefully studied, especially the creep properties. In 
this work, we systematically investigated the microstructure and 
high-temperature mechanical properties of WA-DED Al-Ce-Mg alloy 
blocky component, including tension, compression, and creep from 220 
◦
C to 300 
◦
C, and analyzed the failure behavior and mechanism of the 
alloy specimens. The alloy exhibits good microstructural stability and 
high-temperature mechanical properties, demonstrating that WA-DED is 
a favorable method for fabricating Al-Ce alloys.
2. Material and experimental details
Based on our previous thermodynamic calculations and microstruc -
ture analysis [36] , a hypereutectic Al – 15Ce – 3Mg (wt%) alloy is 
designed and manufactured by WA-DED. The high Ce content (15 wt%) 
was designed to increase the volume fraction of Al
11 
Ce
3 
strengthening 
phase, thereby increasing the strength of the alloy [29,38] . Mg content 
(3 wt%) was designed to strengthen the aluminum matrix through solid 
solution strengthening. The excessive addition of Mg was avoided, 
because it not only destroys the coupled growth of eutectics, but also 
contributes to generating divorced eutectics and interdendritic β -Al
3
Mg
2 
phases, which damage the mechanical properties of the alloy [36,39] .
The schematic diagram of the wire-arc directed energy deposition 
equipment was shown in Fig. 1 (a). The alloy wires were self- 
manufactured by extruding and drawing cast bars to serve as raw ma -
terial for WA-DED. The composition of Al-Ce-Mg alloy wire is Al- 
14.22Ce-2.66Mg (wt%), and its diameter is 1.6 mm. During the 
manufacturing process, the wire-feeding device feeded the alloy wire, 
and the tungsten inert gas (TIG) welding torch guided by the machine 
tool melted the fed wire. The molten wire was continuously deposited on 
an Al-2024 alloy substrate with dimensions of 200 mm × 200 mm × 5 mm. 
When one layer was finished, the next layer was built continually on the 
top surface. The layer-by-layer deposition process was continuous 
without special control of the interlayer idle time or interlayer tem -
perature. The shielding gas for the WA-DED process was Ar at a flow rate 
of 20 L/min. The Zigzag scanning strategy is used, and it is a parallel 
reciprocating scanning strategy, as shown in Fig. 1 (b). This scanning 
strategy produces a more level surface possibly because it gives the 
melted material more opportunity to spread evenly across the entire 
surface [40] . Other processing parameters of WA-DED were optimized 
and determined as the arc current of 146.2 A, arc voltage of 21 V, wire 
feeding speed of 250 cm/min, scanning speed of 240 mm/min, layer 
height of 3.0 mm, and the heat input of 2917 J/mm
3 
to ensure that the 
Al-Ce-Mg alloy blocky component has good formability. Finally the bulk 
component with a nominal composition of Al – 15Ce – 3Mg (wt%) was 
formed by layer-by-layer deposition.

3. WA-DED Al-Ce-Mg alloy and its microstructure 
characterization
The macroscopic morphologies of the WA-DED Al-Ce-Mg blocky 
component is shown in Fig. 3 (a) and (b). The deposited component has 
good forming quality and no collapse occurs along the deposition di -
rection, indicating that the designed process parameters are effective for 
manufacturing the Al-15Ce-3Mg alloy blocks. The size of block 
component is about 90 mm × 30 mm × 15 mm. Fig. 3 (c) shows the 
sectional metallographic microstructure of WA-DED Al-Ce-Mg alloy, and 
it can be seen that pores are formed during WA-DED. As one of the most 
common defects in the additive manufacturing process, pores have a 
significant impact on the mechanical properties of additive 
manufacturing alloys. The pores are unevenly distributed along the 
deposition direction. There are very few pores at the bottom of the 
deposited layers, but the pores gradually increase as the number of 
deposited layers increases. The relative density is lowest at the top of 
block component, and more large-sized keyhole pores are formed. The 
reason is that the heat accumulation becomes more significant when the 
deposition thickness is high enough, and the evaporation of the material 
is accelerated, promoting the formation of gas pores and keyhole pores 
in the new deposited layers [44,45] . Moreover, Mg has a high vapor 
pressure and tends to preferentially vaporize under the higher power 
density, which promotes the formation of pores in the Al-Ce-Mg alloys 
[26] . Therefore, in order to control the porosity and improve the quality 
of the WA-DED Al-Ce-Mg alloy, the energy input needs to be more 
uniform and the accumulation of heat input needs to be reduced, which 
can be achieved by optimizing the process such as the scanning path.
In order to further reveal the effect of porosity on the properties of 
the WA-DED Al-Ce-Mg alloy, the micro-Vickers hardness (HV) of the 
alloy was tested. The measurement region is shown in Fig. 2 (c), which is 
2.8 mm × 2.8 mm. The measured hardness mapping is shown in Fig. 2
(d). Porosity defects affect the hardness of alloy by affecting its density 
[46,47] . Combining Fig. 2 (c) and (d), it can be seen that there is a certain 
correlation between hardness distribution and pore distribution. There 
are more pores in the upper left corner and the lower right corner of the 
hardness measurement region, and the corresponding hardness of these 
locations is lower. Whereas the locations with fewer pore defects, the 
Vickers hardness is higher and mostly distributed between 81 HV and 94 
HV. The unit of Vickers hardness, HV, can be converted to GPa, based on 
the equation of 1 GPa = 102.04 HV [48] , and the Vickers hardness and 
nanohardness (Hn) have a conversion relationship of HV = 0.927 Hn 
[49] . Therefore, the nanohardness of the locations with fewer pores in 
the hardness measurement region is estimated to be 
0.74 GPa~0.85 GPa, which is slightly higher than that of the as-cast 
Al-Ce-Mg alloy (0.7 GPa) [30] .

5. Conclusions
The Al-Ce-Mg alloy was manufactured by wire-arc directed energy 
deposition based additive manufacturing. The high-temperature tensile, 
compression and creep properties of the alloy were systematically 
studied. The main conclusions were drawn: 
(1) Pore defects are formed in the Al-Ce-Mg alloy blocky component 
fabricated by WA-DED, and the porosity increases with the in -
crease of the deposited layer height. The hypereutectic micro -
structure of the WA-DED Al-Ce-Mg alloy mainly includes large 
primary Al
11
Ce
3 
intermetallics and fine Al
11
Ce
3
/ α -Al eutectics. 
The fine eutectic phase contributes greatly to the strength of the 
alloy.
(2) The hardness of the WA-DED Al-Ce-Mg alloy can reach 81 – 94 HV 
and is slightly higher than that of the as-cast Al-Ce-Mg alloy. The 
yield strength and tensile strength of WA-DED Al-Ce-Mg alloy are 
76 – 117 MPa and 86 – 132 MPa respectively at 220 – 300 
◦
C. They 
exceed those of Al-Sc alloy and AlSi10Mg alloy, and are close to 
that of LPBF-fabricated and HIP-treated Al-Ce-Mg alloy. More -
over, the elongation of WA-DED Al-Ce-Mg alloy reaches 
12.9~29 %, which is higher than that of LPBF Al-Ce-Mg alloy. In 
addition, the WA-DED Al-15Ce-3Mg also exhibits good high 
temperature compressive strength, which is 360 MPa at 220 
◦
C, 
310 MPa at 250 
◦
C and 240 MPa at 300 
◦
C at the compressive 
strain of 30 %.
(3) This work first studied the high-temperature creep properties of 
the WA-DED Al-Ce-Mg alloy. WA-DED Al-Ce-Mg alloy has better 
high-temperature creep performance compared to Al-Mg alloy at 
similar temperature. The fine Al
11
Ce
3 
phase is beneficial to 
improving creep resistance of the alloy, but primary Al
11
Ce
3 
phase is not favorable. The main creep deformation mechanism is 
dislocation creep.
In summary, WA-DED Al-Ce-Mg alloy exhibits excellent high- 
temperature mechanical properties, and the raw material and 
manufacturing costs of the alloy are low. Therefore, the alloy has good 
development potential and application prospects.
