Abstract 
High entropy alloys have shown great potential to be utilized in applications like mechanical parts which necessitate 
high strength for optimal performance. This work aims to investigate the microstructural evolution, phase formation 
and microhardness properties of novel equiatomic TiAlMoSiW high entropy alloy synthesized via spark plasma 
sintering. The effect of Spark plasma sintering temperature of the fabricated equi atomic TiAlMoSiW HEAs was 
also investigated. The microstructure and the phases formed for the developed HEAs were examined using scanning 
electron microscopy (SEM) an d X-ray diffractometry (XRD) respectively. The microhardness properties were 
studied using a diamond base microhardness tester. The synthesized alloys at high sintering temperature showed 
enhanced microhardness and densification.

1. Introduction 
Since the time of their evolution in 2004 by Yeh et al [1], high entropy alloys (HEA) gained more attention by 
many research groups around the world because of the nature of producing single stable phases and their promising 
properties [2, 3]

HEAs are alloys that comprise of five or more principal elements in equivalent or near -equal atomic percent (at.%). 
Normally, the used atomic fracti on of each element is greater than 5 at.% and less than 35at.% [1–4]. Due to 
industrial development, advanced engineering materials with good weight to strength ratio, wear and oxidation 
resistant materials are needed [3, 5] . Therefore, high entropy alloys are promising multi principal element alloys 
with good material properties for advanced engineering application [1, 6]. Numerous approaches for the fabrication 
of HEAs have been developed. These process includes methods such as spark plasma sintering, mechanical alloying 
(MA), sputtering, laser additive manufacturing (LAM) and casting [7]. Spark Plasma Sintering can readily produce a 
material of excellent mechanical properties by maintaining homogenous small grains as compared to other 
conventional methods [8, 9] . The investigated HEAs synthesized by SP technique shows that HEAs p ossess 
excellent properties, these properties includes high microhardness, good oxidation and corrosion resistance, which 
are beneficial in potential engineering applications [2, 3, 12–14]. 
AlCoCrFeNi high entrop y alloy prepared via spark plasma sintering was studied by Zhang et al. [13]. From the 
results, it was found that the AlCoCrFeNi HEA consisted of FCC phase and BCC structure which was composed of 
BCC phases. Microh ardness properties of the alloy was found to be 518HV. Pohan et al. [14] did investigated the 
phase evolution of synthesized Al 0.3CoCrFeMnNi HEAs after spark plasma sintering. After synthesizing the 
powders via spark plasma sintering, i t was discovered that ordered B2 precipitates and chromium carbide 
precipitated were present. Effect of Sintering temperature optimization showed tha t at 900oC, high densification and 
hardness were obtained. Wang, Ji and Fu, [15] explored the microstructural evolution and mechanical properties of 
CoCrFeNiMnAl HEA synthesized via mechanical alloying followed by SPS. The as -milled powder was then fused 
by SPS at 800 oC. The alloy had good mech anical properties with Vickers hardness of 662 HV and compressive 
strength of 2142 MPa due to the presence of BCC phase and FCC phases which were present in the alloy after 
sintering. Therefore, in this study, attempts will be made to fabricate TiAlMoSiW HEAs under different sintering 
temperature. Therefore, in depth characterization of the synthesized HEAs will be undertaken to understand the 
effect of sintering temperature on the relative density, microhardness properties, microstructures and formation of 
phases of the developed high entropy alloys. 
2. Experimental method 
The TiAlMoSiW powders were mixed in equi atomic fraction. A graphite die of 40mm diameter was used to sinter 
the HEAs, the powder was consolidated via SPS system (FCT Systeme GmbH Rauenste in, Gewerbepark 96528 
Frankenblick). The powders were heated to the sintering temperatures of 800, 900 and 1000 ºC at a rate of 
100ºC/min with a holding time of 8min for each sintering temperature. The operating temperature was monitored by 
means of optical pyrometer focused through a small hole in the die during sintering process. A constant pressure of 
50MPa was used and was practically applied at the initial and be retained to the sintering temperature during the 
sintering process.

The densities of the 40 mm fabricated Ti-Al-Mo-Si-W high entropy alloys were measured by Archimedes method. 
The fabricated high entropy alloy samples were c ut tr ansversely. After cutting the samples were mounted in an 
epoxy-based resin. The mounted samples were then polished using typical metallography procedures and prior to 
microstructural analysis, the samples were etched with krolls agent solution which consist of 92 ml distilled water, 6 
ml nitric acid and 2 ml hydroflouric acid . The microstructural analysis of the alloys was analysed using Scanning 
Electron Microscopy. X -ray diffractometer was used to determine the phases present in the synthesized samples. 
Vickers microhardness (HVN) was measured using an Emco microhardness tester. For accuracy, the samples were 
indented with a load of 100kgf and a dwell time of 10s at five different positions and the average of the readings was 
recorded.

4. Conclusion 
Ti-Al-Mo-Si-W high entropy alloy was successfully sintered via SPS and the influence of sintering temperature on 
the microstructural evolution, densification and microhardness were examined. Maximum relative density and 
microhardness of 96.6% and 802.93HV respectively were achieved at a sintering temperature of 1000 oC. From the 
above observations, it is clear that SPS offers a potentially practical means to sinter the HEAs for higher 
performance applications which requires high strength. An increase in sintering temperature could result in 
enhanced densification and microhardness of the developed HEAs.
