Keywords: Magnesium-silicon alloy, Alloying, Yttrium, Hot extrusion, High temperature mechanical

Abstract. Mg-1Si alloy doped with 1%Y was prepared by in-situ reaction synthesis. The effect of hot 
extrusion on the microstructure and elevated-temper ature mechanical properties of the alloy was 
studied. The microstructures were analyzed by optic al microscopy, scanning electron microscopy 
with energy dispersive X-ray spectroscopy and X-ray diffractometry. The results show that as-cast 
Mg-1Si-1Y alloy consists of dendritic α-Mg phase, e utectic needle-like Mg 2Si phase and Mg 24 +x Y5 
phase precipitated from α-Mg, Mg 2Si can be modified and refined by yttrium, and α-Mg grains can be 
refined by dynamic recrystallization occurred in ho t extrusion process. The tensile strength and 
elongation of the alloy at ambient temperature are improved prominently by hot extrusion. The tensile 
strength and elongation of the extruded alloy is 18 5.3MPa and 24.3% at 120 ℃. The improved 
elevated-temperature properties of the alloy are as cribed to the fine-grained strengthening and 
dispersion strengthening from Mg 2Si and Mg 24 +x Y5 particles. 
Introduction 
Magnesium alloy is a particularly desirable metal d ue to its high strength-to-weight ratio, high 
damping capacities and the opportunities it offers for weight reduction, particularly in the aviation, 
aerospace and automobile industry. However, its application is limited to low temperatures due to the 
rapid degradation of mechanical properties with temperature increasing. Therefore, it is a hot topic to 
improve high temperature properties of magnesium alloys. Alloying is one of the effective methods to 
improve elevated-temperature behavior. The widely u sed alloying elements in heat-resistant 
magnesium alloys include rare earth, silicon, thori um and calcium, etc 
[1-3] . Compared with other 
elements, silicon is cheap in price and low in dens ity. In addition, Mg 2Si compound is thermally 
stable, and can be formed in magnesium alloy by adding silicon. Mg 2Si possesses many merits such as 
high melting point, high hardness, low coefficient of expansion, high elastic modulus and low density, 
which can improve the properties of magnesium alloy at high temperature effectively, especially the 
creep resistance [4,5] . According to silicon content in magnesium alloys, Mg 2Si compound appears 
with different morphologies (e.g. Chinese scripts, dendrites and small blocks) [6] . 
It has been found that silicon degrades the room-te mperature mechanical properties of AZ91D 
magnesium alloy, and the tensile failure often occurs in the interface between α-Mg matrix and Mg 2Si 
compound with Chinese script morphology [7] . It is clear that the characteristics of Mg 2Si, especially 
its morphology, size, volume fraction and distribution, play an important role in the magnesium alloys 
doped with silicon. It was found that Mg 2Si phase can be modified and refined by alloying wi th a 
small amount of Ca, Sb and RE [8-10] , etc. Particularly, the rare-earth element yttrium can inhibit the 
growth of Mg 2Si crystals and induce to the refined Mg 2Si particles [10] ；furthermore, the solubility of 
yttrium in magnesium at high temperature is large ( 12.4% at 567 ℃) and decreases as temperature 
drops, thus the magnesium alloys doped with yttrium can be strengthened by solution strengthening 
and aging strengthening [11] . In addition, the ε-Mg 24+ xY5 compound formed between yttrium and

However, few researches have been done on Mg-Si-Y a lloy, particularly on its microstructure and 
mechanical properties. The present paper investigat ed the phase composition and mechanical 
properties of as-cast Mg-1wt%Si alloy doped with 1w t%Y. Then the effect of hot extrusion on the 
microstructure and high temperature tensile propert ies of the alloy was studied. At last, the 
microstructure refinement mechanism and strengthening mechanisms of the alloy were discussed. 
Experimental 
Mg-1Si(wt%) alloy was prepared from industrial purity magnesium and highly pure quartz powders 
by in-situ reaction synthesis 
 [6]. The melting process was proceeded in crucible resistance furnace and 
protected from oxidation by a flux cover. After the pure magnesium was melted in a graphite-clay 
crucible, a proper amount of quartz powders was added into the crucible when the melt was at 720 ℃. 
Then the melt was heated to 780 ℃, and held at this temperature for 10 minutes. Afte r that, the melt 
was cooled to 750 ℃ and stewed for 15 minutes. Then the melt was skimmed, and poured into a cast 
iron mould preheated to 300 ℃ to produce a columned ingot. For the preparation of Mg-1Si-1Y(wt%) 
alloy, the proper amount of Mg-30%Y master alloy was added into the crucible when the temperature 
of the Mg-1Si melt was lowered to 750 ℃. After the ingots were machined into columnar bill ets 50 
mm in diameter, they were hot extruded into 14 mm diameter bars at 350 ℃. 
The microstructure was observed by Olympus-GX71 opt ical microscope, FEI QUANTA 400 
scanning electron microscopy with energy dispersive X-ray spectroscopy was used to measure 
chemical compositions, X-ray diffraction analysis was carried out to identify phases presented in the 
experimental alloys using a Rigaku D/Max-3C X-ray d iffractometer with Cu K α radiation, and the 
diffraction angle (2 θ) ranged from 20 ° to 80 °. The tensile properties were tested at room temperature 
and 120 ℃~280 ℃ on a HT-2402 universal material testing machine an d a CMT electromechanical 
universal material testing machine, respectively.

Discussion 
According to the theories of coherence for heterogeneous nucleation of molten metals, nucleation can 
be resulted from a small lattice disregistry betwee n nucleating agents and substrates. The lattice 
structures of magnesium and yttrium are all hexagonal-close-packed (hcp) structure, and their lattice 
constants are close to each other. Therefore, yttrium can be used as the nucleating agents to refine the 
microstructure of magnesium alloys. 
It is well-known that the formation of compound bet ween elements depends on their electro 
negativity difference. Larger electro negativity di fference tends to make the binding force stronger 
and therefore the formation of compound easier. The electro negativities of magnesium, yttrium and 
silicon are 1.2, 1.2 and 1.8, respectively, and the electro negativity difference between magnesium and 
yttrium is equal to that of magnesium and silicon. That is to say that silicon and magnesium (or 
yttrium) can combine to form compounds. It can be s een from the binary phase diagram of Mg-Si, 
Mg-Y and Y-Si 
[13] that the eutectic-reaction temperature of Mg-Si and Si-Y are 637.6 ℃ and 1260 ℃ 
respectively. In consideration of relatively low me lting temperature (lower than 760 ℃) of the 
Mg-1Si-1Y alloy, there is no possibility to form compound between silicon and yttrium in the alloy,

whereas eutectic Mg 2Si phases formed during the solidification according to the eutectic reaction of 
the alloy. After solidification, the solid solubili ty of yttrium in the alloy decreases as temperature 
decreases, and thus surplus yttrium precipitated as ε-Mg 24+ xY5 phases from the alloy. 
After adding 1% yttrium element in Mg-1%Si alloy, e utectic Mg 2Si phases shortened and some 
appeared as fine granular, this phenomenon demonstrated that crystal morphologies of Mg 2Si can be 
modified by yttrium. As one of the surface-active e lements, yttrium segregated at the solid-liquid 
interface during solidification. It turns out that Mg 2Si grew isotropically, and its growth velocity was 
decreased. As a result, the Mg 2Si was refined and its morphology was also modified. 
Furthermore, hot extrusion is beneficial to the fra gmentation of Mg 2Si phases, and the refined 
Mg 2Si particles and ε-Mg 24 +x Y5 particles can hinder the growth rate for recrystallized grains, and help 
to obtain fine grains. It was showed that coarse dendritic primary Mg 2Si phases in magnesium alloys 
can substantially impair mechanical properties, so the fine, granular and uniformly distributed Mg 2Si 
phases are desirable [7] . After modification by yttrium and subsequent hot extrusion, the grain was 
refined, and eutectic Mg 2Si phases in the Mg-1Si-1Y alloys were fragmented to small particles. As a 
result, the room temperature mechanical properties of the alloy were improved by both fine-grain 
strengthening and dispersion strengthening. 
The elevated-temperature properties of magnesium alloys depend on intracrystalline slip and grain 
boundary gliding. Higher grain-boundary strength is the key to increasing elevated-temperature 
properties. For extruded Mg-1Y-1Si alloy, thermally stable fine Mg 2Si particles located in the grain 
boundaries pinned the grain boundaries firmly and i mpeded the grain boundary gliding, thus the 
grain-boundary was strengthened effectively [14] . The higher elongation of the alloy at elevated 
temperature results from intracrystalline slip. On the one hand, high temperature enhanced atom 
mobility and resulted in the higher plastic deforma tion ability. On the other hand, hot extrusion 
refined the grains of the alloy. It is reported tha t uniform plastic deformation and grain rotation is 
easily occurred in the fine-grained alloy; moreover, the small grain is also favorable for the activation 
of non-basal slip system in prismatic plane and con ical plane of magnesium [15] . As a result, the 
elongation of the alloy was improved by grain refin ement. Therefore, the excellent 
elevated-temperature properties of extruded Mg-1Y-1 Si alloy are ascribed to the fine-grained 
strengthening and particles dispersion strengthening. 
Conclusions 
(1) The as-cast Mg-1Si-1Y alloy consists of dendrit ic α-Mg, eutectic Mg 
2Si and ε-Mg 24 +x Y5 
precipitated from α-Mg during cooling process. Mg 2Si compound in magnesium alloys can be 
modified and refine by yttrium. 
(2) α-Mg grains of the as-cast Mg-1Si-1Y alloy can be refined by dynamic recrystallization during hot 
extrusion. The tensile strength and elongation of t he alloy at ambient temperature were improved 
prominently by hot extrusion. 
(3) The tensile strength and elongation is 185.3MPa and 24.3% at 120 ℃. The improved elevated 
temperature properties of extruded Mg-1Y-1Si alloy are ascribed to the fine-grained strengthening 
and dispersion strengthening form Mg 2Si and ε-Mg 24 +x Y5 particles.
