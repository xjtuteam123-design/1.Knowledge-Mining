abstract
The microstructure and mechanical behavior of Nb-22Ti-16Si- xZr alloys ( x ¼ 0, 1, 5, 10, 15 at.%) were
investigated by using laser solid forming (LSF) additive manufacturing from elemental powder mixture
of Nb, Ti, Si and Zr. The as-deposited Nb-22Ti-16Si alloy presented a microstructure consisted of primary
Nb-base solid solution (Nbss) dendrite and Nbss/(Nb)
3Si eutectic dendrites. The eutectic Nbss/ g-(Nb)5Si3
appeared with a morphology of lamellar structural feature when 1 at.%Zr was added, and its content
increased with Zr content. Nbss/(Nb)
3Si eutectic dendrites disappeared when Zr content reaches 10 at.%.
Further increasing of Zr (15 at.%) result in the appearance of a hypereutectic microstructure, which
consisted by primary g-(Nb)5Si3 and eutectic Nbss/ g-(Nb)5Si3. As the nominal Zr content increased, the
fracture toughness of LSFed Nb-22Ti-16Si- xZr alloy increases ﬁrst and reaches the maximum value
(15.28 MPa m 1/2) in LSFed Nb-22Ti-16Si-5Zr alloy, and then decreases.

1. Introduction
Niobium silicide based alloys have been consider to replace
nickel-based superalloys as a hot sections material for a new gen-
eration of aero-engines, given to their high melting temperature ( >
1750 /C14 C), relatively low density (6.6 e7.2 g/cm3), high temperature
strength and good creep resistance properties [ 1,2]. Niobium sili-
cide based alloys is a kind of in-situ composites that consist of
ductile Nb-base solid solution (Nbss) and hard-brittle silicide phase
((Nb)3Si and (Nb) 5Si3)[ 3]. In this composites, Nbss offer room-
temperature toughness and niobium silicide phase (for example,
KQ of (Nb) 5Si3 is about 3 MPa m 1/2 [4]) provides high-temperature
strength and creep resistance. However, a limited plastic defor-
mation capability and low fracture toughness at ambient temper-
ature is one of the current bottlenecks that limited the application
of Nb-Si based alloys. To date, a considerable research work has
been performed to improve the room-temperature fracture
toughness of Nb-Si alloy, which can be divided into two ap-
proaches: i) using different processing methods, ii) elements
alloying.
Hitherto a variety of processing technologies have been
explored for fabrication of Nb-Si based alloys, including vacuum
consumable and non-consumables arc-melting [ 5,6], spark plasma
sintering [7], reactive hot press sintering [ 8] and different kinds of
directional solidi ﬁcations [ 9,10]. To some extent, microstructure
controlling and mechanical improving has been obtained by using
traditional processing schemes. For instance, The fracture tough-
ness of Nb-Si based alloy increase after directional solidi ﬁcations
due to the microstructure grows in a form of regularly aligned di-
rection [11]. However, as the hot sections material for engine blade,
the Nb-Si based alloy needs to be prepared in complex form, which
is very dif ﬁcult to realize by using traditional processing tech-
niques. Laser solid forming (LSF) is a typical laser directed energy
deposition metal additive manufacturing (AM) technologies, which
builds up metal components layer-by-layer to achieve design
freedom and manufacture ﬂexibility [12,13]. Additionally, using LSF
process from powder mixture can rapidly change the alloy
composition of the molten pool by ﬂexible changing the proportion

of powder mixture, thereby to realize the real-time control of the
microstructure and mechanical properties of the alloys. This tech-
nical also provides a new high-throughput design of materials for
the optimization of alloy compositions [ 14]. Therefore, using LSF
process from powder mixture can potentially reduce the costs of
processing to a large extent [15,16]. And it can also be used to create
function structures, such as graded compositions within the same
specimen [ 17]. Up to now, little literature has reported the LSFed
Nb-Si based alloy on the microstructure and mechanical properties.
Recently, similar alloys based on Mo-Si-B system were fabricated by
laser additive manufacturing methods [ 18,19].
Lots of alloying elements include Ti, Hf, Cr, Al, V, Ta, B, Ga, Mo etc.
have been added to improving the overall properties of Nb-Si based
alloys [ 6,20e25]. Among them, Ti addition could improve the
fracture toughness of Nb-Si alloy (for example, addition 27 at% Ti
into Nb-16Si alloy improve the K Q values of alloy from 5.4 to
1 1.3 MPa m1/2 [26]) by decreasing the Peierls-Nabarro (P-N) barrier
energy, which promotes the dislocation movement in Nbss [27]. Shi
et al. using a ﬁrst-principles method calculating the idea cleavage
energy and cleavage strength ﬁnd that a moderate amount of Ti will
improved the toughness of Nb 5Si3 [22]. However, the addition of Ti
in Nb-Si alloy reduces its melting temperature, which inhibits the
high temperature practical application. Therefore, in order to insure
the high melting temperature of Nb-Si based alloys, the content of
Ti is generally required to be less than 25 at.% [ 28]. The work of Tian
et al. [ 29] reported that the addition of Zr in the cast Nb-22Ti-16Si
alloy improve the hardness and the yield strength at room tem-
perature with formation of the lamellar Nbss/ g-(Nb)5Si3 eutectic
structure. Qiao et al. [ 30] studied the effect of Zr on the micro-
structure and mechanical properties of cast Nb-22Ti-15Si-5Cr-3Hf-
3Al-xZr ( x ¼ 0,1,2,4,8 at.%) alloys, and found that the addition of Zr
does not affect the microstructure and phase constituents of Nb-Ti-
Si based alloys, but it improves room-temperature toughness of Nb-
Ti-Si based alloys from 1 1.94 to 15.01 MPa m1/2. Thus, the effect of Zr
on the microstructure and mechanical properties of Nb-Ti-Si alloys
are still not fully clear.
In present work, the typical hypoeutectic alloy Nb-22Ti-16Si
was selected as the base alloy to investigate the effect of Zr addi-
tion on the microstructure and mechanical behaviors of LSFed Nb-
Si based alloy. The LSF technique from powder mixture was used to
prepare Nb-22Ti-16Si- xZr alloys with Zr content from 1 at.% to
15 at.%. This work potentially provides a novel high-throughput
design and preparation method for high performance niobium
silicide based alloys.
2. Experimental procedures
Five alloy block specimens of Nb-22Ti-16Si- xZr ( x ¼ 0, 1, 5, 10
and 15 at.%, which are denoted as 0Zr, 1Zr, 5Zr, 10Zr and 15Zr
respectively) were deposited by using powder mixture from LSF
process. The laser processing parameter are set as follows: laser
power of 2.7 kW, beam diameter of 6 mm, scanning velocity of
720 mm/min, powder feed rate of 12.5 g/min and Z axis increment
of 0.2 mm. The purities of all raw materials were measured to be
higher than 99.5 wt% (commercial powder). Fig. 1(aed) display the
morphologies and size of pure elemental powder particles, and the
sizes of elemental powder were 100e150
mm for Nb, 30e150 mm for
Ti, 50 e250 mm for Si, 50 e150 mm for Zr, respectively. Prior to the
experiments, the elemental powder particles were weighed pro-
portionally according to the designated compositions and dried in a
vacuum oven at 373 K for 3 h to remove the absorbed water, then
mixed in a planetary ball mill for 2 h. The backscattered electron
(BSE) image of the powder mixture of Nb-22Ti-16Si-10Zr, is shown
in Fig. 1 (e), which indicated a homogenous distribution of powder.
The substrate used in this experiment was a pure titanium plate
with dimensions of 140 mm /C2 50 mm /C2 6 mm, which were grinded
using an abrasive paper, and the surfaces were washed using ethyl
alcohol and acetone prior to deposit. Moreover, the schematic di-
agram of LSF process including the as-deposited component, sub-
strate, scanning path and build direction, are shown in Fig. 2 . The
scanning direction within each layer was identical. The size of each
straight wall sample was about 40 mm /C2 7m m /C2 35 mm.
Fracture toughness (K Q) at room temperature was measured
using a three-point bending test (Instron 3382) without insertion of
an initial fatigue crack. The test process, samples direction of the
three-point bending specimen and actual size of the specimen were
shown in Fig. 3 . The dimensions of three-point specimens were
3 mm in thickness(B), 6 mm in width(W) and 30 mm in length,
which were prepared by electro discharge matching from the as-
deposited alloy block specimens ( Fig. 3 b). The depth of the notch
was 2.9e3.0 mm. The tests were conducted at a cross-head speed of
0.05 mm/min. The peak load in a load-displacement curve was used
to calculate the fracture toughness value of samples by using
standard equation, that is KQ¼ (Pmax S/BW3/2)*(f(a/W)), where S is a
loading span, a is notch length, P max is the peak load and f(a/W) is
geometrical factor. Three specimens were tested and the average
value was employed for room-temperature fracture toughness of
each specimen condition. During the three-point bending test, the
initial crack observation was performed as follow: (1) increasing
the applied force with the appearance of force-drop to about of
50 N; (2) then, the non-fractured sample was polished to observe
crack propagation, (3) applied force until the sample breaks, then
observe the fracture surface.
X-ray diffraction analysis (XRD, Panalytical X'Pert PRO, Cu K
a),
scanning electron microscopy(SEM, VEGA Ⅱ-LMH) and Electron
Probe Micro Analysis (EPMA, SHIMADZU-1 720) were used to
identify the phase constituents and compositional distribution. The
middle of as-deposited samples were considered to be the micro-
structure characterization of the alloys.
3. Results and discussion
3.1. Microstructure of the alloys
The XRD patterns of powder mixture and all the as-deposited
Nb-22Ti-16Si-xZr alloys are shown in Fig. 4 . Individual phases and
their crystal structures were identi ﬁed by matching the charac-
teristic XRD peaks against JCPDS databases (Nb:35-0789,
(Nb)
3Si:71-0732, g-(Nb)5Si3: 65-3599) and denoted with various
marks to indicate the corresponding phases. The XRD spectra
consisted of characteristic diffraction peaks of four elements from
the Nb-22Ti-16Si-10Zr powder mixture, with no peaks of inter-
metallic compounds among the four elements being observed,
which also indicates that the alloying elements did not undergo
alloying during the mixing process. As shown in Fig.4, 0Zr alloy
consist of Nbss and (Nb) 3Si phases. With the increase of Zr content,
the diffraction peak of g-(Nb)5Si3 appears in the diffraction pattern
peak of the Nb-22Ti-16Si- xZr alloys, and the relative intensity of g-
(Nb)5Si3 diffraction peaks gradually increases. The Nb 3Si phase
disappears in 10Zr alloy, which consists of Nbss phase and g-
(Nb)5Si3 phase. In addition, the 15Zr alloy is also composed of Nbss
and g-(Nb)5Si3. It is worth mentioning that, compared to the 0Zr
alloy, the positions of the Nbss peaks shows a shift towards lower
angles with increasing of Zr content. This is because the atomic
radius of the Zr element (0.216 nm) is larger than the atomic radius
of the Nb element (0.208 nm). Therefore, the addition of Zr in Nbss
will expand the lattice and hence result in increase in the inter-
planar spacing. It can be known from the Bragg equation
2dsinq ¼ nl that when l is constant, as the lattice constant in-
creases, the corresponding interplanar spacing d increases,

4. Conclusion
A series of Nb-22Ti-16Si- xZr(x ¼ 0, 1, 5, 10, 15 at.%) alloys were
prepared elemental powder mixture from LSF. The in ﬂuence of Zr
addition on their microstructural characterization and fracture
toughness of the as-deposited alloys were examined. The following
conclusions can be drawn:
(1) With an increase of Zr content, the microstructure of the as-
deposited sample is transition from hypoeutectic micro-
structure to hypereutectic microstructure. That is, the 0Zr
alloy composed of primary Nbss and Nbss/(Nb) 3Si eutectic
microstructure; 1Zr and 5Zr alloys consisted of the primary
Nbss, Nbss/(Nb) 3Si eutectic and Nbss/ g-(Nb)5Si3 eutectic
microstructure; 10Zr alloy consisted of the primary Nbss and
Nbss/g-(Nb)5Si3 eutectic microstructure. However, 15Zr alloy
composed of primary g-(Nb)5Si3 and Nbss/ g-(Nb)5Si3
eutectic microstructure.
(2) The Nbss/ g-(Nb)5Si3 lamellar eutectic microstructure are
formed in interdendritic due to the segregation of Zr and Si
elements. The addition of Zr affected the solidi ﬁcation tem-
perature range and the Nbss/ g-(Nb)5Si3 eutectic micro-
structure content of Nb-22Ti-16Si- xZr alloys.
(3) The fracture toughness (K Q) of Nb-22Ti-16Si alloys are
improved by addition of Zr. The K Q of as-deposited 0Zr alloy
is 1 1.3 MPa m1/2, the maximum value is reached when the Zr
content is 5 at.%, which is 15.28 MPa m 1/2, then it underwent
a slight drop with increased of Zr content.
