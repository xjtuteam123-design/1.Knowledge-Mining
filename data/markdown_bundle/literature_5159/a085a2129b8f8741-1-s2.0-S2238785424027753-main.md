ABSTRACT
A novel equiatomic refractory AlNbTiVY high-entropy alloy containing rare earth elements was fabricated by 
vacuum arc melting. AlNbTiVY alloy possesses a dual-phase structure, consisting of a B2 ordered matrix and an 
Orthorhombic structured Y
2
Al second phase. The alloy has a dendritic microstructure with a density of only 5.18 
g/cm
3
. As the testing temperature increases, the compressive yield strength of the alloy gradually decreases from 
1104 MPa at room temperature to 321 MPa at 800 
◦
C, with improved deformability and no fracture occurring in 
the 800 
◦
C environment. Based on the insights of physics, a simple calculation was conducted on the 
temperature-dependent model of the alloy, and the theoretical application temperature of the alloy was pre -
dicted to exceed 600 
◦
C.
Inspired by the idea of high-entropy alloys, researchers have intro -
duced refractory metals such as Ta and W into high-entropy alloys, 
forming refractory high-entropy alloys (RHEAs) with refractory metal 
elements as the principle element [ 1 ]. The properties of these new alloys 
are superior to Ni-based superalloys and other traditional alloys. 
Therefore, RHEAs are generally considered as the main material type for 
future high-temperature structural applications. Developing high en -
tropy alloys with excellent mechanical properties (such as strength, 
ductility, and corrosion resistance) is of great significance for the 
research and development of materials science.
RHEAs are mostly composed of refractory metals of transition groups 
V and VI, with relatively high solubility between each element. Coupled 
with the high-entropy effect between multiple principal elements, most 
of them exhibit a single BCC structure [ 1 – 4 ]. Of course, from a broad 
perspective of alloy design, RHEAs also include some low melting point 
or non-metallic elements such as Al, C, Si, etc. [ 4 – 9 ]. Due to differences 
in atomic size or mixing enthalpy between elements, there are also some 
dual-phase or even multi-phase RHEAs, which often result in severe 
local lattice distortion inside these alloys, enhancing their strength [ 5 ,
7 ]. Rare earth elements, also known as "industrial vitamins", are widely 
used for modifying the properties of metal materials [ 8 , 9 ]. However, 
high entropy alloys have only been studied for a short period of two 
decades, and these studies mainly focus on the influence of transition 
metal elements. Scholars have paid less attention to rare earth 
high-entropy alloys, and there are still many aspects that urgently need 
to be explored.
The Y element has a larger atomic radius and a lower density than the 
transition metals. Therefore, in the present work, a novel lightweight 
AlNbTiVY RHEA was prepared by adding rare earth Y element on the 
basis of AlNbTiV alloy. The microstructure and mechanical properties of 
AlNbTiVY RHEA were thoroughly studied and its service temperature 
was discussed.

The XRD pattern of the equiatomic AlNbTiVY refractory high- 
entropy alloy is shown in Fig. 1 (a). The AlNbTiVY alloy exhibits a ma -
trix of ordered BCC (B2) and a second phase of Orthorhombic structure 
(Y
2
Al, Pnma space group, denoted as O-phase). The lattice parameters of 
O-phase are a = 0.668 ± 0.004 nm, b = 0.495 ± 0.008 nm, and c =
0.971 ± 0.006 nm. Generally, the presence of ordered B2 phase can be 
detected in high-entropy alloys with high Al content [ 4 , 7 , 8 ]. The lattice 
parameter of B2 phase is determined to be 0.321 ± 0.003 nm, slightly 
larger than that of AlNbTiV RHEA (0.318 ± 0.003 nm) [ 4 ]. The reason 
for the increase in lattice parameter is that the Y atom has a larger 
radius, which leads to more severe lattice distortion in the alloy.
Fig. 1 (b) shows the SEM image of AlNbTiVY alloy composed of two 
phases, and also indicate that the alloy is composed of a typical dendritic 
structure. Combined with XRD, the dark gray area represents the B2 
matrix, and the light colored area represents the O-phase. The difference 
in atomic number of the elements results in a significant contrast dif -
ference between the two phases [ 10 ]. The volume fraction of the two 
phases was measured using Image J software, and the results showed 
that the volume fraction of the matrix phase was about 69.1%, and the 
volume fraction of the O-phase was about 30.9%.
The microstructure of AlNbTiVY RHEA under nano-scale was further 
characterized through TEM, as shown in Fig. 2 . The bright field (BF) 
image in Fig. 2 (a) further confirm that the alloy is composed of two 
phases. Selected area electron diffraction (SAED) patterns were per -
formed on both phases, as illustrated in Fig. 2 (b) and (c). According to 
the BF images and SAED pattern along the [001] zone axis ( Fig. 2 (b)), 
the AlNbTiVY alloy exhibits extra characteristic (100) superlattice spots, 
indicating that the matrix possesses a B2 ordered structure. Relevant 
studies have shown that B2 phase exhibits a CsCl-type crystal structure, 
where each element atom in the lattice has a specific occupation, making 
it an ordered structure. Therefore, superlattice diffraction can be 
observed in selected area diffraction [ 7 ]. In addition to the AlNbTiVY 
RHEA studied in our study, B2 ordered structures are often observed in 
some Al-contained HEAs (such as AlNbTiV [ 7 ], AlMoNbTaTiZr [ 8 ]), as 
Al is considered to be a stable presence for both BCC and B2 structures, 
but B2 structures seem to be more favored. The SAED pattern along the 
[131] zone axis in Fig. 2 (c) confirms that the second phase of AlNbTiVY 
alloy is Y
2
Al with an orthorhombic structure.
A detailed TEM-EDS point scanning was performed on the two 
phases in Fig. 2 (a) , and the results are listed in Table 1 . Due to the 
higher bonding ability of Al and Y compared to elements such as Al and 
Nb, Ti and V, stable intermetallic compounds are more likely to form 
between Al and Y, and therefore significant element segregation can be 
observed in the alloy, which is consistent with the SEM results.
Yurchenko [ 7 ] et al. showed that the AlNbTiV-based high-entropy 
alloy with B2 ordered structure could be observed to have 
irregular-shaped B2 domains separated by anti-phase boundaries (APBs) 
in the dark field (DF) image under the {100} superlattice spots. High 
angle annular dark field (HAADF) TEM observation was performed on 
the matrix of AlNbTiVY alloy, as shown in Fig. 2 (e) , and the presence of 
APBs can also be observed.
Furthermore, we observed the crystal structure between the B2 
matrix and the B2/O-phase. High-resolution transmission electron mi -
croscopy (HRTEM) images with inversed fast Fourier transform (IFFT) 
and fast Fourier transform (FFT) are shown in Fig. 3 (a) ~ (c). There is a 
high density of lattice misfit inside the matrix and at the phase interface 
of AlNbTiVY alloy, while no obvious lattice misfit was found inside the O 
phase, indicating that lattice distortion caused by interstitial atoms is 
higher inside the matrix and at the phase interface. Because of the 
greater frictional resistance of lattice distortion on dislocation migra -
tion, AlNbTiVY alloy may have better mechanical properties.

TaNbHfZrTi [ 2 ], AlNbTiV/AlNbTiZr [ 4 , 6 ], the greater advantage of 
AlNbTiVY alloy lies in its lower density, which allows it to exhibit higher

alloy is more promising as a structural material to replace Ni-based su -
peralloys and other traditional alloys.

In summary, AlNbTiVY refractory high-entropy alloy was prepared 
by vacuum arc melting, and the microstructure and mechanical prop -
erties at room and elevated temperatures of the alloy were investigated. 
The density of AlNbTiVY alloy is only 5.18 g/cm
3
, which is a lightweight 
high-entropy alloy. The alloy is composed of a matrix with an ordered B2 
structure and a O-phase enriched Al and Y. The volume fraction of the 
matrix phase is about 69.1%, and 30.9% of the second phase. The 
compression test results show that the AlNbTiVY alloy did not yield and 
directly fractured at room temperature, with a strength of 1104 MPa. At 
800 
◦
C, the yield strength of the alloy was only 321 MPa, but no fracture 
occurred. The specific strengths at each test temperature were 213, 150, 
and 62 MPa kg/m
3
, respectively. The high specific strengths are 
attributed to the combined effect of lower densities of elements such as 
Al, Ti, and Y.
