Abstract
The effects of Ta on the tensile behavior and deformation mechanisms of a Ni-based single crystal superalloy were inves-
tigated in this study from room temperature to elevated temperature. The findings demonstrated that the higher content of 
Ta could improve the tensile properties of the alloy at different temperatures. Due to the different deformation mechanisms 
at various temperatures, the influence of Ta on tensile deformation varied. At room temperature, the higher content of Ta 
enhanced the solid solution strengthening, which would enhance the tensile strength of 6.5Ta alloy. After standard heat treat-
ment of 6.5Ta alloy, precipitation of the secondary γʹ phase would hinder the movement of dislocations. When the temperature 
was elevated to 760 °C, the higher content of Ta not only promoted the interaction of stacking faults to form Lomer–Cottrell 
(L-C) locks that impeded dislocation motion, but also reduced the occurrence of dislocation pile-up groups, thus enhancing 
the yield strength. At 1120 °C, due to the narrower γ channels and higher APB energy in γ ʹ phase of the alloy with higher 
Ta addition, the processes of bypassing and shearing of dislocations were hindered, respectively. Meanwhile, the denser and 
more regular dislocation networks were formed in 6.5Ta alloy; and thus, the tensile strength of 6.5Ta alloy was enhanced. 
This study systematically investigated the effect of Ta on the tensile behavior at three different temperatures, which provided 
an important theoretical basis for the design of nickel-based single crystal superalloys in the future.

1 Introduction
Nickel-based single crystal (SX) superalloys are exten-
sively employed in aerospace due to their exceptional 
mechanical properties, favorable high-temperature struc-
tural stability, and excellent resistance to oxidation and 
corrosion [1 –3]. The primary strengthening mechanisms 
of nickel-based single crystal superalloys involve the solid 
solution strengthening of the FCC-structured γ matrix and 
the precipitation strengthening of the coherent embedded 
 L12-structured γʹ phase, which ensure outstanding tensile 
strength and creep resistance of the alloy at elevated tem-
peratures [4 , 5]. The tensile experiment is regarded as a 
fundamental mechanical test for nickel-based single crystal 
superalloys, and its results can provide a significant refer -
ence basis for creep and fatigue properties [6 ]. The yield 
strength, as a pivotal result of the tensile test, is predomi-
nantly governed by the chemical composition, volume frac-
tion of γʹ phase, morphology, and distribution of γʹ particles 
[7, 8]. Numerous research studies have documented the 
influence of refractory elements such as Re [ 7, 9], W [ 7, 
10], Cr [11], and Ru [12, 13] on the tensile properties of 
single crystal superalloys. However, limited investigations 
have been conducted to investigate the effects of Ta on the 
tensile behaviors of these alloys.
Throughout the evolution of SX superalloys, Ta has con-
sistently maintained a high concentration as a vital strength-
ening element, as shown in Fig.  1. As a crucial constituent

3.2 Tensile Behavior at Different Temperatures
The tensile engineering stress–strain curves of two experi-
mental alloys under different temperatures are presented in 
Fig. 3. Evidently, two experimental alloys exhibited distinct 
tensile behaviors at different temperatures. At RT, the flow 
stress remained constant and formed a steady platform in 
both alloys after the yield point. And no work hardening 
phenomenon was detected, which distinguished it from 
previous reports [13, 24, 25]. At 760 °C, the stress–strain 
curves of the two alloys were different. For 5.5Ta alloy, 
the stress–strain curve indicated that the stress remained 
constant after the yield point, and as the deformation pro-
gressed, a work hardening occurred until the tensile frac -
ture. However, for 6.5Ta alloy, the stress decreased first 
after the yield point and then rose to occur work hardening. 
Subsequently, as the deformation proceeded, the softening 
behavior occurred until the fracture of alloy. And this dif-
ference would be further explained in the discussion. As 
the temperature rose to 1120 °C, work hardening occurred 
after two alloys reached a less pronounced yield point. When 
the stress increased to a maximum, the significant softening 
behavior occurred until the fracture of specimens and the 
5.5Ta alloy had a longer softening process.
The yield strength and elongation of the two experimental 
alloys from RT to 1120 °C are shown in Fig.  4. It was dis-
cernible that the yield strength of the two alloys was com-
paratively similar at RT, with a slightly higher yield strength 
observed in 6.5Ta alloy compared to the 5.5Ta alloy. When 
the temperature rose to 760 °C, the yield strength of the 
two alloys reached their peak values, namely 915 MPa in 
5.5Ta alloy and 1050 MPa in 6.5Ta alloy, respectively. 
Subsequently, as the temperature rose to 1120 °C, the yield 
strength rapidly reduced to 272 MPa in 5.5Ta alloy and 
331 MPa in 6.5Ta alloy. Overall, the increase in Ta in the 
alloy resulted in an increase in yield strength. Figure  4b 
illustrates the variation of elongation in two alloys from RT 
to 1120 °C. In 5.5Ta alloy, the elongation decreased from

3.3 Fracture Characteristics
The morphologies of the fracture surfaces after tensile test 
at different temperatures, along with magnified details in 
inserted images, are presented in Fig.  5. On the macro-
scopic scale, the fracture surfaces of two alloys exhibited 
an elliptical shape without any necking phenomenon at RT. 
As the thermal activation process was weak, slip became 
the dominant deformation mechanism [26]. In the mag-
nified image, a large amount of slip steps could be seen, 
which were also observed in the Ref. [27]. Furthermore, the 
generation of slip was primarily driven by external tensile 
forces, usually activating one slip systems. A plethora of 
river patterns and cleavage steps were discerned on the frac-
ture surface of the specimens, hence indicating the cleavage 
fracture mechanism at RT.

wedge-shaped fracture surface was observed macroscopi-

were activated during the process of tensile deformation

attributed to an enhanced plastic deformation capability of 
the γ matrix as the temperature rose from RT to 760 °C. 
Consequently, the dislocations on {111} slip plane became 
considerably more prevalent compared to those at RT [9 ].

the magnified insets of Fig.  5b, e. It could be seen that as 
the Ta content increased, the fracture surface consisted of a

4.4 Effect of Ta and Temperature 
on the Deformation Mechanisms
The plastic deformation mechanism of the superalloys was 
influenced by the competition between different planar 
defects in energy level, such as complex stacking faults and 
APB, etc. [7 , 12]. And the temperature had a significant 
effect on the energy and hence on the deformation mecha-
nism of the alloy [38]. At RT and 760 °C, since the thermal 
activation was weak, the dislocation shearing into the γ ʹ 
phase and forming stacking faults were considered to be one 
of the main deformation mechanisms. When temperature 
rose to 1120 °C, thermal activation was enhanced and dis-
location climbing and Orowan bypass mechanism occurred.

5 Conclusions
The effects of Ta on the tensile behaviors of a nickel-based 
single crystal superalloy from RT to 1120 °C were evaluated 
in this study. The main conclusions were presented below:
1. The higher content of Ta increased the volume fraction 
of γʹ phase and refined γ ʹ phase. The fracture mecha-
nisms of the alloys at different temperatures were not 
changed with the addition of Ta. Furthermore, multiple 
fracture surfaces were observed due to the activation of 
numerous slip systems in 6.5Ta alloy at 760 °C.
2. At RT, the higher content of Ta enhanced the solid 
solution strength and promoted the precipitation of the 
secondary γʹ phase during cooling after heat treatment, 
which would hinder the movement of dislocations and 
thus enhance the tensile properties of 6.5Ta alloy.
3. When temperature increased to 760 °C, fewer dislo-
cation pile-up groups were observed in 6.5Ta alloys. 
Meanwhile, more Ta reduced the /u1D70ESF and promoted the 
formation of stacking faults. More stacking faults inter-
acted with each other to form the L-C locks, impeding 
the movement of dislocations and thus enhancing the 
yield strength of 6.5Ta alloy at 760 °C.
4. The higher content of Ta would narrow the width of the 
γ channel and increase the γ APB of the γ ʹ phase, which 
hindered the movement of dislocation in 6.5Ta alloy at 
1120 °C. Furthermore, the higher content of Ta pro-
moted the formation of denser and more regular disloca-
tion network, which would enhance the tensile strength 
of 6.5Ta alloy.
