The influence of Ni on the microstructure of Co-base ODS alloys

Keywords: Cobalt base alloy; Mechanical alloying; Oxide dispersion strengthening; γ΄ precipitates; 
Microstructure 
Abstract: Co-based ODS alloys strengthened by γ΄ precipitates and nanosized oxides are promising 
high-temperature structural materials. Single solid solution of Al and W in the matrix can not be 
achieved after mechanical alloying, resulting in th e formation of low volume fraction of γ΄ phase 
and several kinds of secondary phases. The addition of Ni promotes the precipitation of γ΄ phase 
and reduces the amount of secondary phases by the enlargement of the solid solution limit of Al and 
W within the matrix. In comparison with the lower fracture strength and cleavage fracture mode of 
the alloy without the addition of Ni, Ni-containing Co-base ODS alloys exhibit much higher 
fracture strength and obvious ductile facture mode. 
 Introduction 
The combination of γ´ precipitates and nanosized ox ides provides the possibility to extend 
high-temperature strength limit of Co-base alloy [1 ]. γ´ phase [Co 3(Al,W)] was discovered in 
Co-Al-W system by Sato et al. [2]. γ´ precipitates in cast Co-base alloy has been approved to have 
high stability and anomalous temperature dependence of flow stress, rendering it an attractive 
strengthening phase for extending the intermediate- temperature strength of mechanically alloyed 
Co-base oxide dispersion strengthened (ODS) alloys [3-6]. However, the precipitation of γ´ phase in 
Co-base ODS alloy becomes difficult because of the extremely low solubility of Al and W in the 
matrix at room temperature, and fully alloyed powder can not be obtained after mechanical alloying, 
leading to the formation of a large amount of secon dary phases such as Co 7W6, Co 3W and Co xAl 
phases [7-9]. The main challenge during the fabrica tion of Co-base ODS alloy by mechanical 
alloying is how to increase the volume fraction of γ´ phase and optimize their precipitation 
morphology. Thus, Ni was introduced in Co-base ODS alloys with the aim to promote the 
precipitation of γ´ phase [10-11]. The microstructural characteristics and the fractural behaviour of 
the Co-base ODS alloys with the addition of Ni were examined.

3. Results and discussion 
Fig. 1 shows the microstructures of the Co-base ODS alloys solutionized at 1300 oC for 6 h and 
aged at 900 oC for 24 h. A high population of near spherical white phase was observed in Fig. 1a. It 
is also seen that the distribution of γ´ phase is u niform and the size is about 0.5 µm. The number 
density of the white phase was significantly reduce d and the size is larger than that of the alloy 
without the addition of Ni, as indicated in Fig. 1b . The microstructure of the mechanically alloyed 
Co-base ODS alloys is much different from that of c ast Co-base alloy with γ/γ´ two-phase 
microstructure. This difference was mainly attributed to the extremely low solubility of Al and W in 
the matrix, and a large amount of Al and W can not participate in the formation of γ´ phase and 
precipitates in the form of secondary phases [12-13]. With the addition of 40 wt.% Ni, white phase 
was significantly reduced due to the enlarged solid solution of Al and W within the matrix. 
However, the white phase can not be completely removed with the addition of Ni.

It is obvious that the intensities of the peaks of AlxCo, Co 3W and Co 7W6 phases are intense and the 
peaks of γ΄ precipitates are relatively low for the 0Ni alloy. With regard to the 40Ni alloy, the peaks 
of AlxCo are evidently decreased and the intensity for γ΄ phase is enhanced. At the same time, the 
intensity of Co 3W and Co 7W6 phases is obviously weakened, indicating that the a ddition of Ni 
promotes the formation of γ΄ phase and suppresses the precipitation of secondary phases. 
The nature of γ´ precipitates (volume fraction, siz e, morphology, distribution homogeneity and 
thermal stability) is closely associated with the mechanical properties [14]. Fig. 3 demonstrates the 
morphology of γ´ precipitates in Co-base ODS alloys with and without the addition of Ni. It is seen 
from Fig. 3a that the γ´ phase in the alloy without the addition of Ni is irregular and size is not 
uniform, and cuboidal γ´ precipitates was observed. The size of the largest γ´ phase is around 0.75 
µm, while the smallest γ´ phase is only 0.21 µm. The volume fraction of γ´ precipitates in the 40Ni 
alloy is about 41 vol.%, which is much higher than that of the alloy without the addition of Ni (13 
vol.%). It is worth noting that the volume fraction of γ´ precipitates is still lower than that of the 
cast Co-base alloy with similar nominal composition s (>50 vol.%) [4]. The nonhomogeneous 
composition of γ´ phase induced different lattice m ismatch between γ´ precipitates and the matrix, 
resulting in the variance of morphology and particl e size. The homogeneity of γ´ precipitates 
depends greatly on the uniform distribution of alloying elements in the matrix.

The fracture strength of 40Ni alloy (1801 MPa) is much larger than that of the 0Ni alloy (1670 
MPa). The SEM fracture micrographs of the compression specimens are displayed in Fig. 4. As for 
the alloy without the addition of Ni, typical cleavage fracture mode with river pattern was observed, 
as indicated in Fig. 4a. A high density of γ´ precipitates were observed with the presence of a lot of 
cracks. High volume fraction of γ´ precipitates, hi gh population oxide dispersion and fine-grained 
microstructure are responsible for the increase of internal stress, leading to the propagation of 
cracks [14-15]. This is agreed with its feature of high strength and low ductility. The fracture mode 
changes from brittle to ductile with decreasing γ´ forming elements. Fig. 4b shows the fractural 
surface of the alloy with the addition of 40 wt.% N i. It is seen that the fracture surface of the allo y 
with the addition of 40 wt.% Ni revealed marked duc tile fracture characteristics and dimple 
morphology. It is concluded that excessive strength ening of γ´ phase and oxides tend to induce 
cleavage fracture and low ductility, which is detrimental to the plastic deformability. The alloy with 
the addition of Ni offers the promise of good toughness and relatively high strength.

Conclusions 
 (1) The volume fraction and homogeneity of γ΄ ph ase was enhanced, and the amount of 
secondary phases such as Al
xCo, Co 3W, Co 7W6 and W phases were significantly reduced in the 
Co-base ODS alloys with the addition of Ni, which c an be attributed to the enlarged solid solution 
limit of Al and W within the matrix. 
(2) Ni-containing Co-base ODS alloys exhibit much h igher fracture strength than that of the 
alloys without the addition of Ni. With the increasing Ni content, the fracture mode of the Co-base 
ODS alloys has transformed from cleavage fracture mode to ductile facture mode.
