ABSTRACT
This present work investigated the temperature-affected microstructural sta-
bility of coherent cuboidal B2 nanoprecipitates in the body-centered-cubic
(BCC)-based Al
0.7CoCr2FeNi high-entropy alloy with temperature. Alloy spec-
imens were prepared by suction-cast processing and then heat-treated at dif-
ferent temperatures of 673–1273 K for 2 h, respectively. It was found that this
coherent microstructure with cuboidal B2 nanoprecipitates in BCC matrix can be
maintained up to a high temperature of 873 K with an average particle size of
100–120 nm. Even after heat treatment at 773 K for 1080 h, the cuboidal B2
nanoprecipitates are still stabilized without any coarsening. Further increasing
temperature (973 K B T B 1173 K), the B2 particles were coarsened obviously
and the r phase became dominant gradually that leads to a heavy brittleness.
Besides, the face-centered-cubic phase appeared after treatment at a higher
temperature of 1173 K, resulting in a softening of alloy. Moreover, the calcula-
tion of phase diagrams approach also demonstrated the microstructural evo-
lution derived from experimental results. In addition, high strength caused by
cuboidal B2 particles in as-cast and heat-treated alloy specimens (compressive
yield strength, r
y = 1727–2190 MPa) was discussed with the precipitation
strengthening mechanism.

Introduction
Precipitation strengthening, or aging strengthening,
plays a profound effect on mechanical properties of
alloys, including strength, ductility and fracture
toughness [ 1–10]. Intensive researches have demon-
strated that coherent intermetallic precipitates are
signiﬁcant for strengthening alloy matrix and
improving microstructural stability at elevated tem-
peratures, which has been successfully applied to
conventional solid solution-based alloys [ 1–10]. For
example, ordered spherical L1
2-c0 (Al3Sc, cP-AuCu3
type) nanoparticles could be coherently precipitated
in Sc-alloyed supersaturated face-centered-cubic
(FCC) Al alloys during aging, resulting in an
enhancement of strength at 600 K [ 5]. More impor-
tantly, it is the coherent precipitation of cuboidal L1
2-
c0 (Ni3Al) particles in FCC matrix that renders Ni-
based superalloys with excellent creep-resistant
properties due to its high microstructural stability at
higher temperatures [ 6, 7]. This precipitation
strengthening mechanism has been recently applied
into high-entropy alloys (HEAs), including both FCC-
based [ 11–14] and BCC-based (body-centered-cubic)
HEAs [ 15–17]. As a new class of compositionally
complex alloys, HEAs generally contain multiple
principle elements with equimolar, near-equimolar
or non-equimolar mixing, as a result of the formation
of simple crystal structures, such as FCC, BCC and
HCP (hexagonal close-packed), as well as their
derivatives [18–21].
Currently, solid solution-based HEAs with ordered
nanoprecipitates have gotten more attention since
they exhibit prominent mechanical properties, espe-
cially high strength. Through multi-element alloying,
a (NiCoFeCr)
94Ti2Al4 (in atomic percent, at.%) HEA
possesses a special coherent microstructure with ﬁne
spherical L1
2-c0 (Ni3(Al, Ti)) nanoprecipitates in FCC
matrix, resulting in a signiﬁcant strength improve-
ment with a yield strength over 1 GPa [11]. Moreover,
these precipitates are very stable and can be
remained even after aging at 1073 K for 503 h, in
which the precipitate coarsening rate is one to two
orders of magnitude lower than that in traditional Ni-
based superalloys [ 12]. However, it is quite chal-
lenging to achieve similar microstructure with
ordered spherical or cuboidal B2 ( cP-CsCl type)
particles in BCC solid solution matrix due to a large
composition difference between BCC and B2 phases.
It will induce a large lattice misﬁt and then form a
ubiquitous weave-like microstructure, which can
lead to a heavy brittleness [22–26]. For instance, high-
strength BCC AlNiCoFeCr HEA has virtually no
tensile ductility owing to the weave-like microstruc-
ture of BCC and B2 [ 22]. In our previous work, it is
interesting that a coherent microstructure with
cuboidal B2 nanoprecipitates in BCC-based HEAs
was achieved based on the compositions of Al
2(Ni,
Co, Fe, Cr) 14 (i.e., Al 12.5(Ni, Co, Fe, Cr) 87.5, at.%)
[15, 16]. In addition, cuboidal and plate-like BCC
precipitates were also found to be coherently
embedded into B2 matrix in a refractory AlMo
0.5-
NbTa0.5TiZr HEA [ 27].
Besides alloy composition, the microstructures and
phase constitutions of HEAs are metastable and
prone to be affected by heat treatments. Especially, in
Al–Ni–Co–Fe–Cr HEAs series, a Cr-rich r phase (tP-
FeCr), which was closely related to the Cr-rich BCC
phase, was preferred to form within the temperature
range of 773–1273 K [ 15, 28]. Annealed twins were
also existed in FCC regions of Al–Ni–Co–Fe–Cr
HEAs at elevated temperatures [ 28]. Besides, the
thermodynamic stabilities of Al–Ni–Co–Fe–Cr HEAs
could be well predicted by using the CALPHAD (s-
tands for CALculation of PHAse Diagrams)
approach, which helps us to further understand the
effect of Al content on the phase transformation, the
nucleation path of the r phase and the formation of
annealed twins in the FCC matrix [ 28]. Moreover, it
could be summarized via the CALPHAD calculations
that Al is a strong stabilizer for the ordered B2 phase,
Cr is rich in the BCC phase, while Co and Ni are often
enriched in the FCC phase [ 29]. However, the
microstructural stability of coherent B2 precipitates at
elevated temperatures has seldom been investigated
in BCC-based Al–Ni–Co–Fe–Cr HEAs, which is cru-
cial for the application of B2-strengthened BCC HEAs
at elevated temperatures.
For the coherent precipitation of ordered B2 into
the BCC matrix, the cuboidal B2 nano-sized precipi-
tates were found in as-cast BCC Al
0.7CoCr2FeNi
HEA, which was obtained with the guide of the
composition formula of Al
2(Ni, Co, Fe, Cr) 14 through
mutating the combination of Ni, Co, Fe and Cr ele-
ments [ 15, 16]. Therefore, in the present study, the
microstructural evolution of BCC Al 0.7CoCr2FeNi
HEA with cuboidal B2 precipitation will be investi-
gated systematically through heat treatments at dif-
ferent temperatures. Then the thermodynamic
stability of the HEA will be calculated by the

Experimental
The Al0.7CoCr2FeNi (Al12.5Co17.5Cr35Fe17.5Ni17.5 at.%)
HEA specimens used in this study were prepared by
means of arc melting and suction cast into a 3-mm-
diameter cylindrical copper mold under an argon
atmosphere. Purities of the raw elemental metals are
99.99% for Al, Co, Fe and Ni, and 99.9% for Cr,
respectively. Mixtures of raw materials with appro-
priate compositions and a total weight of about 6 g
for each rod were remelted at least four times to
ensure chemical homogeneity before suction-casting.
The as-cast cylindrical rods with a diameter of 3 mm
were then heat-treated at temperatures of 673 K,
773 K, 873 K, 973 K, 1073 K, 1173 K and 1273 K for
2 h, respectively, followed by water-quenching.
Especially, the treatment time at 773 K was pro-
longed up to 24 h, 240 h, 720 h and 1080 h, respec-
tively, and then followed by water-quenching.
Crystalline phases were identiﬁed using a Bruker
D8 X-ray diffractometer (XRD) with the Cu-K
a radi-

dard method and peak splitting [in (110) diffraction]
were applied to calculate the lattice constants [ 30].

Thermodynamic calculations
Up to now, the CALPHAD approach has played a
more and more important role in the design and
development of HEAs [ 28, 29, 32–36]. For better
understanding the stable phases and the microstruc-
tural evolution, equilibrium calculations were carried
out on Al
0.7CoCr2FeNi HEA within the temperature
range from 500 K to 1900 K (as shown in Fig. 7). It
can be seen that the primary phase during solidiﬁ-
cation is the BCC phase and the ordered B2 phase is
stable within the entire temperature range within the
solid state. At lower temperatures ( T \ 774 K), the
miscibility gap within the BCC phase starts to form,
resulting in a phase decomposition of BCC #1 and
BCC #2 phases. In addition, it is also noticed that a
small amount of an ordered c
0-L12 phase is stable at
the temperature of T \ 770 K from calculation. The
calculated stable range of the r phase, which is
originated from the Cr–Fe and Co–Cr binary systems,
is within 632–1213 K. In addition, the disordered
BCC phase will disappear within the temperature
range of 792–1199 K, which is the range of the exis-
tence of r phase. Then, with further increasing the
temperature, the BCC phase will reform and coexist
with the FCC and B2 at elevated temperatures of
T [ 1199 K. Moreover, the calculated upper limit for
the FCC stabilization can reach T = 1600 K.
The thermodynamic stability of the Al
0.7CoCr2FeNi
HEA calculated by the CALPHAD approach could
help us for better understanding of our experimental
observations and provide us an overall picture for the
phase transformation of this alloy versus tempera-
ture. Actually, the temperatures of heat treatments
were determined by referring the CALPHAD calcu-
lation, which shows the evolution of BCC, r, and FCC
phases with temperature. In addition, the observed
as-cast microstructure of the Al
0.7CoCr2FeNi HEA is
the major BCC and B2 phases, which are consistent
with the thermodynamic prediction. The

Conclusions
In the present study, the temperature-affected
microstructural stability and precipitation strength-
ening of cuboidal B2 nanoprecipitates in BCC Al
0.7
CoCr2FeNi HEA have been examined systematically
from the viewpoints of both experiments and ther-
modynamic calculation. This coherent microstructure
with cuboidal B2 nanoprecipitates in BCC matrix can
be maintained up to a high temperature of 873 K with
an average particle size of 100–120 nm. Even after
heat treatment at 773 K for 1080 h, the cuboidal B2
nanoprecipitates are still stabilized without any
coarsening. The as-cast and heat-treated alloy speci-
mens at T B 873 K exhibit a higher strength with
r
y = 1727–2190 MPa, which is derived from the fact
that the experimental particle size of B2 nanoprecip-
itates is close to the ideally optimal value for
achievement of the largest strength increment at a
moderate lattice misﬁt ( e * 0.45%) with a ﬁxed vol-
ume fraction, according to the precipitation
strengthening mechanism. With increasing the tem-
perature (973 K B T B 1273 K), the B2 nanoprecipi-
tates could be coarsened, and the r phase becomes
dominant gradually, leading to a heavy brittleness
with an increased microhardness (the highest HV =
859 after treatment at 1073 K). Besides, a plate-like
FCC phase appears at 1173 K and becomes dominant
when heat-treated at 1273 K, softening the alloy with
the lowest r
y = 1306 MPa.
