ABSTRACT 
In this study, a novel Ti – Zr – Cu – Ni amorphous filler metal was fabricated and applied to vacuum brazing of TiAl 
alloy and Ni-based superalloy (GH536). The effects of brazing temperature on the interfacial microstructure and 
mechanical properties of the joints were analyzed. Three different regions, the isothermal solidification layer I, 
continuous reaction layer II and continuous diffusion layer III, were formed in the joints after brazing. The SEM 
and EDS results show that brazing temperature plays an important role in element diffusion, microstructure 
evolution and metallurgical quality of brazed joints, while the thickness of layer II and III was basically not 
affected by brazing temperature. The obtained joints showed excellent shear strength both at room temperature 
and elevated temperature. With the increase of brazing temperature, the shear strength of joints at room tem -
perature firstly increased and then decreased. The optimal shear strength of 279 MPa was obtained after brazing 
at 1100 
◦
C for 10 min. With the increase of test temperature, the shear strength of joints obtained at 1110 
◦
C/10 
min firstly decreased, then increased, and decreased again, with the maximum value of 156 MPa at 600 
◦
C. The 
shear fracture mainly occurred in the thinnest layer II and the fracture morphology exhibited typical cleavage 
fracture characteristics. 
1. Introduction 
TiAl alloy is one of the most promising light-weight elevated tem -
perature structural materials. It is regarded as an ideal material that can 
replace Ni-based superalloy for its attractive properties of not only 
relatively low density (only half that of traditional Ni-based superalloy), 
but also excellent strength, high creep resistance and oxidation resis -
tance at elevated temperature [ 1 – 8 ]. Compared with Ni-based superal -
loy widely employed in aero engines and gas turbines [ 9 – 11 ], TiAl alloy 
has many advantages but a much higher cost. Therefore, TiAl alloy is 
often combined with Ni-based superalloy to obtain outstanding 
comprehensive performance at a lower cost. As elevated temperature 
structural materials, components made of TiAl alloy and Ni-based su -
peralloy are generally exposed to high temperature environment, which 
means their joints should not only have ideal mechanical properties at 
room temperature, but also maintain good mechanical properties and 
oxidation resistance at elevated temperature. In fact, how to produce 
dissimilar joints with excellent performance has become a hotspot in 
material research for a long time. 
However, joining TiAl alloy to Ni-based superalloy is a huge 
challenge due to the great differences in physical and chemical prop -
erties. Firstly, the thermal expansion coefficient of TiAl alloy is much 
lower than that of Ni-based superalloy [ 12 ]. The difference will yield 
large residual stress in the joining process, and consequently form fatal 
micro-cracks easily in the joints which seriously weaken the joint 
strength. Furthermore, the dissimilar joints of TiAl alloy and Ni-based 
superalloy suffer from severe reactions between each other. Many 
intermetallic compounds, such as AlNi
2
Ti, Al
3
NiTi
2 
and TiNi
3
, are prone 
to form in the joints, due to the dissolution enthalpy of Ti in liquid Ni 
solvent is up to 170 kJ mol
 1 
[ 13 ]. The formation of intermetallic 
compounds will essentially embrittle the joints and thus deteriorate the 
joint performance. Therefore, how to effectively restrain the formation 
of brittle phase and ensure favorable metallurgical quality at the joining 
interface is an extremely critical issue.

alloy mainly focuses on diffusion bonding [ 14 – 16 ], fusion welding [ 1 , 
17 ] and brazing [ 18 , 19 ]. Zhou et al. [ 20 ] joined TiAl alloy to Ni-based 
superalloy (GH3536) by diffusion bonding with Cu – Ti alloy as inter -
mediate layer. The joints bonded at 935

in microstructure and had the maximum shear strength. However, the

In this paper, a novel Ti – Zr – Cu – Ni amorphous filler metal was 
fabricated by rapid solidification technology and used to braze TiAl 
alloy and GH536 Ni-based superalloy. The microstructure of the dis -
similar joints was analyzed in detail. The effects of brazing temperature 
on the microstructure evolution and mechanical properties of the joints 
were studied. Furthermore, the joint fracture mechanism was also 
investigated. 
2. Materials and experimental procedures 
2.1. Materials 
The nominal chemical compositions of TiAl alloy and GH536 Ni- 
based superalloy used in this study are listed in Table 1 . TiAl alloy 
and GH536 alloy specimens were sectioned into the size of 22 × 6 × 3 
mm and 5 × 6 × 3 mm by electro-discharge machine, respectively. All 
the joining surfaces were polished by up to 800-grit SiC paper, then 
cleaned ultrasonically in absolute ethyl alcohol for 5 min and dried in 
air. The brazing foil with a nominal composition of Ti-27.5Zr-22.5Cu- 
22.5Ni (wt%) produced by rapid solidification technology in a vacuum 
single roller spinning quenching system (WK-II), was 10 mm wide and 
50 μ m thick. 
2.2. Brazing process 
The brazing foil was placed between the TiAl and GH536 specimens 
to form a sandwich structure, as shown in Fig. 1 (a). The assembly was 
placed into a graphite mould and a slight pressure was applied to ensure 
a fine interfacial contact between the base metals and the brazing foil. 
The brazing experiments were carried out at 1070 – 1150

2.3. Examination of microstructure and mechanical properties of joints 
The melting temperature of the amorphous filler metal was exam -

a heating rate of 20

energy dispersive X-ray spectrometer (EDS). Furthermore, the detailed 
microstructure of the joints was observed by a transmission electron 
microscope (TEM, FEI Tecnai F30) with selected area electron diffrac -

2

3.5. Mechanical properties of TiAl/GH536 joints 
Fig. 10 displays the room temperature shear strength of the TiAl/ 
GH536 joints brazed at various temperatures for 10 min. With the in -
crease of brazing temperature in 1070 – 1150 
◦
C, the joint shear strength 
increases firstly and then gradually decreases. At a lower brazing tem -
perature, the fluidity of the liquid filler metal is poor and the dissolution 
and reaction between the substrates and the liquid filler metal are 
insufficient, which results in the formation of Al(Ni,Cu)
2
(Ti,Zr) and (Ti, 
Zr)(Cu,Ni,Al) intermetallic compounds. The brittle phases present in the 
joints will lead to microcracks in cooling after brazing, so a relatively 
low strength will be obtained (e.g., 144 and 234 MPa for 1070 and 
1090 
◦
C, respectively), as shown in Fig. 7 (a) and (b). With the brazing 
temperature rises up to 1110 
◦
C, the joint strength is improved to 279 
MPa due to the formation of appropriate interfacial reaction layers. 
However, over high brazing temperature will induce higher residual 
stress [ 37 ] and consequently will cause microcracks in the brazing seam, 
as shown in Fig. 7 (d) and (e). Additionally, the brazing beam widening 
with the increase of brazing temperature also lead to dramatic deterio -
ration of bonding strength [ 38 , 39 ]. Therefore, when the brazing tem -
perature rises to 1130 and 1150 
◦
C, the shear strength decreases to 255 
and 250 MPa, respectively. 
To further investigate the performance of TiAl/GH536 joint, the 
joints brazed at 1110 
◦
C for 10 min were selected due to the highest 
shear strength at room temperature. Fig. 11 shows the joint shear 
strength as function of test temperature. With the increase of test tem -
perature, the joint shear strength of decreases firstly, then increases, and 
finally decreases again. Metal materials commonly will become soft as 
the test temperature rises. Therefore, when the test temperature is up to 
200 
◦
C and 400 
◦
C from room temperature, the joint shear strength 
decreases from 279 MPa to 153 MPa and 137 MPa, respectively. 
Generally, the increase of test temperature not only softens the metal 
materials in the brazed joints, but also relaxes the residual stress in the 
joints which is helpful to improve the joint performance. It is speculated 
that at 600 
◦

joint area is greater than the weakening effect caused by material soft -
ening, leading to the improvement of the overall strength. Thus the joint 
shear strength increases to 156 MPa at 600 
◦
C from 137 MPa at 400 
◦
C. 
In addition, the positive temperature effect of the intermetallic com -
pounds also enhance the joint strength. As the test temperature con -
tinues to rise up to 800 
◦
C, the grain size of joint coarsens markedly and

the great differences in physical and chemical properties. To solve this 
problem, a novel Ti – Zr – Cu – Ni amorphous filler metal was fabricated 
and applied to vacuum brazing of TiAl alloy and Ni-based superalloy
