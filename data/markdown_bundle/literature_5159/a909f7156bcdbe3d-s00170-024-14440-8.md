and microstructure evolution of aeroengine high-temperature alloy 
steel tube
Tiantai Tian1,2 · Wenbin Zhan2 · Hongtu Xu2 · Yu Zhang2 · Huaibei Zheng1 · Haosong Zhu2 · Qi Zhang2
Received: 15 May 2024 / Accepted: 6 September 2024 / Published online: 30 September 2024

Abstract
Rotary swaging (RS) with mandrel was performed on aeroengine high-temperature alloy steel tubes at room temperature. 
Annealing experiments were conducted on both the non-RS and RS sections at various temperatures. The evolution of 
microstructure and mechanical properties was analyzed. Mechanical testing results revealed that RS resulted in a greater 
increase in yield strength compared to tensile strength, with a less pronounced recovery of yield strength enhancement after 
annealing. Annealing at 700 °C showed the most effective recovery of elongation. Microstructural observations showed short-
ening and coarsening of martensite packets and blocks after annealing, along with the evolution of precipitates. The higher 
dislocation density introduced by the RS process led to recovery during annealing and extensive recrystallization at 900 °C. 
Both annealing temperature and dislocations jointly influenced microstructure evolution and the work-hardening behavior. 
The thickness of shear layers induced by the RS process on the surface could be reduced after annealing and the elongated 
grains underwent recovery and recrystallization. This study provides a comprehensive investigation into the mechanisms of 
mechanical performance and microstructure evolution. It holds practical insights for applying the RS-annealing process to 
aeroengine high-temperature alloy steel tubes and for developing advanced processing routes for aeroengine shafts.
Keywords Rotary swaging · Aeroengine shaft · Microstructure evolution · High-temperature steel · Mechanical properties
1 Introduction
The advancement of the aviation industry has imposed 
higher performance requirements on aeroengines. Shafts 
play a crucial role, particularly in small and medium-sized 
aeroengines. These shafts are often designed as hollow with 
inner-stepped structures, significantly increasing the com-
plexity of manufacturing. Additionally, high-performance 
materials are commonly used to meet the demanding ser -
vice conditions at elevated temperatures [1 ]. The materials 
and design features present significant challenges for the 
precision manufacturing of aeroengine shafts. Traditional 
processing route relies on deep hole drilling and boring. 
Maintaining coaxiality, straightness of the inner-stepped 
hole, and controlling wall thickness errors can be challeng-
ing, potentially leading to tolerance deviations. [2 ].
Rotary swaging (RS) is an incremental forming technol-
ogy well-suited for the production of hollow shafts. In the 
RS process, a tube billet shown in Fig.  1 is shaped through 
continuous small deformation applied by the rotating dies in 
high frequency and short strokes [3]. A mandrel can be used 
to ensure the accuracy and surface quality of the inner hole. 
The RS process offers advantages such as smooth surfaces, 
high productivity, and good accuracy [4]. It provides a pro-
spective solution for the existing manufacturing problems 
in aeroengine shafts compared with processes like rotary 
compression, skew rolling, precision extrusion, and cross 
wedge rolling which are mainly studied and used in auto-
mobile hollow shafts [5–8].
AM355 stainless steel is an important high-temperature 
alloy steel because of its high strength and corrosion resist-
ance [9]. Compared with nickel-based superalloys, AM355 has 
an obvious cost advantage at service temperature below 600

Many studies have been conducted on the microstructure 
evolution of various bar billets processed by RS. Meng et al. 
studied the RS of titanium bars and analyzed grain evolution 
[13]. Mao et al. have been carrying out research on the effects 
of RS on the grain boundaries and properties of bar materials, 
including Al [14], Cu [15], and Cu-Cr [16]. Klumpp et al. dis-

in swaged Inconel 718 bars [17]. Wan et al. revealed the forma-
tion mechanism of the gradient structure, radial microbeams, 
and nanocrystals produced by multi-pass RS in magnesium 
alloys [18, 19]. Panov et al. found that lamellar structure intro-
duced by radial forging in metastable austenitic steel could be 
transformed into equiaxed grains [20]. Kocich et al. found that 
large diameter reduction introduced dynamic recrystallization 
into Al/Cu composite rods [21, 22]. They also explored the 
effects on tungsten heavy alloys [23, 24].
At present, there are relatively fewer researches on the 
microstructure of RS tubes. Zhang et al. studied Ti-6Al-4 V 
seamless tubes and analyzed their texture and recrystallization 
[25, 26]. Nikulin et al. processed a Fe-V-Fe three-layer tube 
and discovered a transition zone of diffusion interaction which 
could be widened by annealing [27]. Xu et al. investigated 
grain evolution and texture anisotropy after RS [28, 29]. Yang 
et al. analyzed the effect of annealing on texture intensity in 
RS steel tubes [30, 31]. However, research on the RS process 
for aeroengine material tubes is still limited.
In summary, the RS process holds significant potential for 
the manufacturing of aeroengine shafts. Therefore, understand-
ing the mechanical properties and microstructure evolution of 
swaged AM355 tubes is crucial for developing an effective 
processing route for manufacturing AM355 aeroengine shafts. 
This research will provide valuable insights for the RS produc-
tion of aeroengine shafts.
2 Materials and methods
The chemical composition of AM355 steel used in the present 
study is presented in Table 1.
Due to the large size of aeroengine shafts (e.g., Φ50 
mm × 350 mm), studying a series of shafts at their actual 
dimensions proved challenging. On this account, the study 
was performed on small tubes. The AM355 rod billets were 
received from an aeroengine manufacturing company in China. 
The processing route is demonstrated in Fig. 2. Φ20.5 mm rods 
were initially cut from the AM355 rod by wire-electrode cut-

the RS process to form an inner-stepped hole with a diam-
eter of Φ8 mm. The RS process reduced the outer diameter 
to Φ17.75 mm. Tubes after RS were then heat-treated at 700 
℃, 800 ℃, and 900 ℃ in a furnace for 100 min, followed by 
air cooling.

3 Experimental results
3.1 Tensile properties
Figure 4 shows the tensile behaviors under different condi-
tions. AP represented as-processed tubes which were only 
deep holes drilled without any following processes. RS rep-
resented for rotary swaged tubes.
Apparently, different annealing temperatures had com-
pletely different effects on the tensile behaviors.
For AP tubes, the annealing process under 900 ℃ tended 
to increase tensile strength (TS) while reducing yield 
strength (YS). The elongation of AP tubes was enhanced 
to some extent at 700 ℃ and 800 ℃ but marginally reduced 
at 900 ℃. The elongation was moderately affected and con-
sistently maintained above 21%. Compared to annealing at 
700 ℃ and 800 ℃, 900 ℃ annealing would greatly increase 
the TS while weakening the influence on YS. This phenom-
enon might imply that the microstructure had experienced 
substantial evolution.
For RS tubes, the elongation was sharply reduced by more 
than 50% after RS; however, it was restored close to the level 
of 25-AP with 700 ℃ annealing. When the temperature was 
elevated to 800 ℃ , the elongation could still restore quite 
well though it was less than that of 700-RS. Nevertheless, 
with 900 ℃ annealing, the elongation could only restore to 
below 15%. The influence pattern of annealing on TS and 
YS in AP tubes persisted in RS tubes. The annealed AP and 
RS tubes had similar TS. However, the impact of the cold 
RS process on YS could not be eliminated despite annealing.
In Fig.  4, we could conclude that the annealing pro-
cess mainly controlled the TS while the RS process before 
annealing introduced strengthening of the YS. The elonga-
tion was regulated by the combined interaction of RS and 
annealing.
Figure  5 shows the strength comparison and varia-
tion under different conditions to further demonstrate 
the strengthening effect. In Fig.  5a and b, after RS fol-
lowed by annealing at different temperatures, although 
the strengthening effect diminished, it still resulted in 
YS enhancement of about 120 ~ 160 MPa. On the other 
hand, annealing at different temperatures only slightly 
increased TS. Under 900 ℃ condition, the annealed RS 
tube even failed to reach the TS of the annealed AP tube. 
Figure  5c straightly illustrates that cold RS enhanced YS 
by 48.74% while only by 9.14% for TS. The annealing 
after RS confined the variation in TS within a small range 
of − 1.8 ~ 4.5%, indicating a limited impact, whereas the

3.2 Hardness
Figure 6 presents the hardness measurement distribution and 
average results, where position 0.0 mm stood for the outer 
surface and position 5.0 mm stood for the inner surface. The 
hardness kept steady values under all conditions and fluc-
tuations were slight, implying a homogeneous microstruc-

empirical relationships between TS and hardness [32]. Com-
bining Figs. 6b and 5, it could also be observed that hardness 
is primarily correlated with TS, and the differences in average 
hardness after annealing were slighter than those in TS.
3.3 Microstructure observation
3.3.1 Microstructure in the middle area
The microstructure observed in the middle area is shown 
in Fig.  7. The grain boundaries were revealed through the 
corrosion reactant network, so the sample surface was first 
etched and then slightly polished.
Martensite lath structures in prior austenite grain bounda-
ries (GBs) could be observed under all conditions except 
900-RS. Grains of all tubes after RS were observed to have 
been flattened to a certain extent along the radial direction. 
However, the lath structures were more fragmented and 
discontinuous at 800-RS. Under the 900-RS condition, the 
martensite structures were eliminated and replaced by fine

radius of about 20 ~ 30 µm and the new recrystallized grains 
only owned a radius of less than 8 µm. In contrast, under 
the 900-AP condition, the disappearance of the martensite 
structures was not observed.
SEM observation shows more details in Fig.  8. There 
was no significant difference for the martensite lath struc-
tures under other conditions except for annealing at 900 ℃. 
Under 900-RS condition, distinct boundaries of recrystal-
lized grains were evidently observed. The recrystallized 
grains were distributed uniformly in each prior grain, 
indicating that the microstructure transformation had

3.4 Fracture analysis
Fracture surfaces are shown in Fig.  10. Typical cup-and-
cone ductile fracture surface under 25-AP condition could 
be divided into shear lip zone (SLZ) and crack radial zone 
(CRZ) by the yellow line. The width which was closer to 
the short edge of SLZ under 25-RS condition narrowed 
from about 300 µm to about 190 µm. Under 700-RS and 
800-RS conditions, the surface undulation became obvious 
compared to the surface of AP tubes. The reason might be 
the combined effect of the RS strengthening along the radial 
direction and the uneven recovery of the microstructure 
caused by heat treatment, resulting in the steps and bulges 
on the fracture surface. Under 900-RS condition, the fracture 
surface became flat again in the middle area.
Further observation in the middle areas is shown in 
Fig. 11. A large number of dimples were distributed in all 
fracture surfaces, indicating that the fracture was dominated 
by ductile fracture mode in all states. The segmentation of 
the quasi-cleavage planes could be considered the result of 
intergranular cracks formed by tearing along the prior aus-
tenite GBs [36]. This could also be proved by the size of 
these cracks which were close to the prior austenite grains 
mentioned above. The RS process introduced more second-
ary cracks and developed the intergranular cracks.
3.5 EBSD analysis
The FCC γ-Fe, BCC α-Fe, and Cr2N phases were tested and 
Fig. 12 shows the fraction of the FCC phase and Cr2N phase.
BCC phase predominated under all conditions. The FCC 
phase content of the 25-AP condition was evidently higher 
compared to other conditions, reaching 3.47%, which could 
be considered residual austenite. Direct annealing of AP 
tubes could eliminate the FCC phase, and annealing after 
RS further reduced the residual FCC phase to less than 0.2%. 
The stored energy of deformation might be related to this 
phenomenon [37]. It was interesting the RS tubes exhibited 
higher Cr2N phase content, and as the temperature increased, 
the Cr2N phase content reached a peak at 800 ℃ and then 
declined at 900 ℃ . However, direct annealing of AP tubes 
only led to a reduction in the Cr2N phase content.

the relation to microstructure, further analysis is discussed 
as follows:

4.2 Recovery and recrystallization
The recrystallization status of annealed RS tubes is shown 
in Fig. 22, in which the grains with an orientation difference 
of less than 2° are defined as recrystallized, and between 2 
and 15° are defined as substructured. The recrystallized frac-
tion was nearly kept the same at 700 ℃ and 800 ℃ , which 
was 11.13% and 12.20%, respectively. However, the sub-
structured fraction experienced a significant increase from 
53.65 to 68.51% due to the recovery. When the temperature 
reached 900 ℃, apparently, the recrystallized region showed 
great expansion and its fraction reached 50.66%, reducing 
the substructured fraction to 38.31% at the same time. The 
deformed fraction decreased from 35.22 to 19.29% and then 
to 11.03% as the temperature increased.
From the above conclusions could be drawn: recovery 
developed below 900 ℃ as the temperature rose, but recrys-
tallization nearly did not develop until the temperature 
finally reached 900 ℃. Recrystallization made the martensite 
structure gradually disappear, and more substructured and 
deformed areas remained along the prior austenite grain 
boundaries.

4.3 Texture
Pole and inverse pole figures are shown in Fig. 23. After RS, 
the < 101 > //RD texture was produced after RS and further 
developed as the annealing temperature increased to 700 ℃ 
and 800 ℃. However, this texture was eliminated at 900 ℃. 
The < 001 > //TD texture, which likely developed from the 
remaining texture in the AP billet, was also formed after 
RS. This texture was significantly weakened upon annealing 
and disappeared above 800 °C. In the ND direction, no sig-
nificant texture was observed. In summary, annealing would 
strengthen the texture in the RD direction and weaken the 
texture in the TD direction. At 900 ℃, annealing would lead 
to a relatively homogeneous state in the swaged tubes. It 
should also be noted that none of the existing textures was 
particularly intense.
4.4 Mechanism of microstructure evolution
The mechanism of microstructure evolution of AP and RS 
tubes is further illustrated in Fig.  24.
The initial AP tubes had distinct martensite structures. 
Annealing below 900 ℃ resulted in the enlargement of sub-
structures, particularly the martensite packets, leading to 
a reduction in the small microstructure. Annealing at 900 
℃ caused significant decomposition of martensite blocks, 
resulting in their shortening and coarsening.
Compared to the initial condition, accumulation of dis-
locations occurred after RS due to cold plastic deformation. 
Annealing below 900 ℃ triggered substantial recovery of 
the material, gradually eliminating the accumulated dislo-
cations. Although the martensite structure still remained, 
the martensite packets enlarged and the martensite blocks 
decreased. When the annealing temperature reached 900 
℃, extensive recrystallization occurred, causing the prior 
austenite grains to be segmented by the newly recrystallized 
grains. Meanwhile, the RS process also promoted the pre-
cipitation during the annealing process.
5 Conclusions
In this study, RS and annealing experiments of the aeroen-
gine high-temperature alloy steel tubes were carried out. The 
mechanical properties and microstructure evolution were 
analyzed then. The main conclusions can be summarized 
as follows:
(1) The strengthening effect of the RS process on YS was 
greater than that on TS. Under the AP conditions, RS 
could enhance the axial YS of the tube by 49.77%.
(2) Although annealing processes could reduce both YS 
and TS, the strengthening effect on YS was preserved 
to a greater extent after annealing. Annealing at 900 
℃ resulted in a lower TS of RS tubes than AP tubes. 
Annealing at 700 ℃ restored the RS tubes to a similar 
hardening mode to AP tubes.

(3) In annealed AP tubes, martensite packets enlarged and

(4) At 900 ℃ , significant recrystallization occurs in RS
