The equiatomic TiZrHfNbTa alloy is one of the few refractory high entropy alloys that exhibit tensile duc- 
tility at room temperature, the deformation of which is only dominated by dislocation slip. Here, we 
observed the activation of {112} < 111 > nano-twinning accompanied by the deformation induced body- 
centered cubic structure (BCC) to non-closed packed hexagonal ω phase transformation along with the 
dislocation slip during tensile deformation at cryogenic temperatures, which indicates the intrinsic me- 
chanical instability of the single-phase BCC TiZrHfNbTa solid solution. The alloy maintains a high tensile 
elongation of 20.8% while the yield strength increases signiﬁcantly up to 1,549 MPa as the temperature 
is decreased from 277 K to 77 K, without obvious ductile to brittle transition. This exceptional combi- 
nation of high strength and high ductility at cryogenic temperatures can be interpreted by considering 
the synergistic effect of screw dislocation glide, {112} < 111 > mechanical twinning and BCC → ω phase 
transformation. These results provide new insights on our understanding of the refractory TiZrHfNbTa- 
based alloys and extend their application to cryogenic temperatures at the extreme service conditions like 
aerospace, marine shipbuilding and natural gas industries, albeit they are promising for high-temperature 
application.

1. Introduction 
Unlike traditional alloys, which modify the properties of metals 
by adding relatively small amounts of other elements, high entropy 
alloys (HEAs) mix together multiple principal elements at/near 
equiatomic proportions [1] . Over the past decade, the HEAs have 
intrigued substantial research enthusiasm due to the formation of 
a single-phase microstructure with unprecedented mechanical and 
functional properties [ 1 , 2 ]. The formation, stability and properties 
of single-phase concentrated solid solutions are the core issues in 
the ﬁeld of HEAs both in terms of the basic physics (e.g. elastic 
constants, lattice friction stress, stacking-fault energy and diffusion 
coeﬃcients, etc.) and the design of high-performance multi-phase 
HEAs [2] . 
It has been shown that the high conﬁgurational entropy orig- 
inating from high concentration mixing itself is not suﬃcient 
to stabilize the single-phase solid solutions [ 3 , 4 ]. In fact, almost

and CoCrFeNi [7] MEA has also been experimentally veriﬁed af- 
ter prolonged annealing at intermediate temperatures. The chem- 
ical short-range order can be enhanced after aging due to the 
enthalpic interactions among constituent elements, which renders 
the instability of disordered solid solutions [ 8 , 9 ]. Similarly, in the 
prototypical body-centered cubic (BCC) TiZrHfNbTa (also referred 
to Senkov alloy) [ 10 , 11 ] and Hf 0.5 Nb 0.5 Ta 0.5 Ti 1.5 Zr [12] refractory 
HEAs, the Nb-Ta-rich BCC phase and/or Zr-Hf-rich hexagonal close- 
packed (HCP) phase precipitate after decomposition depending on 
the speciﬁc aging temperature and time. 
Apart from thermal instability, mechanical instability of FCC 
solid solutions has been revealed both in equiatomic HEAs and 
MEAs [13–15] . The martensitic or polymorphism transformation for 
FCC to HCP phase has been activated in the prototypical CrMnFe- 
CoNi HEA under high-pressure at room temperature (RT) [ 13 , 14 ].

The HCP lamellae were triggered coherently with the deformation 
twin boundaries in CoCrNi alloy during the tensile process, espe- 
cially at a cryogenic temperature [15] . The above results are in 
good agreement with the ab initio calculations that the HCP struc- 
ture is energetically favorable relative to FCC at or below RT [15–
17] . The Phase stability of FCC solid solutions can also be deliber- 
ately reduced with the compositional tuning of the stacking-fault 
energy to realize superior strength-ductility combination, known 
as “metastability engineering” [ 18 , 19 ]. Through this strategy, mod- 
est deviations from the equiatomic compositions of the Cantor 
alloy promote twinning induced plasticity (TWIP) [ 18 , 20 ] and/or 
transformation induced plasticity (TRIP) [ 21 , 22 ] and, thus, signiﬁ- 
cantly improve the strain-hardening capabilities. 
Compared with the extensive investigated FCC Cantor alloy and 
its non-equiatomic derivatives, the study of refractory BCC HEAs 
is still in their infancy, especially about the mechanical instabil- 
ity. The Senkov alloy TiZrHfNbTa has been received great atten- 
tion because it is almost the only one system that exhibits ex- 
cellent RT tensile ductility. The plastic deformation of equiatomic 
TiZrHfNbTa at RT is mainly controlled by screw dislocations with 
Burgers vector of a/2 < 111 > [ 23 , 24 ], and as we know now no de- 
formation twinning or martensite phase was discovered for this 
alloy. However, there exists high-temperature phase transforma- 
tion from BCC to HCP and HCP to ω phase transition under high 
pressures for pure Ti, Zr and Hf [25] . Similar to non-equiatomic 
FCC HEAs, TRIP effects can also be triggered in off-equiatomic 
metastable variants of the TiZrHfNbTa alloy by decreasing the con- 
tent of Nb and/or Ta, e.g. the stress-induced BCC → α" [26] and 
BCC → HCP [27] martensite phase transformations were activated in 
non-equiatomic Ti 35 Zr 27.5 Hf 27.5 Nb 5 Ta 5 [26] and TiZrHfTa 0.5 [27] al- 
loys, respectively, during deformation. Although the martensites 
show evidence of internal twinning, no twinning of the parent BCC 
phase is observed. Moreover, while the activation of the stress- 
induced martensite transformation enables substantial gains in 
work-hardening capability and ductility, it inevitably leads to a sig- 
niﬁcant decrease in yield strength [ 26 , 27 ] owing to the weakened 
solid solution hardening effect. One intriguing doubt here is that 
whether the prototypical TiZrHfNbTa HEA itself is mechanically un- 
stable at a state that has not yet been explored, without resorting 
to “metastability engineering”? 
For refractory HEAs, the lack of RT ductility remains to be 
one of the key bottlenecks that limit their potential applications, 
which is related to the drastic rising of the ductile to brittle tran- 
sition (DTBT) temperature [ 28 , 29 ]. For traditional refractory met- 
als or alloys, the DTBT involves the competition between shear in- 
stability and cleavage fracture [30] , i.e. the intrinsic ductile (brit- 
tle) materials are determined by whether dislocation emission oc- 
curs to blunt the crack tip before (after) cracks propagation to 
cause cleavage under external loading. As the temperature de- 
creases, when the rising yield stress cross with the cleavage frac- 
ture stress, the DTBT occurs [29] . For refractory HEAs, however, the 
DTBT has not been systematically investigated and the exact mech- 
anism responsible for the embrittlement tendency has not been 
fully understood. Among the explored refractory HEAs, in contrast 
to brittle VNbTaMoW which exhibit the plastic strain less than 
2% under compression [31] , the Ti-Zr-Hf-Nb-Ta system including 
equiatomic TiZrHfNbTa [ 32 , 33 ] and its derivative TiZrHfNb [34] and 
Hf 0.5 Nb 0.5 Ta 0.5 Ti 1.5 Zr [35] HEAs all exhibit excellent RT tensile 
ductility of about 10%, with their DTBT temperatures below RT 
[28] . 
In this work, we report on the microstructure, deformation 
behavior and tensile properties of the equiatomic TiZrHfNbTa al- 
loy at cryogenic temperatures down to 77 K. We ﬁnd the activa- 
tion of {112} < 111 > mechanical nanotwinning accompanied by the 
stress-induced BCC → ω martensite transformation along with dis- 
location slip during cryogenic tensile deformation, which indicates 
the intrinsic mechanical instability of the single-phase BCC solid 
solution. Relative to RT tensile properties, the yield strength was 
increased by 175% to 1,549 MPa while the high ductility was al- 
most maintained at 20.8% without obvious DTBT at 77 K.

2. Experimental methods 
The equiatomic TiZrHfNbTa alloy ingot (650 g in weight) was 
melted in a cold crucible levitation melting equipment using pure 
elements Ti (99.99 wt.%), Zr (99.5 wt.%), Hf (99.9 wt.%), Nb (99.95 
wt.%), and Ta (99.95 wt.%) under a high purity argon atmosphere. 
To ensure uniformity, the ingot was remelted three times. The 
composition (in at.%) of the ingot was measured by using SEM- 
EDS to be Ti: 20.36 ±0.14, Zr: 18.69 ±0.02, Hf: 20.83 ±0.15, Nb: 
18.88 ±0.03 and Ta: 21.25 ±0.01. To eliminate the microsegregation, 
homogenized treatment at 1300 °C for 5 h were performed, fol- 
lowed by water quenching. The cold rolling (with 75% thickness 
reduction) and 1200 °C / 45 min annealing were used to obtain 
a fully recrystallized microstructure. The heat treatments includ- 
ing homogenization and recrystallization were all performed in the 
tube furnace with high purity argon ﬂowing through the tube dur- 
ing the whole process. The interstitial impurities of the sample 
(as recrystallized) for tensile deformation were determined using 
the LECO TCH600 analyzer. The content of interstitials including 
O, N and H are tested to be 0.034 wt.%, 0.003 wt.% and 0.0012 
wt.%, respectively. Tensile tests at ﬁve different tem peratures (77 
K, 127 K, 177 K, 227 K and 277 K) were performed with an ex- 
tensometer at a strain rate of 1.0 ×10 −3 . Flat tensile specimens 
with a gauge size of 4 mm width, 1.5 mm thickness and 10 mm 
length (16 mm parallel length) were taken along the rolling di- 
rection from the recrystallized sheets. The schematic drawing of 
the tensile sample is inserted in Fig. 1 . The X-ray diffraction (XRD, 
Bruker, D8 ADVANCE DaVinci3kW) with Cu-K α radiation ( λ= 0.154 
nm), scanning electron microscope (SEM, Tescan, MIRA3), elec- 
tron backscattered diffraction (EBSD) and transmission electron 
microscopy (TEM, Philips-FEI, FEI Talos F200X G2) were used to 
characterize the crystal structure and microstructure. Samples for 
EBSD analysis were electrochemically polished with a solution of 
10% HClO 4 and 90% CH 4 O at -35 °C. To avoid the thermal effects of

3. Results and discussion 
3.1. Tensile properties of the TiZrHfNbTa alloy at 77K 
The tensile curve at 77 K for the as recrystallized samples is 
shown in Fig. 1 (a), where the yield strength is up to 1,549 ±10 MPa 
while still maintaining a high tensile elongation of 20.8% ±0.7%. For 
comparison, the tensile curve at 277 K shows the yield strength 
of 875 ±18 MPa and elongation of 22.8% ±1.6%, which is close to 
the RT tensile properties reported in the literature [32] . The true 
stress-strain curves are given in Fig. 1 (b), where the ultimate ten- 
sile strength is up to a high level of 1762 ±22 MPa while keeping a 
uniform elongation of 15.2% ±0.8% slightly higher than that at 277 
K. Note that after cold rolling and recrystallization annealing, as 
shown in Fig. 1 (c), the equiaxed grain microstructure was obtained 
with the grain size around 80 μm. These unprecedented tensile 
properties at 77 K are consistent with the transgranular ductile 
fracture surface covered in dimples of varying sizes, as shown in 
Fig. 1 (d). According to the XRD curves in Fig. 1 (e), there is no visi- 
ble second phase except the single-phase BCC matrix for as recrys- 
tallized and deformed samples both at 277 K and 77 K. The lattice 
parameter a of the BCC phase in fully recrystallized TiZrHfNbTa is 
calculated to be 0.341 nm, which is consistent with existing re- 
ports [ 36 , 37 ].

3.3. Formation mechanisms of ω phase and twinning in the 
TiZrHfNbTa alloy deformed at cryogenic temperature 
The ω phase triggered in the cryogenically deformed 
TiZrHfNbTa alloy indicates that the TiZrHfNbTa HEA is intrin- 
sically unstable under the synergic effect of temperature and 
internal stress. The ω phase has been reported to occur in pure 
elemental Ti, Zr and Hf under high pressures as an equilibrium 
phase or appear as a metastable phase in titanium and zirconium 
alloys by appropriate adding β stabilizing elements Cr, V, Nb, Ta, 
Mo, etc [25] . Based on the transformation process from the BCC 
parent phase, the ω phase can be generally classiﬁed into three 
categories [ 38 , 39 ]: (i) isothermal ω precipitated from the β matrix 
during aging at a moderate temperature [40] , (ii) athermal ω 
formed during quenching from the high-temperature β phase [41] , 
and (iii) deformation induced ω activated by applied strain and/or 
stress [42] . Regardless of the different formation processes of the

ω phase, the occurrence of ω phase in Ti- and Zr-based alloys is 
always originated from the metastable BCC parent phase [25] . The 
isothermal ω phase has been observed recently in “high entropy 
superalloys” of Ti-Ta-Zr constituent system after 700 °C and 1000 
°C exposure, which precipitates from the spinodal decomposition 
of TiZr enriched BCC matrix [43] . The plate-like deformation 
induced ω phase usually appears together with the granular 
athermal ω phase in Ti alloys because of the high martensite 
transformation temperature [ 38 , 39 ]. For the TiZrHfNbTa alloy, 
no athermal ω phase has been observed after quenching, which 
means that the BCC to ω phase transformation temperature is be- 
low RT. This is due to the alloying of high content Nb and Ta into 
TiZrHf, evidently stabilizing the single-phase BCC structure. With 
a small amount of alloying Nb into TiZrHf, the athermal ω phase 
formed in the TiZrHfNb 0.2 alloy while no deformation induced 
ω phase was found in the whole metastable TiZrHfNb (0.2~0.6) 
composition range [44] . Through cryogenic deformation, we 
demonstrate that the temperature can be used as an important 
variable to tailor the mechanical stability of the equiatomic 
TiZrHfNbTa alloy.

present in the deformed TiZrHfNbTa alloy at 277 K, but they can 
be triggered at 227 K. The results mean that the starting acti- 
vation temperature of the mechanical twinning and ω phase is 
in the range of 227~277 K which is very close to RT. As dis- 
tinct from conventional metastable Ti-based alloys, where concur- 
rent {112} < 111 > mechanical twinning and deformation induced 
ω phase have been observed at ambient temperature [ 46 , 47 ], the 
deformation of the TiZrHfNbTa alloy is only dominated by dislo- 
cation slip at RT [ 23 , 24 ]. Different from the traditional dilute tita- 
nium alloys [ 46 , 47 ], the inherent severe lattice distortion [24] of 
the TiZrHfNbTa alloy would inevitably decrease the symmetry and 
regularity of the BCC lattice, which suppresses the necessarily ho- 
mogeneous lattice shear for twinning and ω phase formation and 
thus delays their occurrence to low temperatures. Besides, there 
may exist chemical short-range ordering (SRO) in the TiZrHfNbTa 
alloy after 1200 °C annealing considering the strong enthalpic in- 
teractions among Zr-Hf-Nb-Ta [48] , which renders the formation of 
{112} twinning or ω phase more diﬃcult since it would always in- 
cur additional energy penalty to destroy this SRO. 
3.4. Deformation mechanism of TiZrHfNbTa alloy at cryogenic 
temperature 
For the TiZrHfNbTa alloy, like pure BCC metals, the plastic 
deformation at RT has been reported to be dominated by glid- 
ing of screw dislocation with a/2 < 111 > Burgers vector, with the 
high yield strength originated from the large short-range obstacles 
induced by strong lattice friction [ 23 , 24 ]. At cryogenic temperature, 
screw dislocation glide also dominates the deformation process 
even down to the liquid nitrogen temperature. As shown in Fig. 7 , 
in the deformed sample at 77 K, apart from complex deformation 
bands comprising of mechanical twinning and/or ω phase in some 
grains ( Figs. 7 a-c), the dislocation glide appears in many grains, 
where dislocations tangled with mechanical twins ( Figs. 7 d-f) or 
formed band structure consisting of dense dislocations ( Figs. 7 g-i). 
Fig. 8 shows the engineering stress-strain curves of TiZrHfNbTa 
at different tem peratures in the range of 77~277 K. In the entire 
temperature range, TiZrHfNbTa alloy has no evident DTBT. As the 
deformation temperature decreases, the yield strength increases 
rapidly while the elongation does not decrease signiﬁcantly. This 
strong temperature dependence of yield strength indicates the dis- 
location glide controlled deformation in TiZrHfNbTa alloy, which 
had been observed recently [49] where the thermally activated 
kink nucleation and Peierls-type mechanism are revealed. The in- 
teresting phenomenon in Fig. 8 is that strain hardening ability ex- 
perience a process of ﬁrst decreasing from 277 K to 127 K and then 
increasing to 77 K. The exact mechanism has not been revealed. 
For BCC metals and alloys, the critical twinning stress rises more 
slowly with decreasing temperature than the ﬂow stress for plastic 
deformation by dislocation slip [45] . Therefore, we speculate that 
this phenomenon would be related to the competition between 
dislocation and mechanical twin, i.e. the decreasing tensile temper- 
ature inhibits the screw dislocation glide while promotes the acti- 
vation of mechanical twinning. At 77 K, the interaction between 
{112} nanotwinning and screw dislocation would be the reason for 
recovering moderate strain hardening ability. 
For BCC crystals, the screw dislocations are mainly conﬁned to 
the {110} and {112} slip planes [ 45 , 50 ], while the shear direction 
of twins, η1 = < 111 > , coincides with the Burgers vector b = < 111 > 
of screw dislocations. Therefore, when a screw dislocation en- 
counters a (1 ¯2 1) [111] twin boundary, it can penetrate the twin- 
ning band by double-cross slip and pass through the intersected 
( ¯2 11), (11 ¯2 ), (1 ¯1 0), (10 ¯1 ) and (01 ¯1 ) lattice planes or cross slip onto 
the K 1 = (1 ¯2 1) invariant twinning plane, or just be blocked by the 
twin boundary depending on the relative resolved shear stress. 
The interaction between twin boundaries and screw dislocations 
behavior would effectively facilitate the cross-slip and dislocation 
multiplication. The BCC → ω phase transformation can release the 
stress concentration at the BCC/ ω interface, and the localized de- 
formation induced ω phase has been reported to exhibit a higher 
young’s modulus than β matrix in Ti-based alloys [38] , which in- 
dicate that it can share the load and promote continuous plastic 
deformation.

4. Conclusion 
In summary, our results demonstrate that the equiatomic 
TiZrHfNbTa HEA shows an outstanding combination of yield 
strength and uniform elongation at 77 K, exceeding the RT ten- 
sile properties of the existing refractory MEAs and HEAs. The high 
strength-ductility combination of the TiZrHfNbTa alloy at cryogenic 
temperatures can be interpreted by considering the synergistic ef- 
fect of screw dislocation glide, {112} < 111 > mechanical twinning 
and BCC → ω phase transformation. The exceptional combination of 
high strength and high ductility of the TiZrHfNbTa HEA at 77 K 
demonstrated in this work extends the application of BCC refrac- 
tory HEAs to cryogenic temperatures at the extreme service con- 
ditions like aerospace, marine shipbuilding and natural gas indus- 
tries. This extremely low DTBT temperature also offers a vast op- 
portunity for compositional optimization of the TiZrHfNbTa-based 
alloys when ensuring suﬃcient RT ductility.
