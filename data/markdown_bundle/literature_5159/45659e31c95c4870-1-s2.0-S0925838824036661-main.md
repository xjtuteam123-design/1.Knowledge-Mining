In recent years, refractory high-entropy alloys (RHEAs) have 
emerged as the ideal materials for ultra-high-temperature applications 
due to their superior mechanical properties at elevated temperatures 
[1 – 5 ] . The AlMo
0.5
NbTa
0.5
TiZr RHEA with low density (7.4 g/cm

excellent high-temperature strength ( σ

700 MPa at 1000

researchers [6 – 9 ] . The high specific strength (0.1 MPa ⋅ cm

⋅ kg
 1

1000
◦

0.5
NbTa

nickel-based superalloys [6,8 ] . However, the oxidation resistance of 
AlMo
0.5
NbTa
0.5
TiZr RHEA is inadequate since it is hard to form a 
continuous and protective oxide layer [8,10] . Poor oxidation resistance 
at elevated temperatures severely limits its further development [9] . The 
reason is supposed to be the evaporation of MoO
3 
[8,9 , 11] . The melting 
point of the oxidation product MoO
3 
is only 795 ℃ , and the solubility of 
oxygen during high temperature oxidation is relatively high [10 , 12] . 
The liquid MoO
3 
has a high saturated vapor pressure and evaporates 
quickly, which leads to numerous pores in the oxide scale when MoO
3 
evaporates [13] . The porous and loosely structural scale causes oxygen 
to penetrate and react with the substrate, accelerating oxidation [14] . 
Nevertheless, the addition of Mo is the guarantee to improve the 
high-temperature strength of the alloy and against softening at high 
temperatures [10] . Therefore, there is an urgent need to adjust the 
composition to achieve a match between the oxidation resistance and 
mechanical properties at elevated temperatures. The addition of Ta can 
not only improve the high-temperature strength, but also further 
improve the oxidation resistance by generating the AlTaO
4 
composite 
oxide scale [15 , 16] . Here, we propose a strategic element substitution 
strategy, which involves replacing Mo with Ta in the AlMo
0.5
NbTa
0.5 -
TiZr alloy and investigate the oxidation resistance and strength at 
1000 ℃ of AlNbTaTiZr.
In the present study, laser-directed energy deposition (L-DED) 
method was used to fabricate AlMo
0.5
NbTa
0.5
TiZr and AlNbTaTiZr 
samples. The AlNbTaTiZr alloy has the same superior high-temperature 
strength as the AlMo
0.5
NbTa
0.5
TiZr alloy, whereas AlNbTaTiZr has a 
better oxidation resistance. It must be noted that our findings open the 
possibility for the potential high-temperature applications of RHEAs.
High-purity (all in 99.99 % wt%) Al, Mo, Nb, Ta, Ti and Zr powders 
were used as raw materials to prepare AlMo
0.5
NbTa
0.5
TiZr and AlNb -
TaTiZr, which were denoted as Ta0.5 and Ta1, respectively. The pro -
cessing parameters of L-DED were as follows: laser power of 
4000 – 5000 W, scanning rate of 800 mm/min, layer height of 0.4 mm, 
and powder feed rate of 35 – 45 g/min.
Isothermal oxidation experiments were carried out at 1000 
◦
C for 2, 
5, 10, 15, 20, 35,50,70 and 100 h in air using a box furnace. After 
oxidation, the specimens were immediately removed from the furnace 
and cooled to room temperature. The mass of specimens was measured 
by discontinuous weighing method. A D8 DISCOVER X-ray diffractom -
eter (XRD) was performed to investigate the crystal structure of alloys 
and phases of the oxidation products. The microstructure of alloys was 
observed using scanning electron microscope (SEM) equipped with an 
electron backscatter diffraction (EBSD) detector. Chemical composition 
was characterized by electron probe microanalysis (EPMA, JAX-8230, 
JEOL). A JEOL-2100 transmission electron microscope (TEM) was 
employed to analyze the fine microstructure. The Φ 4*4 mm cylindrical 
specimens were used for high-temperature compression experiments at 
1000 ℃ with a strain rate of 1 × 10
 3
/s.
Fig. 1 shows the XRD patterns and the EBSD phase maps of the Ta0.5 
and Ta1 alloys. BCC and HCP phases are detected in Ta0.5 and Ta1 al -
loys. Quantitative calculations based on the EBSD phase map ( Fig. 1 b) 
reveal that the volume fraction of BCC and HCP phases in the Ta0.5 alloy 
account for 72.6 % and 27.4 %, while in Ta1 alloy make up 87.5 % and 
12.5 %. Ta substitution for Mo improves the BCC phase volume fraction 
by 20.5 % and reduced the HCP phase volume fraction by 54.4 %.

TEM analysis is used to investigate the crystal structure and fine

Oxidation at elevated temperature is essentially a complex mass 
transfer process involving chemical reactions. The oxidation rate may be 
controlled by diffusion, the interface reaction, or a mixture of both [10] . 
The oxidation behavior of alloys can be precisely described by several 
typical kinetic laws, including linear, parabolic and logarithmic

The cross-sectional SEM micrographs of Ta0.5 and Ta1 alloys after 
oxidation at 1000 ℃ for 5 h are shown in Fig. 5 . After oxidation, severe 
oxidation occurred in the Ta0.5 alloy, and the average thickness of the 
oxide layer increases sharply to 1657 μ m ( Supplementary Figure1a ). The 
interior of the oxide layer exhibits a lamellar loose and porous structure, 
accompanied by cracking. This is mainly caused by the evaporation of 
the oxidation product of Mo (MoO
3

replacing Mo with Ta, the oxide layer thickness of Ta1 alloy under the 
same conditions is significantly reduced, with the average thickness of 
only 77 μ m ( Supplementary Figure1b ). Compared to the Ta0.5 alloy, the 
Ta1 alloy has a significantly denser oxide layer.

adopting a combined approach of machine learning and 
high-throughput methods to achieve the integration of various excellent 
properties in alloys.
In summary, by replacing Mo with Ta, AlNbTaTiZr (Ta1) alloy with a 
combination of better high-temperature oxidation resistance and high- 
temperature strength was prepared by L-DED. The Ta1 alloy has a 
thinner oxide layer and a more complete and continuous oxide scale, 
which results in slower oxidation rate and better high-temperature 
oxidation resistance. Meanwhile the Ta1 alloy maintains superior 
strength at 1000
◦
C comparable to those of Ta0.5 alloy due to the BCC/ 
B2 microstructure. This work provides a promising alternative for the 
subsequent simultaneous improvement of strength and oxidation resis -
tance at elevated temperatures of RHEAs.
