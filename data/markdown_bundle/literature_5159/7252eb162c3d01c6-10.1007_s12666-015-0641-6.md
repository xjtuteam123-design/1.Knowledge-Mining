Effect of Chromium on Microstructure and Mechanical
Properties of Nb–Si Hypoeutectic and Eutectic Alloys
B. Paira 1 • T. Kansabanik 1 • Krishanu Biswas 1 • R. Tewari 2
Received: 30 June 2015 / Accepted: 11 August 2015 / Published online: 25 September 2015
/C211 The Indian Institute of Metals - IIM 2015
Abstract The niobium silicide based alloys have been
found to be the best alternative over Ni-based superalloys
as they provide the balance between both ductility and
fracture toughness at low temperature and strength and
creep resistance at elevated temperature. The concept of
making Nb-silicide based alloys is to form hard silicide
phase which is thermodynamically stable and imparts
adequate strength to the material at high temperature,
within the primary ductile solid solution phase ‘of niobium.
However, the main concern of using these alloys at high
temperature is their low oxidation resistance and strength at
high temperature. To overcome these limitations, alloying
elements can be added, which will improve mechanical
properties as well as high temperature oxidation resistance
of the alloys. Our present investigation is to study the effect
of chromium on the microstructure of Nb–Si hypoeutectic
and eutectic alloys. Cylindrical rods of pre-set composition
are synthesized by vacuum-arc-melting-cum-suction cast-
ing using argon atmosphere. The binary hypoeutectic
alloys show the presence of primary (Nb) ss dendrites and
lamellar eutectic between (Nb)ss and Nb 3Si whereas pri-
mary Nb3Si along with eutectic (Nb)ss/Nb 3Si are observed
in the eutectic binary alloy. In the hypoeutectic Nb–Si–Cr
ternary alloys, primary dendritic (Nb) ss, silicide (Nb 3Si
and/b-Nb5Si3), Laves (Cr 2Nb)(depends on chromium con-
centration) and their eutectic phases are observed whereas
Laves phase is found in the microstructure of eutectic Nb–
Si–Cr ternary alloys even at very low concentration of Cr
(2 at.%). At room temperature, compressive properties of
these rods have been reported.

1 Introduction
In recent times, there has been remarkable activities on the
development of high and ultra high temperature materials
([1200 /C176 C) for various applications. This includes devel-
opment of thrust cones, turbine blades, gas turbine engines,
heat pipe applications in compact nuclear reactors and
spacecrafts [ 1–4]. These applications require a material
which can perform better than conventional Ni-based
superalloys. In this regard, Nb-based Nb–Si in situ com-
posites have been found to exhibit potentials for such
applications. For high temperature ( [1700 /C176 C) applica-
tions, alloys of refractory metals of group V and VI (Nb,
W, Cr, Ta, Mo, V etc.) are now considered as promising
materials. Nb–Si alloys exhibit formation of different sili-
cide phases, including Nb 5Si3 (melting tempera-
ture = 2515 /C176 C) and Nb 3Si (melting temperature =
1975 /C176 C), which exhibit better combination of high tem-
perature strength, low density (6.6–7.2 gm/cm 3) as com-
pared to Ni-based superalloys [ 5–7]. However the basic
problems lie in high temperature strength, ductility as well
as oxidation resistance [ 7–9]. Therefore, different alloying

When certain quantity of Cr is added to Nb–Si alloys, it is
likely that hard and brittle intermetallic phases will form.
Thus, it is always important to distribute these brittle
phases, such as Laves and silicides phases, within the
ductile matrix consisting of (Nb)
ss to obtain an optimum
balance between the strength and ductility. It is expected
that Cr addition will improve oxidation resistance by
forming different heat-resistance oxides [ 10], provided
optimum concentration of Si is present.
The main idea behind adding Cr, Al, Mo, Ti etc. to Nb–
Si alloys is to control the microstructure judiciously during
solidiﬁcation or process to obtain a good combination of
strength and ductility. Cr addition is expected to modify the
microstructure, especially the distribution of brittle phases.
Thus, a systematic study is needed to see the effect of
change in Cr concentration replacing Si in eutectic and to
hypoeutectic alloys. It is to be noted that, the balance of
properties can be achieved by controlling the concentration
of Si and Cr which will ultimately change the mechanical
properties. Mechanical properties of the alloys also depend
on the grain morphology of the microstructure which can
be controlled by controlling the cooling rates of the
solidiﬁcation process [11, 12].In the present work; we shall
discuss the microstructure evolution during suction casting
of hypoeutectic and eutectic Nb–Si–Cr alloys. In addition,
the room temperature compression test results of selected
alloys will also be discussed.
2 Experimental Method and Characterizations
Depending on the binary Nb–Si [13] and ternary Nb–Si–Cr
phase diagrams [ 14], compositions of the binary and tern-
ary alloy systems were chosen. The compositions of those
alloy systems are shown in Table 1. These in situ com-
posites of pre-set compositions were synthesized by suction
casting vacuum-arc-melting-cum casting technique under
high purity argon atmosphere. The as-cast alloys were
prepared using the high purity (99.9 %) elements. To
ensure compositional and chemical homogeneity, the as-
cast buttons were re-melted 4–5 times. Subsequently, the
as-cast cylinders were synthesized by novel arc melting
cum suction casting using the water cooled split copper
mould to obtain cylindrical rods of diameter ( u) = 5 mm.
Identiﬁcation of the phases were done using BRUKER,
D8 FOCUS X-ray diffractometer with Ni-ﬁltered CuK
a
(k = 0.154056 nm) radiation. The phase distribution and
compositional analysis of all the samples were carried out
using a Carl Zeiss EVO 50, scanning electron microscope
(SEM) coupled with an energy dispersive spectrometer
(EDS, INCA PENTA FET X3), operated at 20 kV. Room
temperature compression test of the cylindrical specimens
(aspect ratio = 1.5) were carried out using Instron 1195 at
a strain rate 10
-3s-1.

4 Conclusions
The following conclusions can be made from the present
investigation.
1. Laves phase Cr 2Nb is observed in the ternary hypo-
eutectic Nb 80Si20-xCrx (x = 10, 15) and eutectic
Nb81.3Si18.7-xCrx (x = 2, 6, 8.7, 10) alloys. No Laves
phase is found in the Nb 80Si15Cr5 alloy.
2. For the Nb 80Si20-xCrx (x = 5, 10, 15) alloys, a
bimodal eutectic is observed. Bimodal eutectic (Nb) ss/
Nb3Si and (Nb) ss/Nb5Si3 are witnessed in the Nb 80-
Si15Cr5 whereas bimodal eutectic (Nb) ss/Cr2Nb and
(Nb)ss/Nb5Si3 are found in Nb80Si20-xCrx (x = 10, 15)
alloys.
3. A well balanced room temperature compressive
strength and plasticity is obtained for Nb 81.3Si8.7Cr10
alloy among all the eutectic alloys.
4. With increase in the concentration of Cr to the
hypoeutectic Nb–Si binary alloys, strength increases
but plasticity decreases.
