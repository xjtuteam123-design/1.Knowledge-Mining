ABSTRACT 
Oxide dispersion strengthened tungsten (ODS-W) alloys are a class of important materials that widely used for 
high temperature applications due to their unique physical and mechanical properties. However, the insufficient 
ductility of ODS-W alloys at low or high temperatures limits their widespread application, especially as structural 
materials. It is always a great challenge to efficiently disperse oxide second phase nanoparticles within W matrix 
for achieving ODS-W alloy with excellent balance of strength and ductility. Here we report an innovative strategy 
for the fabrication of high-performance ODS-W alloy with strength of up to 1134 MPa and total elongation over 
30% at 700 
�
C. The innovative route involves the preparation of W – Y
2
O
3 
composite powder precursor via 
alkaline hydrothermal method and subsequent low-temperature sintering in hydrogen atmosphere. The internal 
flake structure with uniformly dispersed Y component and external coating of W component on this flake 
structure can be formed in alkaline hydrothermal product and then be passed on to the reduced W – Y
2
O
3 
com -
posite nanopowders. As a result, the distribution of second phase oxide particles in the low-temperature sintered 
alloys using these composite nanopowders as precursor is optimized significantly including an increase in the 
volume fraction of intragranular oxide particles and a decrease in the size of both intergranular and intragranular 
oxide particles. Besides, the average W grain size is also refined dramatically (410 nm). More importantly, the 
intragranular oxide particle / W matrix interface is regulated to be coherent relationship. The combined action of 
these microstructural factors is responsible for the significantly enhanced strength and ductility of ODS-W alloy. 
These results indicate that the newly developed alkaline hydrothermal method in our work is a promising way to 
fabricate high performance ODS tungsten based alloys. 
1. Introduction 
Tungsten, due to its excellent mechanical and physical properties, 
such as high thermal conductivity, high melting point, low tritium 
retention, good corrosion resistance as well as low thermal expansion 
under extreme environments [ 1 , 2 ], has a wide application in various 
fields [ 3 – 5 ]. Even so, the inherent brittleness of tungsten materials 
including low temperature brittleness, recrystallization brittleness and 
irradiation brittleness is always the bottleneck restricting its widespread 
application, especially in nuclear field [ 6 – 8 ]. Therefore, alleviating the 
brittleness and then improving the ductility of tungsten materials at 
room or high temperatures is an important development direction for 
high performance tungsten based alloys. 
The presence of interstitial impurity elements such as O and N in 
tungsten matrix (bcc structure) is the main reason leading to its low 
temperature brittleness [ 9 – 11 ]. With low solubility in tungsten, these 
impurity elements tend to form corresponding compounds at grain 
boundary, resulting in the decrease in connection strength between 
grains and the generation of stress concentration at grain boundary [ 12 ]. 
Therefore, tailoring the grain boundary and then mitigating the 
enrichment of impurity elements at grain boundary is an effective 
method to improve the ductility of tungsten materials. Recently, grain 
refinement and hot-working treatment (forging or rolling and so on) are 
two main approaches to achieve this goal [ 13 ].

components, the working temperature for tungsten materials is

Recently, the composite powder precursors for sintering ODS-W al -
loys are prepared mainly by mechanical alloying and wet chemical 
method [ 20 – 23 ]. However, the oxide second phase particles are larger 
or uneven in size and tend to be aggregate in composite powder. During 
subsequent sintering, these oxide particles coarsen and aggregate easily 
at W grain boundaries so that they usually have a relatively large size

tion can be introduced around these particles, thus they are generally 
the initiation position for crack generation during loading. Therefore, 
the improvement effect of rare earth oxides on the properties of tungsten 
based alloys, especially the ductile-brittle transition temperature 
(DBTT), is weakened greatly. Based on above reasons, reducing the size 
and volume fraction of oxide second phase particles distributed at grain 
boundary may be an effective method to suppress intergranular fracture. 
Given a certain amount of oxide particles, this means the volume frac -
tion of oxide second phase particles dispersed within tungsten grain is 
increased. Dislocations can be generated, pinned down and accumulated 
by these intragranular particles, giving rise to pronounced work hard -
ening during plastic deformation. As a result, the strength and ductility 
of tungsten based alloys are further improved. Therefore, the distribu -
tion of oxide second phase particles seem to be a critical factor for the 
preparation of high performance ODS-W alloys.

4. Discussion 
4.1. The understanding of strengthening and toughening mechanism based 
on the sintering microstructure 
As is well known, the grain microstructure of W matrix and the 
distribution of oxide second phase particles in W matrix are the two most 
important factors that determine the mechanical properties of ODS-W 
alloy. Next, analysis will be carried out from these two perspectives. 
Fig. 2 b and f shows the intergranular oxide particles (indicated by white 
arrows) in sintered 3W and 10W alloy, respectively, and Fig. 2 c and g 
shows the intragranular oxide particles (indicated by red arrows) in 
sintered 3W and 10W alloy, respectively. The particle size and volume 
fraction of both intergranular and intragranular particles were analysed 
statistically based on about 300 intergranular and 500 intragranular 
oxide particles. As a result, it was found that the improved dispersion of 
second phase oxide particles, that is, an increase in the volume fraction 
of intragranular oxide nanoparticles (from 6.7% to 35.4%, see Table 2 ) 
and a decrease in the size of intergranular particles (from 420 nm to 105 
nm, see Table 2 ), is an important microstructural feature of the sintered 
10W alloy. Besides, the average size of intragranular particles is also 
refined (about 40 nm, see Table 2 ). The migration of dislocation within 
the W matrix could be hindered by the second phase particles on one 
hand, and the dislocation loop around these particles on the other hand. 
According to the Orowan equation, the shear stress driving dislocation 
to move is directly proportional to the volume fraction of intragranular 
particles but inversely proportional to their size. Therefore, the 
strengthening effect of intragranular particles in 10W alloy is signifi -
cantly better than that in 3W alloy. Moreover, the volume fraction and 
size of intergranular oxide particles in sintered 10W alloy are reduced 
obviously, as discussed above. The strain concentration induced by 
deformation incompatibility between matrix and intergranular oxide 
particles is hence lowered, thereby alleviating the tendency for inter -
granular fracture and increasing the total elongation before failure [ 35 ]. 
Fig. 2 a and e show SEM images taken from the fractured surfaces formed 
at room temperature for sintered 3W and 10W alloys respectively, and 
about 300 W grains on these surfaces were selected randomly to 
calculate the grain average size of sintered microstructure. Compared 
with the sintered 3W alloy (580 nm), the ultrafine-grained microstruc -
ture (410 nm) is another important feature of sintered 10W alloy, which 
mainly benefits from the optimized oxide distribution in this alloy. 
During sintering process, the Zener drag exerted by intergranular oxide 
particle will impede the grain boundary migration to some extent. Ac -
cording to the Zener relation, the smaller the oxide size is, the stronger 
the pinning effect will be [ 36 ]. As described above, the intergranular 
oxide particles in sintered 10W alloy are refined significantly, thus 
numerous oxide particles can interact with grain boundary, leading to 
the refined grain microstructure of 10W alloy. Under load, the disloca -
tion source excited by external force can transfer series of dislocations to 
adjacent grain boundaries. With high energy, irregularly arranged 
atoms, the abundant grain boundaries introduced by refined grain will 
have a significantly improved capacity to pile up dislocations, which 
thus contributes to its high strength. The relationship between yield 
strength and grain size can be understood easily through Hall-Patch 
equation. Therefore, it seems that the strengthening effect of inter -
granular oxide particles is achieved via fine-grain strengthening indi -
rectly. Besides, the room temperature is well below the ductile brittle 
transition temperature (DBTT) of tungsten alloy (about 400 
�
C). 
Therefore, as observed from Fig. 2 a and e, brittle fracture, presenting in 
the form of intergranular fracture, is the dominant fracture mode for 
both 3W and 10W samples.

5. Conclusions 
In this work, an improved hydrothermal method under alkaline 
condition was successfully developed to synthesize high-quality com -
posite powder precursor for the sintering of high performance Y
2
O
3 
dispersion strengthened W alloy. This synthesis strategy can produce 
high-quality W – Y
2
O
3 
composite powder precursor with an unique flake 
structure with uniformly distributed Y component and an external 
coating of W on this flake structure. This process enables the acquisition 
of ultrafine W grains (410 nm) and optimized second phase oxide par -
ticles distribution (increase in the amount of intragranular oxide parti -
cles and decrease in size of both intergranular and intragranular oxide 
particles) after low-temperature sintering in hydrogen atmosphere. 
More importantly, the intragranular oxide particles / W matrix interface 
can be controlled to be coherent to a large extent. As a result, the as- 
obtained ODS-W alloy present simultaneously high strength (1134 
MPa) and superior elongation (30%) at high temperature. Furthermore, 
this unique strategy will shed light on the fabrication of other refractory 
materials with outstanding mechanical performances.
