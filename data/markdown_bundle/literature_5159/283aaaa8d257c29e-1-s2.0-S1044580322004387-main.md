ABSTRACT 
The precipitation hardening high-entropy alloys (HEAs) have attracted extensive research interest for their 
enhanced strength yet considerable ductility. In this study, the Al
0.5
CoCrFeNi HEA strengthened by nanoscale B2 
phases was obtained after aging at temperature of 800 
◦
C – 900 
◦
C. The evolution of the B2 precipitation and the 
mechanical properties of Al
0.5
CoCrFeNi alloy were investigated after different thermomechanical treatments. 
The volume fraction and the length – width ratio of the B2 phases in the alloy increase with the increase of the 
aging time. After aging at 850 
◦
C for 16 h, the yield strength of the alloy reaches 514 MPa via Orowan 
strengthening mechanism of the B2 phases. Additionally, the introduction of pre-deformation before aging 
promotes the formation of B2 phases, which results in strength enhancement of the alloy via precipitation 
strengthening and work hardening. 
1. Introduction 
High-entropy alloys (HEAs) are the potential materials for structural 
applications due to their special alloying principle and excellent me -
chanical properties [ 1 – 5 ]. They are composed of five or more elements 
with equiatomic or near-equiatomic proportions, which can increase the 
configurational entropy of mixing and promote the formation of a 
single-phase solid solution [ 6 , 7 ]. However, recent studies have shown 
that some secondary phases tend to precipitate in most HEAs when aging 
at moderate temperature [ 8 – 11 ]. For example, the σ phase precipitated 
in the CrMnFeCoNi alloy after aging at temperature of 700 
◦
C [ 12 ] 
suggesting that the entropic stability of the solid solution state in high- 
entropy alloys is limited. Remarkably, the formation of such secondary 
phases allows the design of precipitation strengthening alloys, which 
provides more options for the strengthening of high-entropy alloys 
[ 13 – 15 ]. 
Until now, tremendous researches have been carried out on precip -
itation strengthening HEAs, such as L1
2
, D022, and B2 phase strength -
ening HEAs [ 16 – 19 ]. Studies have demonstrated that coherent L1
2 
phase can be obtained by adding Al and Ti elements. As a kind of ductile 
precipitated phase, L1
2 
phase can significantly increase the alloy 
strength and ductility, which attracted a wide range of research interest 
[ 20 – 23 ]. The DO22 precipitation phase is usually produced by alloying 
Nb to form Ni
3
Nb-typed particles in the CoCrFeNi based alloy. The 
coherent DO22 phase with higher anti-phase boundary energy can 
achieve superior reinforcement compared with L1
2 
phase in FCC HEAs 
[ 16 ]. In addition to L1
2 
and DO22 precipitation, the semi-coherent NiAl- 
typed B2 phase has also attracted the attention of material researchers. 
Gwalani et al. have found that B2 phase precipitates around the grain 
boundaries in Al
0.3
CoCrFeNi alloy after aging at 700 
◦
C. And the 
improvement in yield strength due to B2 phase was only 56 MPa [ 19 ]. 
Also, the increase of direct B2 precipitation strengthening is only 28 MPa 
in Fe
42
Ni
12
Mn
36
Al
8
Ti
2 
alloy [ 24 ]. The strengthening effect of B2 phase is 
not obvious in HEAs. On the other hand, numerous studies have 
demonstrated that B2 phase can substantially increase the strength of 
traditional alloys, such as steels and superalloys [ 25 – 27 ]. This difference 
may be attributed to the lower volume fraction of the B2 phase in HEAs. 
Therefore, it is pivotal to increase the content of B2 phase in order to 
enhance its strengthening effect in the FCC HEAs. Additionally, the 
precipitation and strengthening behavior of B2 phase still need to be 
explored.

to obtain single FCC – B2 phases according to Al

3. Results and discussion 
3.1. Recrystallized microstructure of the alloy 
Fig. 1 shows the EBSD images of the CRSA Al
0.5
CoCrFeNi alloy. As 
can be seen from Fig. 1 (a) and (b), there are randomly oriented 
recrystallized grains except for a small number of deformed grains in the 
CRSA alloy. The recrystallized microstructure can be divided into two 
regions: coarse grain region and fine grain region. In addition, BCC 
phase is generally around the fine FCC grains as shown in Fig. 1 (c). The 
as-cast Al
0.5
CoCrFeNi alloy consists of coarse FCC grains and BCC phase 
at the grain boundaries, which was analyzed in our previous work [ 29 ]. 
During cold rolling process, the BCC phases are broken into small par -
ticles due to the existence of shear stress. After subsequent heat treat -
ment, the cracked BCC phase become heterogeneous nucleation cores 
which promote the occurrence of recrystallization and contribute to 
grain refinement.

4. Conclusions 
In the present study, the Al
0.5
CoCrFeNi alloy with high content of 
precipitated B2 phases was successfully obtained by adjustment of 
thermomechanical treatment process. The precipitation and strength -
ening behaviors of the B2 phases in Al
0.5
CoCrFeNi alloy were investi -
gated. The mean conclusions are as follows: 
• The nanoscale B2 phases precipitate in the Al
0.5
CoCrFeNi alloy after 
aging at 850 
◦
C, and the volume fraction and the length-width ratio 
of the B2 phases increase with the increase of aging time. As the B2 
phases nucleates on the specific crystal planes of the FCC matrix, the 
network-like morphology is produced after long time aging. 
• The formation of B2 phase plays a dazzling role on the mechanical 
properties of the alloy. With increasing volume fraction of B2 phase, 
the hardness and YTS of the Al
0.5
CoCrFeNi alloy increased signifi -
cantly via Orowan bypass mechanism of the nanoscale B2 phases. 
After aging for 16 h, the YTS of the alloy can reach 514 MPa with an 
excellent ductility of 26.2%.
