ABSTRACT 
Visual defect-free CoCrFeNiAl
0.3 
high entropy alloy butt joint was successfully obtained by using laser welding. 
The welded part shows great microstructural inhomogeneity, with average grain area increased from 14.67 μ m
2 
in based metal to 438.75 μ m
2 
in welded metal. At room temperature, the welded alloy displayed excellent 
mechanical property with the ultimate tensile strength up to 1150 MPa and ductility maintained 30%. Both the 
hardness and strength of the base metal are greater than that of the weld metal, which can be ascribed to the 
grain refinement. However, the tensile strength decreased to 50 MPa at 700 
◦
C. And the failure shifted to the base 
metal, indicating strong grain boundary weakening. 
1. Introduction 
High entropy alloys (HEAs) have great flexibility in material design 
and a series of unique advantages, such as high strength, excellent 
ductility and good resistance to irradiation and corrosion [1 – 3] . 
Recently, the addition of elements with large atomic radius, such as Al, 
Nb, Mo, V, Ti etc., has attracted much attention in research on HEAs [4] . 
As a strong body-centered cubic (BCC) forming element, the addition of 
Al element could make the matrix change from face-centered cubic 
(FCC) structure to BCC [5] . Besides, it promotes the formation of inco -
herent B2, σ phase and coherent L12 phase [6] in FCC matrix at medium 
temperature. Interestingly, these precipitates greatly improve the 
comprehensive mechanical properties of the HEAs due to the 
precipitation-hardening effect. Therefore, Al
x
CoCrFeNi have been the 
most systematically studied HEAs until now [7] . 
Considering potential applications of HEAs in a wide variety of in -
dustries, the development of effective joining technologies is one of the 
essential issues for HEAs. Accordingly, it is significant to have a clear 
understanding of the fundamental microstructural features in welded 
alloys and the resultant effects on the mechanics. Several studies on the 
weldability of equiatomic NiCoFeCrMn (Cantor ’ s alloy) were carried 
out, indicating good weldability of HEAs [8] . However, quite limited 
information for welding of CoCrFeNiAl
x 
alloy is available to date. As one 
typical representative, the CoCrFeNiAl
0.3 
alloy has excellent 
comprehensive properties [9] . Zhu et al. reported successful friction stir 
welding of CoCrFeNiAl
0.3 
alloy, showing that free of welding defects and 
the stir zone exhibited the highest hardness due to the grain refinement 
[10] . In comparison, laser beam welding has higher flexibility and 
welding efficiency. Nevertheless, it is easier for the low-melting-point Al 
element to segregate along grain boundaries, which will increase the 
crack susceptibility [11] . Thus, whether the CoCrFeNiAl
0.3 
alloy can be 
successfully welded by laser welding is not clear, and the effect of laser 
welding on the microstructure and mechanical properties of the welded 
joint need further investigation. 
In the current work, we aim to successfully weld the CoCrFeNiAl
0.3 
high-entropy alloy plates by fiber laser welding and investigate the in -
homogeneity of microstructure and mechanics of laser welded joints of 
CoCrFeNiAl0.3 alloy, as well as to discuss the relationship between the 
microstructure and mechanical properties. 
2. Material and methods

purity argon atmosphere. The raw materials were re-melted at least five 
times for chemical homogeneity. After homogenized at 1200 
◦
C for 24 h, 
the cast ingot was cold rolled to a 95% reduction to acquire a recrys -
tallization microstructure. The cold rolled plate with the thickness of 
1.0 mm was autogenously welded by laser beam welding in argon

3. Results and discussion 
The laser welded plate and optical micrograph of the cross-section of 
the joint are shown in Fig. 1 (a). Three zones can be distinguished in the 
optical micrographs including base metal, heat-affected zone (HAZ) and 
weld metal. The middle of the weld metal consists of oriented columnar 
dendrites and the grains are relatively coarse. The growth of dendrites 
has obvious directionality due to the cooling temperature gradient. The 
crystal structure of the base metal and welded metal alloy was charac -
terized by X-ray diffraction (XRD, shown in Fig. 1 (b)), 2 strong peaks 
corresponding to the FCC structure ((1 1 1), (2 2 0)) are observed, while 
(2 0 0) peak is missing. It can be ascribed to the preferred orientation 
induced by cold rolling [3] . Besides, minor σ peaks can be identified in 
the base metal, which is due to the phase instability during medium 
temperature annealing at 700 
◦
C [12] . The lattice parameter of the 
CoCrFeNiAl
0.3 
alloy is 3.5907 Å. As for the welded joint, no new phase 
was observed. And there are no obvious changes in FWHM (Full width at 
half maximum). To note that the diffraction peak of (2 0 0) is visible after 
welding. Generally, 〈 100 〉 is the preferred direction for grain growth in 
FCC alloy [8] , which may reasonably interpret the appearance of the 
(2 0 0) peak. 
A strong inhomogeneity of the grain size in the cross-section of the 
welded joint is observed in Fig. 2 (a). The base metal exhibited a quite 
fine and equiaxed crystals microstructure with the mean grain area of 
14.67 μ m
2
, with increasing distance approaching to the fusion line, the 
grain size increased obviously due to the influence of welding heat 
input. In the weld, it shows a coarse columnar crystal microstructure, 
which is quite different from that in the base metal and HAZ. The grain 
boundary characteristic map in Fig. 2 (b) shows that a large number of 
twin boundaries are in the base metal and HAZ, indicating a high strain 
hardenability and good ductility [13,14] . To further distinguish and 
determine the change of microstructure in the welded joint, HAZ was 
divided into three regions and their grain sizes were calculated respec -
tively. Results in Fig. 2 (c) show that the grains area grew up signifi -
cantly from 19.41 μ m
2 
in the HAZ 1 to 103.05 μ m
2 
in HAZ 3, then 
dramatically increased to 438.75 μ m
2 
in the weld metal. 
The microhardness distribution of the cross-section is displayed in 
Fig. 3 (a). A sharp decrease in microhardness occurred within the joint 
from the HAZ to the weld metal. The microhardness of the base metal is 
~ 450 HV, which is almost three times of the weld metal (175 HV). Fig. 3 
(b) shows the tensile properties of laser welded HEAs. At room tem -
perature, the welded samples fractured at the weld metal, however, the 
welded alloy displayed excellent mechanical property with the ultimate 
tensile strength up to 1150 MPa and ductility maintained 30%. This can 
be reasonably explained by the great differences in hardness lie between 
the base metal and welded metal, which could result in back-stress 
strengthening effect and consequently improve the mechanical proper -
ties to the welded alloy [15] . As the testing temperature increasing to 
700 
◦
C, the tensile strength of the laser welded joint was only 50 MPa 
and the sample fractured at base metal. This is quite different from the 
result under room temperature. As shown in Fig. 2 , great inhomogeneity 
in the microstructure of the welded joint occurred, especially the grain 
size. The base metal shows fine equiaxed grains, while the weld metal is 
coarse columnar crystal. The difference in size between the two regions 
of crystal is more than 20 times. Obviously, this would exert a great 
impact on the welded joint, resulting in the uneven distribution of me -
chanical properties. It is known from Hall-Petch relationship that the 
(yield) strength is inversely proportional to the square root of grain size. 
Grain refinement indicates stronger ability to resist dislocation 
movement. And grain boundary plays a crucial role in improving per -
formance. Therefore, the mechanical properties in Fig. 3 showed that 
the hardness of the base metal was much higher than that of the weld, 
and the tensile fracture did not occur in the base metal at 25 
◦
C. How -
ever, the grain boundary strength is weaker than intergranular strength 
at high temperature [16] . Due to the melting and re-solidification pro -
cess, the grains in the weld are usually coarse, which greatly reduces the 
number of grain boundaries and the probability of grain boundary 
weakening and cracking at high temperature. Therefore, the tensile 
fracture occurred in the fine-grained region of the base metal at 700 
◦
C. 
4. Conclusions 
CoCrFeNiAl0.3 high entropy alloy was successfully welded by laser 
welding technology. Inhomogeneity of microstructure and mechanics 
were obviously observed. Compared with the base metal, the grain area 
in weld metal increased for 30 times, from 14.67 μ m
2 
to 438.75 μ m
2
. At 
the meantime, both the room-temperature hardness and strength of the 
weld metal are less than that of the base metal, ascribed to the fine 
grains. It is worth noting that the grain refinement effect weakens with 
increasing temperature. The joint was fractured at based metal under 
700 
◦
C and the ultimate tensile strength decreased to 50 MPa, in contrast 
to room-temperature strength of 1150 MPa.
