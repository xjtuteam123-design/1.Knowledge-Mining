The microstructures, high-temperature mechanical properties, and fracture behavior of Mg–Gd–Y–Zr
alloy components produced by low-pressure sand casting with different Gd and Zr contents, have
been investigated. The ultimate tensile strength (U TS), tensile yield strength, and total elongation
(EL) were measured within the 25 –300 °C range. At the same temperatures, the UTS and yield
strength (YS) of the T6 treated Mg –xGd–3Y–0.5Zr alloys increased with Gd content increasing from
9 to 11%, which was attributed to the improvement of precipitation strengthening. Increasing the Zr
content from 0.3 to 0.5% leads to dramatic decrease in grain size and improved tensile properties of
T6 treated Mg–10Gd–3Y–yZr alloys which is considered to be due to grain-boundary strengthening.
With the increase of tensile temperature, both UTS and YS of the T6 treated Mg –xGd–3Y–yZr
alloys initially increase and then decrease. The b9 precipitates provide important strengthening
sources in experimental alloys, especially at elevated temperatures. The Mg –10Gd–3Y–0.5Zr alloy
shows good combination of strength and EL within the 25 –300 °C range.

II. EXPERIMENTAL DETAILS
A. Materials
The magnesium alloys were prepared in a mild steel
crucible in an electrical resistance furnace. Alloys with
nominal compositions Mg –xGd–3Y–0.5Zr ( x 5 9, 10,
and 11) (all compositions are in weight percentage unless
stated otherwise) and Mg –10Gd–3Y–yZr ( y 5 0.3, 0.4,
and 0.5) were prepared from high-purity magnesium
ingot ( $99.9%), and Mg –25%Y, Mg –25%Gd, and
Mg–30%Zr master alloys. The magnesium ingot was
melted in the steel crucible, and then Mg –25%Y and
Mg–25%Gd master alloys were added into the steel crucible
when the melt temperature reached 730 –750 °C followed
by manual stirring for two min. The melt was subsequently
held at 780 °C for 40 min to allow the RE to completely
dissolve. The preheated Mg –3 0 % Z rm a s t e ra l l o yw a s
introduced to the melt at 780–800 °C. After it was puri ﬁed
by 1.5% JDMJ ﬂux, the melt was then held at 780 °C for 40
min before casting. About 250 kg of the melt was poured
into the prepared no-bake resin sand mold using a low-
pressure casting system. The details of the casting system
and the casting shape were schematically shown in Fig. 1.
The casting was divided into eight regions, marked as B1 to
B4 and C1 to C4, and the corresponding ingate was placed
at region C4. The actual chemical composition was de-
termined by an inductively coupled plasma analyzer (ICP,
Plasma-400, Perkin Elmer Inc., Waltham, MA) and listed in
Table I. The component was solution treated at 525 °C for
12 h and cooled in air at 250 °C for 10 h, which was
denoted as sand-cast-T6 treatment. The samples of cast-on
bar for microstructure characterization observation and the
mechanical properties tests were all cut from the region (B4,
C2, and C3) of the T6 treated component.

B. Hardness and mechanical properties
Table II shows the variation of hardness with the Gd and
Zr contents. It is obvious that the hardness is slightly
increased with increasing Gd content. Mg–11Gd–3Y–0.5Zr
alloy has a hardness value of 111 HV, which is about 7 HV
higher than that of Mg –9Gd–3Y–0.5Zr alloy. The increase
of hardness is attributed to the increase in the number
density of ﬁne cuboid-shaped precipitates which distribute
within the matrix when Gd content is increased as shown in
Figs. 8(a)–8(c). In addition, the Zr content has a negligible
inﬂuence on hardness.
Figure 9 presents the mechanical properties of sand-
cast-T6 Mg–xGd–3Y–0.5Zr alloys at temperatures varying
from 25 to 300 °C. When the temperature is 25 °C and Gd
content is 9%, the ultimate tensile strength(UTS), YS, and
EL are 334 MPa, 227 MPa, and 3.4%, respectively. With
increasing the Gd content from 9 to 11%, the UTS and YS
increase gradually from 334 to 350 MPa and from 227 to
251 MPa, respectively, while the elongation (EL) is greatly
reduced from 3.4 to 1.8 %. When the test temperature is
between 100 and 150 °C, similar changes are observed in
the strength of Mg –xGd–3Y–0.5Zr alloys. Both UTS and
YS of 11% Gd alloy achieve the peaks 365 MPa and 265 MPa
at 150 °C, while the EL was greatly increased from 1.8%
at room temperature to 8.2%. When the test temperature
is 200 °C, the variation in Gd content from 9 to 11% does
not lead to a signi ﬁcantly change in UTS and YS. When
the temperature for tensile test is between 250 and
300 °C, both UTS and YS slightly decrease [Figs. 9(a)
and 9(b)]. However, compared with the test at 200 °C, the
EL increases gradually in the test temperature range of
250–300 °C.
With increasing Gd content from 9 to 11%, the
mechanical properties of T6 treated Mg –xGd–3Y–0.5Zr
alloys are signi ﬁcantly improved with the increase of
tensile temperature. The improvement of tensile property
is closely related to the precipitation strengthening of
Mg–xGd–3Y–0.5Zr alloys.

IV. CONCLUSIONS
In the present study, the microstructures and high-
temperature mechanical behavior of the T6 treated
Mg–xGd–3Y–0.5Zr ( x 5 9, 10, 11) and Mg –10Gd–3Y–
yZr ( y 5 0.3, 0.4, 0.5) alloys were investigated. The
conclusions are drawn as follows:
(1) With Gd content increasing from 9 to 11%, the UTS
and YS of the T6 treated Mg –xGd–3Y–0.5Zr alloys in-
creased at same test temperatures. The Mg –11Gd–3Y–
0.5Zr alloy exhibited higher UTS and YS values within
the 25 –300 °C range. However, the Mg –11Gd–3Y–0.5Zr
alloy exhibited a lower EL of 1.84 and 4.2% at 25 and
100 °C, respectively.
(2) The increase of Zr content from 0.3 to 0.5% leads to
dramatic grain reﬁnement, and improved the tensile prop-
erties of T6 treated Mg –10Gd–3Y–yZr alloys at same
tensile temperature. Meanwhile, the Mg –10Gd–3Y–0.5Zr
alloy exhibited highest UTS and YS at both room and
elevated temperatures.

fracture mode, and the fracture mode of T6-treated Mg –
xGd–3Y–yZr alloys is mainly transgranular cleavage
between 25 and 200 °C, while the typical microvoid
coalescence is observed between 250 and 300 °C.
(4) EL of both Mg –xGd–3Y–0.5Zr and Mg –10Gd–

(5) Mg –
