abstract
First-principles density functional theory has been used to evaluate the shear and cleavage strength in
terms of Grifﬁth work and generalized stacking fault energy (GSF) of (0 0 1) plane for c, c0 and c–c0 system
as a function of distance from the c/c0 interface. Calculation of Grifﬁth work suggests higher cleavage
energy for bulk c as compared to c0 while the GSF calculation suggests higher shear strength for bulk
c0 as compared to c. It has been found that the shear strength of the cubic plane of the c/c0 interface is
marginally lower than those of bulk c and c0 phases.

1. Introduction
Ni-based single-crystal (SC) superalloys are widely used as high
temperature structural materials because of their superior
mechanical strength and resistance to creep. An important
strengthening mechanism in these materials is the precipitation
hardening associated with the precipitation of ordered intermetal-
lic c0 phase (based on Ni 3Al) with L12 crystal structure in a Ni rich c
matrix with FCC crystal structure. The c/c0 interface is coherent and
due to a small lattice parameter mismatch between the matrix and
the precipitate phases, the system is elastically strained. The prop-
erties of the superalloys at higher temperature are found to depend
critically on the coherency of the c/c0 interface [1]. Even though the
technology of making superalloys has advanced over many dec-
ades through experimental trial and error methods, there is an
increasing focus on the modeling and simulation aspects, espe-
cially in the context of Integrated Computational Materials
Engineering (ICME) and Materials Genomic Initiative (MGI).
Numerous studies have been done using classical molecular
dynamics (MD) [2] and ﬁrst-principles using bond overlap popula-
tions (BOP) and Grifﬁth work [3–7] to investigate the strengthen-
ing effects of the c/c0 interface with and without alloying
elements in SC superalloys. Chen et al. [4–6] made use of the DMol
molecular orbital package, which employs the ﬁrst-principles
density functional theory (DFT), to calculate the electronic
structures and bonding characteristics of the c/c0 interface using
a cluster model. They determined the change of cohesive and shear
strengths in terms of charge density distribution and bond order
values with alloying, e.g. Re, Ru, S, etc., and calculated the ratio
of shear to cohesive strength and related it to the measure of
increasing embrittlement with increasing value of this ratio. Using
Vienna Ab-Initio Simulation Package (VASP), Peng et al . [7] have
investigated the effect of Re while Gong et al. [3] have investigated
the effect of other alloying elements in 3d, 4d and 5d transition
metals on cohesive strength in terms of Grifﬁth work of
c/c0 inter-
face. Generalized stacking fault (GSF) energies or C-surface have
been used to determine the stacking fault energy of Ni nanoﬁlms
[8] and of Ni 3Al [9] as a function of alloying elements. C-surface,
ﬁrst introduced by Vitek [10], is the energy proﬁle for sliding one
half of crystal over the other. Rice [11] introduced the unstable
stacking fault energy ( Cus) which is obtained from the C-surface
and this parameter is a measure of the energy release rate for dis-
location nucleation relevant to ductile response of the material.
Thus, the previous investigations of the c/c0 interface are mostly
related to the calculation of Grifﬁth work of rupture to determine
the strengthening effect of alloying elements or dopants on the
c–c0 system. To the best of our knowledge, there is no report on
systematic study of the strengthening effect in terms of cohesive
and shear strength as a function of distance from the c/c0 interface
towards the bulk of both c and c0. For the ﬁrst time C-surface has
been used to estimate Cus for the cubic plane of the c–c0 system.
While the close packed {1 1 1} octahedral plane in the FCC
materials is the preferred slip plane, in the c0 phase cross slip of
dislocations from the {1 1 1} planes to the {0 0 1} planes is the root

the interfacial effects and their range in Ni-base superalloys.
2. Computational details and methods
Our ﬁrst-principles calculations are based on DFT with a Plane
Wave Self Consistent Field (PWSCF) approach, as implemented
through Quantum ESPRESSO package [12]. Plane wave basis with
an energy cutoff of 25 Ry is used to represent the wave functions
and that with a cutoff of 200 Ry for the density. Ultrasoft (US)
pseudopotentials [13] are used with a generalized gradient
approximation (GGA) for the electron correlation and exchange
energy with the Perdew–Burke–Ernzerhof (PBE) function [14].
Sampling of the irreducible wedge of the Brillouin zone is done
by adopting Monkhorst–Pack scheme [15] to produce a uniform
grid of k-points with resolution depending on the size of the super-
cell used in the calculations to give convergent results. Hellman-
Feynman forces and stresses on atoms are used to determine opti-
mal atomic positions, through minimization of total energy with
the Broyden–Fletcher–Goldfarb–Shanno (BFGS) scheme. Conver-
gence is assumed when either the difference in total energy
between successive self-consistent cycles is less than 10 /C0 5 eV or
the average force in the system is less than 0.02 eV/Å.
A c–c0 system is simulated using a supercell of 1 /C2 1 /C2 10 cubic
unit cells (40 atoms corresponding to 20 layers with 2 atoms per
layer) constructed along the [0 0 1] direction ( Fig. 1 ). c/c0 interface
is considered as (0 0 2) c||(0 0 1)c0 plane based on the fact that the
{0 0 2} interface plane is coherent between the c-Ni and c0-Ni3Al
blocks [1]. The supercell is divided by the interface with onehalf
consisting of consecutive layers of Ni atoms (corresponding to c
phase) and the other half with alternating layers of pure Ni and
50% Ni – 50% Al (corresponding to
c0-Ni3Al phase) ( Fig. 1 ). Seven
different conﬁgurations are considered for the estimation of Grif-
ﬁth work and the C-surface, and are designated based on the loca-
tion of the plane of cleavage or slip relative to the c/c0 interface. For
example, 00_Ni represents the interface plane, while 01_Ni, 02_Ni,
03_Ni represent various planes within c and 01_Ni 3Al, 02_Ni 3Al,
03_Ni3Al for those within c0 (Fig. 1 ), 01_Ni refers to the ﬁrst plane
from the interface on the c side. Calculations have been performed
by considering nickel atoms in both ferromagnetic (spin-polarized)
and paramagnetic (non-spin-polarized) states.
For benchmarking, the equilibrium lattice parameters ( ao) of the
bulk c, bulk c0 and c–c0 system are determined by minimizing the
energy for different lattice parameters ( Table 1). While calculating
the lattice parameter of c–c0 supercell, the starting lattice parame-
ter of the c and c0 phases are considered to be equal because of the
assumption of complete coherency between the phases. Accord-
ingly the lattice parameter for c–c0 is calculated using a system
made of 20 layers (10 layers of c and c0) with 2 atoms per layer.
Values of ao of the bulk structures estimated with the present
methodology ( Table 1 ) agree well with experimental values and
other ﬁrst-principles calculations within the acceptable errors of
DFT.
3. Simulation of cleavage in c, c0 and c–c0 systems
We calculate the Grifﬁth work of rupture ( Gc) to determine the
tensile cleavage strength of the bulk c, bulk c0 and c–c0 system. The
Gc is deﬁned as the energy needed to cleave a crystal about a plane
[3]. Within the DFT framework, Gc can be calculated using
the simple model of ideal brittle cleavage between two planes of
the crystal which are separated by a distance xd + x (sufﬁciently
large such that the interaction between the interfaces vanishes)
i.e. cleaving the crystal into two semi-inﬁnite parts ( Fig. 2(a)). Here
xd is the equilibrium interplanar separation. The total energy of the
system ( Etot(x)), with increasing separation x, is obtained from
ab-initio calculations ( Fig. 2 (b)). Gc is calculated using

cleaved interface are too small beyond this separation. Thus, in
the present study, xm is taken to be equal to 8 Å.

3.1. Cleavage study for (0 0 1) plane of c–c0 system
To investigate the cleavage strength, we ﬁrst determine Gc of
bulk c, bulk c0 and c–c0 systems. Convergence with respect to sys-
tem size (slab thickness) is checked by calculating Gc for 8, 12 and
20 layered supercells for c and c0. It is observed that the change in
Gc is less than 1% with an increase in the supercell size from 8 to 20
layers in all systems, except in paramagnetic Ni where there is a
change of about 2%, which itself is not very signiﬁcant. In any case,
in this paper, values corresponding to 20 layers are reported. The
(0 0 1) cleavage strengths of bulk c and c0 are estimated to be
4.18 and 4.04 J/m 2 respectively, when Ni is considered to be ferro-
magnetic. Thus c has a marginally higher (3.5%) cleavage energy
than c0. When the spin-polarization is turned off, the cleavage
energy of the c and c0 phases are 4.29 and 4.07 J/m 2, respectively.
By cleaving across (0 0 1) plane, only Ni–Ni bonds are broken in
the c-phase, whereas both Ni–Ni and Ni–Al bonds are broken in
the c0-phase. Since c has higher cleavage energy than c0, it can be
inferred that Ni–Ni bonds are stronger than Ni–Al bonds under ten-
sile deformation along [0 0 1] direction. Calculated cleavage energy
for (0 0 1) for bulk c is in between the reported experimental value
of 4.56 J/m 2 and the simulated classical MD value of 3.86 J/m 2 [2].
However, for the bulk c0 our value deviates by about /C0 43% from
another ﬁrst-principles calculation by Yoo and Fu [19]. This dis-
crepancy can mostly be due to (1) the use of LDA based pseudopo-
tential by Yoo and Fu as compared to GGA in the present study and
(2) the fact that no relaxation has been carried out by them. Yoo
and Fu [19] have calculated the bulk lattice parameter for c0 to
be 3.49 Å as compared to our result of 3.58 Å, which is closer to
the experimental value of 3.57 Å. Since their calculated lattice
parameter is less compared to our results, their system may be
tightly bound and it requires more energy to cleave such a system.
For the c–c0 system, we expect variation in the cleavage energy
depending on the location of the cleavage plane ( Fig. 4 ) with
respect to the c/c0 interface because of the difference in chemical
environment at different planes. In Fig. 4 , results corresponding
to both ferromagnetic and paramagnetic of Ni are presented.
While, in general, the paramagnetic system has higher cleavage
energy than the ferromagnetic system, the effect is more pro-
nounced in the c side than the c0 side with a maximum deviation
of about 5.7% at the interface. The interface is found to have the
least cleavage energy irrespective of whether spin-polarization is
turned on or off for Ni. It can be seen that c has marginally higher
cleavage energy than the c0 phase and the variations within the c
and c0 phases are weak. In the case of paramagnetic Ni, cleavage
strength of interface is comparable to that of the c0 phase. An impli-
cation of this result is that damage through creep cavitations in
single crystal alloy loaded along h001 i is more likely to happen
at the c/c0 interface at room temperature (where Ni is ferromag-
netic), whereas at high temperatures, it is equally likely to happen
within the c0 phase. The cleavage energy of the c/c0 interface is
4.11 J/m2, which is in very good agreement with earlier ﬁrst-prin-
ciples estimates of 4.05 J/m 2 [3], 4.22 J/m 2 [18] and 4.3 J/m 2 [20].
Also, the cleavage energy for 01_Ni is found to be 4.34 J/m 2
compared to the reported value (ﬁrst-principles calculations) of
4.4 J/m
2 [20]. At about three planes away on either side from the
interface, the cleavage energy of the c–c0 system converges to their
respective bulk values. Thus, our results suggest that the effect of
the interface on the cleavage strength is not appreciable beyond
a distance of 4–5 Å on either side of the c/c0 interface.
4. C-surface for c, c0 and c–c0 systems
Vitek introduced the concept of GSF energy or C-surface rele-
vant to dislocations and related aspects of ductility of a material
[10]. C-surface is deﬁned as the energy or work done to slide
one half of a crystal with respect to the other half by a shift s par-
allel to the sliding plane. In our calculations, ﬁve different fault
vectors s = {(0.2, 0.0), (0.4, 0.0), (0.2, 0.2), (0.4, 0.2) and (0.4, 0.4)}
in crystal coordinates are considered on the (0 0 1) plane. The sys-
tem is subjected to rigid slips by these vectors, and atoms are
allowed to relax only along [0 0 1] direction. The GSF energy for a
particular s is the increase in energy for the translated system with
respect to the perfect non-translated system normalized by the
area of the supercell in the plane on which translation is effected
(in the present case (0 0 1) plane). Since C-surface is periodic and
calculated at discrete points, Fourier transform is used to obtain
the interpolated values, and hence the entire C-surface. Rice [11]
proposed the ﬁrst general model for modeling ductile materials
through the emission of the dislocations from the crack tip based
on the Peierls stress concept. In this analysis, an important
parameter called unstable stacking fault energy ( Cus) controls the

Our results show that (a) Cus for a slip at the interface is about

the barrier energy decreases for the rest of the sliding planes away

magnetization of Ni is considered). This result suggests that the
effect of the interface is felt around 4–5 Å towards c0-side while
it is a bit farther on the c-side.
5. Discussion and conclusions
We have used DFT based ﬁrst-principles calculations to carry
out a systematic analysis of the cleavage energy or the Grifﬁth
work of rupture and Cus from the C-surface of for pure c, c0 and
c–c0 in Ni-based superalloys. We ﬁnd an appreciable variation in
the cleavage energy and the Cus in the vicinity of the c/c0 interface.
Our inferences are summarized as follows:

1. In order to understand the interaction across the interfaces,
energy of the system as a function of separation between the
cleaved surfaces was analyzed. We have shown that there is
an appreciable interaction between the surfaces up to a separa-
tion of 1.59 Å (i.e. the system is able to accommodate the sepa-
ration, as strain, within the system), beyond which the provided
gap between the adjacent planes appear as physical separation.
The cleavage energies for bulk c and c0 are 4.18 and 4.04 J/m 2
respectively, suggesting that bulk c exhibits slightly higher
cohesive strength than the ordered c0.
2. From the analysis of cleavage strength for c–c0 system for vari-
ous planes on either side of the c/c0 interface, we establish that
there is a sharp variation of cleavage energy across the interface
and demonstrating that the matrix phase is stronger as com-
pared to the precipitates under tensile deformation. The calcu-
lated cleavage energy for planes away from the interface
converges to the bulk values of
c and c0. Thus, the effect of
the c/c0 interface on the cleavage strength is not appreciable
beyond a distance of 4–5 Å from the interface.
3. Calculated Cus for bulk c and c0 system for (0 0 1) plane suggests
that Cus for bulk c0 is around 7% higher than that for bulk c and
hence it is difﬁcult to shear c0 than c on (0 0 1).
4. Cus estimated for various planes of 20 layer c–c0 system on
either side of the interface shows that Cus right at the interface
is around 6–8% lower than the immediate plane on either sides.
Generally, Cus on planes in the c side of the interface is lower
than that in c0 . Thus, for slip on (0 0 1), the interfacial plane or
three-four layers towards c side of the interface are preferable
locations. Convergence of Cus to bulk values suggests that the
effect of the interface is around 4–5 Å towards c0 while it is even
further for c.
5. When the magnetization of Ni is included in the calculations,
the cleavage decreases whereas the unstable stacking fault
energy increases.
