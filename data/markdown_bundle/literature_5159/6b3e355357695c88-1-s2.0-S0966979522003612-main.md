1. Introduction 
As a new type of structural materials, high-entropy alloys (HEAs) 
[ 1 – 3 ] have subverted the traditional design concept of single principal 
component alloys, and rapidly become the focus of attention. The high 
strength, high hardness, good wear resistance, corrosion resistance and 
other prominent comprehensive properties make them unique in a va -
riety of metal materials [ 4 , 5 ], and widely used in aerospace, nuclear 
reactors, pressure vessels and other cardinal fields. 
Face-centered-cubic (fcc) HEAs have been extensively studied as a 
result of their superior toughness and eminent work hardening ability 
[ 6 , 7 ]. However, the natural characteristics of low strength determined 
by their crystal structures are not satisfactory in practical engineering 
applications [ 8 ]. Surprisingly, the introduction of L1
2
-type coherent 
precipitates in fcc matrix shows an effective strengthening effect and 
have achieved the desired performance [ 9 , 10 ]. The L1
2
-(Ni,Co)
3
(Al,Ti)

2.2 GPa and a uniform elongation of 13%, and the combined properties 
are superior to existing fcc or body-centered-cubic (bcc) 
HEAs/medium-entropy alloys (MEAs) [ 11 ]. Similarly, the Co

Especially at 77 K, the yield strength of the alloy is 2.02 GPa, the ulti -

and plastic loss. Encouragingly, the coherent interfaces between the 
matrix and L1

role in enhancing ductility [ 14 , 15 ]. 
In this work, coherent L1

small amount of Ti element to the CoCrFeNi matrix. The tensile prop -

temperatures are scrutinized, compared and evaluated by combining 
experiments with theoretical calculation. The present results provide 
guidance for further optimizing the mechanical properties of alloys by 
introducing coherent precipitates.

3. Results 
3.1. Microstructure analyses 
The XRD patterns obtained from the homogenized and aged samples 
are presented in Fig. 1 (a). The homogenized samples are single fcc 
phases. In contrast, additional diffraction peaks of L1
2 
phases appeared 
in the aged samples, indicating the formation of the second phases, 
which are consistent with the results predicted in the previous equilib -
rium simulation phase diagram [ 16 ]. Fig. 1 (b) shows the OM images of 
the homogenized and aged samples, which are all uniform equiaxed 
crystal microstructures. The grains of the homogenized samples are 
relatively coarse, and the average grain size is about 135 μ m. The grains 
of the samples aged for 10 min and 1 h are slightly refined, and both 
grain sizes are within the range of 20 – 100 μ m. When the aging time 
increased to 48 h, the grain size correspondingly increased to about 150 
μ m. In addition, a large number of annealing twins exist in these sam -
ples, suggesting that the stacking fault energy of the current HEA are 
relatively low. No precipitates are observed in OM images.

4.2. Deformation mechanisms 
In order to further clarify the micro-deformation mechanisms of the 
current HEA, the samples after tensile ruptures at 293 K and 77 K were 
analyzed in detail by TEM. Fig. 5(a–b) are the TEM images of the sam -
ples aged at 1173 K for 10 min at 293 K. Apparently, the deformation at 
293 K is dominated by dislocations, accompanied by a small amount of 
stacking faults. However, no deformation twins are found. In addition, 
Fig. 5(a) shows the high-density dislocation walls formed by dislocations 
along two slip directions, which are typical planar slip feature. The 
distance between adjacent dislocation walls is 100–400 nm. These 
dislocation walls hinder the movement of dislocations in them, causing 
dislocation pile up and promoting the successive work hardening. At 77 
K, the density of stacking faults in the current HEA increases greatly, as 
shown in Fig. 5(c), which is due to the corresponding decrease of 
stacking fault energy when the temperature decreases. For instance, for 
the single-phase FeCoNiCr HEA, when the temperature drops from 293 K 
to 77 K, the stacking fault energy decreases from 32.5 mJ/m
2 
to 13 mJ/ 
m
2 
correspondingly [28]. As shown in Fig. 5(d), a large number of 
stacking faults are generated and multiplied in plastic deformation at 77 
K, forming stacking fault networks. With the increase of strain, the 
distance between adjacent stacking faults decreases and the networks 
are refined. The distance between adjacent stacking faults is only 15 nm. 
It is these nano-spaced stacking fault networks that play a leading role in 
the work hardening stage of the alloy. It has been reported that the work 
hardening ability of stacking faults mainly depends on the distance be -
tween them [29]. The formation and refinement of the stacking fault 
networks can hinder the movement of dislocations by reducing the 
average free path, thus promoting the stacking of dislocations. Addi -
tionally, similar to the twinning-induced plasticity effect, these 
nano-spaced stacking fault networks can increase the work hardening 
response of the alloy through the dynamic Hall-Petch effect [30,31], so 
that the current HEA can undergo stable and durable uniform plastic 
deformation at low temperatures. 
As mentioned above, deformation twins should be formed when 
stacking fault energy is low. However, TEM results manifested that no 
deformation twins are found in the current HEA both at 293 K and 77 K. 
The inhibition of deformation twins is mainly due to two reasons. On the 
one hand, L1
2
-type precipitates have high stacking fault energy. The 
stacking fault energy of Ni
3
Ti precipitates is about 468 mJ/m
2
, much 
higher than that of fcc matrix, which leads to a marked increase in the 
critical stress for twins formation [32]. On the other hand, fine 
nano-precipitates will hinder the formation of twins [33]. For the cur -
rent HEA, the channel width in the matrix is reduced to only 15 nm due 
to a large number of dispersed L1
2 
particles, which makes the transition 
from stacking faults to twins more difficult. So, the plastic deformation 
of the current HEA is dominated by stacking faults rather than twins at 
77 K. It should be noted that the crystal structure type of precipitates is 
also important for the formation of twins. The precipitates with coherent 
interfaces with the matrix, such as L1
2 
phases in this study, is not 
conducive to twins nucleation. An analogous phenomenon has been 
revealed previously [11,34]. On the contrary, twins can still be formed 
in the matrix strengthened by incoherent precipitation [35,36]. 
5. Conclusion 
In summary, an L1
2
-strengthened HEA has been developed, which 
present excellent tensile mechanical properties both at 293 K and 77 K. 
The conclusions are summarized as follows. 
(1) The homogenized alloy are single-phase fcc structures. After 
aging at 1173 K, lamellar precipitates and spherical L1
2 
particles 
appeared in the current HEA, which are coherent with the matrix. 
Both precipitates are enriched with Ni and Ti elements, and the 
stoichiometry can be expressed as (Ni,Co)
3
Ti. 
(2) The current HEA aged at 1173 K for 10 min displayed eminent 
tensile mechanical properties both at 293 K and 77 K. At 293 K, 
the yield strength and ultimate tensile strength are about 650 
MPa and 1100 MPa, respectively, and the plastic strain is about 
35%. While at 77 K, the strength and plasticity are improved at 
the same time, the yield strength and ultimate tensile strength are 
about 900 MPa and 1500 MPa, respectively, and the elongation is 
markedly increased to 50%, realizing the most excellent strength- 
plasticity synergy. 
(3) The precipitation strengthening of L1
2 
nano-particles is the main 
source of strengthening at 293 K and 77 K. The deformation 
mechanisms of the current HEA are different at two temperatures. 
At 293 K, dislocation slip dominates plastic deformation, and 
only a few stacking faults occurred in the alloy. At 77 K, the 
significantly increased stacking fault density results in an addi -
tional increase in the work hardening rate curve. 
(4) The precipitation of nanoscale L1
2 
particles is the reason for the 
suppression of twins at 77 K. The stacking fault energies of L1
2 
precipitates are relatively high, and the fine and dispersed L1
2 
particles greatly reduce the matrix channel. Under the combined 
action of these two aspects, the critical stress of twins formation is 
substantially increased.
