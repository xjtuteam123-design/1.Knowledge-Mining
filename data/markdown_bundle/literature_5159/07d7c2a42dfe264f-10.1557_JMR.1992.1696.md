Influence of processing on the microstructure and mechanical
properties of a NbAl3-base alloy
Mohan G. Hebsur
Sverdrup Technology Inc., LeRC Group, Brook Park, Ohio 44142
Ivan E. Locci, S. V. Raj, and Michael V. Nathal
NASA-Lewis Research Center, 21000 Brookpark Road, Cleveland, Ohio 44135
(Received 16 December 1991; accepted 25 March 1992)
A multiphase oxidation resistant composition (Nb-67Al-7Cr-0.5Y-0.25W) based
on NbAl 3 was prepared by both induction melting and rapid solidification processing
(RSP), followed by grinding to 75 /xm powder and consolidating by powder metallurgy
techniques (hot pressing, hot isostatic pressing, and Ceracon pressing). Constant strain
rate compression tests conducted on consolidated materials in the temperature range
300-1300 K indicated that the RSP material exhibited significantly higher strength and
ductility than the induction melted alloy up to 1200 K. Bend strengths measured on
induction melted material were significantly lower than the corresponding compressive
strengths, suggesting the brittle, flaw-sensitive nature of this alloy. The NbAlCrYW
alloy exhibits a brittle-to-ductile transition around 1000 K. The constant load creep
tests conducted on the induction melted alloy in the 1200-1300 K temperature range
indicated that this alloy shows a power law creep dependency with a stress exponent,
n, of 3.2. It was found that the specific strength of this alloy is competitive with other
aluminide intermetallics.
I. INTRODUCTION
The intermetallic compound NbAl 3 is an attractive
material for advanced aerospace structural applications
because of its favorable combination of high melting
temperature (>1873 K), low density (4.5 kg/m 3), and
low coefficient of thermal expansion (10.5 x 10~ 6 K" 1).
However, NbAl 3 is extremely brittle and does not pos-
sess good oxidation resistance. The overall objective
of this work is to understand and develop low density
NbAl3-base alloys with greatly improved oxidation re-
sistance to serve eventually as matrices for structural
composite applications in advanced air breathing engines
at temperatures up to 1500 K.
Although NbAl 3 has the lowest oxidation rate among
the binary Nb-Al alloys, it does not form exclusive lay-
ers of protective A1 2O3 scales. 1 Recently, Perkins et al. 2
have shown the feasibility of forming alumina scales
on Nb-Al alloys at greatly reduced Al contents. Hebsur
et al.
3 were successful in maintaining the high aluminum
content and therefore low density, while achieving the
capability of growing protective A1 2O3 scales by a
ternary addition of 7 at. % Cr to NbAl 3. A quaternary
addition of 0.5 at. % Y to Nb-Al-Cr promoted oxide
adherence, reduced scale growth rates, and eliminated
convolutions within the oxides.
3"5
An addition of 0.25 at. % W to the NbAlCrY alloy
appeared to be beneficial to strength and processibility
while not deleteriously affecting the oxidation behavior.
The isothermal oxide growth kinetics on a NbAlCrYW
alloy4 are the same as those of conventional A1 2O3 form-
ing alloys. 5 This alloy also exhibited excellent cyclic
oxidation resistance at 1473 K, being nearly equiva-
lent to NiAl + 0.10 at. % Zr. 6 Cr and other additions
formed complex intermetallic phases along the grain
boundaries. 3"5
NbAl3-base alloys processed by conventional cast-
ing have a tendency to suffer from severe embrittlement,
segregation, and coarse grain structure, and therefore
have limited engineering properties. 7 Therefore, the ob-
jective of this investigation was to examine the influence
of various processing techniques on the microstructure
and mechanical properties of the oxidation resistant
NbAlCrYW alloy. In particular, rapid solidification pro-
cessing (RSP) was considered as a means to obtain
homogeneous, fine-grained material with a fine disper-
sion of grain boundary phases.

III. RESULTS
The experimental results consist of microstructures
and the mechanical properties of the NbAlCrYW alloys
processed by various techniques. The results are divided
into alloy microstructures from as-cast, rapidly solidified,
and consolidated alloys, and mechanical properties from
compression, bend, and high temperature compressive
creep tests of the consolidated alloys.

The minor grain boundary phase (a dark phase in

hexagonal phase of similar composition was observed

B. Deformation behavior
For specimens tested to failure below the ductile-
to-brittle transition temperature, there was no evidence
of slip traces or any other deformation marking, even
near the fracture surface. The brittle fracture was prob-
ably triggered by burst of intense localized deformation
such as coarse twins. A previous study has suggested
that twinning plays an important role in the plastic
deformation of NbAl
3.19 Similar suggestions have been
made for other D0 22 compounds such as Ni 3V and
Al3Ti.20'21 These twins appear to be difficult to generate
at low temperatures. At higher temperatures, in addition
to twinning, the dislocation mobility may increase due
to thermally activated slip, resulting in higher ductility.
This is supported by the onset of strain rate dependent
deformation behavior at about the same temperature as
the ductile-to-brittle transition.

From the present study, clearly the two major ob-
stacles limiting the use of this NbAlCrYW alloy as a
structural material are its brittleness at low temperature
and low strength at elevated temperature above 1200 K.
Although macro- and microalloying and processing may
lead to further improvements in the mechanical proper-

It appears that these properties will be dramatically
improved only by compositing with both ductile and
high strength/high aspect ratio reinforcing phases. Work
is in progress to examine the benefits of compositing the

V. SUMMARY
An oxidation resistant NbAlCrYW alloy was pre-
pared by various processing techniques to obtain a
fully dense, homogeneous, and fine-grained product.
Mechanical properties such as compression and bend
strength were evaluated. Microstructures of the as-cast

NbAlCrYW alloy indicated that Cr and other alloying
elements tend to form additional phases along the grain
boundaries. Rapid solidification processing of the alloy
resulted in improved homogeneity and a finer grain size,
and more finely distributed second phase particles. The
major second phase, AINbCr, which has been shown to
be beneficial to oxidation resistance, was also beneficial
for improving some mechanical properties. Compressive
strength and compressive ductility of the NbAlCrYW
alloy improved remarkably with a decrease in grain
size and an increase in Cr content. The NbAlCrYW
alloy exhibits a brittle-to-ductile transition temperature
around 1000 K, beyond which thermally activated dis-
location creep dominates the deformation. The specific
strength of this alloy is competitive with many aluminide
intermetallic alloys.
VI. CONCLUSION
Improved processing of an oxidation resistant
NbAl3-base alloy has resulted in a very fine-grained
and homogeneous microstructure with good compressive
properties. Further work on toughening concepts, and
exploration of other properties, is therefore warranted.
