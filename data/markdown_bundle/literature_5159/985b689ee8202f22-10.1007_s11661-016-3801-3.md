Mechanical Properties of the TiAl IRIS Alloy
THOMAS VOISIN, JEAN-PHILIPPE MONCHOUX, MARC THOMAS,
CHRISTOPHE DESHAYES, and ALAIN COURET
This paper presents a study of the mechanical properties at room and high temperature of the
boron and tungsten containing IRIS alloy (Ti-48Al-2W-0.08B at. pct). This alloy was densiﬁed
by Spark Plasma Sintering (SPS). The resultant microstructure consists of small lamellar
colonies surrounded by c regions containing B2 precipitates. Tensile tests are performed from
room temperature to 1273 K (1000 /C176C). Creep properties are determined at 973 K (700 /C176C)/
300 MPa, 1023 K (750 /C176C)/120 MPa, and 1023 K (750 /C176C)/200 MPa. The tensile strength and
the creep resistance at high temperature are found to be very high compared to the data reported
in the current literature while a plastic elongation of 1.6 pct is preserved at room temperature. A
grain size dependence of both ductility and strength is highlighted at room temperature. The
deformation mechanisms are studied by post-mortem analyses on deformed samples and by
in situ straining experiments, both performed in a transmission electron microscope. In
particular, a low mobility of non-screw segments of dislocations at room temperature and the
activation of a mixed-climb mechanism during creep have been identiﬁed. The mechanical
properties of this IRIS alloy processed by SPS are compared to those of other TiAl alloys
developed for high-temperature structural applications as well as to those of similar tungsten
containing alloys obtained by more conventional processing techniques. Finally, the relation-
ships between mechanical properties and microstructural features together with the elementary
deformation mechanisms are discussed.

I. INTRODUCTION
AFTER several decades of research, TiAl low-pres-
sure turbine (LPT) blades are now implemented in the
new generation of aircraft engines by several companies
such as the GEnX of G ENERAL ELECTRIC and the LEAP
of SNECMA-SAFRAN. Such LPT blades are produced with
the commercial 48-2-2 alloy (Ti-48Al-2Cr-2Nb) by
investment casting or ingot machining, resulting in a
duplex microstructure. The TiAl blade implementation
is at present limited to the last low-pressure stages of
these engines and a real breakthrough in the mechanical
properties is required for their introduction into hotter
stages. In the recent past, several competitive routes
have been investigated in order to develop both an alloy
composition having enhanced properties and a process
for manufacturing aero-engine components. This
includes for instance forging of TNM alloys
(Ti-(41-45)Al-4Nb-1Mo-0.1B at. pct) [1] or extrusion of
TNB alloys (Ti-(42-45)Al-(5-10)Nb-(0-0.5)B at. pct). [2]
More recently, the electron beam melting (EBM) tech-
nique associated with powder metallurgy is attracting
some interest for part manufacturing with a high
buy-to-ﬂy ratio. [3]
In this context, an alternative processing route is
currently explored by the CEMES institute, based on a
Powder Metallurgy (PM) approach with Spark Plasma
Sintering (SPS), thereby leading to texture-free alloys
with homogenous microstructures. [4] Using the so-called
IRIS alloy with the Ti-48Al-2W-0.08B composition, a
ﬁne near-lamellar microstructure has been obtained by
SPS processing in super-transus conditions, i.e., samples
were heated, during the SPS cycle, in the a + b ﬁeld,
above the a + b + c/a + b line transformation (one can
ﬁnd the phase diagram corresponding to the speciﬁc
composition of the IRIS alloy in the following Refer-
ences 5,6). Its as-densiﬁed microstructure consists of
small lamellar colonies, with a grain size of 35 to 45 lm,
surrounded by c borders containing b0 precipitates. The
width of the c border varies between 5 and 10 lm.[6] In
this paper, mechanical properties of the IRIS-SPS alloy
are studied in a wide temperature range. Complemen-
tary investigations are carried out on the microstructural
features and on the elementary deformation mechanisms
in order to establish some relationships with the
mechanical properties. The latter are evaluated with
respect to the industrial requirements for turbine appli-
cations, which are based on an acceptable room-temper-
ature ductility and a high creep resistance at service
temperatures.

AK503 ( T
Dwell = 1628 K (1355 /C176C)). Tensile curves

B. Microstructure and Mechanical Properties of the
as-SPSed IRIS Alloy
The ﬁne near-lamellar microstructure of the as-SPSed
IRIS alloy can guarantee an excellent compromise
between ductility at room temperature and strength at
high temperatures. Its good ductility results from several
factors. First, a small lamellar grain size is known to
improve the ductility, as reported by Liu et al .
[21] This
size is controlled by both incorporation of boron [35] and
the presence of b borders at high temperature resulting
from the heating of samples in the b + a ﬁeld.[6] A small
grain size limits the formation of dislocation pileups that
could lead to the failure of tested specimens through
elevated internal stresses. It also reduces the size of
cleavage facets that form along the lamellar interfaces as
evidenced in Figure 2(b). Second, the IRIS alloy with-
stands a certain amount of plastic deformation thanks
to the presence of c borders that are highly deformable.
Likewise, lamellar zones can also greatly deform owing
to the high proportion of c phase in lamellar zones,
unlike in the as-SPSed near-lamellar TNM alloy,
[34]
where the high amount of a2 phase, due to the lower
aluminum content, led to a plastic elongation below 1
pct.
The high strength of the IRIS alloy, at high temper-
atures, is related to the presence of tungsten atoms in the
c phase, as conﬁrmed by SEM-EDS. During creep, the
mixed-climb mechanism involves diﬀusion that is
impeded by such heavy atoms. The relatively large size
of the c areas (over a few micrometers), where disloca-
tions evolve, also contributes to the strengthening of the
material because of a low fraction of interfaces and
boundaries, which accelerate the diﬀusion process as
they contain more vacancies.
[36] At room temperature,
nano-clusters of tungsten atoms are probably the origin
of the unusual anchoring of the non-screw segments,
thus strengthening the alloy. It is noteworthy that these
strengthening mechanisms are by nature diﬀerent for
samples deformed at room temperature or at high
temperatures. Indeed, dislocations are locally pinned

onto extrinsic obstacles during room-temperature tensile
test whereas the intrinsic mobility of dislocations is
slowed down during creep. The very low deformation of
lamellar colonies also contribute to the high creep
properties of the alloy as well as the absence of primary
b phase in the microstructure, which is highly deform-
able at high temperatures.
As a conclusion, the three major microstructural
features that allow the as-SPSed IRIS alloy to reach a
good compromise between ductility and creep strength
are the optimized size of c borders, the proportion of c
phase in lamellar zones and the homogeneous distribu-
tion of tungsten atoms in the matrix which limits the
mobility of dislocations at high temperature.
We demonstrated here that combining Spark Plasma
Sintering with a TiAl alloy containing boron, tungsten,
and about 47 to 48 at. pct of aluminum was the key to
satisfy and greatly exceed the industrial requirements for
aero-engine turbine blade applications. The thermal and
pressure cycle was adapted to the speciﬁc chemical
composition of the IRIS alloy. In these conditions, SPS
allows to obtain a TiAl alloy exhibiting high mechanical
properties in one cycle, without any subsequent thermal
treatments. In addition, as a PM route, SPS provides a
high repeatability of mechanical properties thanks to the
homogeneity of the resulting microstructures and the
absence of texture. This could be a signiﬁcant advantage
given that the scattering properties inherent to cast/
extrusion routes
[37] still raises substantive problems.
Moreover, the control and optimization of both SPS
cycle and alloy chemical composition allows to obtain
high-performance material over a relatively wide range
of dwell temperatures (Figure 1). This is very important
in the case of sintering of components with complex
geometry as that enables the sintered components to
withstand important temperature gradients without
microstructural modiﬁcations, and thus with no impact
on the product quality.
VII. CONCLUSION
This work is aimed at investigating the mechanical
properties of the IRIS alloy (Ti-48Al-2W-0.08B) densi-
ﬁed by Spark Plasma Sintering. At room temperature, a
YS of about 500 MPa and a UTS of 600 MPa associated
with a plastic elongation of 1.6 pct have been achieved.
In comparison to the current literature, outstanding
creep properties at 973 K and 1023 K (700 /C176Ca n d
750 /C176C) have been obtained with this alloy.
The room-temperature deformation was governed by
both ordinary dislocation slip and twinning in the c
phase whereas, at high temperature, ordinary disloca-
tions moving by mixed climb were found. With respect
to conventional deformation mechanisms of TiAl alloys,
it is worth noting that the non-screw segments of
dislocations were also pinned at room temperature on
some nano-clusters segregating some tungsten atoms.
Likewise, the addition of tungsten slowed down the
dislocation mobility during creep. Lamellar colonies
were found to deform at room temperature, thus
enhancing the ductility, unlike at high temperature
under moderate stress, thus reducing the strain rate
during creep.
Finally, the c borders that surrounded the lamellar
colonies play a key role in the mechanical properties of
the as-SPSed IRIS alloy. On the one hand, c borders
participate to improve the ductility by both limiting the
lamellar grain size and providing more deformable
phase during the deformation at room temperature. On
the other hand, their optimized size is determinant for
limiting the diﬀusion process occurring at interfaces at
high temperature, thus contributing to the enhancement
of the creep resistance.
