ABSTRACT 
The high-temperature tensile and fatigue performance play a crucial role in the applications of repaired Ni-based 
superalloys by Laser directed energy deposition (LDED). This work investigates the effect of microstructure on 
the high temperature (650 
◦
C) mechanical behaviors of GH4169 superalloys repaired by LDED with the 
microstructure characterizations and crystal plasticity modelling. Results indicate that the solution and aging 
heat treatment (STA) is necessary to homogenize the distributions of precipitations. Microstructure character -
izations and crystal plasticity modelling imply that the plasticity deformations occur mainly in columnar den -
drites of fusion area and twin grains of substrate, which enhance the tensile strength and elongation rate of 
repaired area and demonstrates comparable characteristics with the casting alloy. At the same time, the existence 
of the Laves phase and carbide cannot eliminate the dynamic strain aging phenomenon of repaired material. 
Similarly, under high stress levels (850 – 950 MPa), the fatigue performance of repaired material is primarily 
influenced by the plastic deformation and exhibits similarity to that of the substrate; however, under low stress 
levels (650 – 750 MPa), it is primarily influenced by the presence of defects, resulting in an obviously longer 
fatigue life for the substrate. 
1. Introduction 
Ni-based superalloys GH4169 with good mechanical strength at high 
temperature has been widely used in the hot-end components of aircraft 
engines, rocket motors and gas turbine [ 1 , 2 ]. Due to its effectiveness and 
cost-efficiency, the restoration of the damage and defects of GH4169 
hot-end components through Laser directed energy deposition (LDED) 
have attracted more and more attention [ 3 – 7 ]. Similar to the IN718 
alloy, the GH4169 alloy is a γ’ -Ni

3. Results 
3.1. Microstructure and microhardness 
The unique grain morphologies in repaired area after heat treatment 
have been shown in the Fig. 2 (a), and the uniformly distributed equi -
axed grains exist in the matrix region, with grain sizes of repaired area 
are much smaller than the recrystallized grains in the heat-affected area. 
The repaired area contains columnar, cellular, and equiaxed dendritic, 
and the growth of columnar dendritic from the fusion line to the interior 
of the melt pool can be explained by the CET theory [ 33 – 35 ]. During the 
repair of the V-shaped groove using LDED, distinct boundaries exist 
between the tracks, which are referred to as overlapping lines. More 
importantly, no obvious thermal cracks were detected in the repaired 
sample. As shown in Fig. 2 (b – c), during the repair of the side of the V- 
shaped groove, changes in cooling conditions affected the direction of 
temperature gradients, causing the growth direction of columnar den -
dritic to no longer be directed towards the center of the melt pool. 
Additionally, due to the transitional changes in temperature gradients, 
cellular dendritic exist between columnar grains in different directions. 
Furthermore, the cooling rate during LDED deposition of GH4169 su -
peralloys is as high as 10
2
– 10
3 
K/s [ 36 – 38 ]. As a result, pores in the melt 
pool cannot completely escape, and pores are formed in the solidified 
alloy. Fig. 2 (a – d) shows the distribution of pores in the repaired V- 
shaped groove by LDED, the pores is mainly distributed in the fusion 
area. According to the Fig. 2 (d), the average pore quantity per sample is 
42 and the predominant range of pore diameters falls within 20 – 50 μ m, 
with the maximum pore size being < 80 μ m. The difference in grain size 
between the repaired area and the substrate, as well as the presence of 
pores in the repaired area, will significantly affect the mechanical 
properties of repaired materials. 
Fig. 3 shows the microstructure of repaired area with solution-aging 
treatment, and the Laves phase can be found in the inter dendritic re -
gion. The Laves phase is a brittle intermetallic phase, so the mechanical 
properties of repaired material can be improved by dissolving the Laves 
phase in the repaired area. The formation of Laves phase is result from 
the micro-segregation of niobium element when the V shape groove 
repaired by the LDED, and the long-striped or island-like Laves phase 
was identified by its representative morphology [ 39 ], which also can 
been found in the Fig. 3 (a). With the change of columnar dendrite 
growth direction, the distribution of long-striped Laves phase also is 
different. Some studies have found that the heat treatment or LDED 
process can significantly decrease the volume fraction of Laves phase 
[ 13 , 20 ]. As shown in the Fig. 3 (b), the long-striped Laves phase was 
dissolved into fine particles.

3.2. Tensile properties 
Tensile properties are the basic mechanical property of materials, as 
for the repaired V shape groove by LDED, it is necessary to investigate 
the room and high temperature tensile properties and behaviors. As 
shown in Fig. 5 , according to the analysis of the engineering stress-strain 
curves of repaired sample with room temperature (20 
◦
C) and high 
temperature (650 
◦

erties and plastic deformation mechanism between the repaired area 
and the substrate under two kinds temperatures. 
As can be seen from the Fig. 5 (a), both the repaired area and sub -
strate materials exhibit significantly higher tensile strength and elastic 
modulus at room temperature than at high temperature. This is because 
high temperatures promote atomic diffusion, which reduces the ability 
of alloy to resist deformation. The tensile strength of the substrate ma -
terial at room temperature is 255 MPa higher than that at high tem -
perature, while the tensile strength of the repaired sample at room 
temperature is 306 MPa higher than that at high temperature. The above 
phenomena may be result from the increase of the thermal activation 
energy at high temperature, and the variation of the tensile strength of 
repaired area and substrate materials with high temperature is similar to 
that of cast IN718 alloy. Meanwhile, the elastic modulus and tensile 
strength of both repaired area and substrate materials remain nearly 
constant, regardless of whether they are tested at room temperature or 
at a high temperature of high temperature. This consistency is in line 
with the previously discussed precipitation of strengthened phase. 
However, the two materials show a significant difference in elongation 
rate. At room temperature, the elongation rate of the repaired sample is 
only half that of the substrate. This is because the repaired area material 
contains brittle phases such as Laves phase and carbides. As a result, the 
material experiences less deformation from brittle phases when sub -
jected to tension, causing localized stress concentration and leading to a 
reduction in the elongation rate. It is worth noting that at high tem -
perature, the elongation rate of the two materials is almost identical and 
only slightly higher than the elongation rate of the repaired sample at 
room temperature. This means that the elongation rate of the substrate 
material will decrease significantly at high temperature, but the change 
in temperature has little effect on the elongation rate of the repaired 
material. 
More importantly, when repaired area and substrate were subjected 
to the tensile load of 2 mm/min with high temperature, the obvious 
Portevin-Le Chatelier (PLC) phenomenon was observed, also known as 
the Dynamic strain aging (DSA), as shown in Fig. 5 (a). The mechanism 
of DSA is attributed to the interaction of mobile dislocation with 
diffusing interstitial elements as well as substitutional solute atoms 
[ 43 , 44 ]. At the same time, owing to the unique microstructure of finely 
dispersed secondary phases and texture, the serrations completely ab -
sent in additive manufacturing IN625, and research shows that the DSA 
effect is significantly reduced by the closely spaced secondary phases, 
which preventing the critical carbon build-up needed for dislocation 
locking and unlocking [ 45 ]. However, the 650 
◦
C may be the critical 
temperature of the PLC effect occurrence of additive manufacturing 
IN718 [ 24 ], and this work also shows that the DAS can be found in 
repaired Ni-based superalloys by LDED easily under high temperature 
monotonic tensile. Fig. 5 (b) shows the true stress-strain curves at plastic 
range, which reflect the DAS features of two kinds materials, including 
serrations type, critical strains and stress change. In terms of the clas -
sifications [ 46 , 47 ], two kinds materials both contain Type B and Type C 
serrations, and the drop range of true stress exceed 100 MPa. Based on 
the analysis of true stress – strain curves, the DSA rules of repaired ma -
terial are similar to the substrate, which means the effect of secondary 
phase on the DSA phenomenon of repaired samples can be ignored.

4. Discussion 
Owing to the existence of repaired area, the high temperature tensile 
and fatigue behaviors of repaired samples are quite different from the 
conventional casting GH4169 superalloys. This work mainly pay 
attention to the deformation mechanism and damage process of grains 
on the subject of repaired samples under tensile test, and the fatigue 
crack growth process and microstructure evolution are deserved special 
attention on the subject of repaired samples under fatigue tests with the 
high stress level.

4.2. Fatigue behavior and deformation mechanism 
According to the general suggestions, the slip systems of FCC crystals 
which likely to be activated are commonly the {111} < 110 > and the 
{111} 〈 112 〉 under the fatigue damage process [ 11 , 58 ], and the Schmid 
factor (SF) distributions of grains can be used to evaluate the difficulty of 
grain slip of repaired materials. The SF distributions of repaired sample 
after cycle loading with 850 MPa stress level have been shown in Fig. 15 
(a-1, a-2), namely {111} 〈 110 〉 and {111} 〈 112 〉 , and the Fig. 15 (b-1, b- 
2) shows the SF distributions of repaired sample with 950 MPa stress 
level, which also contain two groups of slip systems. In the Fig. 15 , the 
color of SF change from gray to red implies the grains change from hard 
to soft. 
The analysis of the differences in SF distribution between repaired 
area and substrate have found that the repaired area shows the soft 
orientation while the substrate shows the hard orientation. In this case, 
unmatched deformation among the grains occurred during fatigue 
loading [ 11 ]. It is worthing note that the soft orientation of repaired area 
mainly concentrates on the middle of material, and the fracture area of 
repaired area contains the hard orientation. The strain localization and 
accumulation could mainly found near the hard grains, which can pro -
vide a long dislocation slip distance [ 59 ], which reveal the crack prop -
agation process of repaired material. At the same time, there are less soft 
orientation when the stress level is 850 MPa than that of the 950 MPa, 
which means that the repaired material has larger plastic deformation 
and is softer with the increase of stress level. However, the smaller 
plastic deformation with lower stress level did not decrease the crack 
initiation rate and crack propagation rate of repaired samples. At the 
same time, owing to the large local stress around large size pores, it is 
not means for the fatigue lifetime to increase with the lower SF [ 60 ]. 
Similar to the analysis of tensile deformation mechanism, the grain 
size, shape and orientation of cross-sections of the post fracture speci -
mens have been analyzed in Fig. 16 . Compared to the coarse grains in 
the substrate, the fine equiaxed grains and the columnar microstructure 
of the repaired area exhibited among the two produced microstructures. 
In addition, the fusion area and inter-channel boundary have the 
smallest grain. KAM map of the repaired sample clearly shows that the 
strain localization at the grains situated near the repaired area of frac -
ture plane, as showed in Fig. 16 (a-2, b-2), the more strain is showed by 
the red color compared to the gray regions. Fig. 16 (a-2, b-2) shows the 
larger plastic deformation occurred in the fine grains of repaired area, 
which can hinder the fatigue crack propagation and explain the longer 
fatigue lives benefit from smaller grain size [ 60 ]. According to the KAM 
maps, the GNDs density of the repaired area of main fracture plane at 
850 MPa is higher than that of the 950 MPa, but the overall GNDs 
density of the interior of repaired area at 850 MPa is lower. However, 
there are also exist the high GNDs density at the fusion and boundary 
area, as identified by red arrow in Fig. 16 (a-2, b-2). The difference in 
Twinning Boundary (TB) distributions between the repaired area and 
the substrate can also be identified under two kinds stress levels, as 
illustrated in Fig. 16 (a-3, b-3). The shorter and denser TBs of repaired 
area mainly distribute in the fine equiaxed grains, and there are no TBs 
inside the columnar grains under 950 MPa stress level. At the same time, 
the long and straight TBs can facilitate the crack nucleation and prop -
agation [ 48 ], which may can explain the crack of several repaired 
samples initiate from the substrate. Owing to the complex distributions 
of grain sizes, grain orientations and dendrite structure of repaired 
sample, the plastic deformation mechanism of repaired samples with 
different stress level are difficult to explain.

Phase transformation in repaired GH4169 superalloys by LDED is 
accompanied by the elemental segregation, which effect the size and 
distribution of strengthening phases, Laves phases and Carbide. Hence, 
the increase of the precipitations of Laves phases mean the content 
reduction of the main strengthening phase in repaired area, which could 
weaken the mechanical strength. At the same time, as the unique brittle 
phase, the Laves phase and carbide are both decrease the plastic 
deformation ability. Hence, it is quite important to identify the defor -
mation mechanism of pores and precipitations phase under different 
stress levels, then the high temperature fatigue deformation mechanism 
of repaired materials can be obtained. 
As discussed in Section 3.1 , the repaired area contains the pores and 
Laves phases. Our previous work found that the solution and aging 
treatment significantly could affect the fraction, size and morphology of 
the Laves phase [ 31 ], but hardly affected the fractions and size of pore. 
To analyze the plastic deformation mechanism of pores and pre -
cipitations during fatigue loading, the microstructure observations on 
the longitudinal sections of the fatigue fractures under two kinds stress 
levels are performed in Figs. 17 – 18 . The pores of repaired area elongate 
along the direction of loading and changes from the spheroid to ellip -
soid, but the differences of deformation of pores under two kinds stress 
level are inconspicuous. Combined with the fracture analysis in Section 
3.3 , it can be found that the pores have little influence on deformation of 
repaired material. At the same time, the long chain Laves phase broken 
into the granular shape and formed on the interdendritic under fatigue 
testing at elevated temperature. Comparing the microstructure of 
repaired specimens tested in 850 MPa ( Fig. 17 ) with that of the repaired 
specimens tested in 950 MPa ( Fig. 18 ), the morphology and fraction of 
carbide and Laves phases are almost the same. 
4.3. The effect of stress on the fatigue behavior of repaired materials 
In this work, all V-shape samples have been repaired by the same 
LDED process and heat treatment process, which means the 
morphology, fraction and size of the pores and secondary phases (Car -
bide, Laves phase and Strengthen phase) are both statistically the same. 
Comparing the low and high stress level, the fatigue crack initiate site 
and secondary crack propagation path are both different, which can be 
explained by the influence of pores on the crack initiation stage and the 
influence of microstructure on the deformation mechanism, respec -
tively, discussed in Section 3.3 . According to the differences of crack 
initiation site and secondary crack propagation seen on the fracture 
surface of the repaired specimens at the high stress level, as shown in 
Fig. 8 , the crack initiation in oxidation of subsurface and the secondary 
crack growth mode is transgranular. However, the crack initiation in 
surface and the secondary crack growth mode of repaired specimens is 
intergranular under low stress level.

fatigue performance of the repaired area is comparable to that of the 
substrate. Upon decreasing the stress level from 950 MPa to 750 MPa, no

5. Conclusions 
The microhardness, high temperature tensile and fatigue properties 
of the repaired GH4169 superalloys by LDED have been investigated. 
The mechanism of high temperature mechanical properties 
enhancement has been analyzed. The major conclusions can be drawn as 
follows: 
a) The complex changing process of the temperature gradient and the 
cooling rate resulting the unique dendrite structure of repaired GH4169 
superalloys by LDED. The STA effect the formation of Laves phase and 
carbide, but could not eliminate pores in repaired area. The average 
pores quantity is 42 and the predominant range of pore diameters falls 
within 20 – 50 μ m, with the maximum pore size being < 80 μ m. 
b) Compared to room temperature tensile, high temperatures notably 
diminish the tensile properties. Microstructure characterizations and 
crystal plasticity modelling imply that the plasticity deformations occur 
mainly in fusion area and twin grain, which result in the tensile strength 
and elongation rate of both the substrate and the repaired area dem -
onstrates comparable characteristics, and the difference in elongation 
rate between the two materials is close to 0, and Laves phase and carbide 
cannot eliminate the dynamic strain aging phenomenon of repaired 
material. 
c) Microstructure and defects have a direct impact on the high 
temperature fatigue behavior of the repaired specimens. Under high 
stress levels (850 – 950 MPa), and the fatigue performance of the 
repaired materials primarily influenced by the plastic deformation of the 
microstructure, which result in the fatigue life of both the substrate and 
the repaired area demonstrates comparable characteristic; under low 
stress levels (650 – 750 MPa), the fatigue life of the substrate is longer 
obviously, and fatigue performance of the repaired materials the pri -
marily influenced by the presence of defects.
