High-throughput mapping method for mechanical properties, 
oxidation resistance, and phase stability in Ni-based superalloys using 
composition-graded unidirectional solidiﬁed alloys

We propose a new approach for the high-throughput experimental evaluation of the phase stability, me- 
chanical and oxidation properties of a Ni-based superalloy. The Bridgman method is used to introduce 
the gradation of nine elements in a long distance ( ∼24 mm) and a wide range of microstructural features 
with η, μ and γ’ phase volume fractions in one sample. Nanoindentation and microstructural observa- 
tions are utilized to extract the elastic modulus/hardness of a γ- γ’ two-phase structure and evaluate 
the oxidation layers in an oxidized sample. Our approach makes it possible to obtain huge number of 
datasets linked to the composition and microstructure of the superalloys.

Ni-based superalloys are widely used as turbine discs in aircraft 
engines and land-based gas turbines, which are rotating compo- 
nents that endure high stress and temperatures ( > 700 °C), with 
strict safety requirements [1 , 2] . Advanced disc superalloys are de- 
signed to meet the required mechanical properties at service tem- 
perature, i.e., strength, creep, low cycle fatigue, and crack growth 
resistance, which can be achieved by solid solution strengthen- 
ing with over 10 additive elements, precipitation strengthening 
by γ’ with bimodal or trimodal distributions, and grain boundary 
strengthening by ﬁne grains ( < 50 μm) [3] . Furthermore, oxidation 
and corrosion resistance are important for such superalloys be- 
cause of their high service temperature ( > 700 °C) [4] . Hence, elu- 
cidating the relationship between their composition, microstruc- 
ture, and properties is crucial to determine their best composi- 
tions. However, this becomes diﬃcult because of the complicated 
microstructures and high number of additive elements, which lead 
to an exponential increase in the number of required experiments. 
A combinatorial approach using thin ﬁlm techniques that maps 
properties as functions of composition is employed to optimize 
functional materials (magnetic and optical) [5 , 6] . Applying such ap- 
proach for high-temperature structural materials, large amount of 
all-in-one datasets can be collected for exploring alloys of multi- 
element system. However, the mechanical properties and corro- 
sion resistance for applications in bulk form cannot be reason-

suﬃciently large to obtain the suﬃciently large area that can be

the composition gradient of the other elements owing to the for- 
mation of precipitates of the other phases or segregation caused by 
large deviation of diffusion paths from the straight line joining the

the composition gradient of slow diffusants as it only depends on 
a solidus curve of alloy composition and not on diffusivity. Further- 
more, strengthening factors can be extracted without the effects of 
crystal orientation and grain boundaries because the method forms 
a single crystal or well-grown coarse grains in one sample during 
solidiﬁcation. 
In this study, we mapped the properties of a composition- 
graded Ni-based superalloy with coarse grains unidirectionally so- 
lidiﬁed by the Bridgman method. We conducted electron backscat- 
ter diffraction (EBSD), electron-probe microanalysis (EPMA), mi- 
crostructural analysis, instrumented indentation, and a continuous 
oxidation test at numerous points along the solidiﬁcation direction, 
which provided property maps as functions of composition. Fur- 
thermore, thermodynamic information, such as the compositions 
of phase boundaries and volume fraction of the γ’ phase, were ob- 
tained in a continuous composition range. 
The recently developed ME501 disc superalloy [1] was used 
in this study. To investigate strengthening within grains, compo- 
sitions of ME501 without C, Zr, and B as grain boundary strength- 
ening elements were prepared as starting material by vacuum in- 
duction melting (VIM) and casting. An ingot was remelted and so- 
lidiﬁed through unidirectional solidiﬁcation in a Bridgman furnace 
using an Al 2 O 3 crucible with a lowering speed of 0.5 mm/h. Sub- 
sequently, the ingot (diameter = 12 mm; length = 30 mm) was an- 
nealed at 1280 °C for 5 h under a vacuum and then furnace-cooled. 
The ingot was cut into plate-shaped specimens (thickness = 2 mm) 
as as-grown specimens. Each specimen included the centerline of 
the ingot oriented towards the growth direction. One piece of spec- 
imen, call as-aged specimen, was exposed to air at 800 °C for 1000 
h. The compositions were determined at positions along the cen- 
terline at 1.0 mm intervals on the γ- γ’ matrix (excluding plate-like 
precipitates) through EPMA point analysis using a Shimazu EPMA- 
8050G (Beam voltage = 15 kV). The intensities of Al-K α, Ti-K α, Cr- 
K α, Co-K α, Ni-K α, W-L α, Mo-L α, Ta-L α, Nb-L α, and Hf-L α charac- 
teristic X-rays were measured simultaneously, and were converted 
to concentrations utilizing the ZAF method with the pure metals 
of each element as references. The crystallographic orientations of 
the specimens were determined via EBSD using Gemini300 (Zeiss; 
Beam voltage = 20 kV), secondary electron images were collected 
along the centerline at 0.5 or 1.0 mm intervals at 10 kV using Au- 
riga (Zeiss) at 0.5 kV and Gemini300 at 10 kV. Areas of γ’ precipi- 
tates were determined using ImageJ [18] .

the determined compositions of the specimen at different posi- 
tions. Fig. 1 (b) shows the microstructures of the as-aged speci- 
men at low magniﬁcation. Plate-like precipitates were observed in 
as-grown specimen at x = 0–9 mm, and in as-aged specimen at 
x = 0–6.5 mm. Widmanstatten structures and ﬁne lamellar struc- 
tures were observed at 0–2.5 mm and 2.5–4.0 mm, respectively. 
Wider lamellar intervals were observed at x greater than 4.0 mm. 
Fig. 1 (c) shows the element maps of the plate-like (TCP1) and grain 
(TCP2) precipitates. TCP1 is observed both as-grown and as-aged 
specimen. TCP1 has higher Nb, Ti, and Hf contents than that in the 
γ matrix. These are expected to be in the η-phase based on their 
shape and composition and may have formed during solidiﬁcation, 
based on their size. TCP2 phase with higher W and Mo concen- 
trations than the γ matrix were observed in the as-aged speci- 
men; it is expected to be μ phase. The high-magniﬁcation images 
in Fig. 1 (d) indicate the presence of cuboidal-shaped secondary γ’ 
precipitates (diameter = ∼100 nm) and ternary γ’ precipitates (di- 
ameter < 10 nm) in the matrix of the overall region. 
The mechanical properties were investigated via instrumented 
indentation tests using TI900 (Bruker) and the 115 ° Berkovich in- 
denter, with a maximum load of 50 mN and loading rate of 
5 mN/s. We selected a suﬃciently large indent size to evalu- 
ate the strengthening of the γ’ precipitates [19] . No less than 
three indentation tests were conducted at positions 0.5 mm apart 
along the crystal growth direction. Fig. 2 shows the typical load–
displacement curve obtained, with an image of the correspond- 
ing indenter impressions. The indented regions were assumed to 
be homogeneous γ/ γ’ matrix phases, based on the impression 
size ( ∼5 μm). A few impressions contained plate-like precipitates. 
However, the load–displacement curves obtained with and with- 
out the precipitates did not differ. The reduced elastic modulus, E r , 
and hardness, H , were calculated using the Oliver–Pharr method 
[8] with calibration of contact stiffness according to Feng and 
Ngan’s method [20] . 
The cross-section of the oxidation surface was observed by 
EPMA. Fig. 3 (a) presents the typical element maps of this cross- 
section at x = 11 mm. Fig. 3 (b) shows the line proﬁles of the 
speciﬁc x-ray intensities along the depth direction. The outermost 
layer was rutile, followed by the chromia and Al 2 O 3 layers. Al, Ti, 
and Cr-depleted regions and dispersed TCP precipitates were ob- 
served beneath the oxide layers. A continuous Al 2 O 3 layer was not 
formed. The depth of the oxidation layer was approximately 30 
μm.

The proposed method realizes large distribution of important fac- 
tors contributing to overall hardness, i.e., solid solution strength- 
ening elements, and volume fraction and size of γ’, which makes 
it possible to provide huge amount of dataset linked to the com- 
position and microstructure. 
Fig. 4 (k) shows the depth of the oxidation layers as a function 
of x . The proﬁles shown here indicate that the alloys were chromia 
formers. The structures of the oxidation layers were very similar. 
Therefore, it is diﬃcult to determine which composition is better 
for oxidation resistance. However, the depth proﬁle of the region in 
which TCP grains were formed varied considerably and was similar 
to those of the Ti, Ta, and Nb concentrations. 
In summary, a high-throughput mapping method for examining 
high-temperature structural materials using a composition-graded 
bulk specimen with large single crystals was developed herein. 
This approach realized the high-throughput mapping of phase sta- 
bility, mechanical properties, and oxidation properties in superal- 
loys with various compositions.
