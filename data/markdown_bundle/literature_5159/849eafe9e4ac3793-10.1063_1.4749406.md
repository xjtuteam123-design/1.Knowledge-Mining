Effects of alloying elements and temperature on the elastic properties
of dilute Ni-base superalloys from first-principles calculations
S. L. Shang,1,a) D. E. Kim,1 C. L. Zacherl,1 Y . Wang,1 Y . Du,2 and Z. K. Liu 1
1Department of Materials Science and Engineering, The Pennsylvania State University, Steidle Building,
University Park, Pennsylvania 16802, USA
2State Key Laboratory of Powder Metallurgy, Central South University, Changsha, Hunan 410083, China
(Received 13 January 2012; accepted 9 August 2012; published online 6 September 2012)
The variation of elastic properties, e.g., elastic constants, bulk modulus, and shear modulus of
dilute Ni-base superalloys due to alloying elements (X’s) and temperature, has been studied via
ﬁrst-principles calculations. Here, 26 alloying elements are considered: Al, Co, Cr, Cu, Fe, Hf, Ir,
Mn, Mo, Nb, Os, Pd, Pt, Re, Rh, Ru, Sc, Si, Ta, Tc, Ti, V, W, Y, Zn, and Zr. It is found that
(i) both the bulk and shear moduli of Ni-X decrease approximately linearly with increasing
equilibrium volume, especially within each group of 3d, 4d, or 5d transition-metal alloying
elements; (ii) all alloying elements considered herein increase the ratio of bulk to shear modulus
(i.e., the ductility) and the elastic anisotropy of the Ni-X alloys; and (iii) the largest decrease of
elastic properties of Ni is caused by alloying element Y. It is observed that the change of elastic
properties of Ni due to various alloying elements is traceable from the distribution of
(magnetization) charge density, for instance the spherical distribution of charge density facilitates
shear deformation, resulting in a lower shear-related property. Using a proposed quasistatic
approach based on the predicted elasticity-volume-temperature relationship, the isothermal and the
isentropic elastic properties are predicted for the dilute Ni-X alloys at ﬁnite temperatures,
displaying a decreasing trend with respect to temperature for each Ni-X system. Computed elastic
properties are in favorable accord with available experimental data.

I. INTRODUCTION
With ten or more alloying elements typically added,
Ni-base superalloys are amongst the most complex alloys
developed in order to increase their lifetime and service tem-
peratures,1 e.g., Al, Ti, and Ta to improve strength via the
Ni3(Al; Ti; Ta) phase, Re, W, and Mo to improve creep
resistance, and Al, Cr, and Co to improve resistance to oxi-
dation, corrosion, and sulphidation. 2 To understand, and
especially to guide, the development of the next generation
of Ni-base superalloys, it is desirable to gain a systematic
understanding of fundamental properties and the effect of
alloying elements on their temperature-dependent properties.
In the present work, elasticity is selected for study via ﬁrst-
principles calculations. Elasticity measures the capability of
a material to return to its original shape after deformation,
providing implications for a vast number of material-related
behaviors. For example, single crystal elastic constants and
the associated polycrystalline aggregate properties
3 provide
information about interatomic bonding and its anisotropy, 4
the criterion of mechanical stability, 5 the measure of lattice
vibration of acoustic branches, 6 and indications of ductility/
brittleness,7,8 hardness,9 melting points, 10 fracture tough-
ness,11 creep rate,12 etc.
Experimentally, single crystal elastic constants data are
scarce for Ni-base superalloys, and the limited measurements
were reported for fcc Ni, 3 Ni-Al,13 and Ni-Cu alloys. 14 At
ﬁnite temperatures, the available measurements exist only for
fcc Ni.3 Theoretically, effects of 15 alloying elements on the
elastic properties of dilute Ni-X alloys were studied at 0 K
based on ﬁrst-principles calculations. 10 Even so, the under-
standing of elasticity of dilute Ni-base superalloys is still far
from complete, especially at high temperatures. The incom-
pleteness of studies on the elastic properties motivates the
present ﬁrst-principles calculations in two-fold as an extension
of our previous work.
10 First, the following 26 dilute alloying
elements in Ni-base superalloys are considered, viz., Al, Co,
Cr, Cu, Fe, Hf, Ir, Mn, Mo, Nb, Os, Pd, Pt, Re, Rh, Ru, Sc, Si,
Ta, Tc, Ti, V, W, Y, Zn, and Zr. Second, the temperature de-
pendence of the elastic properties is studied based on a quasi-
static approach proposed recently,
15,16 where the main input is
the predicted elasticity-volume-temperature relationship. To
these ends, methodologies regarding ﬁrst-principles elastic
constants at 0 K and ﬁnite temperatures are outlined in Sec.II;
the obtained elastic properties at 0 K and ﬁnite temperatures
as well as their electronic origins are presented and discussed
in Sec. III; and ﬁnally, the conclusions of the present work are
given in Sec. IV.

formed using the VASP code,30 with the ion-electron interac-

IV . CONCLUSIONS
As a systematic effort to study the fundamental proper-
ties of Ni-base superalloys, the effects of alloying elements
(X’s) and temperature on the variation of elastic properties
of fcc Ni, encompassing isothermal and isentropic elastic
constants, bulk modulus ( B), shear modulus ( G), B/G ratio,
and elastic anisotropy, has been studied via ﬁrst-principles
calculations. 26 alloying elements have been considered:

R u ,S c ,S i ,T a ,T c ,T i ,V ,W ,Y ,Z n ,a n dZ r .B a s e do nt h e
ﬁrst-principles results at 0 K, the main ﬁndings are as fol-
lows: (i) both the bulk modulus and shear modulus of the
dilute Ni-X alloys decrease as a function of equilibrium
volume, particularly within in each group of 3d, 4d, or 5d
transition-metal alloying elem ents, (ii) all alloying ele-
ments considered herein increase the B/G ratio (i.e., the
ductility) and the elastic anisotropy of the dilute Ni-X
alloys, (iii) the largest decrease of elastic properties of Ni is
caused by alloying element Y, and (iv) the effect of alloy-
ing elements on the variation of elastic properties of fcc Ni
is traceable from the distribution of (magnetization) charge
density, for example, the spherical distribution of charge
density facilitates shear defor mation. At ﬁnite temperatures,
the isothermal and the isentropi c elastic properties are pre-
dicted for the dilute Ni-X alloys in terms of a quasistatic
approach based on the predicted elasticity-volume-tempera-
ture relationship. It is shown that a decreasing trend with
respect to temperature is observed for the dilute Ni-X
alloys, especially for c
11. Computed elastic properties are
compared to and are in favorable accord with available ex-
perimental data.
