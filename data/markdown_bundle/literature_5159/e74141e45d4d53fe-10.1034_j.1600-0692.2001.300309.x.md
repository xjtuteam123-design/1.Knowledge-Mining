A thermodynamic database for Ni-base superalloys
N. Dupin1 and B. Sundman 2
1Calcul Thermodynamique, 63670 Orcet, France; 2Department of Materials Science and Engineering, Royal Institute of Technology, Stockholm, Sweden
A thermodynamic database has been developed for Ni-base su-
peralloys. Following the CALPHAD procedure, it is based on the
assessment of thermodynamic behaviour of the relevant con-
stituting sub-systems of the complex system of interest. The cur-
rent database contains 7 elements: Al, Co, Cr, Ni, Ti, W, Re. The
thermodynamics of the g and g¿ phases are modelled with a
single Gibbs energy function taking into account the crystallo-
graphic relation between these two phases. The formalism of the
thermodynamic models used is brieﬂy introduced. A few phase
diagrams calculated with the current database in assessed bi-
nary and ternary systems are presented. Some experiments
available in higher order systems are compared with the results
of calculations with the current database. The ability of extrapol-
Complex compositions as well as careful processing
of elaboration and heat treatments allow the Ni base
superalloys to get their exceptional properties. In or-
der to develop new compositions or to better control
the engineering of industrial alloys, the phase equilib-
ria and thermodynamic behaviour of the complex sys-
tems under consideration need to be known. The
CALPHAD procedure allows an estimation of them
from the combination of the thermodynamic descrip-
tion of lower order sub-systems. In the current work,
it has been applied in order to obtain a thermody-
namic database for Ni superalloys.

Discussion
The current database contitutes a revision and an ex-
tension of previous work [9, 14]. It is based on the
thermodynamic assessment of important sub-system
for Ni-superalloys. It also allows extrapolation to
multicomponent compositions of industrial interest.
The agreement of calculated results is reasonable for
most of the available experiments. Some sub-systems
are still tentative, among them the systems with Re,

and experiments are thus desirable. The planning of
experiments in multicomponent alloys should be
made easier by the present database.
The ﬁnal goal is to get a reliable thermodynamic
database for industrial use because of the cost, the dif-
ﬁculties, and the uncertainty of experiments for these
complex alloys. The steps presently in progress are
the introduction of Ta, in collaboration with RWTH
Aachen, and Mo. The effects of interstitials, mainly C
and B, will also be rapidly taken into account. It will
beneﬁt of the work done by the international CAL-
PHAD community in others classes of materials
(Steel, hard materials). The others metallic elements
planed to be introduced are Nb, Zr, Hf and Fe.
