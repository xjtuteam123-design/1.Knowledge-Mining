EFFECT OF OXIDATION AND SULFIDE CORROSION IN THE 
CONDITIONS OF A HIGH-TEMPERATURE GAS FLOW ON THE 
STRENGTH CHARACTERISTICS OF 1~I-958 ALLOY 
A. L. Rozhko, V. P. Lesnikov, and B. F. Rybalko UDC 669.15:620.72 
In this work, we examined the effect of the flow of combustion products of diesel fuel with an addition of a solution of 
sea salt on the strength and ductility characteristics of l~I-958 corrosion-resisting nickel alloy [1] as well as changes in the 
microstructure and phase and chemical composition of surface layers using the results obtained in optical metallography, x-ray 
spectral microanalysis, and hardness measurements. 
Flat cast specimens with a neck after stepped quenching (1170~ 4 h, cooling in air, 1050~ 4 h, cooling in air) and 
aging (16 h at 860"C) were held for 1, 8, and 25 h in products of combustion of diesel fuel with an addition of 2% aqueous 
solution of sea salt in the amount of 6 mg of salt per 1 kg of air at 900~ +_ 10~ and a constant tensile stress of 150 MPa. 
Subsequently, the specimens were loaded under the same conditions to failure a tensile rate of 0.16 mm/min. 
To examine in greater detail the structural changes taking place in the surface layers and evaluate their effect on the 
properties, we carried out special experiments which differed from those described previously by the fact that blowing for 3 h was 
carried out on specimens polished to the condition of the metallographie section. 
The detected reduction of strength (Fig. 1) in the conditions of dynamic contact with the high-temperature gas flow is 
not adequate to the reduction of the effective cross section of the specimens and is considerably greater. This may indicate that 
extensive structural changes take place in the surface layers. This is also confirmed by a reduction of the ductility of the alloy 
(Fig. 1). 
Examination of the structure of the surface of polished specimens showed that the oxide film is highly nonuniform both 
in the thickness and composition. Oxide particles have a simple geometrical form and are only slightly bonded together, i.e., the 
film contained defects and does not prevent the penetration of S- and 0 2- ions to the alloy-oxide boundary. 
Sulfur ions can be absorbed by Cr20 3 oxide and increase the diffusion rate of cations. Consequently, pores form on the 
metal--oxide surface. In likelihood, these pores are caused by a powerful flow of cation vacancies and the generation of volatile 
reaction products [2]. In the initial moment, this is reflected in swelling of the oxide film (Fig. 2). The effect of the high-speed 
gas flow removes scale and leads to the formation of scars (pits). Local stress risers (microcracks) form at the bottom of the 
lattice. The surface of the material is coated simultaneously with a fragmented separated oxide film and pits. Operation of the 
separation mechanism as a result of formation of the gas component at the oxide--metal boundary is indicated by the form of 
defects. 
The tensile tests carried out after 3 h of the effect of combustion products showed a strong effect of the oxide film on 
the characteristics of the alloy and the deformation mechanism of the surface. 
In tensile loading the specimens (with no blowing) at room temperature in IMASh-20-75 equipment at a rate of 0.16 
ram/rain the surface showed a relief typical of foe metals. Slip lines in a single system were unitormly distributed inside the grains 
and multiple slip was detected only in the vicinity of the failure zone. The slip lines changed their direction depending on the 
crystallographic orientation of the grains in relation to the loading plane. 
Failure starts with the formation of pores around carbides which grow into microcracks when the strain is increased 
(Fig. 3). Failure occurs along the grain boundaries. At room temperature the alloy has the following characteristics: %.2 = 
680-720 MPa; a13 = 830- 910 MPa; 3 -- 7-10%. The tensile diagram shows a distinctive nonlinear section. Experimental data are 
in satifactory agreement with the results obtained on standard specimens.

Because of the low ductility of the heterophase oxide films, the film fails in a brittle manner. Cracks normal to the 
applied load form on the surface, irrespective of the crystallographic orientation of the grains. The cracks which act as stress 
raisers embrittle the material. The nonlinear section of the o--e curve does not form and a B = or0. 2 = 680-720 MPa, 8 = 0.1%. 
As a result of examining the effect of holding time in the flow or structural changes taking place in the surface layer of 
the alloy, the corrosion effect was divided into three stages: preferential development of corrosion along the grain boundaries 
(Fig. 4a); propagation through the body of the grain (Fig. 4b); formation on the surface of a porous, separating scale and 
penetration of corrosion into the thickness of the alloy with the same sequence retained (Fig. 4c). 
There are two types of sulfide corrosion: frontal and pit which evidently depend on many factors. In this work, we 
determined the relationship of the nature of corrosion damage with the conditions of the flow of gas around the specimen. On 
the "shadow" side of the specimen (in relation to the incident flow) the thickness of the scale is greater and the damage is mainly 
of the frontal type. On the side of the direct effect of the flow, the scale is considerably thinner and corrosion is of the pit type. 
It should be mentioned that the data described satisfactorily the pattern of corrosion damage of components under actual service 
conditions. 
The development of a specific type of corrosion damage can be explained by the erosive effect of the flow. On the 
shadow side the flow does not interfere with the formation of a film of liquid salts through which S- and 0 2-- ions are trans- 
ferred and which, it is assumed, prevents the formation of a protective oxide film. Erosion failure of oxides takes place on the 
side of the incident flow. The formation of a continuous film of liquid salts is also inhibited in this area. Consequently, corrosion 
attacks mainly "weak" surface areas in the relief, structural and chemical respect. 
The initiation and propagation of cracks typical of oxidizing and slightly corrosive media and detected in tensile loading 
at room temperature was not recorded in any test stage. It is evident that this is associated with a high rate of corrosion damage 
which suppresses this process.

However, a reduction of strengthened ductility is caused mainly by corrosion along the grain boundaries where the depth 
of penetration of corrosion is often an order of magnitude greater than the depth frontal damage. This leads to softening of the 
grain boundaries and creates suitable condition for the formation of macrodamage in the area of separation of individual surface 
grains. 
According to the chemical composition of the surface layers of the alloy, the development of corrosion in the initial 
stage of testing (holding to 1 h) accompanied by the formation of a multilayer scale whose external layer (10-15/~m thick) 
consists mainly of oxides of nickel and titanium with inclusions of oxides of aluminum, cobalt, and chrome (it is evident that the 
spinels of these elements are also present). The molybdenum content in the internal part of the layer rapidly increases. At a 
depth of 20 and 30/~m there are two layers consisting of alumina. They are separated by a layer whose chemical composition is 
identical with that of the external layer but its chrome and tungsten content is higher and the molybdenum content lower. The 
concentration of chrome in the zone with the changed chemical composition (0-35/~m) decreases. No sulfides form in this stage. 
With an increase of holding time to 25 h the extent of penetration of frontal corrosion reaches a depth of 100/~m and 
greater (without taking into account the thickness of the separated scale). The composition of the scale changes in comparison 
with the initial composition. The external layer (0-25/~m) consists mainly of oxides of chrome, nickel, and aluminum. Oxides of 
titanium, cobalt, and tungsten were detected at a depth of 25/zm. In deeper layers (35-85/~m) large precipitates of the chrome 
sulfides alternate with oxides of nickel and cobalt (probably complex spinels). The zone in which sulfides form is characterized 
by a reduced titanium content. Molybdenum was not detected in the entire analyzed layer (100/~m). 
Changes in the chemical composition cause softening of the alloy, as indicated by the results of microhardness measure- 
ments (Fig. 5). It is evident that this is caused by dissolution of the y'-phase, by the absence of hardening elements of the solid 
solution such as molybdenum, tungsten, and chrome, as well as by the formation of porous corrosion products. 
Existence of the incubation period of sulfide corrosion can be explained by changes in the activity of the oxide--ion of 
sulfur during chemical reactions and by establishment of a limiting concentration in the surface layers of the material of 
elements such as chrome, molybdenum, and aluminum which affect the sulfidizing process. 
The higher rate of sulfide corrosion is associated with the fact that the initially formed oxide film does not have 
sufficiently effective properties in the high-speed flow of combustion products (its failure mechanism was described previously). 
In addition, the aluminum-rich layers formed as a result of internal oxidation easily dissolving contact with liquid Na2SO 4 
penetrating through cracks and pores. 
Depletion of the surface layers in chrome, i.e., the main element providing protection against sulfide corrosion, prevents 
the formation of a continuous layer of chrome spinel. Depletion may by caused by selective oxidation with further formation of 
chrome sulfides and their removal by the flow in the composition of a porous, loose scale based on nickel and titanium as well 
as by inhibition of diffusion from the matrix by alumina barriers. 
Molybdenum plays a highly negative role in the corrosion process [3, 4]. This is expressed in the development of porosity 
as a result of generation of MoO 3 volatile compound and in a reduction of the activity 0 2- ions in the Na2SO 4 layer at the 
boundary with the oxides, and the time to establishment of the critical activity of the ions determines the duration of the 
incubation period. A reduction of the activity of 02- ions is accompanied by an increase of the activity of S- ions and suitable 
conditions are generated for dissolution of nickel and aluminum oxides with the formation of a loose porous scale which is in all 
likelyhood removed by the flow. This process is self-sustaining and this results in the depletion of the alloy in aluminum and 
molybdenum accompanied by rapid sulfide corrosion. 
This sequence of events is concerned by the presence of an incubation period, a reduction of the aluminum concentra- 
tion in the zone with sulfides, and by the absence of molybdenum in the external layer up to 100/~m thick after testing for 25 h.

It appears that the initially formed oxide film does not prevent the penetration of oxygen or sulfur and, in subsequent 
stages, of melts of salts into the volume of the alloy with dissolutions of internal aluminum-rich layers. Formation of the sulfides 
at a large depth from the surface is confirmed by the occurrence of "strong" corrosion in which the sulfur, released during the 
oxidation, penetrated into the bulk of the material. As a result of high diffusibility, sulfide corrosion outstrips the oxidation front 
[4]. 
Thus, the reduction of the strength and ductility properties of the alloy is caused by changes in the properties of their 
surface layers induced by oxidation and corrosion processes: 
-- formation in the initial stage of the test of a heterophase oxide Him with low ductility and with stress raisers which 
does not act as an obstacle to the penetration of oxygen and sulfur into the alloy; 
-- softening of the grain boundaries accompanied by formation of stress raisers and easier intergranular failure; 
-- softening of the matrix as a result of changes in the chemical composition and dissolution of the hardening phases; 
-- reduction of the effective cross section as a result of separation and removal of the scale by the flow.
