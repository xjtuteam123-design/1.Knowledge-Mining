1. Introduction
In recent years, high entropy alloys (HEAs) have become new
frontiers of research in the materials science community [1].
Their design concept differs greatly from that of their con-
ventional counterparts [2–4]. These alloys contain at least ﬁve
major components as compared to conventional alloys which
contains only one main component consisting of minor con-
centrations of other elements [5–7]. Several researchers have
reported that these alloys possess various desirable prop-
erties such as excellent mechanical strength and hardness,

2. Experimental procedures
Chemical composition of an equiatomic AlCrFeMnNi HEA was
prepared using high purity metal powders including Al, Fe, Ni,
Cr and Mn. The mass ratios of each element were calculated
using the respective atomic ratios of the elements. The mix-
ture of the calculated metal powders was then subjected to a
tubular mixer for 8 h to ensure even distribution. Finally, the
mixture was compacted into blocks by the tablet machine. The
block samples were inserted into the arc melting furnace and
high purity argon gas was injected into the chamber to obtain
a more inert atmosphere. Melting was carried out in a copper
crucible and the buttons were ﬂipped over 5 times to improve
chemical homogeneity. The as-cast buttons had a diameter of
about 20 mm and a height of 10 mm. The ingots were sectioned
for heat treatment and a series of analysis and tests. Anneal-
ing took place at 400, 600, 800 and 1000◦C for 2 h in a mufﬂe
furnace. Microstructure and phase composition of the HEAs in
the as-cast, as well as annealed state, were investigated using
scanning electron microscopy (SEM) equipped with energy dis-
persive spectrometer (EDS), and X-ray diffraction (XRD) with
Cu target K/H9251 radiation. Samples for SEM observations were
mounted in an epoxy-based resin, polished and then etched

3.3. Microhardness
Hardness measurements usually provide ﬁrst insights into
the mechanical properties of the alloy at room tempera-
ture. Fig. 5 shows the measured values of microhardness of
alloy annealed at different temperatures. It is discovered that
annealing at low temperatures resulted in a slight increase
in hardness from the as-cast condition. However, increasing
the
 annealing temperature caused a decrease in hardness.
The latter behaviour is evident, where annealing at 400◦C
(HV ≈ 423.9) comparatively resulted in an increased HV value
than
 the specimen annealed at 1000◦C (HV ≈ 308.1). A suit-
able explanation can be linked to the fact that increasing the
annealing temperature causes increases the volume fraction
of fcc
 phase leading to a decrease in hardness [21]. It is note-
worthy
 to highlight that soft fcc phases are responsible for the
decrease in hardness. This is in agreement with the recently
conducted study [22]. Nonetheless, grain growth may also con-
tribute towards the decrease in hardness [7].
3.4. Compressive strength
Fig. 6 shows the compression curves of the equiatomic AlCr-
FeMnNi HEA annealed at different temperatures. As can
be seen, the ultimate compressive strength (UCS) of the
alloy in the as-cast condition is 1013 MPa with a strain of
10%. It can be noted that applying heat treatment altered
mechanical behaviour of the alloy. The increase in plasticity
indicates an augmentation in the material’s ductility which
also accounts for the increased hardness value. In spite of sim-
ilar microstructures possessed by the as-cast and heat-treated
alloys, the considerable difference in mechanical properties
may be due to the amount of modulated BCC/B2 (Al + Ni) phase
which tends to migrate in the ID regions (see Table 1) of the
alloy and characterized as hard and brittle [20,22]. Therefore, it
can be speculated that the cross-section area of the ID region
is greater than soft DC. Thus, untreated AlCrFeMnNi HEA pos-
sesses an undesirable combination of soft DC and hard brittle
ID region rendering heat treatment as an imperative process
to achieve the desired chemical homogeneity. Heat treatment
at 600◦C caused a signiﬁcant increase in compressive strength
and strain to 1595 MPa and 21% respectively. This behaviour
can be related to the amount of black and light phase that
has
 formed. Based on the mentioned analysis of SEM images
and EDS
 result, the light phase is rich in Fe + Ni. According to
Bertotti and Florillo [23], the Fe + Ni phase is identiﬁed to be
a stable FCC solid solution phase obtained by choice of heat

3.5. Wear behaviour
The frictional behaviour of equiatomic AlCrFeMnNi high
entropy alloy was examined by studying the inﬂuence of
annealing at 800◦C on the coefﬁcient of friction (COF). From
Fig. 7, it can be deduced that the heat-treated alloy possesses
a higher COF value (0.31 /H9262) than in as-cast condition (0.26 /H9262).
This indicates that the annealed sample exhibits compara-
tively inferior wear resistance. Friction value of the as-cast
specimen gradually

thermore, the friction coefﬁcient shows fewer ﬂuctuations
as compared to its annealed counterpart. The ﬂuctuations
observed in the COF value of annealed material may be due
to the presence of debris from the worn surface. Additionally,
spalling debris adheres to the friction surface which conse-
quently contributes to abrasive wear. This results in further
reduction in wear resistance of the alloy. Surface fracture as
a result of the applied load during sliding may also have con-
tributed to the ﬂuctuations [24]. Fig. 8 presents the wear rate
of the fabricated alloys. It is evident that the AlCrFeMnNi HEA
heat treated at 800◦C possesses a high wear rate as compared
to its untreated counterpart. This behaviour is attributable to
low hardness. It is worthwhile to note that HEAs with low
microhardness exhibits reduced wear loss resistance. This is
consistent with the reported alloys [19,25]. Fig. 9 presents SEM
images of worn surface of (a) as-cast sample and (b) spec-

4. Conclusion
The relation of annealing temperature, morphology,
mechanical and electrochemical properties in multi-
principal equiatomic AlCrFeMnNi high entropy alloy
was
 investigated by SEM, XRD, hardness, wear, com-
pression and electrochemical tests. The following was
concluded:
(1) Microstructures comprised of dendritic cores and inter-
dendritic regions. The chemical composition of ID regions
consisted
 of BCC Al + Ni matrix. This structure remarkably
improved the hardness of HEAs.
(2) The main phase composition of AlCrFeMnNi high entropy
alloy in the as-cast condition is a BCC structure. However,
heat treatment beyond 400◦C signiﬁcantly encouraged the
formation of soft and ductile FCC phase.
(3) The relationship between the coefﬁcient of friction and
wear
 performance was clearly revealed. As-cast HEAs
with BCC phases exhibited superior resistance to wear,
wear resistance was reduced due to the formation of
debris which further aggravated abrasion during wear
process.
(4) As cast alloy possessed inferior compressive strength
than its annealed counterpart at 600 ◦C. How-
ever, heat treatment beyond 800 ◦C signiﬁcantly
lowered the yield strength of the specimen. There-
fore, excessive annealing temperatures proved to be
counter-effective.
(5) Anti-corrosion properties of the HEAs are diverse
under
 different corrosive environments. Annealed
alloys displayed considerable resistance to corrosion
as compared to its as-cast counterpart. The corro-
sion rate was observed to be the highest in acidic
solution.
