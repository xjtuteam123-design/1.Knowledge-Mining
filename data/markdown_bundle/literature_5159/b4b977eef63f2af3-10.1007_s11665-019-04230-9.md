Effect of Eutectic Characteristics on Hot Tearing of Cast
Superalloys
Z. Zhao and J.X. Dong
(Submitted January 14, 2019; in revised form July 8, 2019)
The occurrence of hot cracks in the blades of Ni-based superalloy turbocharger wheels is thought to be
closely related to eutectic characteristics. Different alloys that are susceptible to hot tearing, namely K418,
K419 and K424, were studied. SEM was used to obtain the microstructure of the alloys. DSC, isothermal
solidiﬁcation quenching process and EPMA were conducted to determine the relationship between the
eutectic characteristics and solidiﬁcation process, segregation behavior and dendrite coalescence. The re-
sults show that a large volume fraction of c/c¢ eutectic indicates a wide-temperature solidiﬁcation range and
severe segregation. A large c/c¢ eutectic size is a result of element segregation and poor dendrite coalescence.
Ti is thought to promote the formation of a large isolated residual liquid pool and lead to a large eutectic
size, leading to the poor coalescence behavior of the dendrites. The volume fraction and size of the c/c¢
eutectic were considered, and a eutectic inﬂuence factor (E) was deﬁned to evaluate the effect of eutectic
characteristics on hot tearing in cast superalloys. It was found that a high E corresponds to high hot-tearing
susceptibility.
Keywords eutectic characteristics, hot-tearing susceptibility, Ni-
based superalloys, solidiﬁcation
1. Introduction
It is well known that turbochargers can improve engine
work efﬁciency, satisfy antipollution regulations and reduce
fuel consumption by providing increased air ﬂows, which
increases the power density and decreases the overall weight
(Ref 1). To increase the operating efﬁciency of turbines,
continuous efforts have been made in recent decades in the
development of high-temperature casting materials (Ref 2).
Among different casting materials, Ni-based superalloys are
widely used due to their excellent high-temperature strength,
favorable comprehensive properties, and good structural sta-
bility and reliability over a wide range of temperatures (Ref 3–
6). Ti and Al are the most commonly added elements in Ni-
based superalloys to form the hardening c¢-Ni
3 (Al, Ti)
precipitate and improve the mechanical performance. However,
it is widely recognized that once the combined amount of Al
and Ti exceeds 6 wt.%, the alloy becomes extremely suscep-
tible to hot cracking during welding (similar to casting) (Ref 7,
8). On the other hand, due to the complexity of turbine wheels,
particularly changes in cross-sectional dimensions of the
blades, cracks could occur in the blades during investment
casting (IC) (Ref 1, 9, 10) because of thermal stress concen-
tration (Ref 3, 5, 6), leading to a low rate of qualiﬁed products.
In general, many alloys are susceptible to cracking during
solidiﬁcation, which is called hot tearing during casting (Ref
11). It is generally believed that hot tearing is initiated during
the last stages of solidiﬁcation by stresses perpendicular to
grain boundaries or interdendritic structures induced by thermal
contraction or solidiﬁcation shrinkage (Ref 2, 12, 13).
Presently, it is accepted that a complex interplay between two
factors, solidiﬁcation and restraint, leads to hot tearing (Ref 14).
Mechanical factors are a concern, including thermal stresses,
phase transformation stresses and die system effects (Ref 15).
Metallurgical factors are dependent on the stress-susceptible
microstructural regions that affect the cracking susceptibility,
including the solidiﬁcation temperature range, dendrite coher-
ency, surface tension of the interdendritic liquid, viscosity,
liquid feeding tendency, grain size and shape, and solute
segregation during the ﬁnal stages of solidiﬁcation (Ref 14).
The freezing range model (Ref 16), Clyne and Davies model
(Ref 17), c/c¢ eutectic model (Ref 18) and theoretical model by
Rappaz (Ref 19) are commonly used to explain hot-tearing
susceptibility in Ni-based superalloys. The solidiﬁcation tem-
perature range, volume fraction of eutectic and coalescence
behavior are mainly involved when these models are used to
explain hot-tearing susceptibility (castability) in different alloys
and the effect of elements on hot-tearing susceptibility in one
alloy.
Since the solidiﬁcation of Ni-based superalloys usually ends
with a c/c¢ eutectic reaction, the c/c¢ eutectic fraction model is
used to explain the hot-tearing susceptibility. At high contents
of c/c¢ eutectic, there may be sufﬁcient liquid to wet the grain
boundaries, but the width is smaller, so feeding becomes more
difﬁcult and the strain is higher, making hot tearing more likely.
At low eutectic content, it is possible that there is insufﬁcient
liquid to completely wet the grain boundaries, and the eutectic
is then only present as isolated pockets. In this case, much of
the grain boundaries are neighbors, and the grain boundaries
possess enough strength to reduce the possibility of strain
localization and hot tearing. A relationship with the volume
fraction of coarse c/c¢ eutectic has been proposed previously; at
low (5%) and high (22%) volume fractions of c
/c¢ eutectic, it
was possible to obtain sound casting (Ref 18). Zhang showed
that variations in Ti and Ta contents affect the tendency for hot

K418, K419 and K424 (Ref 25), which exhibit different hot-
tearing susceptibilities. Nickel-based superalloy K418 (713C)

used for turbocharger turbine wheels (Ref 3, 5). Superalloy
K419 (Ref 5) contains an increased amount of
c¢-forming

polycrystalline material strengthened by c¢ precipitates that has
an increased amount of c¢-forming elements Al and Ti. The
compositions of these three alloys cause them to exhibit
different eutectic characteristics. The effect of eutectic charac-
teristics on hot tearing is clariﬁed accordingly. The goal of this
study is to achieve a deeper understanding of the eutectic
characteristics of cast superalloys.

2.1 Materials
The tested turbine wheels were made of different alloys,
including K418 (different furnaces referred to as K418-1,
K418-2 and K418-3), K419 and K424. The chemical compo-

To evaluate the relationship between the eutectic character-

2.4 Isothermal Solidiﬁcation Quenching Process
The isothermal solidiﬁcation quenching process (ISQP) was
conducted in a high-temperature box resistance furnace.
Specimens for the ISQP were cut from the master alloys with
a size of A8.5 mm 9 10 mm. Afterward, samples were placed
into an alumina crucible and covered with a mixture of silica
sol and alumina powder to protect the melts from ﬂowing out
and oxidizing. As shown in Fig. 1, the samples were ﬁrst
heated to 1420 /C176 C and held isothermally for 20 min to achieve
a homogeneous complete melt. Then, the samples were cooled
at a rate of 5 /C176 C/min to different quenching temperatures and
held for 20 min. Finally, the samples were quenched in salty
water. The quenching temperature was obtained from the DSC
cooling curve, and the temperature interval was 20 /C176 C.
The solidiﬁcation microstructure of the samples at different
quenching temperatures was also analyzed by OM and SEM.
Moreover, the segregation behavior at the ﬁnal stage of
solidiﬁcation was also investigated by electron probe microan-
alyzer (EPMA).
3. Result
3.1 Eutectic and the Formation of Hot Cracks
Several batches of commercially produced turbines were
analyzed. We found that the qualiﬁed product rate of the K418
alloy was more than 90%. For the K424 alloy turbines, the hot
tearing was so serious that cracks occurred in almost every
blade. The qualiﬁed product rate was likely below 50%. The
qualiﬁed product rate for the K419 was approximately
60 /C24 70%. Based on the qualiﬁed product rates, K424, K419
and K418 showed different hot-tearing susceptibilities and are
listed in the order of high-to-low susceptibility.
Figure 2(a) shows a typical hot crack that occurred in the
K424 turbocharger wheel blade. As shown in Fig. 2(a) and (b),
the crack initiated at the blade tip and developed toward the
bulk of the blade. At the inner edge of the crack, as shown in
Fig. 2(c), a wide main crack together with a few microcracks
can be observed. The microcracks initiated from the eutectic
structure and did not stop developing until there were no large
c/c¢ eutectic structures, as shown in Fig. 2(d). This illustrated
that the c/c¢ eutectic has an impact on the formation of hot
tearing. On the other hand, the c/c¢ eutectic has long been
considered as a casting defect in superalloys since cracks
nucleate at the interface between the c matrix phase and c/c¢
eutectic phase and because there is a deformation incompati-
bility due to different lattice parameters (Ref 31, 32).
It is necessary to clarify the relationship between the eutectic
characteristics and hot-tearing susceptibility. Thus, the
microstructure of three alloys, including K418 (K418-1),
K419 and K424, was studied.
3.2 Eutectic Characteristics in Different Alloys Susceptible
to Hot Tearing
Figure 3 displays the SEM images of three alloys: K418,
K419 and K424. Most of the c/c¢ eutectics were distributed in
the interdendritic regions, which indicated that the c/c¢ eutectics
were formed during the last stage of solidiﬁcation. However,
there are signiﬁcant differences among these three tested alloys
with respect to volume fraction and size of the c/c¢ eutectic.
As shown in Fig. 3(a), K418 exhibits the smallest size and
the lowest volume fraction of the c/c¢ eutectic among the three
alloys. It is obvious that a substantial amount of the eutectic
formed in K419. The prevalence of the c/c¢ eutectic in K424 is
less than that in K419. However, the size of the c/c¢ eutectic in
K424 is much larger than that in K418 and K419. There seems
to be a positive correlation between the c/c¢ eutectics and hot-
tearing susceptibility. However, it is worth mentioning that
K419 exhibits the largest volume fraction of the c/c¢ eutectic
among the samples considered herein despite the qualiﬁed
product rate for K419 being higher than that of K424.
To better understand the relationship between c/c¢ eutectics
and hot-tearing susceptibility, the eutectic characteristics were
calculated. Figure 4 shows the volume fraction and the average
size of the c/c¢ eutectic in different alloys. The volume fraction
of the c/c¢ eutectic in the K418 alloys is 0.8%, 1.2% and 3.5%.

in K418 varies from 9.0 lm to 11.5 lm, and the median size is
8.7 lm. The average sizes of eutectics in K419 and K424 are

3.3 Validation and Veriﬁcation Relationship Between
Eutectic Characteristics and Hot Tearing
The effect of the eutectic characteristics on the hot tearing of
different alloys is shown in section 3.2. Since Ti is a c¢-forming
element, several K418-1 alloys with different Ti contents were
prepared by V AM to test the effect of the eutectic characteristics
on the hot-tearing susceptibility in one alloy.
Alloy K418 with different Ti contents (K418Ti1.6, K418
Ti2.6 and K418Ti4.5) were cast into conical copper molds in
water cooling conditions. Figure 6 shows the cross-sectional
SEMs of the conical samples cast with K418Ti1.6 and
K418Ti4.5. There are a few microcracks formed around the
c/c¢ eutectic in K418Ti1.6, as shown in Fig. 6(a). With
increasing Ti content, a large crack appeared and is observed
in the cross section of K418Ti4.5, as shown in Fig. 6(b). The
hot tearing is so serious that fracture even occurred at the
bottom of the conical sample, as shown in the red dashed
rectangle in Fig. 6(b).
Figure 7 shows the microstructure of the eutectics in
K418Ti1.6, K418Ti2.6 and K418Ti4.5. With increasing Ti
content, the size of the c/c¢ eutectic increases and the volume
fraction of c/c¢ eutectic increases as well. Meanwhile, the shape
of c/c¢ eutectic becomes more irregular with increasing Ti
content.
Figure 8 illustrates the statistical results of the size and
volume fraction of the c/c¢ eutectic in K418-1 alloys with
various Ti contents. The volume fraction of the c/c¢ eutectic is
1.65% and 4.42% in K418Ti1.6 and K418Ti2.6, respectively.
The average size of the c/c¢ eutectic in K418Ti1.6 and
K418Ti2.6 is 4.93 lm and 5.97 lm, respectively. When the Ti
content increases to 4.5%, both the volume fraction and size of
c/c¢ eutectic increase dramatically. The volume fraction of the
c/c¢ eutectic reaches 21.5%, and the average size of the c/c¢
eutectic increases to 17.17 lm. The median sizes of K418Ti1.6,
K418Ti2.6 and K418Ti4.5 are 4.47 lm, 6.14 l
m and
15.08 lm, respectively. In addition, the average size is similar
to the median size.
In summary, hot-tearing susceptibility is closely related to
the eutectic characteristics. In K418-1 with different Ti
contents, the c/c¢ eutectic volume fraction increases because
of the increasing c/c¢ eutectic size. Thus, there is a positive
correlation between the volume fraction of the c/c¢ eutectic and
hot-tearing susceptibility. In different alloys, the c/c¢ eutectic
volume fraction model cannot explain hot-tearing susceptibility
well. A greater volume fraction of c/c¢ corresponds to strong
elemental segregation and a greater amount of residual liquid.
Thus, the solidiﬁcation behavior and segregation behavior of
three alloys were studied.

4. Discussion
4.1 Solidiﬁcation Behavior and Eutectic Characteristics
According to the results of the DSC analysis and isothermal
solidiﬁcation experiment, a diagram (Fig. 11) can be drawn to
describe the inﬂuence of the solidiﬁcation characteristics on the
formation of the c/c¢ eutectic.
It was generally thought that an increased DT suggests an
increased hot-tearing susceptibility (Ref 33, 34). The temper-
ature range is thought to reﬂect the degree of segregation. The
broadening of the mushy zone temperature range is higher
when the segregation of the alloying is more severe. On the
other hand, a small DT gives a small mushy zone, which avoids
severe feeding problems and results in good castability. Thus,
the solidiﬁcation temperature range is considered an appropri-
ate criterion for assessing the castability, which is directly a
function of chemical composition. However, common expla-
nations based on freezing range are not able to explain the
results in this study.

The K419 alloy with a high DT shows lower hot-tearing
susceptibility when compared with K424. It is interesting to

employed to display the morphologies of the remaining liquid

4.2 Eutectic Characteristics and Hot Tearing
Figure 13 contains diagrams that explain the impact of
eutectic characteristics on hot-tearing susceptibility. Alloys
with a small size and small volume fraction of eutectic show
low hot-tearing susceptibility. First, a eutectic with a small size
means that the coalescence of the dendrites is rather good. Even
if hot cracks initiate because of solidiﬁcation stress, it is
difﬁcult for cracks to further propagate. Second, a small volume
fraction of eutectic means that there are fewer possible eutectic
reaction locations during the ﬁnal stage of solidiﬁcation. A c/c¢
eutectic with a large size and signiﬁcant eutectic-affected zone
indicates the presence of substantial segregation and poor
coalescence of the dendrites. Once a crack is initiated in the
eutectic, it is easy for cracks to grow because of the poor
coalescence. The hot-tearing susceptibility can be rather high in
this situation. Moreover, cracks can further propagate along the
eutectic-affected zone and the grain boundaries.
Minor elements, such as B and Zr, are thought to contribute
to the formation of a continuous thin liquid ﬁlms (Ref 36, 37),
which provide more possible locations for the c/c¢ eutectic
reactions, increasing the volume fraction of the c/c¢ eutectic.
Continuous thin liquid ﬁlms increase the hot-tearing suscepti-
bility. Ti promotes the formation of a large isolated liquid pool,
which forms c/c¢ eutectic. A large isolated liquid pool indicates
the coalescence behavior of the dendrites, leading to high hot-
tearing susceptibility.
The hot-tearing susceptibility is closely related to the
eutectic characteristics. A large volume fraction of the c/c¢
eutectic represents a large solidiﬁcation temperature range and
severe segregation at the ﬁnal stage of solidiﬁcation. A c/c¢
eutectic with a large size indicates that the isolated residual
liquid pool was large, which indicates poor coalescence of the
dendrites. The volume fraction and size of the c/c¢ eutectic
were combined and deﬁned as a eutectic inﬂuence factor E:
E ¼ Se /C3 Ve
where Se represents the average size of c/c¢ eutectic and V e is
the volume fraction of c/c¢ eutectic.
As shown in Fig. 14, all of the K418 alloys exhibit
relatively low E values, and the increasing order is K418-1,
K418-2 and K418-3. In comparison with K418, K419 and
K424 show higher E values, where K424 has the highest E
value. In fact, when the eutectic-affected zone was studied, the
differences in the E value can be greater. On the other hand, we
assessed the hot-tearing susceptibility based on the qualiﬁed
product rate and showed the results as a line chart in Fig. 14.
The result indicates that the eutectic inﬂuence factor E agrees
with the hot-tearing susceptibility well. The deﬁned E value can
be used to evaluate the effect of eutectic characteristics on hot
tearing.
5. Conclusion
(1) Hot-tearing susceptibility is closely related to the eutec-
tic characteristics since they reﬂect the solidiﬁcation pro-
cess, segregation behavior and coalescence behavior of
the dendrites. A single factor, such as the size or volume
fraction of the c/c¢ eutectic, is not sufﬁcient to evaluate
the effect of the eutectic characteristics on the hot-tear-
ing susceptibility.
(2) A large volume fraction of eutectic can reﬂect a wide-
temperature solidiﬁcation range and severe segregation.
K419 has a wide solidiﬁcation temperature range. Ele-
ment segregation also promotes the formation of a large
volume fraction of solid/liquid interfaces, resulting in a
large volume fraction of c/c¢ eutectic. A large c/c¢
eutectic size can reﬂect the element segregation behavior
and poor coalescence of the dendrites. Among the three
alloys, K424 exhibited the largest average size of the c/
c¢ eutectic and the largest size of the residual liquid
pool. Ti is thought to promote the formation of a large
isolated residual liquid pool, leading to poor coalescence
of the dendrites.
